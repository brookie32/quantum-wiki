arXiv:2507.15453v5 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Understanding how decoherence-induced loss affects entanglement and nonlocal correlations in quantum memories is important for quantum information science. Here we develop a quantitative description of photonic quantum states retrieved from electromagnetically induced transparency (EIT) memories in a ground-state-decoherence-limited regime. Using the dark-state-polariton formalism, we derive the attenuation factor of the retrieved probe mode from the EIT dynamics. This attenuation depends on the coupling field and ground-state coherence decay and is not introduced as an independent phenomenological loss parameter. Within a specified quantum-limited pure-loss model, we use this attenuation to obtain the reduced photonic density operator. We show that, in the symmetric case, the complete retrieved state of an initially path-entangled single photon remains entangled for any nonzero storage efficiency, despite acquiring a vacuum component. In contrast, violation of the unconditional Clauser-Horne inequality under displaced on/off detection without post-selection requires an intrinsic efficiency above 89.7%. We further derive the retrieved N-qubit photonic density operator for multiple independently operating EIT memories. These results establish a quantitative connection between EIT memory dynamics, retrieved quantum states, and operational nonlocality benchmarks under explicit channel assumptions.
