arXiv:2611.33854v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The distribution of the qubit energy-relaxation time (T1) in a superconducting quantum processor affects its logical error rates. However, reports on qubit fabrication quality focus on the median or best-case T1 from measurements that are not reproducible due to resonant coupling to two-level system (TLS) defects whose frequencies fluctuate in time. Here, we sweep the frequency f01 of a flux-tunable qubit over several GHz with SWAP spectroscopy, quoting the quality factor Q = 2 pi f01 T1 to make T1 comparable across frequencies. We show that the cumulative distribution of Q pooled over the sweep is reproducible across four thermal cycles spanning 326 days, up to the reshuffling of strongly coupled TLSs. In contrast to repeated measurements at a single frequency, the medians of the broadband Q distributions spread by an order of magnitude less over 3 days. The distance between the cumulative distributions of repeats comes close to its sampling floor only for the broadband sweeps. The broadband Q distribution further resolves small coherence differences between identically designed qubits on two dies of the same wafer. These results establish the broadband Q distribution as a reproducible metric for qubit fabrication quality.
