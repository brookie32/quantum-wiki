In May, Xanadu halved the best-known cost of QROM, the first major reduction in over half a decade. Four months later, Danial Motlagh, Lead Quantum Scientist at Xanadu, has halved it again. Quantum read-only memory (QROM) is how quantum algorithms load classical data, and it often accounts for the majority of their resource cost, from Hamiltonian simulation to state preparation. The idea is dense encoding: store two bits in each borrowed "dirty" qubit instead of one, using both its X and Z bases. On its own, that halves the leading Toffoli count compared with SelectSwap. Combined with our earlier sequential bit packets, it gives nearly 4x fewer Toffolis than SelectSwap (3.9x for 32-bit entries). Read the paper: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02321
