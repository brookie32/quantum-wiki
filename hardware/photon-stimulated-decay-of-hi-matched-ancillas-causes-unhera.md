arXiv:2610.17017v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Bosonic codes often read out their syndrome with a transmon ancilla whose computational levels g and f have equal parity and matched dispersive shifts: a decay fo e is then heralded and leaves the code intact, while the direct fo g is forbidden by parity. We show that the photons of the code open this decay at first order in the ancilla's decay rate. Dressing by the code mode lets the charge noise that limits the ancilla's T_1 drive a photon-stimulated Raman decay |f,nrangleo|g,n+1rangle, at (n+1)(hi_{ge}/2g_{01})^2 times the fo e rate for a smooth noise spectrum. When the matching is set by the detuning, the decay costs approxGamma_{fe}/alpha per gate and photon, whatever the coupling strength; a deliberate mismatch or matching by a drive lowers this cost, and a qubit ancilla has no such channel. The opened decay is unheralded and flips a cat with probability approx 1/2; being first order, it overtakes the second-order double decay fo eo g as the ancilla improves. In a microscopic model of the Ocelot repetition cat code, it makes the bit-flip floor of the gates grow linearly with the cat size, by a factor 1.2-1.7 from four to ten photons, which would limit the noise bias to sim 10^3. The published gate data end at four photons and do not yet test this growth.
