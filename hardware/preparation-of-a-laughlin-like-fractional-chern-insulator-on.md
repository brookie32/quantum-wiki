arXiv:2610.41017v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Fractional Chern insulators (FCIs) are lattice analogues of fractional quantum Hall states, hosting topological order and fractionalized anyonic excitations. Their realization poses a demanding target for quantum simulation. Here, we realize a bosonic FCI on an IBM Nighthawk superconducting quantum processor using classically optimized variational circuits. We target a Laughlin-like state that is an exact zero-energy ground state of a long-range parent Hamiltonian on a torus, eliminating physical edges and providing a favorable geometry for accessing fractional quantum Hall physics in a small system. On a 4imes4 qubit lattice, we prepare both the four-boson FCI ground state and a three-boson state hosting two quasiholes. Measurements of particle densities and correlations reproduce the characteristic structure of the target states, while reconstructed chiral magnetization currents resolve the circulating current pattern associated with the quasiholes. The preparation of these strongly correlated topological states with shallow circuits establishes a route toward the digital simulation and manipulation of fractionalized excitations, including measurements of topological invariants and anyonic braiding.
