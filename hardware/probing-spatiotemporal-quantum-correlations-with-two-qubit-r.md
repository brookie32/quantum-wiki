arXiv:2611.17509v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Quantum impurities and spin qubits are powerful local probes of magnetic fluctuations, but conventional relaxometry reduces their information to a local relaxation rate at a single frequency. Building on advances in two-qubit sensing of spatial correlations, we show how two complementary protocols probe finite-time correlations in interacting quantum matter. Phase-cycled Bell-state relaxometry isolates the signed contribution of correlated relaxation to the joint ground-state population. To second order in the probe-environment coupling, the resulting population contrast measures a finite-time integral of the energy-ordered cross-correlator. In the longitudinal regime, XY8 covariance probes the complementary symmetrized cross-correlator within a tunable frequency window. For a gapless quantum spin chain, the signals reveal interaction-dependent algebraic scaling and ballistic propagation. Luttinger-liquid theory links sign changes as the qubit frequency is swept to the propagation time between the probes. It also predicts distinct spatial power laws for the Bell signal before the correlation front and at late times, when the sensing window resolves the qubit frequency. Matrix-product-state calculations for the spin chain test the pre-front scaling at zero and finite magnetization and extract the propagation velocity from Bell oscillations at zero magnetization. The probe coupling geometry determines which spin and momentum sectors contribute to the measured signals.
