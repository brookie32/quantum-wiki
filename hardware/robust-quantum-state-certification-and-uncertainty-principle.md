arXiv:2607.27184v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We show that nonadaptive single-qubit Pauli measurements suffice to test whether an unknown n-qubit state rho is arepsilon-close to or O(arepsilon)-far from an ideal target state |psirangle, for all but a 2^{-Omega(n)} fraction of target states. The test uses O(arepsilon^{-2}log(1/elta)) copies of rho to achieve confidence 1-elta, which is information-theoretically optimal even among protocols with arbitrary joint measurements. The main technical innovation is an uncertainty principle for weighted generalizations of the total influence of Boolean functions. As a simple example, the unweighted variant states that mathbf{Inf}[f]+mathbf{Inf}[widehat{f}] = Omega(n), which is a natural hypercube analogue of the Heisenberg uncertainty principle (here widehat{,dot,} denotes the 2^{-n/2}-normalized Fourier transform). The weighted case generalizes mathbf{Inf}[,dot,] and mathbf{Inf}[,widehat{,dot,},] to Dirichlet energies associated with Glauber dynamics for certain dual measures on the cube.
