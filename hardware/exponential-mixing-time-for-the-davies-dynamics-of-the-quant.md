arXiv:2611.08064v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We prove exponentially slow thermalization for the quantum random energy model (QREM) under Davies dynamics. The QREM is the n-qubit Hamiltonian H=Gamma T+U, where U assigns independent Gaussian energies with variance equal to the system size to computational-basis configurations and T is the transverse field generated by single-qubit Pauli X operators. Let eta_c=sqrt{2log 2} be the freezing inverse temperature of the classical random energy model. For every inverse temperature eta>eta_c and every transverse-field strength 0leqGamma<Gamma_c(eta), where e^{etaeta_c}=2osh(etaGamma_c(eta)), we show that the mixing time of the dissipative Davies dynamics associated with single-qubit Pauli system--bath couplings grows exponentially with the system size, almost surely; the bound is unchanged if the standard zero-Bohr-frequency dephasing terms are included. The result is uniform over all Davies rates satisfying detailed balance and a polynomial upper bound and does not assume primitivity. In the spin-glass phase, the Gibbs weight is carried by a sparse family of deep classical configurations that are separated by an extensive Hamming distance. A localization theorem for the QREM [Manai, Warzel, Comm. Math. Phys. 402, 1259 (2023)] transfers this geometry to the corresponding low-energy quantum eigenstates. Consequently, the Gibbs weight of the intermediate spectral sector is exponentially small, while single-qubit bath operators have exponentially small matrix elements between the two macroscopic sides of the bottleneck. A Davies-specific flux estimate then converts these two facts into an exponential lower bound on the mixing time. This gives a rigorous open-system dynamical manifestation of the frozen, localized low-energy structure of the QREM throughout its equilibrium spin-glass phase below the quantum transition line.
