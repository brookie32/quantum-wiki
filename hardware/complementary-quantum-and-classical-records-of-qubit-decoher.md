arXiv:2605.15882v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Decoherence is usually viewed as the loss of local coherence, but it also writes information into the environment. Here we examine two complementary forms of this information. One is a recoverable quantum record: conditioning on a transverse qubit measurement reveals a Schrodinger-cat-like state in an occupation-selected collective bath mode. The other is a redundant classical which-path record distributed across physical frequency-band fragments. Using tensor-network simulations of the spin--boson model, we reconstruct the conditional bath Wigner function and resolve its negativity. At zero temperature, one natural orbital carries more than 97% of the bath one-body occupation in the chain representation. For an initial ket{+x} qubit and a pure parity-even bath, a symmetry gives an exact nonperturbative identity between the remaining qubit coherence and the overlap of the two environmental branches. Exact pure-dephasing calculations establish both conditional nonclassicality and redundant pointer information in the same system--bath state. With tunneling, a detector that excludes frequencies below 0.1omega_c resolves transient conditional negativity at the simulated temperatures T/omega_0=0.3,0.5,0.7,1; its suppression reflects both uncollected modes and thermal effects. In pure dephasing, the conditional state of the displacement-matched mode is an exact thermal mixture of displaced cat states. These results connect decoherence to phase-space tomography and motivate a readout combining qubit measurement and collective-mode Wigner tomography.
