arXiv:2611.33559v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Positive-operator-valued measures (POVMs) beyond projective simulation (aka non-projective measurements) offer advantages over projective measurements, motivating their certification directly from observed statistics. Here, we show that this certification can be achieved robustly without input randomness, using only the joint outcome distribution of two measurement devices. We consider two spatially separated observers whose only shared resource is an uncharacterised two-qubit state. Each observer performs a local measurement. In the projective-simulable case, the measurement device privately randomises over qubit projective measurements and applies arbitrary classical post-processing to their outcomes. In the noiseless regime, we identify three- and four-outcome anti-correlations that certify that both local measurements lie beyond projective simulation. To establish noise robustness, we develop a non-linear covariance witness that combines the geometry of projective measurements with a contraction bound valid for every two-qubit state. For the correlations generated by identical qubit trine measurements or identical qubit SIC-POVM on noisy qubit singlet, we obtain exact critical white-noise fractions of (1/4) and (1/3), respectively. Below these thresholds, no pair of projective-simulable measurements on any two-qubit state can reproduce these correlations. We present explicit projective simulations that attain the thresholds and cover the remaining noise range. Finally, the same method yields a strictly positive noise tolerance for certification whenever both observers apply the same extremal qubit POVM with three or four non-zero effects to a depolarised qubit singlet. These results establish a general analytic framework for certifying non-projective qubit measurement resources without input randomness.
