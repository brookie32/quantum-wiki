arXiv:2611.11393v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: A qubit that is continuously monitored from the moment it is prepared in its excited state writes evidence of that preparation into its measurement record. We ask how much control is needed to reset such a qubit to its ground state while keeping its complete record statistically close to the record of an undriven ground-state qubit. The qubit is measured in sigma_z at rate Gamma_m with efficiency eta and has no relaxation. For feedback that acts causally on the record, distinguishability is the Kullback--Leibler divergence D of the controlled record law from the reference law, and the control cost is the quadratic effort J, the integrated squared Rabi frequency. We prove that every open-loop drive leaves D infinite and that every admissible control obeys DJgeGamma_mU(eta,D/kappa_0), where U is given by a one-parameter family of integrals and kappa_0=1-ln2. Our main result is the exact high-effort constant lim_{epsilonownarrow0}inf{DJ/Gamma_m: Dleepsilon}=K(eta), where K(eta)=[int_0^pisqrt{2eta(1+osheta)^2+(1-eta)sin^2heta},dheta]^2; an explicit sequence of two-phase feedback policies attains it. The first term of the integrand is the population evidence that the record receives while the qubit is rotated toward the ground state. The second is the price of the impurity created when the unrecorded part of the measurement dephases the coherence that the rotation needs; the record receives this evidence later, when the measurement repurifies the qubit. The global constant c(eta)=inf DJ/Gamma_m satisfies K/2le F_2le cle K, where F_2 is again given by an integral; c is known exactly at eta=1 and in the limit etao0. With any nonzero relaxation rate the unconstrained infimum of DJ vanishes, so c(eta) is specific to the relaxation-free model.
