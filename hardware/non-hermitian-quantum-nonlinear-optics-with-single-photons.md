arXiv:2607.29313v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Quantum nonlinear optics seeks to harness strong photon-photon interactions for scalable quantum technologies, although dissipative losses still pose a major barrier to near-unity conversion efficiency. Here, we bridge non-Hermitian physics with the quantum nonlinear domain by exploiting perfect absorption to identify and optimize few-photon nonlinear processes. We theoretically investigate two circuit QED systems, operating in the light-matter ultrastrong coupling regime. The first (i) enables simultaneous two-atom excitations by single photons, while the second (ii) realizes the strong coupling between a single-photon and a two-photon Fock states. We demonstrate that, since the strong optical nonlinearities cause quantum spectral features to emerge already at the level of linear response theory, the perfect absorption condition in |S_{11}| enables near-deterministic single-photon down-conversion into (i) a qubit-qubit-correlated pair and (ii) a two-photon pair. We show that the conversion efficiency can be systematically optimized through experimentally accessible parameters, both linked to the emergence of Hermitian subspaces within the effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonians. These findings position non-Hermitian engineering as a broadly applicable route to optimizing quantum devices at the single-photon level, even beyond circuit-QED platforms.
