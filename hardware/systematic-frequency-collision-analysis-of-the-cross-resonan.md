arXiv:2605.07868v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Frequency crowding remains a major obstacle to scaling fixed-frequency transmon processors. Among the widely used all-microwave two-qubit gates, the cross-resonance (CR) gate is particularly sensitive to qubit-frequency spread because the conventional straddling regime condition constrains assignable qubit frequencies tightly and makes the system susceptible to frequency collisions. Here, we present a systematic framework for frequency-collision analysis and frequency-allocation optimization, focusing on the CR gate outside the straddling regime, which we refer to as the far-detuned regime. We numerically characterize frequency collisions among qubits through systematic parameter sweeps and identify collision-free conditions. We then optimize frequency allocation using mixed-integer programming on a unit-cell lattice with periodic boundary conditions. We demonstrate that far-detuned designs significantly reduce collisions compared with designs in the straddling regime. Monte Carlo yield analysis indicates that a 10% collision-free yield for a 1024-qubit square lattice at a 0.1% two-qubit-gate error threshold requires sigma_{f}/2pi le 6.86~MHz. Our frequency-allocation optimization framework could also be useful for other two-qubit gates based on fixed-frequency qubits.
