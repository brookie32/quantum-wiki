arXiv:2611.17842v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We study how the physical representation of a logical model affects sampling by quantum annealers and its use in corrected Monte Carlo. The square-lattice Ising model provides controlled classical references. Fixed embeddings with one physical qubit per logical spin reach 36imes36 spins. Comparisons with uniform two-qubit chains and mixed chain lengths show that matching mean energy leaves differences in magnetization and correlations. Extended-range conditional predictors improve the description of mixed embeddings, distinguishing their residual correlations from those of the tested uniform embeddings. Independent calibration checks and fixed-scale annealing scans show that temperature shifts alone do not determine distributional agreement. Normalized autoregressive models learned from QPU samples provide proposals with exact Metropolis--Hastings and multiple-try corrections on larger periodic lattices. These preserve the target distribution, although block updates alone relax critical magnetization slowly. Complementary direct-QPU updates reveal how block size and fitted-temperature correction affect acceptance and spin motion. Together, the results support prioritizing one-qubit representations and short uniform chains, while separating hardware sampling fidelity from the convergence of corrected Monte Carlo.
