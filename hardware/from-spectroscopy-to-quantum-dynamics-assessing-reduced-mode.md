arXiv:2611.08844v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We investigate how spectroscopic calibration and basis selection affect predictions for a dipolar-exchange gate between two optically trapped ^{23}Na^{133}Cs molecules. We construct a 128-state molecular Hamiltonian restricted to the N=0,1 rotational manifold, fit two effective optical parameters to three bright microwave transitions, and compare the resulting spectrum with four additional lines. In the coherent pair model, changing the optical polarization angle by +0.1^irc reduces the calculated gate fidelity from 94.64% to 1.09% at fixed carrier frequencies. Retuning the carriers and restoring the target Rabi frequencies recovers 89.31%. The remaining sensitivity reflects changes in the multilevel pair dynamics that are not removed by target-frequency and target-Rabi recalibration. Enlarging the molecular basis produces nonmonotonic changes in fidelity, although the 11-state and 25-state calculations give closely agreeing logical maps after phase alignment. Two controls with isolated transfer probabilities near 98.45% yield complete-gate fidelities differing by more than 11 percentage points. We relate these comparisons to a sufficient bound on the error of a projected propagator. The numerical tests quantify agreement among selected bases for a specified coherent Hamiltonian.
