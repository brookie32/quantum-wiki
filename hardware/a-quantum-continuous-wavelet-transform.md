arXiv:2611.25117v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We construct a quantum continuous wavelet transform (QCWT) on a finite scale--translation grid whose scales may be non-dyadic. A quantum Fourier transform places the amplitude-encoded signal in the frequency basis, the scale-dependent multiplier is block-encoded with one flag qubit, and an inverse Fourier transform returns the complex, quadrature-weighted CWT coefficients in the success branch. For heralded circuits that act once on one copy of the input with an input-independent output factor, we bound the success probability and attain the bound by a frequency-conditioned scale preparation. When the scale prior is prepared independently of frequency, normalizing the multiplier separately at each scale is optimal and raises the success probability by factors of 4.9 and 14.6 in our examples; on logarithmic grids this architecture loses, relative to the bound, a factor of about the admissibility constant over the squared spectral peak times the logarithmic scale range. We give a fixed-point resource estimate for a reversible Mexican-hat multiplier, the multinomial law of scale--translation measurements, and Hadamard tests for complex coefficients. We show that a Fourier-basis measurement of the input, followed by classical randomization, reproduces the flag and scale outcomes of every such heralded circuit exactly, so it is at least as informative for global scale energies; a simulation study of Hurst exponent estimation agrees. What the QCWT adds is the joint scale--translation law: two chirps with identical Fourier-basis and time-basis measurement laws have QCWT laws at total-variation distance 0.27.
