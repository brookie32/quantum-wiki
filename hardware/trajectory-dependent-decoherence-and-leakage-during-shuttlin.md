arXiv:2611.09873v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Logical qubits encoded in collective states of multiple spins are promising building blocks for large-scale semiconductor quantum processors. As novel fault-tolerant architectures increasingly rely on qubit shuttling, understanding decoherence of the shuttled multi-spin qubits becomes crucial. We analyze fidelity and leakage of the logical qubits dephased by the spatio-temporally correlated 1/f noise, considering two-, three-, and four-spin encodings. We study different shuttling protocols and establish that for all encodings generally, parallel shuttling provides the best protection against decoherence at short distances, whereas sequential shuttling outperforms others for long-range transport (although the exact boundaries depend on the specific device parameters). We explain this common behavior in terms of the dephasing factors of pairs of the shuttled spins; we provide explicit expressions for fidelities and leakage, thus enabling experimentalists to predict performance of the multi-spin qubits from only several independently measured pairwise dephasing factors. Our results establish a connection between shuttling trajectories, noise correlations, and qubit decoherence, and provide guidance for achieving noise-resilient shuttling in scalable semiconductor quantum processors.
