arXiv:2611.22156v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Which quantum causal structures can account for observed spatiotemporal correlations without access to an intervention-complete description of the underlying process? In particular, can such correlations distinguish a temporal chain, a common cause, and a common effect? Here, we address these questions using three-qubit pseudo-density operators constructed from experimentally accessible Pauli correlations. We derive necessary and sufficient conditions for pseudo-Markov compatibility with the chain, fork, and collider structures by relaxing complete positivity while preserving the corresponding quantum Markov constraints. Despite their distinct causal mechanisms, all three pseudo-Markov compatibility problems reduce to linear feasibility tests, without requiring full-rank marginal states. We further establish a unified semidefinite-programming approach to physical realizability and quantify the minimum violation of complete positivity through Choi negativity. Using a two-parameter family of pseudo-density operators, we demonstrate that different causal structures admit overlapping yet non-nested sets of correlations. Our results show that quantum causal explanations need not be unique, but experimentally accessible correlations can nevertheless distinguish and exclude candidate causal structures.
