arXiv:2611.08233v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: For unknown three-qubit pure states, we characterize the optimal trade-off among GHZ-class certification, binary probability readout of the three-tangle, and conditional state retention. The vanishing ideal of the null class specifies the optimal certification subspace, while a polynomial multiplication operator gives the spectral limit for binary readout. Four copies are necessary for a nonzero certificate. A fifth copy raises the optimal success probability from au^{2}/48 to 5au^{2}/72 and permits the original unknown state to be retained, where au is the three-tangle. We obtain this gain as an instance of a general symmetry-based additional-copy theorem. At six copies the optimal certification probability is (39+2S)au^{2}/296, with S=sum_{X}Tr,rho_{X}^{2}, whereas the largest exact binary probability is au^{2}/8. Local Schur measurements and conditional local operations with classical communication attain these bounds. Retaining the full six-copy branch record yields a weighted estimator with lower variance than binary thinning of the same data. Explicit circuits and fixed-budget comparisons connect the success probabilities to input-copy consumption, retained quantum output, and coherent gate cost.
