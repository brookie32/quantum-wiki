arXiv:2611.05976v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: For more than half a century, the standard quantum limit (SQL) was thought to limit the laser coherence mathfrak{C} -- the number of photons emitted from the laser into the beam in one coherence time -- to a scaling Theta(mu^2), where mu is the mean number of optical-frequency excitations stored inside the laser. However, recently it has been shown [Baker et al., Nat. Phys. 17, 179 (2021)] that the Heisenberg limit -- an achievable ultimate limit set by quantum mechanics for the task of producing a beam with the standard properties of a laser beam -- is mathfrak{C}=Theta(mu^4), a quadratic enhancement. So far, proposals to demonstrate beyond-SQL scaling of mathfrak{C} have been limited to circuit QED, at microwave frequencies (i.e., a maser). Here, we introduce a class of generalized far-above-threshold (FAT) laser models, obtained by replacing the ordinary bosonic lowering operator hat{a} by a general lowering operator hat{L} in the superoperators appearing in the master equation that governs the FAT laser dynamics. We then show that an optical-frequency superradiant laser in the bad-cavity limit, with feedback-controlled collective pumping, realizes one such model, in which hat{L} is identified with the collective superradiant lowering operator hat{J}. This model can surpass the SQL scaling, with the coherence exhibiting a scaling as large as mathfrak{C}= Theta(mu^{8/3}) in the optimal parameter regime. Here, in the bad-cavity limit, muapprox N/2, where N is the number of superradiant atoms.
