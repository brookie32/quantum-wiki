arXiv:2610.06113v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Allowing two qubits to interact during preparation can reduce the total cost relative to preparing them independently. We characterize this advantage for two copies of any pure qubit state, starting with each qubit in |0rangle and measuring the cost by the sum of the absolute Pauli rotation angles. Independent preparation is optimal precisely for targets on the four meridian arcs joining the north pole of the Bloch sphere to the eigenstates of X or Y. Every other pure qubit state admits a collective shortcut. For example, the equatorial states |+rangle and |Trangle differ only in their relative phase, by pi/4. The cost is additive for |+rangle, whereas a four-pulse preparation of two T states costs 13.4% less than the optimal independent preparation. Collective control can also reduce the cost while keeping the qubits unentangled throughout a continuous preparation.
