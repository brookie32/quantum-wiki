arXiv:2611.22621v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Symmetric circuits require every symmetry of the input to extend to an automorphism of the circuit. In the classical setting, their connection to descriptive complexity yields strong unconditional lower bounds. We extend this approach to the quantum model of symmetric computation defined by Castro-Silva, Gur, and Strelchuk (ITCS 2026), proving a tradeoff between circuit size and entanglement width (the smallest b such that, on every computational-basis input, the state after each layer factors into states on at most b qubits each). Our main result identifies a graph property decidable in classical polynomial time that no polynomial-size family of symmetric quantum circuits on n-vertex graphs with entanglement width o(n/log n) can compute. The bound holds for circuits with arbitrary one-qubit and two-qubit gates and unrestricted depth, and remains valid under the weaker notion of symmetry with respect to even permutations of vertices. Our proof is based on a new quantum pebble game based on the partial trace. The resulting indistinguishability theorem transfers classical pebble-game lower bounds to quantum circuits with restricted entanglement. By contrast, without entanglement restrictions, we show that every graph property in BQP can be computed by a uniform family of polynomial-size symmetric quantum circuits. Restricting to circuits with no entanglement at all, we show that polynomial-size symmetric quantum circuits with entanglement width 1 can compute every graph property definable in fixed-point logic with counting. Finally, we give an efficient symmetric implementation of QAOA for MAX 3XOR, providing a starting point for studying entanglement lower bounds in quantum optimization algorithms.
