arXiv:2611.26705v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Recent works have proposed semi-device-independent frameworks to certify non-stabilizerness, but the complexity of the stabilizer polytope makes these methods infeasible beyond a few qubits. In this work, we address this limitation by moving to partially characterized measurements in the context of worst-case random access codes. We first establish a simple condition, which we term the stabilizer pigeonhole criterion, that certifies non-stabilizerness in m-qubit pure states, and show how it can be applied to reduced states. As an application, we certify magic in W states of arbitrary size using only eight preparations and three single-qubit measurement settings. Motivated by recent work on the stabilizer marginal problem, we then construct prepare-and-measure correlations generated by global m-qubit quantum states for which every k-qubit marginal lies inside the stabilizer polytope, yet the combined correlations cannot be reproduced by any global stabilizer mixture. Finally, we derive analytical upper and lower bounds on the worst-case bias attainable with mixed stabilizer preparations in an m-qubit random access code with n Pauli measurements. For the complete set of 4^m-1 non-identity Pauli measurements, we show that the optimal quantum and mixed-stabilizer biases both scale as 2^{-3m/2}, and that their ratio is asymptotically bounded by a constant (lesssim 1.85), showing that in this setting, magic in the preparations alone yields at most a constant-factor advantage over stabilizer mixtures.
