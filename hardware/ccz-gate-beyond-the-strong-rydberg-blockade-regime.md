arXiv:2611.24078v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We present a protocol for implementing a controlled-controlled-Z (CCZ) gate on a neutral-atom platform, employing three individually addressable atoms confined in a linear array of optical tweezers and operating under finite Rydberg-Rydberg interaction (RRI). The protocol combines successive excitation of the Rydberg atoms with analytically determined pulses and uses two Rydberg levels to access opposite-sign interaction shifts. We show two different paths to get the conditional phase in the target atom: a pair of target pulses with opposite phases and a single detuned target pulse followed by a local phase correction. We derive analytical expressions for the time-integrated Rydberg populations to calculate the associated decay errors and also evaluate gate performance through master-equation simulations. Numerical comparisons with a fast Rydberg-antiblockade (RAB)-based CCZ protocol show improved gate performance at larger interatomic separations within the parameter regime considered. Monte Carlo sampling further quantifies the sensitivity to thermally induced position fluctuations. These results identify the tradeoffs between gate duration, Rydberg-state exposure, and interaction sensitivity, providing an analytically specified route to native three-qubit phase gates beyond the strong-blockade regime.
