arXiv:2611.27787v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Embedding hard operational constraints into an unconstrained Hamiltonian via large penalty multipliers induces quadratic spectral aliasing during phase estimation and increases two-qubit gate depth. This paper proposes a penalty-free, closed-loop dynamic quantum circuit scheme, designated as Zeno-decoupled gauge-ratchet optimization (Z-GRO), for constrained binary optimization without outer-loop training. Reversible feasibility phase operators within symmetric dynamical-decoupling echoes confine evolution to the feasible subspace, while an interaction-correlated commutator gauge operator resolves initial symmetry stall. Directional cooling couples the unpenalized objective Hamiltonian to a reusable ancilla with a zero-two-qubit-gate threshold offset, bypassing echoed tunneling gates on retained branches. A survival-filtered Ramsey controller advances the threshold from a linear-programming bound with automatic overshoot rollback. Exact density-matrix and trajectory simulations on spin-glass and quadratic knapsack ensembles show a single-check discrimination gain above four orders of magnitude, improving ground-state probability by factors of 3.0 to 4.3 over quantum Zeno dynamics and 11.4 to 22.5 over penalized schemes. Z-GRO reduces the spin-glass time-to-solution scaling base to 1.529 (compared to 1.684 for quantum Zeno dynamics and 1.909 to 2.047 for penalized schemes) and lowers 14-qubit knapsack time-to-solution by factors of 2.4 to 14.5. For two-qubit gate error rates between 5 imes 10^{-4} and 5 imes 10^{-3}, Z-GRO achieves 2.9 to 7.0 times the optimal probability of noisy penalized feedback, while majority shielding limits loss to 6.3% at a 5.0% readout bit-flip rate, recording 0/24, 0/25, 0/96, 3/25, and 2/80 overshoots.
