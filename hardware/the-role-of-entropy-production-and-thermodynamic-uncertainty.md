arXiv:2510.05072v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: The asymmetry between heating and cooling in open quantum systems is a hallmark of nonequilibrium dynamics. Here, we investigate the thermalization of a quantum system weakly coupled to a bosonic thermal bath under detailed balance, focusing on the entropy production rate and the quantum thermokinetic uncertainty relation (TKUR). Within the sector of states that remain diagonal in the energy basis we derive a perturbative expression for the time-dependent entropy production rate by expanding the relative entropy around equilibrium in terms of Liouvillian eigenmodes. Through this expansion, we determine that the spectral gap, the overlap of the initial state with the slowest decay mode, and the mode weight control the entropy production. Particularly for the thermal qubit, the ratio of the initial rates of heating and cooling has an exact closed form. Heating involves a higher initial entropy production, and hence faster thermalization, solely because of its larger spectral gap, i.e., the bath-temperature dependence of the dissipative rate. The TKUR is then used as a diagnostic tool that relates the asymmetry to the precision bounds on heat-current fluctuations. The analysis presented here is intended to clarify the thermodynamic origin of the asymmetry within the well-controlled Markovian case.
