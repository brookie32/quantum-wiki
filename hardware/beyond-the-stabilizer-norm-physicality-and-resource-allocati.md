arXiv:2611.27724v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Certification from restricted and uncertain Pauli measurements can be posed as a robust optimization problem: among all physical channels consistent with the data, how small can the fidelity with a target channel be? We first study a linear relaxation that ignores positivity and show that it is exactly characterized by a single scalar, the stabilizer norm of the target Choi state, s_{wit}(U)=|C_U|_{st}-1/D. We then ask whether this scalar continues to determine robustness once the positivity constraint is restored. It does not. We derive a necessary and sufficient KKT condition for the relaxation to remain tight, together with a dual obstruction certifying a strict gap, and give a rigorous computer-assisted proof that two distinct two-qubit targets can have exactly the same stabilizer norm but different physical robustness. The KKT condition depends on structure discarded by the stabilizer norm, including the support and relative signs of the Choi-Pauli coefficients and the algebraic relations among the corresponding Pauli operators. We use this structure to derive simpler sufficient certificates for a strict gap. The dual coefficients also determine how measurement uncertainty enters the finite-data fidelity bound. For a fixed budget, we derive exact shot-allocation rules for a fixed certificate and for compatible observables obtained from shared measurement settings, and we formulate the corresponding joint certificate-design problem. The conjecture that every full-support two-qubit target has positive gain remains open.
