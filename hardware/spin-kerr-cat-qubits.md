arXiv:2604.19687v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: The use of noise-robust qubit encodings provides a way of extending the lifetime of quantum information at the hardware level. In this work, we introduce the spin Kerr-cat encoding, which leverages a sweet spot near a clock transition in the spectrum of quadrupolar nuclei (having spin Igeq 1) to achieve a suppression of charge and magnetic noise leading to qubit dephasing. The basis states of the spin Kerr-cat qubit are given by the two lowest levels of a Z_2-symmetric nuclear-spin Hamiltonian and are well approximated by spin cat states for sufficiently high asymmetry parameters. For nuclei in highly asymmetric electric-field gradients, the nuclear spin could therefore be initialized in the spin-Kerr-cat subspace using SU(2) rotations and measurement rather than full SU(2I+1) control. We calculate the dephasing time of the spin Kerr-cat qubit under a model of 1/f noise, as well as relaxation of the qubit due to breaking of the Z_2 symmetry by charge-noise-induced fluctuations of the quadrupolar tensor. Using measured parameters for antimony ({}^{123}Sb) donors in silicon, we estimate that second-scale T_2^* times could be achieved with this encoding, with bitflips and leakage occurring on significantly longer timescales. We propose a two-qubit gate acting on spin Kerr-cat qubits and electron-spin qubits, enabled by adiabatically moving an electron on and off the donor potential. Based on numerical simulations, we estimate that with an enhancement of measured quadrupolar couplings by a factor of {approx} 2, a gate fidelity exceeding 99% could be achieved for spin Kerr cats encoded in {}^{123}Sb, with a gate time under 100 ns. By entangling the nuclear spin with an electron spin in this way, the spin Kerr-cat qubit could then be measured using a Pauli spin blockade readout of the electron.
