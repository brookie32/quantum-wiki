arXiv:2611.26762v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Quantum reservoir computing (QRC) exploits quantum dynamics for temporal information processing. Quantum noise affects the reservoir dynamics and can either impair computational performance or serve as a computational resource. However, the role of pure dephasing in Hamiltonian encoding QRC remains unclear. In this work, we propose a minimum complexity QRC model that harnesses pure dephasing in an open uniform XX chain. We show that, under suitable input encoding, pure dephasing can ensure the echo state and fading memory properties even without amplitude damping. Furthermore, in the presence of amplitude damping, the proposed quantum reservoir has distinct stationary states for distinct inputs, and introducing pure dephasing alters these states, thereby affecting the reservoir dynamics. Information processing capacity analysis shows that joint control of the two decoherence mechanisms regulates memory and nonlinearity, providing access to diverse capacity profiles. The proposed QRC achieves promising performance on synthetic benchmarks, while its 6-qubit configuration outperforms all three multi-reservoir echo state network baselines on over half of the real-world prediction tasks. These findings highlight pure dephasing as a computational resource for QRC.
