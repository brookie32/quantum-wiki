arXiv:2610.13417v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Josephson junctions are the central nonlinear element of superconducting qubits, yet their standard description is reached through a chain of approximations. We propose a purely quantum-mechanical model of the junction, formulated directly in Hilbert space: a charged particle moves on a one-dimensional loop, and the junction is a point at which the wave function obeys self-adjoint boundary conditions, labelled by a coupling strength lambda, which controls the transparency of the junction, and a phase jump elta, which plays the role of the superconducting phase difference. For a loop with a single junction we solve the spectral problem and show that the ground-state momentum expectation, identified with the supercurrent, factorises exactly as langlehat{p}_0rangle = I_c(elta,lambda)sinelta. For opaque junctions I_c becomes independent of elta and the first Josephson relation I=I_csinelta is recovered; for strongly attractive coupling, where the ground state is bound to the junction, the sinelta law is also recovered, but with an exponentially suppressed amplitude. For more transparent junctions, however, the model predicts deviations from the pure sinelta law: I_c acquires a dependence on elta already at order lambda^{-2}, compatible with the higher Josephson harmonics recently found necessary to describe superconducting qubit spectra, and a fully transparent contact yields a current linear in elta. When the phase varies in time, a gauge transformation shows that the voltage drop across the junction is proportional to otelta, so that the second Josephson relation emerges without being imposed. The boundary-condition approach thus reproduces both Josephson relations from first principles and predicts corrections to them, offering a route towards higher-fidelity models of superconducting qubits.
