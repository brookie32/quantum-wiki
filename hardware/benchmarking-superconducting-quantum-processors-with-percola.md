arXiv:2611.17941v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The capability to create large entanglement resources is a key performance attribute of quantum processors, and the size of the resource is often used as a benchmark of processor scale. Recent graph-state experiments have reported entanglement clusters spanning entire chips with hundreds of qubits. However, cluster size alone does not capture the entanglement quality of individual couplings or differences in coupling topology. Here, we introduce percolating entanglement quality (PEQ), a topology-normalized benchmark based on conditional two-qubit negativity and a topology-specific bond-percolation threshold. As an experimental demonstration, we prepare a native graph state on a 64-qubit square-lattice superconducting processor with defects, identify a 52-qubit entanglement cluster, and evaluate the PEQ of the processor. Applying PEQ to previously reported chip-wide graph-state measurements on heavy-hex-lattice processors, we show that PEQ differs substantially among processors whose entanglement clusters span the entire chip. PEQ provides a single chip-wide score without selecting a best-performing qubit subset or path. This score allows processors with different coupling topologies to be compared on a common scale and can inform the design of coupling topologies for scalable chips.
