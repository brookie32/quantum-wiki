arXiv:2609.16172v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Manufacturability is one of several constraints on developing solid-state quantum processors, along with qubit performance, control, connectivity, cryogenic operation, and fault tolerance. Chemical residues, structural disorder, and non-uniform interfaces introduced during fabrication can compromise qubit coherence. Two-dimensional (2D) materials, with their atomically thin crystals and van der Waals interfaces, offer routes to address some of these challenges. Their concurrent development as next-generation CMOS channel materials motivates an examination of whether relevant processes can be transferred to quantum chips. This Perspective evaluates the opportunities and limitations of 2D materials for CMOS-compatible quantum hardware.
