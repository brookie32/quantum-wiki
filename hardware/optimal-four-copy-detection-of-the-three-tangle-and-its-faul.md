arXiv:2610.27260v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The three-tangle, au_3, is a measure of genuine tripartite entanglement that distinguishes GHZ-type from W-type entanglement in pure three-qubit states. Here, we identify the statistically optimal one-sided four-copy detector of the three-tangle and study its implementation cost. The optimal detector has click probability au_3^2/48, while previous four-copy constructions achieve au_3^2/256 and therefore require asymptotically 16/3 times as many experimental repetitions. Using the representation-theoretic structure of the problem, we construct an ancilla-free implementation of the optimal detector using 15 CNOT gates, together with an 11-CNOT detector with click probability au_3^2/96. These results reveal a tradeoff between statistical efficiency and implementation cost, since reducing the number of repetitions requires increasingly expensive circuits. We then show that this tradeoff persists once the circuits are encoded. In a fully fault-tolerant setting, the increased click probability comes with a substantial non-Clifford synthesis cost, while an early fault-tolerant implementation based on T gates and STAR rotations reduces this overhead at the expense of additional encoded resources. We quantify these costs using both the Steane and [[8,3,2]] codes, providing explicit resource estimates for implementing the optimal detector across different computational regimes.
