arXiv:2611.23888v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Spatially varying magnetic fields steer charged particles along directed paths. Where a field reverses sign, particles follow snake-like orbits along the reversal line, in a direction set by both the field orientation and the sign of their energy. Transport measurements have detected such snake states, but imaging their dynamics in a reprogrammable field landscape remains challenging. Here we digitally simulate the Harper--Hofstadter model with programmable synthetic magnetic fields on a 49-qubit superconducting processor. In each Trotter step, every hopping term is one native fractional-iSWAP gate, and single-qubit Z rotations set all 36 plaquette fluxes independently, without additional entangling gates. Uniform, random, and stepped flux landscapes reveal Aharonov--Bohm suppression of diagonal propagation, disorder-induced slowing, and magnetic reflection. At a field reversal, bonding and antibonding initial states, which carry opposite energy biases, propagate in opposite directions, and both directions reverse when the field is inverted. A symmetry analysis of these reversals yields a chiral displacement of nearly one lattice spacing after ten Trotter rounds, with symmetry-breaking residuals below 0.05 lattice spacings. Bond-resolved flux programming with site-resolved imaging thus provides a quantitative tool for synthetic gauge fields and a route to time-dependent gauge fields and interacting particles.
