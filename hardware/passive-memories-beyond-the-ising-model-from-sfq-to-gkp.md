arXiv:2611.25030v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Self-correcting quantum memories are spin Hamiltonians that aim to generalize the classical memory of the magnetic hard disk (i.e. ferromagnetic Ising model) to protect against both bit flips and phase flips. Beyond the magnetic hard disk, there are other passive classical memories that are used in modern computers, notably: the flash memory in laptops (which traps charge on a conducting island), and the flux storage loop in single-flux quantum (SFQ) logic (which traps flux in a superconducting loop). In this paper we review how storage and writing work in these two classical memories, drawing analogies to the charge qubit and the flux qubit. Protection in these systems derives not from scaling a lattice to the thermodynamic limit, but from a large, tunable energy barrier that separates codewords during memory, yet can be suppressed during a write. Towards protecting a qubit: We introduce a 2D ``floating XY model'' that combines the exponential (phase-flip) protection of the floating charge island with the polynomial (bit-flip) protection of the ferromagnetic XY model. We also study a self-dual superconducting circuit that hosts GKP codewords as symmetry-broken ground states of a Z_2 imes Z_2 -symmetric circuit, and discuss its protection and limitations.
