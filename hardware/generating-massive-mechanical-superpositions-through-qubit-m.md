arXiv:2611.22428v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We propose a method towards demonstrating a quantum superposition of a massive mechanical resonator without the need for exact ground-state cooling, a requirement that becomes increasingly difficult to satisfy for low-frequency resonators. Our scheme relies on coupling the resonator to a spin-qubit. Using magnetic force coupling between the motion of the mechanical resonator and the spin-qubit, the resonator is forced into attracted and repelled states as the spin-qubit is coherently controlled. Interference between the resonator states can be observed when a series of frac{pi}{2}-pulses is applied to the spin-qubit within the coherence time of the resonator. The protocol describes a true which-path interference experiment suitable for use at ultra-low temperatures, since it requires only three frac{pi}{2}-pulses, separated in time by half a mechanical period, thus minimizing the heat-load and duration of the experiment. By analysing the experimental requirements necessary to successfully execute the protocol, we show that the protocol is suitable for resonators in a broad regime of resonance frequencies, ranging from 1kHz resonators, which have the advantage of a relatively high spin-coupling, to resonators of more than 100kHz, whose mechanical mode is easier to cool. We show that interference remains visible for resonator mode occupations exceeding 10 phonons. Lastly, we observe that an implementation of the protocol with low-frequency mechanical resonators provides a promising approach towards testing objective collapse models.
