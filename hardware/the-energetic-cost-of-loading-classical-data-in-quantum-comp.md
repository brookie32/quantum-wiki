arXiv:2611.14422v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Quantum algorithms for machine learning, chemistry, and cryptanalysis often assume coherent access to classical data at negligible physical cost. We instead treat data loading as a hardware process and quantify the dynamic energetic cost of a quantum random access memory (QRAM) query on a superconducting processor. Using the metric-noise-resource framework, we relate query fidelity and power to gate duration, relative two-qubit drive strength, and the distribution of attenuation across a multi-stage refrigerator, while accounting for thermal photons and gate errors. We analyze the linear-depth multiplexer and the noise-resilient bucket-brigade architectures, adapting the latter to a native superconducting gate set and refining its fidelity analysis using the fully decomposed gate schedule. For a representative bucket-brigade QRAM query with a 12-qubit address register we obtain a query fidelity of 0.969. The query consumes 3.51imes10^{-4},J of dynamic energy, corresponding to an average dynamic power of 4.38,W and a peak of 64.4,W. Our optimization shows that, at fixed target fidelity, the operating point and attenuation profile can change the average power by a factor of two or more.
