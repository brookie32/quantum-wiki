arXiv:2611.14358v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We develop a control strategy for fluxonium qubits based on picosecond single-flux-quantum (SFQ) pulses generated by Josephson digital circuits. A major challenge is that SFQ protocols originally designed for transmon control become inefficient for low-frequency fluxonium. In this regime, the asymmetry of flux and charge couplings leads either to excessive leakage or excessively long gate times. We show that this limitation can be overcome by two complementary protocols. The first protocol employs high-frequency SFQ pulse packets applied through the flux channel, offering a simple, analytically transparent, and robust approach for high-fidelity single-qubit control. The second protocol combines phase-shifted flux and charge pulse sequences to enhance the resonant drive and reduce the gate duration. Multilevel simulations demonstrate single-qubit gate fidelities above 99.99%; the simpler protocol remains robust under moderate variations of qubit parameters and flux bias, while the combined protocol preserves high fidelity while substantially accelerating the gate. These results establish SFQ-based cryogenic control as a realistic route to fast, accurate, and scalable manipulation of fluxonium qubits.
