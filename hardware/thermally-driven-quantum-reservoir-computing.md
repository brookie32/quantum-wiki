arXiv:2611.07187v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Heat generation limits computation, classical and quantum, from single logic gates to entire data centers. However, an in principle limiting physical factor can sometimes be turned into a resource. In reservoir computing, energy dissipation is what gives a reservoir its fading memory. Here, we introduce a paradigm where the data injection itself is encoded on the time-dependent temperature of a thermal bath. We derive an exact expression for the nonlinear response of any fixed readout, and prove a rigorous memory-nonlinearity trade-off which turns out to be a structural necessity, rather than a tunable dynamical regime, since the same coupling that injects data also enforces equilibration. We illustrate both results with a driven qubit, using a single free parameter, and with a pair of coupled qubits, where the breaking of a discrete symmetry opens a fifteen-dimensional space to a single observable, yet that observable alone recovers just over half of it. This refutes the misconception that large Hilbert spaces guarantee computational power. Our results relocate the memory-nonlinearity trade-off from the reservoir's dynamics to its measurement channel, pointing to observable choice and bath engineering (instead of Hamiltonian control) as the physical levers for a thermally-native quantum reservoir computer.
