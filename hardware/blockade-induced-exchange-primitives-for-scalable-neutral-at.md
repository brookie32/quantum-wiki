arXiv:2511.22214v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Many quantum hardware platforms natively support either phase or exchange operations, yet converting between these two forms of control typically incurs substantial overhead. Rydberg-blockade neutral-atom arrays are highly developed for phase control. Here, controlled exchange is introduced as a native, blockade-programmed phenomenon in a collective excited manifold. Target operations are engineered such that two competing exchange pathways between |01> and |10> destructively interfere, while a single collective four-photon channel mediated by a symmetric Rydberg excitation remains resonant and drives a direct SWAP, with all other qubit configurations undergoing an identity action. Exchange conditionality follows from blockade, where exciting a control atom to a Rydberg state shifts and blocks the target collective resonance, suppressing exchange, whereas leaving the control in the ground manifold enables exchange in a single step. This approach yields a family of controlled-exchange gates with coherent average gate fidelities above 99.99% at selected operating points, while reducing circuit depth and Rydberg-state exposure time by an order of magnitude relative to decomposed implementations. Joint parameter optimization reduces sensitivity to laser-intensity fluctuations, Doppler shifts, and interatomic-distance uncertainty, with several SWAP-family gates retaining fidelities above 99.6% under realistic experimental fluctuations and Rydberg decay. Finally, the anisotropic interaction generalizes the same principle to multiplexed controlled-exchange operations, providing compact hardware-level primitives for conditional information routing in extended neutral-atom arrays.
