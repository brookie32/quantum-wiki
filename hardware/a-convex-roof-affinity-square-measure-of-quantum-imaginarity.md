arXiv:2611.24311v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Quantum imaginarity characterizes the part of a quantum state that cannot be represented by real density matrices in a fixed reference basis. We study an affinity-square measure of imaginarity for pure states and its convex-roof extension to mixed states. The pure state quantity is defined by minimizing the affinity-square measure over the convex set of real states. This optimization admits a closed expression determined by the modulus of the conjugation overlap between a state and its complex conjugate. The measure vanishes precisely when the state is real up to a global phase. We prove that the convex-roof extension is faithful, convex, monotone under real operations, and nonincreasing on average under selective real operations. For arbitrary single-qubit mixed states, we evaluate the convex roof exactly and express it in terms of the imaginary component of the off-diagonal density-matrix element. We also compare pure-state imaginarity with an auxiliary affinity-square coherence functional and give the corresponding phase weights.
