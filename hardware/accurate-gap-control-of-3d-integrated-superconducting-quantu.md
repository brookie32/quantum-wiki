arXiv:2610.21435v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Flip-chip 3D integration is a key enabling technology for overcoming the routing and layout constraints of planar superconducting quantum processors, but it requires accurate and uniform control of the inter-chip spacing while preserving qubit coherence. In conventional processes, thick indium bumps are deposited symmetrically on both chips, while dedicated spacers are introduced to limit the residual tilt. Here, we present a flip-chip bonding process based on an asymmetric indium architecture, combining 20,mum thick indium bumps on the control chip with 200,nm thin indium pads on the qubit chip. Using electrical and microwave measurements, we demonstrate low resistance and low loss indium interconnects, and show that transmon qubits integrated with this process reach relaxation and coherence times of T_1=(159pm14),mus and T_{2,echo}=(294pm42),mus, respectively, comparable to planar devices. Combined with a multi angle compression procedure that suppresses systematic tilt, this approach reduces the average post bonding tilt to (20pm12),murad. These results establish an asymmetric indium architecture combined with multi angle compression as a robust, coherence preserving route toward 3D integrated superconducting quantum processors.
