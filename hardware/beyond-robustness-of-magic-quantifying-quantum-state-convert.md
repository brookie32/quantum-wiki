arXiv:2610.38401v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: In fault-tolerant quantum computing, magic states provide essential resources for implementing non-Clifford operations. The robustness of magic (RoM) quantifies the magic contained in a quantum state and is closely related to the cost of classical simulation. However, knowing how much magic two states contain is not sufficient to determine whether one can be converted into the other using free operations. In this work, we develop a geometric framework in which RoM is represented as half the distance to the set of free states, and extend this framework to state conversion. We introduce a directed conversion deficiency that measures how far a target state lies from the states freely reachable from a given input. This quantity vanishes exactly when free conversion is possible and, beyond being a mathematically well-defined measure, has an operational interpretation in terms of stabilizer quasiprobability. Furthermore, our framework provides lower bounds on the additional magic states required for conversion that can be strictly stronger than conventional RoM-based bounds. We demonstrate this distinction analytically by constructing a continuous family of mixed single-qubit states with identical RoM for which free conversion is possible in one direction but impossible in the reverse. Our results reveal that the usefulness of magic for quantum state conversion depends not only on its amount but also on the specific transformation being performed.
