arXiv:2611.25013v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Superconducting qubits provide microwave-scale transitions and thus offer quantum targets for direct particle scattering with extremely small energy transfer. We develop a general framework for inelastic scattering in which an incident particle exchanges an off-shell photon with a superconducting quantum circuit, inducing a transition from a lower-energy internal state of the circuit to a higher-energy one. The target dependence is encoded in a finite-momentum electromagnetic transition current and, for charge-dominated response, in a qubit transition form factor. A key feature of particle scattering is that a microwave-scale excitation does not necessarily correspond to a long-wavelength interaction, since nonrelativistic particles can transfer spatial momenta much larger than the deposited energy. The full finite-momentum device response is therefore essential. We specialize the formalism to transmon qubits, construct a realistic electrostatic response for a planar Xmon-style transmon, and apply it to photon-mediated scattering of eV-scale bosonic dark matter, including final-state Bose stimulation. We find that the resulting enhancement substantially improves the projected sensitivity at the lowest masses. Owing to the exceptionally low transition threshold of superconducting qubits, this approach opens sensitivity to dark matter in the eV-to-keV mass range, which remains largely unexplored by existing direct-detection experiments. We further show that N independently operated qubits give the expected rate proportional to N, while suitably prepared Dicke states can in principle achieve a collective enhancement approaching N^2 when spatial coherence is maintained across the array. These results establish superconducting qubits as quantum targets for direct particle scattering and provide a framework for optimizing low-threshold dark-matter searches.
