arXiv:2503.12424v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Always-on couplings in qubit arrays enable multi-qubit entanglement but also generate crosstalk. We present a geometric framework for a driven qubit at fixed detuning, in which the SU(2) evolution traces a curve on a sphere whose radius is determined by the detuning and the control pulse is the curve's geodesic curvature, reducing gate design to drawing a curve. Closed loops yield crosstalk-free single-qubit gates, with a zero-area condition for undetuned subspaces, while nonzero-area loops also permit entangling-gate constructions. The framework is non-perturbative in the conditional detuning, within a dispersive block-diagonal approximation valid when the transverse mixing g/Delta is small, and recovers perturbative dynamically corrected gate methods as its flat-space limit. Noise robustness can be imposed as an additional constraint. We show numerically that in two- and three-qubit Heisenberg chains the single-qubit gates are robust to coupling and frequency noise, and outperform a representative perturbative robust gate by over an order of magnitude where perturbation theory breaks down.
