arXiv:2610.36391v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Consensus algorithms are mechanisms driving nodes of a network to agreement. In quantum networks they also redistribute information encoded in the global quantum state, allowing global properties to be inferred from local measurements without reconstructing the global state. Motivated by this, we investigate decentralized quantum consensus in qudit networks governed by discrete random unitary dynamics. We characterize the analytic asymptotic dynamics, identify global information preserved by the evolution, and derive conditions for which different notions of quantum consensus emerge. A general theorem is derived that establishes a unified framework for extracting globally encoded information from local measurements beyond consensus states. First, we analyze CNOT networks and determine which global information can be recovered by individual nodes and which requires joint measurements on cooperating nodes. The CNOT model is well understood and presents a case with entanglement creation - a characteristic quantum feature of networks worth analyzing in detail. Second, we identify the complete class of two-qubit interactions that asymptotically achieve consensus on the average number of excitations, thereby establishing a quantum analogue of classical average consensus. We further show that joint measurements on k cooperating nodes recover exactly the first k moments of the excitation-number distribution.
