arXiv:2611.23383v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Microwave-to-optical quantum transduction is essential for connecting superconducting quantum processors through optical networks. Mechanically mediated transducers, however, remain limited by weak electromechanical coupling, dissipation, and added noise. In this work, we study a transducer in which an ensemble of superconducting qubits couples collectively to a shared optomechanical resonator. For weak signals, we show that ensemble size and optical pump strength together set the conversion efficiency, added noise, and bandwidth, with pump heating limiting efficiency to a finite optimal pump strength. Near half excitation, the collective coupling grows nearly linearly with ensemble size, accelerating transfer of an excitation into the mechanics. We show that this speedup is offset by relaxation of the additional excited qubits, so the single-qubit coupling and lifetime still set the coherence requirement. Transferring exactly one excitation also requires an anharmonic mechanical mode, which an auxiliary qubit coupled to the mechanics can provide. In a lossless prescribed-Kerr benchmark with four qubits and g_{rm sm}/(2pi)=3.81,kHz, a mechanical anharmonicity of 100,kHz raises the final joint single-photon intracavity target probability from 19.6% to 99.7%. Finally, we quantify the effects of decoherence, disorder, residual qubit--qubit interactions, and pump-induced qubit noise, finding that pair-dependent interactions reduce the optical retrieval efficiency from 58% to 35%. These results establish the coherence and selectivity requirements for collective quantum transduction.
