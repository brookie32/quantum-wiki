arXiv:2611.24730v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We consider the problem of a quadratically-dispersing quantum impurity interacting through a contact density-density term with a one-dimensional (1D) superfluid described by Luttinger liquid theory. By means of a field-theoretical approach based on the linked-cluster expansion, we quantitatively address the effects of a finite temperature of the 1D bath on the momentum-space single-particle Green's function. Through a careful identification of the resonant phonon-impurity scattering processes, we obtain asymptotic large-time estimates for the thermally-induced exponential damping of the zero-temperature signal both in the extit{quasiparticle} regime, dominated by the presence of a finite lifetime for the host impurity, and in the extit{infrared-dominated} regime, where the impurity causes a Anderson's orthogonality catastrophe of the 1D bath. In both cases, we identify a temperature-induced coherence time that sets the characteristic timescale of the exponentially-decaying finite-temperature correction to the amplitude of the zero-temperature single-particle Green's function. Moreover, we draw connections between the aforesaid thermal timescale and the microscopic parameters of the Lieb-Liniger model, which admits an effective Luttinger-liquid description, highlighting the dependence of the inverse coherence time on the intra-bath interactions across the different dynamical regimes of the impurity.
