arXiv:2609.03944v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We prove that, for an n-qubit system of dimension d=2^n, every state satisfying operatorname{Tr}(rho^2)le 1/(d-a_ast), with a_ast=0.458327dots, lies inside the stabilizer polytope and is therefore magic-free. Combining this result with general geometric properties of high-dimensional polytopes, we establish quantitative estimates for the Hilbert--Schmidt inradius and volume radius of the stabilizer polytope, and use them to characterize the typicality of magic in random induced states obtained by tracing out a k-dimensional subsystem from a dimes k-dimensional Haar-random pure state. We prove a sharp phase transition in the probability of such states having magic, whose transition dimension k_star is bounded between Omega(d^2/log^2d) and O(d^2). We further prove that the number of facets of the stabilizer polytope lies between exp[Omega(d^2/log^2 d)] and exp[O(d^2log^2 d)], substantially improving upon the previous quasipolynomial lower bound and implying that any exact description of the magic-free region requires a doubly exponential number of linear inequalities in the number of qubits. Overall, our results show that the stabilizer polytope exhibits near-maximal geometric complexity allowed for a high-dimensional polytope with a certain number of vertices.
