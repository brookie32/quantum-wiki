arXiv:2606.00237v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Matter-wave interferometers (MWIs) offer a uniquely quantum route to dark matter (DM) detection: DM can reveal itself through phase and decoherence between spatially separated wavepackets, even when energy deposition is negligible and no resolvable recoil displacement occurs. We formulate these effects in an open effective field theory for MWIs using the Schwinger--Keldysh formalism, which highlights a structural asymmetry between the two detection channels. For elastic spin-independent DM scattering, decoherence inherits novel Bose enhancement or Pauli blocking factors, while the phase is at most linear in the DM occupation number. By retaining the DM's coherence time, this framework spans Markovian and non-Markovian dynamics across a wide range of DM masses, and systematically organizes corrections beyond the heavy-probe limit.
