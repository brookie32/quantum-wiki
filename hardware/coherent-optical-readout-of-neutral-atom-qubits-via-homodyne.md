arXiv:2611.13799v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Fast, non-destructive qubit readout is essential for repeated sampling, mid-circuit measurement, and fault-tolerant neutral-atom quantum processing, but camera-based fluorescence detection introduces electronic latency beyond fluorescence-collection time. We propose coherent readout of the state-dependent resonant fluorescence using balanced homodyne detection with photodiodes. An analytic model quantifies the effect of atomic motion on the time-integrated quadrature signal and noise, showing that harmonic motion causes phase averaging while photon recoils produce trajectory-dependent signal fluctuations; the competition between signal accumulation and motion-induced decoherence sets an optimal detection time. Three-dimensional Monte Carlo simulations validate the model and predict state-detection fidelities exceeding 99% within 100 mus for experimentally realistic parameters. These results establish the readout conditions and physical constraints for rapid coherent fluorescence readout of neutral-atom qubits.
