arXiv:2611.27251v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Uncoupled learning, in which players know only their own payoff, are a standard model of decentralized learning toward Nash equilibrium. We study their quantum counterpart, where strategies are quantum states and, in each round, a player receives a physical copy of their opponent's current strategy and updates their own state by a quantum channel. This adds to uncoupledness a constraint with no classical analogue: every update must be physically realizable. In fact, this constraint is decisive in conjugation games, in which Bob has a strictly dominant pure strategy and Alice's unique best response is its transpose. In this work, we show some limitations of uncoupled (learning) dynamics in finding the Nash equilibrium for conjugation games. First, we prove that no autonomous, memoryless, uncoupled dynamics converge to Nash equilibrium in a family of four single-qubit conjugation games. Specifically, any memoryless channel that keeps all four equilibria fixed will leave Alice's strategy frozen. This result also extends to 4^n games on n-qubit registers. Second, we show that this barrier disappears with a small classical memory: for any finite family of L pure targets, a guess-and-test rule with O(log L) classical bits converges to equilibrium, with an explicit bound on its convergence rate. Finally, we prove that no finite memory, classical or quantum, suffices for the continuum of all pure-qubit targets. Our proof of the impossibility for memoryless channels relies on Stinespring's dilation theorem, while the impossibility for finite-memory channels relies on the optimal universal-NOT (in)fidelity bound by Buzek, Hillery, and Werner.
