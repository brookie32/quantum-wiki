arXiv:2611.22530v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Targeted recovery in modular quantum processors requires identifying the component affected by a fault. Independent qubits can monitor faults through repeated preparation and measurement. Bell-pair probes improve the guaranteed correct-site probability for unknown local channels, but require partners kept away from the fault. Can entanglement among exposed probes supply this advantage without protected storage? We establish a quantum-code criterion for optimal distributed fault sensing of one unknown local qubit channel with strength in a prescribed positive range. For one interrogation with zero false alarms and no background noise, suitable code states attain the optimal worst-case correct-site probability, improving on independent probes by 50% with every qubit exposed. Exact attainment is equivalent to mutually orthogonal input and single-qubit Pauli error subspaces. Pairwise entanglement gives no improvement, and entanglement depth at most four cannot exceed 95.54% of the optimum without protected ancillas. Approaching the optimum without protected ancillas also requires a number of probes proportional to the array size to participate in larger entangled factors. For a persistent fault under memoryless depolarizing background of fixed strength 0 < eta < 1, independent probes already attain the optimal logarithmic interrogation scaling at fixed success between zero and one and a positive false-alarm budget. In three 1000-site comparisons with specified readouts, an encoded-probe protocol certifies fewer interrogations than independent probes and only one more than optimally decoded ideal Bell pairs, while storing no protected qubits.
