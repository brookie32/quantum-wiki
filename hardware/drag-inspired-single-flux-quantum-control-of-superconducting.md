arXiv:2611.17562v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Scalable control remains a major challenge for superconducting quantum processors due to the wiring, thermal load, and hardware complexity associated with conventional microwave-based architectures. A framework for qubit control based on Single Flux Quantum (SFQ) digital electronics is presented. For transmon qubits, optimized control sequences are constructed from regular pulse trains framed by arbitrary subsequences (ramps), enabling DRAG-inspired leakage suppression in the discrete SFQ regime through genetic-algorithm optimization. For representative Y_{pi/2} gates, the resulting sequences achieve simulated coherent-control infidelities below 10^{-4}, including leakage from the computational subspace. The objective of the proposed approach is to demonstrate that coherent errors intrinsic to SFQ control can be reduced to a comparable level for a more scalable cryogenic control architecture. Memory requirements are reduced by storing only the optimized ramps, while a modular controller architecture supporting both regular and arbitrary pulse generation is proposed. The logical complexity of this architecture is estimated from the Josephson-junction counts of its constituent SFQ cells. The proposed approach therefore combines low coherent-control error with reduced memory overhead and provides a basis for scalable cryogenic SFQ control architectures.
