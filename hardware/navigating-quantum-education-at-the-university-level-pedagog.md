arXiv:2611.27632v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Instruction in quantum mechanics (QM) is often categorized by its entry point as 'wave-first' or 'spin-first', yet these labels lack consistent definitions and overlook other established or emerging approaches. Moreover, while capturing broad differences in topic selection and sequencing, this binary framing obscures critical differences in contextualization--how three dimensions (mathematical tools, phenomena and experiments, and theoretical concepts) are framed and connected to support student sensemaking. Research also rarely examines how such pedagogical choices unfold across the undergraduate-to-graduate trajectory. Using textbooks as an analytical lens to map these choices and characterize overarching pathways, encompassing entry points and overall orientations, we comparatively analyze the selection, sequencing, and contextualization of core topics across these 3 dimensions, alongside prefaces and introductions, in 36 university QM textbooks spanning countries and eras. At the topic level, we show that identical topics occupy widely different curricular roles depending on their contextualization. Synthesizing these findings into pathways, we identify three additional entry-point archetypes (labeled path-, qubit-, and axiom-first) and characterize all 5 approaches, showing that most textbooks combine multiple archetypes in their opening chapters. Extending this synthesis beyond entry points, we map textbooks' overall orientations relative to mathematics, experiment and application, and theoretical physics. Interpreting these profiles alongside authors' rationales reveals how educators navigate competing trade-offs among mathematical accessibility, theoretical generality, physical motivation, and modern developments. This study provides a multidimensional framework, substantive examples, and a practical roadmap for textbook selection and curriculum design in QM education.
