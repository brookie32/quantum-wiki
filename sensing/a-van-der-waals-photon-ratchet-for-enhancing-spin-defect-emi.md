A van der Waals heterostructure comprising hexagonal boron nitride with its negatively charged boron vacancy (V_B⁻) and lead iodide (PbI₂) boosts the defects’ photoluminescence by up to 45‑fold at cryogenic temperatures while remaining ultrathin for device integration. A quasi type-I band alignment and strong spectral overlap enable efficient interlayer coupling and fluorescence resonance energy transfer, producing a photon‑ratchet effect where PbI₂ absorption generates a nonlinear polarization that selectively excites V_B⁻. This architecture preserves compatibility with ultra‑thin optoelectronic platforms, offering a powerful route for spin‑defect based quantum sensing and information processing.
