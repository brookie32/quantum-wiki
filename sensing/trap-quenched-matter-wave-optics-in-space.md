arXiv:2606.14577v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Dual-species atomic sources in space promise to be the testbed for a multitude of searches in quantum gas physics such as a precise test of the Universality of Free Fall (UFF), few-body physics, cold molecules and quantum bubbles. These experiments demand exquisite control over the expansion energies of both condensed ensembles as well as over their differential center-of-mass dynamics. We propose a trap-quenched collimation technique featuring in-trap excitations of collective modes compatible with state-of-the-art atom-chip setups. Using NASA's Cold Atom Laboratory aboard the International Space Station, we demonstrate it on a single-species ^{87}Rb condensate. By controlling the center-of-mass release dynamics, we observe free expansion times up to 700 ms and measure a two-dimensional expansion energy of k_B dot 78pm 9 ;pK in the imaging plane. A detailed model of the magnetically-induced dynamics indicates that this corresponds to a two-dimensional expansion energy of about k_B dot 15^{+12}_{-5}; pK along two of the condensate's eigenaxes. Finally, we theoretically study this trap-quenched collimation scheme for a ^{41}K-^{87}Rb mixture, predicting a simultaneous collimation that meets the expansion energy requirements for a state-of-the-art UFF test at the 10^{-15} accuracy level.
