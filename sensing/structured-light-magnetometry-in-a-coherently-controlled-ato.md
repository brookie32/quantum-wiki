arXiv:2603.25781v3 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: A structured-light-based approach to magneto-optical rotation (MOR) magnetometry is proposed, in which magnetically induced polarization rotation is converted into a directly observable spatial displacement. A radially polarized Laguerre--Gaussian beam propagating through cold ^{87}Rb atoms in the presence of a longitudinal magnetic field experiences circular birefringence, which induces a relative phase shift between the sigma_+ and sigma_- polarization components. This phase shift manifests itself as the angular rotation of a petal-shaped interference pattern, enabling the MOR angle to be determined directly from the spatial intensity distribution. Unlike conventional MOR detection techniques based on balanced polarimetry or Stokes-parameter reconstruction, the proposed method requires only a fixed-axis polarization projection and avoids computationally intensive polarization analysis. By translating polarization rotation into a topology-dependent spatial observable, the scheme enables direct and spatially resolved magnetic-field measurements. Furthermore, the use of electromagnetically induced transparency (EIT) allows low-loss operation while preserving high sensitivity. These characteristics make the proposed technique a promising platform for optical magnetometry and structured-light-based quantum sensing.
