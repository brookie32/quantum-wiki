arXiv:2611.17366v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Spatially resolved magnetic-field imaging reveals material properties and local currents in microelectronic devices. Conventional pulsed scanning nitrogen-vacancy (NV) magnetometry (SNVM) faces increasingly stringent microwave (MW) pulse-control requirements for detecting radio-frequency (RF) magnetic fields at frequencies ranging from tens to hundreds of megahertz. To address this challenge, we implement an RF-dressed continuous-wave (CW) sensing scheme for SNVM and demonstrate AC magnetic-field imaging at 33.5 MHz. The method exploits electron-spin double resonance in the NV spin-1 three-level system: the target RF magnetic field coherently couples two spin eigenstates, forming RF-dressed states whose splittings are detected in continuous-wave electron-spin double resonance (CW-ESDR) spectra under continuous laser, MW, and RF excitation. This approach avoids short, high-power MW pulses and synchronization between the RF field and a pulsed sensing sequence. Using an NV-ensemble diamond probe integrated with an atomic force microscope (AFM), we image the Oersted field of an RF-driven L-shaped microcircuit. Successively selecting three crystallographic NV axes by static bias-field control yields maps of RF magnetic-field projection magnitudes, with distinct distributions qualitatively reproduced by Biot-Savart calculations. Transverse bias-field control also tunes the RF-coupled spin-state transition frequency from 14.4 to 89.3 MHz, as characterized by spectroscopy and Hamiltonian analysis, with RF-dressed resonances directly observed in CW-ESDR spectra at 89.3 MHz. Together with previous RF magnetic-field detection up to 632 MHz using the same RF-dressed CW sensing principle in a confocal NV setup, these results support extending AC magnetic-field imaging with SNVM toward the hundreds-of-megahertz regime and, with additional phase information, vector magnetic-field reconstruction.
