arXiv:2611.33481v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Diamond quantum sensors promise both high spatial resolution and high sensitivity; however, conventional photoluminescence-based spin readout can be difficult to integrate efficiently. Infrared absorption by nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centres in diamond provides a promising alternative, although the sensitivity of compact absorption-based sensors is limited by the short optical interaction length between the probe field and the NV ensemble. To overcome this limitation, we demonstrate, for the first time, singlet-absorption magnetometry using spin-dense diamond nanocavities. By probing cavity modes near the 1042 nm NV singlet transition, we observe spin-dependent cavity transmission and perform cavity-enhanced magnetic field sensing. We further develop and experimentally validate a model that accurately predicts the spin-dependent cavity response, describes the optimal operating conditions, and identifies the practical limitations of different optically detected magnetic resonance modalities. Together, these results establish cavity-enhanced singlet absorption as a scalable readout mechanism for integrated diamond quantum sensors and provide a practical framework for engineering next-generation nanophotonic diamond quantum magnetometers.
