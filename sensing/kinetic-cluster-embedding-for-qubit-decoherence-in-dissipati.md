arXiv:2611.08830v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Interpreting the decoherence of a spin qubit requires knowing how the surrounding bath spins behave: whether they fluctuate independently, exchange polarization incoherently, or evolve as coherent groups. These processes can yield the same Hahn-echo coherence time yet produce different coherence signals under pulse control of the qubit. Existing theoretical approaches either capture bath correlations at a cost that grows combinatorially with spin-bath cluster size or neglect polarization transport altogether. We introduce kinetic cluster embedding (KCE), which uses a single dimensionless parameter, comparing the flip-flop coupling of a spin pair with its detuning and linewidth, to decide how each pair must be treated. Pairs that can complete an appreciable exchange oscillation are propagated with the full dissipative quantum dynamics. All other spins are treated as random-telegraph fluctuators coupled through a kinetic network of polarization-transfer rates. We show that for nitrogen-vacancy centers coupled to surface electron spins, KCE propagates about 7 four-spin clusters, compared to more than 10^5 propagated by fourth-order master-equation cluster-correlation (ME-CCE4); nevertheless it reproduces ME-CCE4 coherence times to within 15% for dipolar couplings of order 10^{2} times the bath relaxation rate. For a three-dimensional P1 bath, the agreement is within 16%. Coherent multispin clusters emerge as the leading correction to independent fluctuators, and they leave a distinct signature in the CPMG response. Importantly, we find that whether a rate description suffices is set not by coupling strength alone but by whether a pair can exchange polarization coherently before detuning and damping suppress it.
