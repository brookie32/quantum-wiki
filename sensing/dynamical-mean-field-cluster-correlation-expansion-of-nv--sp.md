arXiv:2611.02528v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: The Hahn-echo time T_2 of nitrogen-vacancy (NV^-) ensembles in nitrogen-rich diamond follows the empirical relation T_2dot[N] = 160 mus ppm, and this relation does not depend on the magnetic field between 2 and 30 mT. Cluster-correlation-expansion (CCE) calculations solve the spin dynamics exactly within small groups of spins. When they treat the Overhauser field of the surrounding P1 centres, that is, the field that these electron spins produce, as frozen, they overestimate T_2 at high field by up to a factor of two. This excess was recently attributed to additional dark defects. We show that the excess comes mostly from the frozen field. In our model the P1 centres outside each cluster flip-flop in pairs: two centres exchange their spin states and conserve their total S_z. Kinetic Monte Carlo generates these events at golden-rule rates, which follow from the dipolar Hamiltonian without adjustable parameters, and each cluster evolves in the resulting time-dependent field. This dynamical mean field lies between the static-mean-field and mean-field-free limits. For a ^{14}N P1 bath it reduces T_2dot[N] at 30 mT from 264 pm 16 to 188 pm 12 mus ppm and gives 167 pm 11 mus ppm at 10 mT, 1.7 and 0.4 standard deviations from experiment, with no dark spins. When each centre jumps independently, T_2dot[N] is 147 pm 9 mus ppm at 30 mT; the correlation of the flip-flop partners thus lengthens T_2 by 20-30 percent at high field. The Overhauser field is neither frozen nor averaged out by fast motion: 68-83 percent of its autocorrelation survives 200 mus. At 2.5 mT, where the P1 electron and nucleus hybridise, T_2 stays below experiment for any choice of rates. Part of the field dependence therefore remains: T_2 rises by a factor 1.69 pm 0.07 instead of 2.15 pm 0.08 from 2.5 to 30 mT.
