arXiv:2611.33360v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Nitrogen is essential in agricultural fertilizers, yet its nanoscale interfacial dynamics remain largely unexplored. Although quadrupolar broadening complicates ^{14}N nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) detection, ^{15}N (I=1/2) provides well-defined nuclear-spin signatures but remains challenging to detect at the nanoscale due to its low natural abundance and small gyromagnetic ratio. Here, we use a microfluidic quantum sensing platform based on shallow nitrogen vacancy (NV) centers in diamond to detect ^{15}N NMR from KNO_{3} solution at room temperature and in a low magnetic field regime. Within femtoliter-scale sensing volumes, we measure a magnetic-field variance of up to ~442 nT^{2}, ~43 times greater than the expected statistical polarization. The ^{15}N spin correlation decays within ~500 mus, mainly limited by the NV spin lattice relaxation time and not by the molecular diffusion of the KNO_{3} molecules. This is consistent with the formation of a KNO_{3} adsorption layer on the diamond surface, supported by surface characterization.
