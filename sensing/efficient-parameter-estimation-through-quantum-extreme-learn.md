arXiv:2611.18329v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Quantum sensing protocols rely on the efficient processing of measurement outcomes to infer unknown physical parameters. Conventional optimal estimators, such as Bayesian inference and maximum likelihood estimation, require to construct a likelihood function whose efficient classical construction requires full knowledge of the dynamics, which may practically be infeasible. Model-free experimental construction of the likelihood is also very resource intensive. Here, we propose a quantum extreme learning-based framework for quantum parameter estimation which avoids the explicit construction of the likelihood function. We demonstrate the effectiveness of this approach using the transverse-field Ising model for estimating the external transverse field. Our results show that high estimation accuracy can be achieved using measurements at only a small number of training points. We further develop an interrogation-time optimization strategy that minimizes the estimation inaccuracy while keeping sampling fluctuations below a prescribed threshold. We further extend the framework to a two-time measurement protocol, in which measurement outcomes obtained at two distinct times are combined to enrich the feature space, thereby significantly enhancing the estimation accuracy. Our analysis shows that the estimation precision improves monotonically with the number of training points, while increasing the system size generally enhances the sensing performance, albeit non-monotonically. Finally, the protocol is shown to be robust against dephasing, making it suitable for near-term quantum devices.
