arXiv:2605.09709v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Detection based on quantum principles such as entanglement has the capacity to achieve finessed levels of sensitivity, bringing transformative impacts to applications. In this study, we propose a rotation sensing protocol using an extended Bose-Hubbard model in a four-site configuration. The design, based on a simple population imbalance measurement to quantify rotation, profits from the property of superintegrability. The implementation of the sensing protocol achieves rotation-detection sensitivity scaling as N^{-3/2}, where N is the number of particles. Remarkably, the sensitivity lies close to the quantum Cramer - Rao bound. Our results illustrate superintegrability opportunities for advancing the field of quantum sensing.
