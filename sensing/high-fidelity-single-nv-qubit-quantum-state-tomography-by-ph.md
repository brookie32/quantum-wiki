arXiv:2603.16502v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: The nitrogen-vacancy (NV) center in diamond is one of the advanced candidates for solid-state quantum computing at room temperature and has proven to be competitive in terms of gate fidelity and the availability of quantum error correction codes. Ambient solid-state qubits may therefore provide quantum computing architectures as an alternative to current solutions. The NV center has an associated electronic spin that is conventionally read out via photoluminescence. As an alternative, the electrical readout is not limited by the confocal resolution of quantum microscopes, which is required in the development of NV-based quantum processors to establish a scalable individual single-spin readout on the order of a few nanometers (necessary for strong dipole-dipole coupling). In this work, the performance of photoelectric readout versus traditional optical readout is investigated to perform Quantum State Tomography measurements on a single NV center. A state fidelity of 0.995 pm 0.006 is achieved using photoelectrical spin-state readout for state reconstruction, comparable to simultaneous optical measurements. We report photocurrent signal delays, which significantly slow down the photocurrent tomography. We develop a model describing their origin, and propose strategies for their mitigation.
