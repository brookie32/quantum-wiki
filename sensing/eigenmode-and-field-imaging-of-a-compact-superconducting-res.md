arXiv:2611.13791v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Superconducting resonator arrays can tailor the photonic environment for quantum information processing, but conventional port-based microwave spectroscopy alone does not resolve the rich spatial structure of their modes. Here, we apply laser scanning microscopy (LSM) and nitrogen-vacancy (NV) center magnetometry to a two-dimensional superconducting resonator array consisting of 33 lumped-element resonators arranged in a honeycomb lattice hosting topological edge states. We demonstrate the complementarity of the two techniques: LSM images the eigenmodes, while NV magnetometry maps the local microwave magnetic field amplitude. Using this spatial information, we experimentally validate that the realized lattice matches the design, we image the topological edge state and measure its sublattice polarization, and place an upper bound of approx0.3% on the relative on-site frequency disorder in the lattice. Our work establishes a combined spatial characterization approach for compact superconducting resonator arrays with applications in hybrid spin--superconducting quantum systems.
