arXiv:2610.28204v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The five-qubit Absolutely Maximally Entangled AME(5,2) state, whose every pair of qubits is maximally mixed so that all its information lives in non-local correlations, could in principle serve as a single shared quantum networking resource for teleporting two unknown qubits in parallel (each via its own Bell measurement and Pauli correction). Nonetheless, its hardware implementations remain scarce despite its theoretical importance. Here, we demonstrate parallel teleportation based on a five-qubit absolutely maximally entangled state, AME(5,2), on the superconducting processor ibm_pittsburgh. We construct three-qubit Clifford decoders that extract two Bell pairs using three logical two-qubit gates for each of 60 inequivalent sender-receiver assignments. Using parallelization and classical Pauli frame post-processing, we retain reference-receiver entanglement on both channels after three teleportation rounds. With XY4 dynamical decoupling and the lowest-cost decoder, the final entanglement fidelities are 0.704pm0.028 and 0.680pm0.022, where uncertainties are pointwise 99% confidence-interval half-widths over eight trials. Their lower bounds exceed the separability threshold of 1/2. The decoder with lower circuit depth generally retains higher mean fidelity, and XY4 generally improves on XX. These results establish a hardware demonstration of two-channel entanglement transmission through repeated AME-assisted teleportation and provide compact decoding circuits for reconfigurable multipartite communication.
