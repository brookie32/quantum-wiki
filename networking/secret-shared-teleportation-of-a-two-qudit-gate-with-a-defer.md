arXiv:2610.42150v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: An unknown quantum operation, like an unknown quantum state, cannot be perfectly copied or stored for later use. A specific gate can be embedded in an entangled resource and teleported, but its destination is fixed when the resource is created. Here we show that the capability to apply a two-qudit entangling gate can instead be secret-shared among many parties: the shares are distributed in advance, and a later key release lets exactly one of them, chosen after distribution, exercise it. A provider encrypts half of a Bell pair held with a user into n shares, one per candidate node. The selected node, given the key qudits, completes an exact non-local mathtt{CSUM}^{k} gate with the user's data, in every measurement branch and for any qudit dimension, with the power k chosen by the user alone. Any coalition of fewer than n candidate nodes, or the key register alone, holds no information, unselected nodes cannot tell whether a selection occurred, and the key cannot decrypt twice. For two candidates the registers form a ((3,5)) threshold scheme. Encoder and decoder are explicit O(n) circuits. The construction supports jointly authorized and anonymous gate application, advancing distributed quantum computing and quantum network capabilities.
