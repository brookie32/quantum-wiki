arXiv:2603.23036v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: We study zero-uncertainty states (ZUSs) for projective readout with quantum memory. In such tasks, Alice and Bob first share a quantum state. Alice then chooses one measurement from a prescribed family of projection-valued measures (PVMs), performs the measurement, and announces her choice. Bob must infer Alice's outcome from his memory system with zero error. We prove that the admissible shared states are governed by the operator algebra generated by the entire PVM family, and in the physically important case where both systems have equal dimension and the measurements generate the full matrix algebra, every ZUS must be pure and maximally entangled. As a further application to quantum state verification, we use this purity result to give a direct proof that the target maximally entangled state is uniquely verified, extending Zhu's uniqueness criterion to degenerate projective measurements. Moreover, for protocols built from rank-one binary settings, we find an optimal spectral gap of (3-sqrt3)/6 using three settings on two qutrits in d=3, and we also characterize all strategies attaining this gap. Finally, we show that different relaxations of exact readout yield inequivalent notions of approximate ZUSs depending on the task.
