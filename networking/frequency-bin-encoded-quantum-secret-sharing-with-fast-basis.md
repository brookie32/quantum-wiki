arXiv:2611.26816v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Frequency-bin encoding offers unique opportunities for fiber-native quantum information processing, yet its full potential has been hampered by challenges in rapid state control and protocol scaling. Leveraging high-speed microwave processing, we overcome several extant hurdles and unlock new fundamental capabilities in frequency-based quantum communications. First, we demonstrate three-party quantum secret sharing, representing, to our knowledge, the first multiparty (Ngeq 3 users) quantum communication protocol implemented entirely with frequency encoding. Second, we demonstrate active, pseudorandom switching of frequency-bin quantum states and measurement bases at 20 MHz symbol rates, enabling real-time protocol operation without relying on hardware-intensive passive basis-selection schemes. Even under fast switching, we preserve high-contrast three-party correlations (geq 0.91) and low quantum bit error rates (leq 4.5%). Our work extends frequency-bin quantum communication beyond static, two-party protocols, opening a pathway toward scalable, reconfigurable, and real-time spectrally encoded quantum networking.
