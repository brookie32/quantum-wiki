arXiv:2611.13625v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The ability to dynamically route quantum information across multiple nodes is a key requirement for realizing versatile and scalable quantum networks. Quantum routers are devices to achieve packet-level routing beyond static point-to-point connections by handling hybrid packets combining classical headers and quantum payloads. Previously proposed methods have selectively accessed the classical and quantum components of the packets utilizing wavelength splitters and optical switches, and repacked the hybrid packets before transmission through the outgoing link with regenerating classical headers. This work reports a novel optical-fiber-based quantum router that minimizes the disturbance and latency on the hybrid packets through more transparent operation. We experimentally demonstrate double-hop routing via two cascaded routers to three distinct receivers without regeneration of the headers. As a practical application, quantum key distribution packets based on time-bin qubits and a BB84 protocol with a two-decoy-state method are successfully delivered to the three receivers achieving nonzero secret key rates. These results demonstrate the feasibility of extensive quantum networks interconnected by multiple-step router nodes.
