arXiv:2610.36972v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We study the persistent quantum and classical memory required for exact heralded storage and retrieval of quantum measurements. Given only black-box access to the measurement channel, we construct an exact compression of the port-based program into an outcome-type record and a quantum register, with total dimension polynomial in the query count. For every universal retrieval processor we prove the bounds Q+Cgelog_2 n and 2Q+Cgelog_2igl((n-1)d^2+1igr) on the numbers Q of qubits and C of bits of the program, for n-outcome POVMs on C^d with dge2. For nge2, no finite classical memory removes the need for quantum memory: any such processor requires a quantum program of dimension at least d, at that dimension its classical alphabet must have at least n symbols, and both minima are attained by a single-query protocol with success 1/d^2. Combining direct-program compression with a converse from approximate measurement programming gives logarithmic total-memory scaling in 1/(1-p) as the uniform success probability p approaches one, at fixed d,nge2. At fixed input dimension and target success below one, the optimal total-memory scaling is logarithmic in n. We also construct self-contained programs under branch-decomposition access and compare the quantum and classical costs of all constructions, including where the outcome-type record is kept: for POVMs with n nonzero rank-one effects the direct program admits a purely quantum allocation whose register is never larger than that of the minimal-Naimark unitary program and which succeeds more often whenever n>d.
