arXiv:2606.24170v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: In surface-code magic-state factories, the interface that passes level-1 T states to level 2, i.e., the transport channels, waiting spots, and |0rangle correction patches, occupies a large part of the factory area. We remove this interface by applying adaptive measurement, a principle known from gate teleportation, to the consumption of T states: in adaptive X/Y consumption, the T-state patch used for a pi/8 rotation is read out in the X or Y basis depending on the merge outcome. The basis choice itself performs the pi/4 correction, so no correction patch is needed and a T state can be consumed wherever it already sits. Building on this property, we present direct-coupled architectures in which level-1 suppliers are attached directly to the level-2 core. D1 consumes each T state in its supplier output slot and removes the interface entirely; D2 adds one small buffer per supplier so that the supplier continues distilling without pause. Because the method changes only how a supplied T state is consumed, it applies to the many factories that perform pi/8 rotations by multi-patch Pauli measurements. As an example, we apply it to a CCZ factory and compare it with Litinski's factory under the same logical-error model and the same area accounting. For a maximum output period of 120 cycles, D1 and D2 reduce the area by 24% and 29% without increasing the space-time volume, and the smallest D1 factory is 37% smaller than the smallest Litinski design. The same approach also reduces the area of two-level T factories.
