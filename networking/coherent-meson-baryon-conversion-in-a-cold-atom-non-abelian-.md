arXiv:2611.31888v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Quantum chromodynamics (QCD), the non-Abelian gauge theory of quarks and gluons, provides a microscopic description of strongly interacting matter, including hadron structure and dynamics. Real-time dynamics and finite-density properties of these systems remain challenging for conventional Monte Carlo methods because of sign problems. Quantum simulation provides a complementary approach, although circuit depth and noise limit digital implementations and non-Abelian gauge-matter interactions remain difficult to engineer in analog systems. Here we realize a non-Abelian lattice gauge model with dynamical matter in an analog cold-atom simulator and directly observe its hadronic dynamics using site- and spin-resolved quantum gas microscopy. This realization is enabled by harnessing two hyperfine states and site-occupation configurations of 87Rb atoms to jointly encode both matter and gauge fields. We tailor resonant tunneling in a spin-dependent optical superlattice to implement a gauge sector of a truncated one-dimensional SU(2) quantum link model with independently tunable mass and gauge coupling. Leveraging this control, we track the conversion of an initially mesonic state into baryonic or vacuum-like configurations through slow mass ramps. Near resonance, we observe coherent oscillations between mesons and baryon-antibaryon pairs and characterize the conversion efficiency as a function of mass, gauge coupling and net baryon density. Our realization establishes an atomic platform for non-Abelian gauge dynamics, opening a route to microscopic studies of nonequilibrium dynamics in strongly interacting matter.
