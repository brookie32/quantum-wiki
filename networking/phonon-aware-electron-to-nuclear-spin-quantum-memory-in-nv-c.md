arXiv:2611.33936v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers in diamond are a promising solid-state platform for quantum memory: the electron spin can be optically initialized and read out, while nearby nuclear spins have long coherence times and can serve as storage qubits. In this work, we develop a phonon-aware open quantum system model for electron-to-nuclear spin state transfer in a single NV center. The model includes the NV electron spin, a coupled 13C nuclear spin, the hyperfine interaction, an effective microwave/radio-frequency-assisted SWAP interaction for writing and reading, a single effective phonon mode with longitudinal spin-phonon coupling, and Lindblad relaxation and dephasing channels. The dynamics are simulated with the Lindblad master equation in QuTiP, using the secular approximation and a hyperfine-resonant drive in the rotating frame. Without spin-phonon coupling and electron decoherence, the write-store-read protocol recovers the stored state with a fidelity above 0.9999. With the default decoherence rates, increasing the spin-phonon coupling from 0 to 0.06 MHz lowers the worst-case retrieval fidelity from 0.9994 to 0.9884, and the fidelity oscillates with the phonon-mode period because electron-phonon entanglement generated during the write step is partially undone during the read step. With a phenomenological temperature-dependent rate model, the fidelity decreases monotonically from 0.9997 at 1 K to 0.9938 at 300 K, while storage-time losses follow the assumed nuclear-spin decoherence rates. The storage-time plots, temperature sweep, and parameter maps identify operating regimes for NV-center quantum memory, and the accompanying open-source code provides a reproducible framework for studying phonon-limited quantum memory.
