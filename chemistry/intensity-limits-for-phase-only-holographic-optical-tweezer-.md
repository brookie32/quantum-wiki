arXiv:2611.10302v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Optical tweezers use tightly focused laser beams to trap and manipulate individual particles, enabling advances in quantum computing, drug discovery, and biological research. Scaling these technologies to large arrays requires delivering sufficient light to every trapping site while minimizing the total laser power required. Here, we investigate the fundamental limits of how efficiently a phase-only hologram can distribute light across an array of optical traps. We derive upper bounds on the minimum intensity across trapping sites, accounting for illumination, target geometry, and diffraction from spatial light modulator pixels. For arrays arranged in a single plane or across two axial planes, we show how these limits scale with the number of traps and SLM pixels, and establish conditions for focusing light in three dimensions. Numerical experiments demonstrate that optimized holograms can closely approach these theoretical limits, suggesting that further improvements in phase optimization alone offer limited power savings. Instead, our results reveal how the choice of illumination and trap geometry can improve light efficiency, providing practical guidelines for designing larger, more power-efficient optical-tweezer arrays.
