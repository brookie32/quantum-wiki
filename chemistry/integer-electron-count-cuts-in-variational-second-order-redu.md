arXiv:2610.36514v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Van Aggelen et al. [Phys. Chem. Chem. Phys. 11, 5558 (2009)] showed that variational second-order reduced-density-matrix (2-RDM) calculations with the standard two-index conditions dissociate diatomic molecules into fractionally charged atoms with energies far below the exact limit, a drastic violation of size consistency; the proposed subsystem constraints require auxiliary calculations with the physical fragment Hamiltonians. We show that two long-known ingredients, combined, remove most of this failure without such calculations: the requirement that the number of electrons in a part of the molecule be an integer, imposed as linear conditions on the 2-RDM, and a chemically motivated partition of the molecule into atomic regions bounded at the bond critical point of the Hartree-Fock density. In a minimal basis, compared with full configuration interaction, a single such condition reduces the energy errors of NO^+ and CN^- at 6 {AA} from 108.9 and 80.7 mE_h to less than 0.02 mE_h. In N_2^+, whose fragments have the correct mean population but too small a fluctuation, the region and three-index conditions act complementarily, reducing the error at 4 {AA} from 138.0 to 1.6 mE_h. At the separated-atom limit, the region cuts make the P, Q, G energy additive over the fragments to within 0.2 mE_h in 7 of 10 systems, 3 already nearly additive without cuts; adding the T_1 and T_2 conditions also makes CO nearly additive, while a nonadditive error remains for N_2^+ and CO^+. Effective partitions are those whose condition is nearly saturated by the exact state.
