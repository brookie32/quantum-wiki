arXiv:2611.18122v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Determining the energy spectrum of a many-body quantum system is a central goal of quantum physics. Ground-state energies underpin materials science and quantum chemistry, whereas excited states govern dynamical phenomena such as quantum thermalization. Quantum simulators have greatly shaped our understanding of many-body systems, for example, by enabling access to microscopic mechanisms and nonlocal order parameters. However, experiments still struggle to access the full range of many-body spectral features and are typically limited to measuring energy gaps and excitations by spectroscopic means. Here, we address this challenge by demonstrating quantum phase estimation on an analog Rydberg quantum simulator. We reconstruct the complex-valued return amplitude, or Loschmidt echo, of an initial probe state. From its Fourier transform, we measure absolute ground-state energies and energy gaps in systems of up to 19 atoms in one and two spatial dimensions. Varying the initial probe state, we select distinct spectral windows within which we compute the longitudinal and transverse magnetization, and observe their competition across the many-body spectrum. Our results thereby demonstrate phase-sensitive many-body spectroscopy on an analog quantum simulator without auxiliary qubits or controlled evolution.
