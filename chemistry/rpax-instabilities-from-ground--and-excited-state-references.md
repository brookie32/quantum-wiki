arXiv:2610.36154v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Random-phase approximation with exchange (RPAx) calculations based on ground- or excited-state references can yield complex excitation energies, causing the corresponding RPAx correlation energy to become complex as well. In this work, we investigate the origin of these complex energies using e{H2} in the 6-31G basis set as a minimal model. For the ground-state restricted Hartree-Fock reference, complex roots emerge at the well-known triplet instability associated with the appearance of a lower-lying unrestricted, symmetry-broken solution. For excited-state references, we identify two distinct mechanisms leading to complex excitation energies. The first is similarly associated with symmetry breaking. The second arises when a positive-energy excitation becomes degenerate with the positive-energy de-excitation associated with a negative-energy transition. Finally, we demonstrate the practical implications of these findings for spin-flip Bethe-Salpeter equation calculations of cyclobutadiene and state-specific core-ionization calculations for selected systems. These examples show that, in the absence of the relevant instabilities, methods sharing the generalized eigenvalue structure of RPAx can be applied to excited-state references without resorting to the Tamm-Dancoff or core-valence separation approximations, while retaining real excitation energies.
