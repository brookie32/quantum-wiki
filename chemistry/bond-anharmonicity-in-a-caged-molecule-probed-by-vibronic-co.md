arXiv:2611.18331v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: X-ray absorption spectroscopy and resonant inelastic X-ray scattering at the nitrogen K-edge show that the NequivN bond in N_2@C_{60} is substantially more anharmonic than in free N_2, with the ground-state (X^1Sigma_g^+) anharmonic constant hbaromega_e x_e a factor of two larger (3.8pm0.3~meV versus sim 1.8~meV) despite an essentially unchanged harmonic frequency. The same enhancement is found across all three vibronic potentials accessible to the experiment---the electronic ground state, the core-excited intermediate state, and the spectator-decay final state---pointing to a common origin rather than an effect specific to one electronic configuration. High-level coupled-cluster calculations of the N_2@C_{60} potential energy surface, however, find no significant change in anharmonicity upon encapsulation, ruling out a simple static distortion of the NequivN bonding potential by the fullerene cage. We propose instead that the dramatic increase in hbaromega_e x_e above the gas-phase value -- which, taken literally, would imply an unphysical halving of the N_2 dissociation energy - reflects the breakdown of the single-molecule Morse description, as previously invoked to explain anomalous anharmonicities in N_2 clusters, and supported by the observation of shifts in the fullerene host's Raman modes.
