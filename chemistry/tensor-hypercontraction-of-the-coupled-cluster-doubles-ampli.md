arXiv:2611.26871v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We present a tensor hypercontraction (THC) approach for compressing coupled-cluster doubles amplitudes using a joint grid in real space and imaginary time. The proposed method extends interpolative separable density fitting (ISDF) to imaginary-time-propagated orbital-pair densities, enabling the direct construction of a compact, separable representation of the doubles amplitudes without explicitly forming or decomposing the full four-index amplitude tensor. Interpolation points are selected through a pivoted Cholesky decomposition of the space-time pair-density Gram matrix, yielding a basis that is particularly well suited to representing the doubles amplitudes in second-order M{o}ller-Plesset (MP2). We investigate the accuracy and transferability of this basis within a rank-reduced coupled-cluster singles and doubles (CCSD) framework. Using a THC rank equal to eight times the number of molecular orbitals, CCSD correlation-energy errors remain below 0.1% for all systems considered. Furthermore, we demonstrate that the proposed approximation accurately reproduces relative CCSD energies for both intramolecular rotations and intermolecular dissociation curves, with deviations below 0.1 kcal mol^{-1} at sufficiently large THC ranks. Although the present implementation is designed to assess accuracy rather than computational efficiency, the proposed representation provides a route toward CCSD calculations with quartic computational scaling and offers a promising foundation for extensions to periodic and finite-temperature systems.
