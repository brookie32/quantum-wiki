Researchers have resolved the long-standing Arrhenius paradox in molecular glasses by demonstrating that activation energy decreases linearly with temperature as a consequence of density-driven variations of the barrier.   Using a constant-volume approach combining broadband dielectric spectroscopy with pressure-volume-temperature analysis to separate thermal and density contributions, the study confirms theoretical predictions made fifty years ago but never experimentally verified.   The findings suggest that density effects, previously hidden in standard measurements, account for anomalous Arrhenius parameters.
