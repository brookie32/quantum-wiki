On 15 October 2026, officials from 69 countries voted at the International Bureau of Weights and Measures in Versailles to abolish leap seconds, allowing Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) to drift up to one hour relative to astronomical time before correction. Since 1972, 27 leap seconds have been inserted into UTC to keep it aligned with Earth’s slowing rotation; the change reflects increasing dependence on precise atomic timing and the difficulty of managing irregular corrections. Consequently, UTC will gradually diverge from solar time, effectively creating a permanent daylight‑saving–like offset.
