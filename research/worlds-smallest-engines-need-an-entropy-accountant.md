A recent study shows that a single atom trapped between two mirrors can operate as the world’s smallest heat engine, continuously exchanging energy with its environment while driven by a laser. The experiment demonstrates that treating only the atom quantum mechanically (and not the emitted light) reduces fluctuations in the outgoing radiation, illustrating how entropy accounting can reconcile thermodynamic behavior with quantum dynamics. These results—published in *Physical Review Letters* by Patrick Potts’s team at the University of Basel—open a pathway for bridging macroscopic thermodynamics with microscopic quantum mechanics in nano‑engine design.
