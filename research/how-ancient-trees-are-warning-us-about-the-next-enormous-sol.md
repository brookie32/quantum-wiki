Ancient trees serve as natural archives of past solar activity, with variations in growth rings linked to changes in the Sun’s magnetic field and radiation output. By analyzing these records, scientists can identify historical solar storms—such as the 1859 Carrington event—and use the data to model future extreme events. This dendrochronological evidence suggests that significant solar flares will likely occur within a few centuries, providing critical lead time for mitigation strategies.
