I'll search for information about the human eye's sensitivity to photons to provide an accurate summary. The human eye can detect light signals as low as 5–7 photons under dark-adapted conditions , though  more recent research demonstrates that humans can detect a single photon incident on the cornea with a probability significantly above chance .  Classical experiments found that approximately 90 photons must enter the eye for reliable detection, but accounting for photon loss through the eye's optics, only about 9 photons actually reach the retina, suggesting rod cells respond to individual photons even when subjects cannot consciously perceive such infrequent events .
