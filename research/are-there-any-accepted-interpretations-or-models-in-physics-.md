While retrocausality—where effects precede causes—lacks mainstream acceptance in physics, some interpretations of quantum mechanics permit backward-in-time influences at the microscopic level, particularly those assuming time-symmetric physics.   Notable proposals include the Wheeler-Feynman absorber theory, the Stueckelberg-Feynman interpretation of positrons as backward-moving electrons, and John Cramer's transactional interpretation.   However, there is no generally agreed-upon interpretation of quantum theory that fully recovers the whole theory while exploiting retrocausality, and the idea remains speculative rather than consensus science.
