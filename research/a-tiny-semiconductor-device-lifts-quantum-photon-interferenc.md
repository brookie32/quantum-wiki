Researchers achieved 90% quantum photon interference visibility by controlling photon emission sequences from a semiconductor quantum dot using an open optical microcavity and the Purcell effect.   An indium gallium arsenide quantum dot inside the microcavity produced the high-visibility result by altering the biexciton-exciton decay lifetime ratio, mitigating timing jitter that normally limits visibility to around 60%.   The second photon achieved 80% visibility, with challenges remaining from crystal vibrations and incomplete photon collection.
