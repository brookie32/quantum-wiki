When a photon is truncated using an optical shutter, the resulting state is not simply a shorter photon but rather a superposition and mix of photon numbers up to infinity.   Despite being complicated, this state is locally equivalent to a single photon or vacuum to the left and right, respectively, of a narrow transition region.   This result has implications for understanding localized states and local equivalence in quantum field theory.
