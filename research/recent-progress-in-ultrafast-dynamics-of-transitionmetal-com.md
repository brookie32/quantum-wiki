This review summarizes recent progress in time-resolved X-ray techniques for studying ultrafast dynamics in transition-metal compounds, focusing on pump-probe measurements of laser-induced demagnetization, spin-state transitions, and valence and structural changes.   Time-resolved X-ray absorption spectroscopy, X-ray magnetic circular dichroism, and resonant soft X-ray scattering provide direct, complementary access to element- and momentum-resolved ultrafast dynamics.   The paper discusses advances in tabletop HHG-based X-ray spectroscopy and its integration with large-scale XFEL facilities, which enable visualization of nonequilibrium evolution of charge, spin, orbital, and lattice degrees of freedom.
