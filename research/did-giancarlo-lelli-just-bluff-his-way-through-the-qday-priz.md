Project Eleven awarded its Q-Day Prize to independent researcher Giancarlo Lelli for breaking a 15-bit elliptic curve key, but this claim was challenged by Google researcher Craig Gidney, who argued the winning submission did not meaningfully demonstrate progress toward a cryptographically relevant quantum attack.   Critics pointed out that the quantum circuit produced output statistically indistinguishable from random coin flips, suggesting luck rather than genuine quantum computation accounted for the result.   Project Eleven's CEO later acknowledged that the contest "was certainly imperfect."
