Physical constants like Planck's constant, the gravitational constant, and electron mass form the foundation of physics, and physicists naturally question whether these parameters truly remain constant across space and time.   Some theories propose that constants could vary in both time and space—for instance, if extra dimensions changed size, the "constants" in our three-dimensional world would change accordingly.   However, extensive tests on Earth and throughout the universe have found no measurable changes in fundamental constants.
