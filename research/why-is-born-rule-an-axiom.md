The Born rule is treated as an axiomatic cornerstone of quantum mechanics in modern textbooks, formulated by Max Born in 1926 , as  it provides the probability that a measurement of a quantum system will yield a given result .  There is ongoing debate about whether the Born rule should be accepted as a fundamental axiom or derived from other quantum mechanical principles .  Some researchers argue that its quadratic dependence can be derived from additional physical assumptions, suggesting the rule could be reduced to a more fundamental statement rather than treated as a complete axiom .
