Classical simulation difficulty depends on the degree of entanglement rather than the number of qubits, with computational resources growing exponentially in entanglement amount.   High-entanglement quantum states become impractical to simulate exactly with classical computers, while low-entanglement circuits—regardless of qubit count—can be efficiently simulated classically.  This insight challenges the conventional assumption that qubit count alone determines the simulation wall's position.
