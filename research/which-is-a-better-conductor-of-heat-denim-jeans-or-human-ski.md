Human skin is the better heat conductor, with thermal conductivity of approximately 0.61 W/m/K for water compared to the range of 0.033-0.09 W/m/K for dry denim fabrics .  Denim becomes a conductor when wet, as cotton absorbs significant moisture that increases thermal conductivity, but in dry conditions denim acts more as an insulator . The question addresses the fundamental distinction between thermal conductivity (heat flow through a material) and thermal insulation properties, with denim designed to resist heat transfer while human skin readily conducts it.
