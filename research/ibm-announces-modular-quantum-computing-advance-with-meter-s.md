IBM successfully connected and cooled two modular cryogenic systems into a single environment, achieving a milestone in scaling quantum computing through its modular architecture designed to link hundreds of quantum chips.   The L-coupler technology enables separate quantum chips to share information and operate as parts of a larger quantum computer through meter-long superconducting cables connecting processors across module boundaries.   IBM's modular approach could eventually support hundreds of quantum chips and is part of its plan to build IBM Quantum Starling, targeted for 2029 as the world's first fault-tolerant quantum computer.
