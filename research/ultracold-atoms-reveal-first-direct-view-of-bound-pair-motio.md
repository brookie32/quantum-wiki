Particles can generally move between neighboring sites in a crystal, a solid in which atoms are arranged in a regular, repeating pattern, or an optical lattice, an arrangement of laser-based traps that confines atoms. A strong potential gradient (i.e., a steep change in potential energy across a crystal or lattice) makes this ordinary particle hopping difficult and energetically costly.
