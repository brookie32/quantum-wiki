When scientists shine a laser beam into a crystal, they typically expect a steady glow as the crystal's atoms are energized and begin to emit light. But in early 2024, while searching for something else entirely, researchers in the lab of Caltech's Andrei Faraon saw a crystal flash in a regular rhythm, like a metronome keeping a beat. The flashes came from billions of atoms embedded in the crystal. Coupled to a tiny superconducting circuit, the atoms had become synchronized and started emitting light together through a special quantum phenomenon known as superradiance.
