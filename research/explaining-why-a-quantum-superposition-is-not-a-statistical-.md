Quantum superposition and statistical mixtures are fundamentally different quantum states despite both being describable by density matrices. A superposition represents a coherent combination where the system exists in multiple states simultaneously with quantum interference effects, while a mixture represents incoherent uncertainty about which classical state the system actually occupies. This distinction is crucial for understanding quantum mechanics and has practical implications for quantum computing applications.
