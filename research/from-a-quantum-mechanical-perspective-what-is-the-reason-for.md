Silicon double bonds are less stable than carbon double bonds because silicon forms weaker π bonds and experiences stronger polarity, making these compounds highly reactive and preferring cyclic or singlet silylenic forms thermodynamically.   Silicon double bonds show only 5-10% bond shortening compared to single bonds, versus approximately 13% in carbon compounds, indicating weaker π bonding in silicon.  This difference arises from silicon's larger atomic size and poorer orbital overlap for π bonding compared to carbon's smaller 2p orbitals.
