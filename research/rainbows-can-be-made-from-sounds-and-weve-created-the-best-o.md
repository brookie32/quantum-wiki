Scientists at South China University of Technology and Tongji University have independently built devices that separate sound waves (phonons) by frequency, producing the most complete “elastic rainbows” yet recorded on silicon‑chip and aluminum substrates. The approach mimics optical rainbow formation, confining phonon groups to distinct locations through engineered potentials. These results suggest new possibilities for sound‑based information processing and energy harvesting.
