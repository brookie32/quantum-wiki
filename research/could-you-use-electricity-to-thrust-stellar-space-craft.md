Electric propulsion systems use electrical energy to accelerate and expel propellant, generating thrust through electric or magnetic fields, and offer much higher specific impulse than chemical rockets but produce much lower thrust, making them better suited to long-duration in-space maneuvers.   Electric propulsion typically uses ion or Hall effect thrusters, which ionize a propellant such as xenon.   While mostly chemical rocket-powered, some interplanetary vehicles have used electric propulsion such as ion thrusters and Hall-effect thrusters.
