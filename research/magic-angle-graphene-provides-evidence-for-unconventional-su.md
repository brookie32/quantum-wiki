A recent study of twisted bilayer (“magic‑angle”) graphene reveals that when two sheets are rotated by ~1.1°, the resulting flat electronic bands produce strong electron correlations that manifest as both correlated insulating phases and superconductivity at temperatures around 1–2 K. The observed superconducting state displays properties inconsistent with conventional BCS theory, suggesting an unconventional pairing mechanism driven by these correlation effects. The findings thus provide direct experimental evidence that magic‑angle graphene hosts a novel type of superconductivity.
