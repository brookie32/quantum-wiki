Laplace resonances occur when planetary or satellite systems with multiple bodies exhibit stable orbits with simple orbital period ratios, characterized by a 1:2:4 period relationship .  Jupiter's moons Io, Europa, and Ganymede exemplify this phenomenon, each inner moon completing two orbits for every orbit of the next moon outward .  The true mechanism behind orbital stability is a pure three-body resonance between all three satellites, which is sufficiently separated from individual two-body resonances to prevent chaotic effects .
