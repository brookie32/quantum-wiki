Q-Day refers to the point when quantum computers can break current public-key cryptography like RSA and elliptic-curve algorithms, marking when post-quantum encryption must fully replace classical systems.   A major present-day threat is "harvest now, decrypt later" attacks, where adversaries collect encrypted data today knowing they can decrypt it when quantum computers become powerful enough, potentially exposing financial records, personal data, and intellectual property years from now.   Research shows that over 90 percent of global businesses still lack a roadmap for dealing with quantum cybersecurity threats.
