Scientists at Cleveland Clinic, RIKEN, and IBM used IBM quantum computers and two supercomputers to simulate protein complexes spanning up to 12,635 atoms.   This quantum-centric supercomputing approach pairs quantum processors with classical computers so each computational tool can solve the parts of a problem where it excels.   These are the largest-known simulations of biologically meaningful molecules performed with quantum hardware yet, and signal that quantum computers are maturing into useful scientific tools which can help solve fundamental problems in biology, chemistry, and life sciences.
