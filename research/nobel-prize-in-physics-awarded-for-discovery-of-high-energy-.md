The 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics was awarded to Francis Halzen for pioneering the detection of high‑energy cosmic neutrinos using the IceCube Neutrino Observatory at the South Pole. Halzen’s proposal to use Antarctic ice as a detector and his leadership of an international collaboration enabled the first measurements of “ghost‑like” neutrinos originating from distant astrophysical sources, providing new probes into the universe. His work demonstrated that such neutrinos can travel unimpeded through matter, delivering information about their origins that is otherwise inaccessible.
