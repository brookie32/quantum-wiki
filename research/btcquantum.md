Bitcoin's security relies on elliptic curve cryptography protecting private keys; quantum computers running Shor's algorithm could potentially derive private keys from exposed public keys and enable large-scale theft.   Google's 2026 research indicates a machine with fewer than 500,000 physical qubits could break this encryption, with Google targeting post-quantum readiness by 2029.   Unlike Ethereum's coordinated post-quantum migration plan, Bitcoin lacks a unified roadmap, and its decentralized culture makes reaching consensus on urgent security upgrades challenging.
