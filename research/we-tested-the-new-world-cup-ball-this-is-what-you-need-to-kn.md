The 2026 World Cup Trionda ball features only four panels thermally bonded together, and wind tunnel testing at the University of Tsukuba measured its drag coefficient compared to previous World Cup balls.   The Trionda was found to be effectively rougher than its predecessors, reaching its drag crisis at a lower speed of about 27 mph (43 kph).   When the drag crisis occurs in the middle of game-relevant speeds, small changes in launch speed, orientation or spin can shift the ball from one aerodynamic regime to another, potentially affecting its flight behavior.
