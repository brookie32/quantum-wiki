Researchers at the University of Tokyo found that hydrogen atoms exhibit pronounced quantum tunnelling through a material when the crystal structure is highly symmetric, while lowering symmetry strongly suppresses this effect. The study used low‑temperature experiments on a model hydrogen–storage material (vanadium) combined with complementary techniques to observe how symmetry alters hydrogen transport. These results identify crystal symmetry as a fundamental parameter for controlling hydrogen’s quantum behavior, offering a pathway to optimize safer and more efficient hydrogen storage systems.
