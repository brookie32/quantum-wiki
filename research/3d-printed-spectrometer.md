3D-printed spectrometers are inexpensive optical instruments that use a smartphone camera, laser, diffraction grating, and cuvette to measure light wavelengths and intensities.   Educational designs include periscope-type visible light spectrometers that enable absorption and fluorescence spectroscopy for less than $40, incorporating 3D-printed components with common optical elements.   Advanced research applications have also produced miniature 3D-printed spectrometers created via two-photon direct laser writing with volumes smaller than 100 × 100 × 300 micrometers.
