Scientists at Brookhaven National Laboratory discovered evidence that particles emerging from proton-proton collisions at the Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider exhibit spin correlations directly linked to virtual quark-antiquark pairs in the quantum vacuum.   Analysis showed that lambda and antilambda particles produced close together display complete spin alignment matching the quantum vacuum's virtual pairs, indicating real particles inherit quantum properties from vacuum fluctuations.   This finding reveals how the quantum vacuum—far from empty—provides the ingredients to transform virtual particles into the observable matter composing our universe.
