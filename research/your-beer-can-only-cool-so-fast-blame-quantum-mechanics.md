New work in *Nature Physics* shows that the minimum time required for a physical system to reach thermal equilibrium is set by quantum mechanics rather than the details of the cooling device.  The authors introduce a formal notion of a “thermalisation machine” – a quantum environment that drives any initial state into a true thermal state at a specified temperature – and prove an intrinsic bound on how rapidly this evolution can occur, independent of device specifics.  This result provides the first rigorous lower limit on the speed of processes such as refrigerating beer or boiling water in a kettle.
