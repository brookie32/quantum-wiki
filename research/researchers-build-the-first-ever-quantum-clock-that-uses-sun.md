Researchers at the University of Colorado Boulder have constructed the first quantum optical clock that employs sunlight as its key reference instead of isolated atoms in a laboratory environment. By using solar radiation to drive an electronic transition and combining it with a conventional laser and frequency‑comb system, the clock can potentially detect changes in the Sun’s velocity or probe surface processes. This represents a novel approach to timekeeping that leverages natural astrophysical light sources for high‑precision frequency standards.
