John Goold examines Maxwell’s Demon—a long‑standing thought experiment that seemingly violates the second law of thermodynamics by reducing entropy without work—highlighting its relevance to modern physics. He explains how the paradox connects entropy, measurement, and information theory, showing that manipulating heat and disorder requires accounting for informational costs in quantum systems. Goold’s analysis illustrates that the Demon’s apparent violation actually reveals fundamental limits of computation, observation, and thermodynamic laws in both classical and quantum regimes.
