Researchers at the University of Oxford demonstrated a new type of quantum interaction using a single trapped ion by creating and controlling increasingly complex forms of "squeezing," including a fourth-order effect known as quadsqueezing, making previously unreachable quantum effects experimentally accessible for the first time.   The team generated different types of squeezing interactions and could select which interaction appeared by changing the frequencies, phases, and strengths of applied forces.   The quadsqueezing interaction was generated more than 100 times faster than expected using conventional approaches.
