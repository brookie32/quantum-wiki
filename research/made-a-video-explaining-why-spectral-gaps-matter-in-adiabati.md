The spectral gap (Δ) is a critical parameter in adiabatic quantum computing that determines the minimum energy difference between the ground state and first excited state during computation. A smaller spectral gap increases the required evolution time proportionally (scaling as 1/Δ), which is captured by the reduced resolvent bound—a fundamental result showing how gap size directly impacts algorithmic runtime and computational complexity. Understanding this relationship is essential for analyzing the performance and feasibility of adiabatic quantum algorithms.
