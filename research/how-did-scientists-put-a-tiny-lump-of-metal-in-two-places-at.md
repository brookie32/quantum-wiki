Scientists demonstrated that tiny metal particles made of thousands of atoms can exist in multiple places at once using advanced laser techniques, with researchers at the University of Vienna observing quantum interference in sodium nanoparticles far larger than those typically studied.   The researchers created ultracold sodium clusters containing 5,000 to 10,000 atoms, passed them through three diffraction gratings generated by ultraviolet laser beams to place them in quantum superposition, and detected a striped interference pattern that matched quantum theory predictions.   This finding pushes quantum mechanics into a new realm, suggesting that even surprisingly "large" objects still obey the bizarre rules of the quantum world.
