Wheeler's delayed-choice quantum eraser experiment demonstrates a counterintuitive aspect of quantum mechanics where the choice to measure or not measure a photon after it has already passed through a double slit apparatus can retroactively affect its behavior, determining whether it exhibits wave-like or particle-like properties. The experiment uses entangled photons and beam splitters positioned after the initial slits to allow experimenters to decide whether to gather "which-path" information, and this choice influences the interference pattern observed even though it occurs after the photon has already traversed the slits. This apparent violation of causality puzzled physicists and led to deeper understanding of how quantum information and measurement work, showing that quantum mechanics doesn't require information to be determined before measurement occurs.
