Ultracold atom experiments—where large ensembles of bosonic atoms are cooled near absolute zero to form a Bose‑Einstein condensate—have been proposed to test the long‑standing problem of defining quantum arrival time in standard and alternative interpretations of quantum theory. Researchers at RIKEN, Paris‑Saclay, and NIST argue that measuring how quickly the condensate expands to reach a detector could distinguish among competing theoretical models. If successful, the experiment would provide empirical data on an aspect of quantum mechanics that has previously lacked clear experimental validation.
