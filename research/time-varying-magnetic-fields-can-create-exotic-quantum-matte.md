Cal Poly researchers demonstrated that periodically varying magnetic fields over time can create quantum states that do not exist in static materials, representing a form of Floquet engineering applied to quantum matter.   These topological phases are valued in quantum research for their resilience, as properties protected by topology are harder to disrupt through noise, defects, or imperfections.   By timing magnetic field pulses, physicists can potentially create more stable quantum technologies resistant to errors—a major challenge in quantum computing.
