SWAP gates are fundamental quantum operations used to exchange qubit states, and their use in quantum circuits does impact coherence.  SWAP gates are among the most time-consuming operations in quantum computers, and because longer circuit execution times are constrained by coherence time, reducing the number of SWAP gates enables quantum circuits to operate with more qubits for a given coherence duration . The relationship between gate count and coherence loss is indirect—using more SWAP gates increases overall circuit execution time, which reduces the effective coherence window available for computation.
