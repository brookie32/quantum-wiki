Researchers have proposed a theoretical cooling protocol that exploits quantum correlations—specifically steering—to withdraw heat more efficiently from an environment than classical methods allow. The scheme uses a finite‑dimensional ensemble of uniquely labelled quantum states (an “assemblage”) that, upon measurement and state preparation, steer the system into a specified energy level; the resulting non‑equilibrium state absorbs heat from a bath before being discarded and regenerated in each cycle. This protocol highlights an intrinsic advantage of quantum resources over classical ones for thermodynamic tasks such as cooling generated waste heat in quantum computing devices.
