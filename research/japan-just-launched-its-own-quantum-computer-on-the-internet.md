Japan has made a trapped-ion quantum device accessible through cloud infrastructure, with researchers at Osaka University announcing that an ion-trap quantum computer can now be operated remotely over the internet without physical access to the laboratory.   The system allows users to submit quantum instructions through the internet, which are converted into control signals that drive lasers and electromagnetic fields to perform single-qubit quantum gate operations on a trapped ytterbium-171 ion.   The success is largely attributed to the system's automated operation framework, marking an important step toward practical remote quantum computing access.
