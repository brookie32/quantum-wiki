A recent re‑analysis of data from the LUX‑ZEPLIN (LZ) dark‑matter experiment in South Dakota has identified a single event that could be the first direct detection of a weakly interacting massive particle (WIMP). If confirmed, this would represent one of the most significant discoveries in physics, providing concrete evidence for the nature of roughly 85 % of the universe’s matter. The LZ detector employs a 7‑tonne liquid xenon chamber shielded by layers of material and positioned over a kilometre underground to minimise background radiation.
