White specks visible in a laser beam's path result from particles in the air, such as chalk dust or microscopic water droplets, scattering the light .  When a laser beam is seen, it is because light has scattered off air molecules in the atmosphere .  Even air molecules can scatter light, though the scattered light is often too dim to see unless additional particles are present to enhance the effect .
