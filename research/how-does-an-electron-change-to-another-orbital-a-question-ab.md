In quantum mechanics, there is no well-defined time associated with an electron changing orbitals, as the electron is not a classical object traveling on a specific trajectory from one orbital to another.   The electron disappears from its current orbit and reappears in a new location without appearing in between, a process called a quantum leap or quantum jump.   The transition appears discontinuous and typically occurs in a few nanoseconds or less, accompanied by the emission or absorption of photons.
