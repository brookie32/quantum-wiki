Researchers at McGill University developed a quantum device that generates controlled sound-like particles called phonons by forcing electrons through an ultra-thin crystal at temperatures near absolute zero.   The breakthrough behavior exceeds current theoretical predictions, suggesting existing theories about energy movement in advanced materials need revision.   Future applications include phonon lasers, faster communications, improved medical technologies, and advanced sensing systems.
