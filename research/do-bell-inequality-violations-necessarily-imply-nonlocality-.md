Bell inequality violations do not necessarily imply nonlocality; they demonstrate that observed quantum statistics cannot be embedded into a single context-independent description.   Bell inequalities follow from joint assumptions of locality, measurement independence, and global value assignment to all observables, so their violation requires abandoning at least one assumption—nonlocality is not the only option.   Some interpretations argue violations prove hidden variables must depend on measurement settings, confirming the contextual character of quantum observables rather than requiring nonlocality.
