 Jump distance depends on horizontal velocity, which is improved by running before the jump to gain speed—the faster an athlete runs, the farther they can travel during the jump.   Vertical velocity gained from the jumping motion also plays a significant role, as greater vertical velocity extends air time and allows horizontal velocity to cover more distance.   Technical factors such as motor program sequencing, arm swing, trunk angle, and takeoff posture differ between jumping types and directly influence jump distance.
