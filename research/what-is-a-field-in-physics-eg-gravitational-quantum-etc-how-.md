A field in physics is a physical quantity assigned to every point in space that mediates forces between particles; examples include gravitational and electromagnetic fields. Fields can be "excited" when energy is added to them, causing quantized excitations that correspond to particles—for instance, exciting the electromagnetic field produces photons. While mass and energy are related through Einstein's equation E=mc², they are distinct concepts: mass is a fundamental property of matter, whereas energy is the capacity to do work, though they can be converted into one another.
