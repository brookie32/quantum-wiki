arXiv:2611.27815v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Quantum machine learning models typically rely on repeated quantum measurements and classical optimization during training, making the quantum-to-classical information-extraction process a fundamental bottleneck for scalability. We propose a framework for autonomous self-training quantum machine learning, based on Riemannian gradient optimization, in which the optimization proceeds entirely within the quantum domain without requiring quantum measurements or classical feedback. We derive the Riemannian gradient update rules for a broad class of learning tasks and identify sufficient conditions under which the resulting training dynamics can be realized fully autonomously. Within this regime, we establish an exponential convergence guarantee, in the training iterations, for sufficiently small step sizes, and further analyze the training cost in terms of the growth of the circuit depth. Importantly, we further show that the trained model can automatically discover symmetries hidden in the training data without extit{a priori} specification. For the canonical examples of quantum phase classification and unitary learning with a hidden symmetry, our numerical study indicates that learning can be achieved at low circuit depths with only a few distinct training examples. Our results thus establish autonomous quantum machine learning as a promising paradigm that removes the classical optimization loop while retaining strong theoretical guarantees and practical performances.
