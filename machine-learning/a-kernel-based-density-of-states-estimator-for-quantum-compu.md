arXiv:2607.24972v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: We introduce a quantum kernel method for estimating the density of states (DoS) of quantum many-body systems. The method exploits the Rodeo algorithm as a physical implementation, requiring no modification of its standard single-ancilla circuit. Averaging the Rodeo response over Haar-random input states reconstructs the DoS convolved with a spectral kernel whose analytical form is determined entirely by the probability distribution of the sampled evolution times. This establishes a direct quantum analogue of the classical Kernel Polynomial Method (KPM): Haar-random states perform stochastic trace estimation, while the temporal sampling distribution plays the role of the reconstruction kernel. We derive the estimator, quantify its statistical and systematic uncertainties, and establish an explicit correspondence between quantum reconstruction kernels and classical signal-processing window functions, enabling concepts such as spectral leakage, side-lobe attenuation, and main-lobe width to guide kernel design. The method is validated numerically on the one-dimensional transverse-field spin--sfrac{1}{2} and spin--1 Ising models, where the reconstructed density of states and the resulting thermodynamic observables agree with exact diagonalization within the estimated statistical uncertainties.
