arXiv:2604.15025v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Where is the true boundary of the quantum advantage region of decoded quantum interferometry (DQI)? The best existing answer is provided by Theorem 7.1 in the Supplementary Material of Jordan et al. (2025), yet we show that this answer systematically underestimates the extent of quantum advantage. On the standard partial-win LDPC benchmark instance, there exist 26 consecutive parameter points (ell in [642, 667]) at which Jordan's analysis declares no quantum advantage (langle srangle/m < 0.5), while quantum advantage is in fact present with an approximation ratio reaching 0.66. The root cause is that Jordan's bound penalizes the entire system with the worst-case Hamming-layer decoding failure rate arepsilon = max_k arepsilon_k, discarding the spectral structure of the DQI tridiagonal matrix. Exploiting the concentration of the Perron eigenvector, we replace the uniform penalty with the eigenvector-weighted average ararepsilon = sum_k arepsilon_k w_k^2 and establish a unified lower bound (Master Theorem) valid over arbitrary finite fields F_q, proving that it strictly improves upon the relaxed form of Jordan's bound by replacing the operator-norm penalty 2arepsilon(q-1)(m+1) with a tighter Rayleigh-quotient penalty 2ararepsilonlambda_{max}.
