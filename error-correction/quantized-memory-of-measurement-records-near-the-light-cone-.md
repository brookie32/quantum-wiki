arXiv:2611.13175v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Quantum processors now measure qubits in the middle of a circuit, and the stream of outcomes is the raw output of error correction, measurement-based state preparation and monitored dynamics. For a scrambling circuit, predicting the statistics of this stream is as hard as simulating the processor, so there is no exact reference to check it against once the device outgrows classical simulation. We show that near the light cone the problem becomes exactly solvable, for any two-qubit gate. In a brickwork circuit with one polarized qubit in a maximally mixed background, measurements along the edge of the light cone form a two-state Markov chain, and measurements d sites inside the edge are generated by lfloor d/2rfloor+1 qubits that move with it, while the rest of the chain, however large, acts on them as a memoryless bath. The memory of the record is therefore quantized, one bit on the edge and at most one more for every site of displacement, a bound that generic gates attain. The same causal structure determines which hardware noise the record can see, removes that sensitivity at no gate cost, and yields a tomography-free witness of dual-unitarity. On a 156-qubit superconducting processor, a demonstration with its analysis specified in advance finds the predicted memory one and two sites inside the light cone, and none where the theory forbids it. This gives dynamic circuits an exact reference at any system size, and shows that the collision models of open-system theory arise exactly inside chaotic dynamics.
