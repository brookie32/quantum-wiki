arXiv:2606.24808v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Quantum low-density parity-check codes promise low-overhead error correction, but high-performing constructions remain hard to find. Here we introduce structured concept evolution, in which a large language model (LLM) evolves construction programs and their internal search strategies by modifying the finite group, protograph shapes or entry-assignment rule. We search for codes with fewer than 600 data qubits and check weight at most 12, optimizing kd^2/n or the circuit-level logical error rate. Our method improves on published lifted-product benchmarks in selected parameter regimes, with exactly certified distances for the leading results. Circuit-level optimization typically yields lower logical error rates than kd^2/n optimization, often despite smaller code distances. With the same evolution framework and step budget, the best LLM outperforms all random-mutation and hill-climbing runs on both objectives. Our results point to LLM-driven program search as an approach to designing general quantum LDPC codes.
