arXiv:2611.05502v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We characterize the complexity of space-time correlated decoding for fault-tolerant quantum computing architectures that implement Clifford gates transversally and realize non-Clifford gates via magic state injection. In this approach, decoding graphs for logical measurements are constructed by backpropagating measurement operators through the circuit, which causes them to grow with circuit volume. However, the decoding volume can be substantially reduced by decoding measurements sequentially, committing corrections, and excluding syndrome data that have already been processed for earlier measurements. For random circuits, we find that the resulting decoding volume associated with mid-circuit measurements approaches a stationary average that is determined by the density of magic state injections. Large decoding instances are exponentially suppressed. We further find that the decoding complexity is remarkably insensitive to different choices of measurement products to be decoded, and that the same qualitative behavior persists across different circuit connectivities and Clifford gate ensembles. These results suggest that scalable space-time correlated decoding of large-scale fault-tolerant quantum algorithms is achievable since the magic states that make transversal architectures universal also keep them decodable.
