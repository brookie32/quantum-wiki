arXiv:2602.20269v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Passive error correction schemes have so far been limited to static memories rather than dynamical qubits. We demonstrate that a driven-dissipative bosonic system, the Bose-Hubbard dimer (BHD), can encode a persistently oscillating qubit within a dynamical noiseless subsystem (DNS). The strong parity symmetry of the system leads to degenerate stationary states, whose mutual coherence breaks the time translation symmetry, giving rise to a time crystal in the thermodynamic limit. This time crystal undergoes a dissipative phase transition controlled by the driving force, with the DNS present above the transition point. This noiseless subsystem, and hence the qubit, is resilient to collective loss and at most quadratically affected when the strong symmetry condition is weakened. The decoherence from collective dephasing also cancels in the leading order, with the cancellation being exact at a specific drive strength, set by the photon-number difference between the two logical states. Tuning the drive extends this cancellation to unbalanced dephasing. These results establish dissipative time crystals as a medium for autonomously stabilized oscillating qubits.
