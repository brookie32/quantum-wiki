Google Quantum AI demonstrated that "magic state cultivation" works on its Willow processor, reducing errors about 40x compared to without it.   Magic states are needed for universal quantum computing but are costly to make, with estimates suggesting up to 95% of a machine's capacity could go to producing them.   The technique uses coordinated error-checking measurements as a filter and involves grafting cultivated states between different error-correcting codes on the hardware.
