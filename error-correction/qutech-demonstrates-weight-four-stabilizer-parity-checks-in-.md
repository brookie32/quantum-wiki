Researchers at QuTech have demonstrated significant advancements in quantum computing, showcasing weight-four X- and Z-type quantum error-correction parity checks and five-qubit Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ) entanglement in a silicon spin-shuttling processor. Their work highlights the successful use of coherent electron shuttling for remote tuning and multi-qubit operations, with an error analysis pointing to two-qubit exchange pulses as the primary source of residual error. These findings suggest the potential for sparse shuttling channels to support a large number of qubits and contribute to near-term fault-tolerant surface-code modules.
