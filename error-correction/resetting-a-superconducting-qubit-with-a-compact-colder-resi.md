arXiv:2611.06216v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Efficient qubit reset has become a key requirement for large-scale superconducting quantum processors, as initialization fidelity increasingly lags behind gate performance and quantum error correction demands rapid, high-fidelity state re-initialization. This work proposes a compact on-chip dissipator consisting of two transmon dissipation modes and a thin-film resistor, and experimentally demonstrates an unconditional reset protocol. With no additional control lines and negligible impact on the qubit T_1, the effective temperature of the transmon qubit is reduced from 49~mK to 35~mK. Quantum process tomography is employed to benchmark the reset process, achieving a process error of 0.0028. This reset protocol is robust to pulse parameter deviations, thereby relaxing calibration requirements. The dissipator architecture provides a practical, calibration-friendly reset solution for large-scale superconducting quantum processors.
