arXiv:2611.13098v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We study the surface code under uniform coherent Z rotations exp{(iheta Z)} followed by perfect syndrome measurements, a setting known to exhibit strong finite-size effects that complicate the extraction of the asymptotic decoding threshold. We identify finite-size behavior of both Pauli decoding measures and the coherent information with two characteristic lengths: an algebraic ballistic-crossover scale d_{ba}sim 1/eta_R^{alpha} (alpha=1 for odd d and alpha=2 for even d) and a much larger renormalization-group scale d_RGsim exp{(C/eta^{4}_R)}, where eta_R=-frac{1}{2}lnanheta and d is the system size. As heta approaches the ballistic SWAP point pi/4, the two length scales diverge at very different rates, leaving a broad intermediate regime d_{ba}ll dll d_RG in which these quantities show substantial finite-size drift and approximate plateaus. To establish the microscopic basis of this crossover picture, we derive a leading inter-flavor quartic interaction of order eta_R^2 in the replicated Majorana theory, which provides a route to the class-D nonlinear sigma model. The resulting marginal RG flow motivates the scaling variable X_4=eta_R^4ln d, in which free-fermion simulations show approximate collapse of decoding measures. These results are consistent with a Pauli-decoding threshold at heta_c=pi/4 in the thermodynamic limit. For any even d, we prove that maximum-likelihood Pauli decoding maximizes entanglement fidelity over all deterministic syndrome-conditioned quantum recoveries, and that its decoding threshold attains the quantum-optimal limit, coinciding with the one defined by coherent information.
