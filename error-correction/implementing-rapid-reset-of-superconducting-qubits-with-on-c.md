arXiv:2611.14391v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Rapid and high-fidelity qubit reset is essential for quantum state initialization and quantum error correction. Conventional reset protocols for superconducting qubits often rely on dissipative elements connected to external circuitry, exposing qubits to thermal noise from higher-temperature environments. Here, we experimentally demonstrate a scalable approach to qubit reset and leakage removal over multiple transmon qubits using dedicated dissipation units comprising resonators coupled to on-chip resistive loads. This architecture provides Purcell protection while isolating the qubits from warmer external circuitry. We achieve a median reset time constant of approximately 48 ns for the |grangle-|erangle transition, with an effective bath temperature of 30 mK. A 400-ns reset pulse yields residual excited-state populations of 0.29% and 0.67% for qubits initialized in |grangle and |erangle, respectively. We further demonstrate efficient leakage removal from the |frangle state, reducing its residual population to 0.51%. These results establish a simple, reproducible, and easy-to-integrate approach to dissipation engineering in superconducting quantum processors.
