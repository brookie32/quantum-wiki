arXiv:2610.26785v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Correlated decoding improves matching and clustering surface-code decoders by reweighting one decoding subgraph conditioned on the other's correction. We show that standard reweighting can cause correlated decoders to fail on size-(d-1)/2 fault configurations that a distance-d code should correct, reducing error suppression deep below threshold. Under circuit-level noise, a single first-pass edge can discount several correlated edges, even though their joint occurrence requires additional independent faults, so the decoder favors an over-discounted correction. Regularizing the reweighting rule restores the nominal distance scaling and a clear correlated-decoding advantage.
