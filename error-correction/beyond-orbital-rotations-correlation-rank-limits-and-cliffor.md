arXiv:2607.16869v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: One should not assume that reconstructing an electronic observable with a smaller set of measurement settings will cut the number of repetitions in proportion. Each setting is simply a basis change and qubit readout, but the savings are not guaranteed to be commensurate. Take two electrons of opposite spin in two spatial orbitals: the orbital degree of freedom of each electron acts as a logical qubit and rotations are independent in the spin sectors. From the observable's one- and two-qubit coefficients we derive an exact floor for the setting count and a decomposition to meet it in no more than three settings. With fewer settings, the minimum error in the two-qubit correlation coefficients is set by the singular values left out. A case in point is an exchange with equal nonzero couplings on the three Pauli axes. This demands three orbital settings yet only one joint measurement. We offer a Clifford circuit for four physical qubits to effect the necessary mapping of Pauli products under conjugation. In the case of a one-parameter family of logical singlet-triplet mixtures, the orbital-to-Clifford shot cost ratio is three at maximum mixing and trends toward 3/2 near the logical singlet. Where spin sectors may be mixed, isotropic exchange needs one orbital setting. For spin-conserving Hamiltonians with N spatial orbitals encoded via Jordan-Wigner, the X-rank, the binary rank of the X parts of its strings, of any commuting Pauli string family is no more than 2(N-1). Searches with the Robust Adaptive Nature-inspired Global Explorer (RANGE) reach this limit for CH_4, with the rank of the Hamiltonian support vouching for the maximality of the search values. And for all nonidentity Pauli strings on n qubits, semidefinite witnesses from the Lovasz theta number give a lower bound on the Clifford setting count that agrees with the integer cover and counting bounds at 2^n+1.
