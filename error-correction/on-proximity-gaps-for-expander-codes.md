Proximity gaps describe a phenomenon concerning random linear combinations of words and their distance from a linear error-correcting code. Informally, they address the following question: if a collection of words is jointly far from the code, is a random linear combination of those words likely to also remain far from the code? Motivated by applications to concretely efficient proof systems, in this note we study this question for expander codes, a class of low-density parity-check (LDPC) codes. Recent works of Goyal and Guruswami (STOC 2026) and Goyal, Guruswami, Sun, and Wootters (RANDOM 2026; arXiv 2026) establish proximity gaps approaching capacity for random LDPC ensembles, but only asymptotically and for very large degrees and field sizes, well beyond what is used in practical proof systems. We focus on general expander codes (not necessarily random) with small degree. Building on the smoothness property of expander codes due to Dinur, Sudan, and Wigderson (RANDOM 2006), we show that such codes have proximity gaps up to nearly half of the distance guaranteed by their expansion.
