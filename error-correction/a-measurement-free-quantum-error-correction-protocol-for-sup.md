arXiv:2611.25095v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The realization of large-scale quantum computation will require quantum error correction (QEC). One way to reduce the demands placed on QEC is to correct dominant physical errors at a lower level before passing the remaining errors to an outer code. Recently, Krastanov et al. [Nat. Commun. 12, 191 (2021)] proposed a measurement-free error-correction protocol based on coherent control of photonic modes that corrects for photon loss. Here, we adapt this approach to a circuit quantum electrodynamics (cQED) architecture using three superconducting microwave cavities coupled by Josephson-junction-based nonlinear elements. We encode the logical qubit in the lowest-order binomial code of one cavity, while the two other cavities serve as ancillas. Using numerical simulations with experimentally realistic parameters, we demonstrate that our protocol can correct single-photon loss with 99% fidelity. Furthermore, we optimize the waiting time between successive rounds of error-correction, and show that the logical lifetime can be extended by a factor of 1.2-1.8 relative to the unprotected Fock {|0rangle, |1rangle} encoding, surpassing break-even performance. Our work highlights the potential of gate-based, measurement-free QEC for fault-tolerant quantum computing.
