arXiv:2611.18192v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The idea of using quantum entropy for quantum error correction (QEC) has existed for a long time. However, its computational complexity has limited its practical use: even a [[4,1]] code for standard independent amplitude damping errors involves a 256imes 256 Hermitian matrix, so the approach scales poorly as the codeword length increases. For correlated errors, the situation is even worse. In this paper, we show that, by using the insights developed in our previous paper and exploiting certain structures of quantum conditional mutual information, we can solve these problems exactly by hand, using only 2imes 2 unitary matrices. By examining the entropy, we identify the leakage channels and the amount of leakage exactly, then define the recovery map accordingly. The rules defining our recovery map are simple, intuitive, and computationally inexpensive: for independent errors, it is based on 16 explicitly given orthonormal vectors and 6 instructions; for correlated errors, it is based on 6 explicitly given orthonormal vectors and 6 instructions. Our method sacrifices little in terms of performance: for the widely used entanglement fidelity, our simple QEC recovery map achieves a sharp result; for the worst-case fidelity, the best result is about 9% better than ours; for correlated errors, there are no comparable results for short codewords. Among the known techniques, our recovery map is the first non-global one. The calculations use elementary linear algebra and calculus, and is presented in great detail.
