arXiv:2610.19676v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Encoding quantum information in d-dimensional systems (qudits) with d>2 offers appealing new directions for resource-efficient quantum computing. However, protecting the coherence of nonclassical qudit resource states remains an open challenge. Dynamical decoupling (DD) is a well-established technique for extending coherence, but its application to qudits is hindered by the quadratic scaling of the decoupling sequence overhead with the dimension d. As a result, demonstrations of qudit DD have so far been limited to low-dimensional qudits. Here, we show that noise-biased qudits enable a nontrivial reduction of this overhead, for example, from quadratic to linear. We perform qudit DD on a model d=8 qudit, the nuclear spin of an antimony donor in silicon. We benchmark DD performance on highly nonclassical states such as uniform superpositions, cat states, and Haar random states and measure coherence improvements up to a factor 5 to 10. For a spin-cat error correction code, continuous DD extends the coherence time of the logical superposition |+rangle_L to 225~ms, compared to 19~ms under free evolution and 85~ms with a Hahn echo. This improvement shows that DD sequences tailored to a system's noise bias can significantly enhance the lifetime of encoded logical qubits.
