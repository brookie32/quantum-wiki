arXiv:2506.18979v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Qubit platforms supporting flexible connectivity have opened the door to theoretical advances in fault-tolerant quantum computation (FTQC), offering a compelling alternative to lattice surgery by leveraging transversal gates. Among such systems, neutral atoms have demonstrated notable progress enabled by large-scale parallel atom transport. However, a crucial gap remains between the theory of FTQC and its practical realization with these systems. Most critically, a key theoretical requirement, that syndrome extraction must be performed frequently enough to keep error accumulation below a threshold constant, is not satisfied in a scalable manner in the existing approach due to increased transport times. In this work, we develop a theoretical framework that closes such a gap, bridging theoretical advances in fault-tolerant protocols with the latest experimental capabilities. Building on the "game of surface code" framework originally developed for superconducting qubits, we introduce an alternative game-based paradigm for transversal-gate FTQC, formulating the protocol as a transversal surface-code game governed by rules for mode transitions of code cells. The rulesets are designed to enable syndrome extraction at any intermediate step during logical gate implementation, ensuring compatibility with the threshold theorem, while fully exploiting the capabilities of the hardware to scale and accelerate FTQC. As an application, our framework offers a systematic methodology and high-level abstraction for resource estimation and optimization; an exemplary estimation for a neutral-atom system demonstrates performance competitive with a baseline lattice-surgery-based approach on superconducting qubits, even with physical operations orders of magnitude slower. These results bridge the theory and experiment of FTQC with reconfigurable qubits toward scalable, fault-tolerant quantum computers.
