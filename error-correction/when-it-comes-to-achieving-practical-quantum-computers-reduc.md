When it comes to achieving practical quantum computers, reducing runtimes of quantum applications is critically important. A quantum computation can slow down each time it waits for a classical processor to interpret measurement results and decide what to do next. In our latest paper, Xanadu’s quantum architecture team formalizes reaction depth, a measure of how many of these feedback steps a circuit may need in sequence. Separating feedback from the quantum operations themselves shows where circuit design can save time. By moving corrections onto auxiliary qubits, we build efficient AND gates, adders, and multipliers using operations suited to quantum LDPC codes. Learn more: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24687
