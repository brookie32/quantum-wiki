arXiv:2611.24852v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Fault tolerance is understood through Pauli algebra, which fails at non-Clifford gates. Circuits such as magic-state cultivation, which checks an encoded T state with physical T gates, have therefore lacked an exact theory of which faults are harmful. We develop such a theory and show that, for any circuit that prepares a magic state with non-Clifford gates and postselection, one stabilizer code on the non-Clifford gates decides which faults are harmful. This ties cultivation to distillation, bounds its T-count from below and gives an exact test. For a single check on an ideally encoded state, the fault distance reduces to Pauli algebra on the code. As a use case, we analyze the S gates that have stood in for T gates in the design of cultivation. We show that this proxy can only overstate the fault distance of such a check, and that the checks in use, on color and surface codes, tolerate at most half as many faults as their code allows. The theory also yields nearest-neighbor checks that keep the full distance, certified up to code distance nine.
