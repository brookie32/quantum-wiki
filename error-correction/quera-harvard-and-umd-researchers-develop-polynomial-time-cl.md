Researchers developed a polynomial-time Monte Carlo method for benchmarking fault-tolerant non-Clifford quantum circuits, which are essential for universal quantum computation. This method efficiently simulates complex circuits and evaluates logical fidelity by backpropagating Pauli faults and reducing errors to Clifford operators. They demonstrated its effectiveness on circuits up to 256 physical qubits and various magic-state factory designs, finding that a shared ancilla block significantly improves efficiency.
