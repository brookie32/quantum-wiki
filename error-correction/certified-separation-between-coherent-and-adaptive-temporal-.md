arXiv:2611.25216v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Repeated quantum-error-correction readout is normally converted into a classical time series: each syndrome pulse is measured on arrival and only the resulting classical record is retained. Nonorthogonal readout signals raise the question of whether preserving quantum information between rounds can improve the final recovery decision. We formulate this comparison as a loss-weighted state-discrimination problem. For an idealized two-round benchmark with quadrature phase-shift-keying (QPSK) signals, a receiver with a seven-dimensional persistent register has certified recovery-decision error below 24.2100%, whereas every separate adaptive receiver has error above 25.1431%. The certified separation is at least 0.933 percentage points. The loss counts incorrect final error-mask decisions in the benchmark. In the strict architecture, where the counted register is the entire persistent state, four dimensions are necessary and sufficient to outperform the adaptive class. Certified separations also occur at three and four rounds with register dimensions 8 and 36, compared with 16 and 64 for full-prefix storage. We relate memory dimension to the structure of the temporal measurement, give a sufficient memory bound for collective measurements, and quantify robustness to register dephasing and accumulated channel error.
