arXiv:2611.17558v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: THz imaging via Rydberg-atom up-conversion offers a powerful full-field modality with high sensitivity and rapid acquisition. However sub-millimeter imaging above 1 THz is still constrained by source power and beam-delivery loss. In this paper, we demonstrate a (50~mm imes 50~mm) full-field imaging at 1.56~THz by implementing the (11P_{3/2} rightarrow 10D_{5/2}) transition of a cesium Rydberg-atom vapor. The measured spatial response FWHMs are (0.314~mm) in horizontal and (0.354~mm) in vertical for a (0.5~mm) pinhole, alongside a sensitivity of (31.7~fW,s^{-1/2}/(6.5 imes 6.5)~mum^{2}), and a frame rate up to (300~fps). Under matched illumination, relay, and image-processing conditions, mask imaging at 1.56 THz reproduces finer features and edges with substantially higher edge contrast, deeper intensity-trough separation in line profiles, and recovered detail that remains blurred or unresolved at 0.55 THz. Furthermore, preliminary imaging of a (200~mum) tissue slice suggests morphological correspondence with H&E histology histology, offered as a proof-of-concept for label-free tissue characterization. By validating the wavelength-dependent resolution scaling in Rydberg THz imaging, this work establishes frequency scaling as a general design principle for sub-millimeter Rydberg-THz imaging.
