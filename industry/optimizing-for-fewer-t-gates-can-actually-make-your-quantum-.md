Optimizing for fewer T-gates can actually make your quantum program slower. For decades, the quantum industry assumed magic states were the ultimate bottleneck, consuming over 90% of fault-tolerant resources. But the math has changed. Recent benchmarks show that cutting T-gates by 3% can paradoxically inflate total physical spacetime volume by 41% due to routing, layout, and memory overhead. In our latest joint paper with Classiq, IonQ’s Zeljko Susnjar and Zheng Cui, Ph.D. break down why full-stack accounting - not isolated gate counts - must define the next generation of fault-tolerant quantum computing.
