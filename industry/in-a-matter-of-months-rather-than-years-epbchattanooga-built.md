In a matter of months rather than years, @EPB_Chattanooga built a dedicated quantum applications team with IonQ's support. Its Quantum Computing Fellows have now published three papers using an IonQ Forte Enterprise quantum computer. Their focus is distribution network reconfiguration (DNR) and the process of deciding which switches to open and close as assets are added, removed, or knocked offline. The aim is to keep power flowing with the least loss. Yet, the number of possible layouts explodes as the grid grows, and every valid layout has to stay radial, with no loops. In this simplified animation, an IonQ Forte system samples promising candidate layouts. A classical solver starts from those candidates and converges sooner, reaching a near-optimal layout in less solver time. Quantum and classical computing each do what they do best. We're continuing this work with EPB using insights from its 600-square-mile distribution system.
