arXiv:2611.27523v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We report two actively stabilized Fabry-Perot cavities operated as reflection-mode notch filters for biphotons generated by spontaneous four-wave mixing in a cold two-level atomic ensemble. In this source, elastic scattering at the excitation frequency increases the single-detector count rates and the accidental-coincidence background, thereby limiting the observed nonclassical correlations. One cavity is placed in each photon-collection mode. An acousto-optic modulator frequency-dithers an auxiliary field, and synchronous demodulation of the transmitted power provides the locking discriminator. Light resonant with the auxiliary field is transmitted, whereas the detuned components are reflected toward the photon detectors. By tuning the auxiliary field to the same frequency as the excitation field, we filter these components and direct a larger portion of the quantum-correlated sidebands to the detection. The cavities have linewidths of approximately 2 MHz and remain locked for longer than a typical correlation acquisition. The measured Cauchy-Schwarz parameter exceeds unity in both configurations, with a higher central value with filtering (overline{R} = 4.15 pm 0.93) than without (overline{R} = 2.33 pm 0.20). This architecture provides actively stabilized rejection of a narrow spectral interval, simultaneously, for two fields detected in different directions. Filter performance could be further improved by adjusting the cavity linewidth to better match the spectral width of the central component and by reducing losses in the filters.
