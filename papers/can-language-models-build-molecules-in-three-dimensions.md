arXiv:2611.25041v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Chemical drawings convey which atoms are bonded and how stereocentres are configured, but not molecular shape. Every simulation starts from three-dimensional coordinates, and building them from a drawing remains a manual step. We introduce MolDraft, a curated benchmark of 300 drawing-structure pairs spanning natural products, hypervalent compounds, and coordination complexes. Given an image and a short instruction, an AI system must generate a complete set of atomic coordinates, which are checked for molecular identity, stereochemical configuration, and coarse ligand geometry. We evaluate nine multimodal language models using the same agent setup. The share of molecules built correctly, the resolve rate, ranges from 25.0% to 76.0% and falls as molecules grow, most sharply above about 80 atoms. Only 29 of the 300 molecules are resolved by all nine models, and literature structures, mostly metal complexes, are the hardest source for every model. Language models can thus build many molecules in three dimensions, but not yet reliably reproduce the coordination chemistry of the research literature.
