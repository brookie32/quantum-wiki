Researchers used a defect‑free unitary Fermi gas in programmable transport geometries to study how superfluid dissipation changes with dimensionality, finding that resistance reaches a minimum at the one‑to‑two‑dimensional crossover and paradoxically increases when widening a channel beyond this point. In quasi‑1D channels the dissipative behavior follows Langer–Ambegaokar–McCumber–Halperin phase‑slip theory, exhibiting an exponential scaling of the activation factor over many orders of magnitude. Quasi‑2D (narrow) channels instead display dissipation consistent with a finite‑size vortex model.
