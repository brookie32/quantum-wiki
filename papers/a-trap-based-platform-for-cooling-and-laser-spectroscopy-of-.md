arXiv:2611.17367v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: We report on the first experiments with the Stopping and Trapping of Radioactive Isotopes for Precision Experiments (STRIPE) segmented linear Paul trap. The intent for this apparatus is to serve as an efficient capture and laser-cooling trap for radioactive isotopes produced at radioactive ion beam facilities. We demonstrate the deceleration of 10 keV ion beams and their trapping and laser cooling into Coulomb crystals. As an initial spectroscopic benchmark, the dipole-forbidden 5s ^2S_{1/2} rightarrow 4d ^2D_{5/2} transition in ^{88}Sr^+ was driven, partially resolving the Zeeman structure and yielding a Gaussian linewidth of 14.5(8) MHz, with the residual width attributed to the ions' micromotion. The lifetime of the metastable 4d ^2D_{5/2} state was measured to be 0.325(5)_{stat}(4)_{sys} s, uncorrected for collisional quenching. Several measurements to quantify the cooling timescale, the detection rate of the photon collection region and the trap storage lifetime were also performed. The developed technique will allow radioactive ion beams to be decelerated and cooled for precision spectroscopy measurements.
