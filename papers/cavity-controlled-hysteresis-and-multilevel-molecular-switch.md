arXiv:2611.26724v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: We investigate cavity-controlled hysteresis and multilevel molecular switching in a hybrid molecular optomechanical system composed of a plasmonic nanocavity, a Fabry-Perot cavity, and an ensemble of molecular vibrations. Molecular-plasmonic optomechanical interaction together with cavity-plasmon hybridization produce bistability and dynamical hysteresis, leading to a history-dependent optical and molecular response. An analytical treatment of the steady-state equations shows that the auxiliary cavity renormalizes the effective optical linewidth and detuning, while the collective molecular interaction generates an effective nonlinearity. Together, these effects enable control of the hysteretic regime through optical detuning, cavity-plasmon coupling, and molecular ensemble size. Beyond bistability, pulsed excitation generates staircase-like molecular dynamics, with ascending or descending vibrational populations depending on the optical driving configuration. In particular, the FP cavity controls the buildup of vibrational excitation, while simultaneous driving of both optical modes enables stepwise vibrational depletion. These results establish hybrid molecular optomechanical systems as a tunable platform for history-dependent and pulse-driven molecular dynamics, with potential applications in nanoscale memory, multilevel information processing, and optically controlled molecular switching.
