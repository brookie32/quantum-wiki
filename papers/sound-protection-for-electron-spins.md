Solid‐state electron spins are vulnerable to decoherence caused by fluctuations in their host material. A recent study demonstrates that acoustic phonons—essentially sound waves—can be used to extend the coherence time of silicon‑vacancy (Si‑V) spin qubits in diamond. By coupling these vibrations to the Si‑V centers, researchers achieved longer spin coherence, providing a new avenue for enhancing the robustness of quantum technologies.
