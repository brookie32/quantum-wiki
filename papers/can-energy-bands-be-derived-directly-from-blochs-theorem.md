Bloch's theorem states that solutions to the Schrödinger equation in a periodic potential can be expressed as plane waves modulated by periodic functions, and this description underlies the concept of electronic band structures.   Energy bands are a direct consequence of Bloch's theorem—all solutions have the Bloch form, allowing energy levels to vary continuously with the wave vector k, creating continuous functions En(k) that define each energy band.   Bloch's theorem predicts that electrons can only have energies within certain allowed bands, separated by band gaps where no states exist.
