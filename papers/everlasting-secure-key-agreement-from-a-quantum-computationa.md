arXiv:2004.10173v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: We introduce the Quantum Computational Timelock (QCT) model, combining time-lock encryption with a quantitative noisy-quantum-storage assumption. Time-lock encryption gives Bob immediate access to the encoding seed and gives Eve the same final information as access to that seed only after a protected interval. Eve may then process her retained information without computational restriction. We state this delayed-access encryption assumption explicitly and construct MUB-QCT from a complete set of mutually unbiased bases in dimension d and an affine pair-wise independent permutation family. For single-copy encoding, every seed-independent immediate measurement has trace-distance advantage at most 1/(2sqrt{d+1}) and mutual information at most 1/(d+1). Effective residual storage at diamond distance at most elta from replacement adds at most elta to the individual-attack distinguishing bound. Under i.i.d. individual attacks and entropy-preserving acceptance, we obtain an asymptotic key-rate bound and formulate final-key secrecy in composable trace distance. For m independently randomized signals sharing one bit and seed, the optimal leakage under non-adaptive product measurements is min{1,m/(d+1)}, allowing a linear-in-d detection enhancement in that attack model. We also construct a seed-independent joint measurement that recovers the bit after disclosure with error at most arepsilon from O(log d+log(1/arepsilon)) signals, retaining only classical information after measurement. This gives a quantitative separation between product and joint measurements. Bob uses two logical detection outcomes independently of d.
