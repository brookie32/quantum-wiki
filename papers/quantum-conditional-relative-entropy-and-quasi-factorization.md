arXiv:2611.27769v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: One of the characteristic features that distinguishes quantum systems from classical ones is the inability, except for some special cases, of defining a conditional expectation map for a given quantum state, namely a map that restricts observables to a given sub-region (or more generally, a sub-algebra of observables) while preserving expectation values. Of the many consequences of this lack of conditional expectation, we consider the problem of defining the quantum conditional relative entropy, i.e., the relative entropy between two quantum states conditioned to a sub-region or sub-algebra. We discuss how, when the conditional expectation map is not defined, there are multiple, non-equivalent ways to define a quantum conditional relative entropy quantity, and we present some of their properties. Classical conditional relative entropy has played a fundamental role in many applications to spin systems, and in particular in proving logarithmic Sobolev inequalities. A key feature of conditional relative entropy that intervenes in these results is its quasi-factorization or approximate tensorization property: informally, the relative entropy is almost super-additive over weakly-dependent sub-algebras. It is then not surprising that the key to obtaining similar applications in the quantum setting, such as quantum logarithmic Sobolev inequalities in spin systems, has been to obtain approximate factorization results for the quantum conditional relative entropy, with the added complication that there is not a unique definition of conditional relative entropy to work with. We review the recent results regarding quasi-factorization of the quantum conditional relative entropy, in strong and weak forms.
