The study demonstrates that coherent mixing of spatial modes in quantum‑correlated optical networks can cause “hyperloss,” an apparent loss that exceeds the intrinsic squeezing, even with only modest mode mismatch. In a two‑node network, an 8 % mismatch destroyed 5.8 dB of observable squeezing, creating an effectively thermal state, but the lost correlations were recoverable by adjusting differential spatial‑mode phases. This result identifies hyperloss as a practical design constraint for future photonic quantum technologies.
