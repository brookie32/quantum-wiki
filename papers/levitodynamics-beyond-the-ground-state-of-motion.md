Levitodynamics has recently achieved ground‑state cooling of levitated nanoparticles, demonstrating full quantum control of their motion.  
Building on this milestone, the field is now shifting its focus toward two promising directions: ultra‑sensitive sensing using levitated probes and engineering non‑classical mechanical states such as squeezed or entangled motional modes.  
These developments open pathways for both deeper fundamental tests of quantum mechanics in macroscopic systems and emerging technological applications, although challenges related to scalability, isolation from noise, and practical integration remain.
