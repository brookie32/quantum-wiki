arXiv:2611.13221v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: An exceptional point of a memory-coefficient equation need not have the same signature in every quantum-state observable. We examine this relation within exact non-Markovian quantum-state diffusion (NMQSD), following the same operator closure from its coefficient dynamics to the non-Markovian master equation obtained by noise averaging. A finite homogeneous lift identifies the Jordan chains reached by the causal initialization, while state reconstruction determines which contributions survive in a specified readout. Two models expose the distinction. For common-bath qubits restricted to zero and one excitation, a noise-independent lowering-form O operator gives a closed master equation. Every defective decaying mode transfers its full pole order to a suitable unconditional excitation--reference coherence. A known third-order configuration illustrates both a quadratic--exponential coherence and cancellation of its highest-order term in another quadrature. For a position-coupled oscillator in a fixed-photon-number optomechanical sector, the exact closure contains a scalar noise-history term. On the regular normalizable branch studied for bath decay rates below the oscillator frequency, two second-order coefficient blocks lead to a double pole in the conditional displacement response to the NMQSD noise history, at long preparation age and fixed lag. The purely algebraic coefficient tail cancels from the conditional Gaussian width. Complete noise averaging changes the master-equation coefficients and combines conditional width with the weighted spread of state centers. Together, the models show how causal preparation, state reconstruction, and averaging give coefficient exceptional points a precise, readout-dependent meaning.
