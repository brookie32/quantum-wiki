Researchers engineered two coupled minimal Kitaev chains—each consisting of a quantum‑dot–superconductor array that hosts two “poor man’s” Majorana zero modes—and performed time‑resolved measurements on the system. They observed coherent oscillations between global even and odd fermion‑parity states, with oscillation frequencies matching within 5 % at an identified sweet spot, indicating that the unprotected Majoranas act largely as isolated modes. The experiments further showed that the oscillation frequency, rotation axis, and coherence systematically depend on inter‑chain coupling strength and quantum‑dot gate tuning, pointing to a viable route for coherent qubit control using minimal Kitaev chains.
