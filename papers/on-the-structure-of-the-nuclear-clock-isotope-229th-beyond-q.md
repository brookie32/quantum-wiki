arXiv:2611.21807v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Precision measurements of the exceptionally low-energy nuclear transition of ^{229}Th, offer an extraordinary sensitivity to a variety of new light fields. However, interpreting such measurements requires nuclear-structure information beyond what is currently constrained by spectroscopy. We develop a systematic description of the electric multipole interactions governing the hyperfine structure of {}^{229}Th, including the hexadecapole contribution, and perform a symmetry-based analysis of the resulting hyperfine splittings in crystalline environments. Hyperfine spectroscopy generally constrains combinations of nuclear moments and crystal-field gradients, leading to degeneracies when higher multipole interactions are allowed. We then combine experimental nuclear observables, particle-rotor relations, and a deformed-Fermi charge-density model to investigate the electromagnetic contribution to the nuclear clock's sensitivity to variations in the fine-structure constant. We find that higher-order nuclear deformation and state-dependent differences in the radial charge distribution of the clock states can substantially alter the predicted sensitivity, introducing large uncertainties that are not constrained by existing measurements. Our results highlight the importance of combining nuclear and solid-state theory with complementary measurements, including spectroscopy of higher-spin states, to constrain the nuclear structure and improve predictions of the clock's sensitivity to new physics. Our symmetry-based analysis also provides a framework for using hyperfine spectroscopy to investigate local defect symmetries in crystalline hosts.
