arXiv:2611.17642v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Whether gravity has quantum degrees of freedom is a question of fundamental importance. We formulate a linear monitoring-feedback framework for gravitational interactions that includes local classical-record models of semiclassical gravity and, when quantum resources are supplied in correlated ancilla inputs, can reproduce linearized quantum-gravity dynamics. Auxiliary systems monitor the positions of massive bodies, and their records are fed back through a chosen gravitational response kernel. At the level of linear input-output theory, this construction makes the deterministic response explicit while exposing the force noise and correlations associated with the auxiliary sector. We organize the allowed ancilla correlations into three regimes. In Regime-I, independent ancillas have unsqueezed, uncorrelated sensing and back-action quadratures. A single object then has a minimum classical-gravity noise (CGN), S_F^min = 2 hbar M Omega_SN^2, where Omega_SN is the Schrodinger-Newton frequency set by the microscopic mass distribution. In Regime-II, arbitrary local sensing--back-action correlations are allowed, but the ancillas associated with different objects remain independent. The single-object CGN is then unrestricted, while the joint force-noise matrix obeys a determinant bound that is equivalent to the local classical-channel/no-entanglement bound. In Regime-III, cross-object correlations and a common nonlocal mediator are allowed. The commuting force noises can then be jointly squeezed, entanglement between the masses can occur, and--with a suitable retarded response and input state--the monitoring-feedback system can reproduce the noise and commutators of linearized quantum gravity, including non-Markovian quantum fluctuations.
