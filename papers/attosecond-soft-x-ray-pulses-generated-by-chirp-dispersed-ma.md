arXiv:2408.03858v5 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Free-electron lasers deliver intense attosecond X-ray pulses, which are the key to studying electron dynamics in specifically targeted electronic states, but come at the expense of complicated generation schemes and stochastic pulse shapes. Here, we demonstrate a novel and simple operation concept based on dispersive manipulation of the lasing electron bunch and capable of working with the full 4.5 MHz repetition rate at the European XFEL in Germany. Using a high-fidelity single-shot temporal characterisation, we detect X-ray pulses with durations of down to 200 attoseconds and peak powers reaching into the terawatt regime at ~1 keV photon energy. As a direct application, we present simultaneous measurements of nonlinear X-ray-matter interaction via time-resolved electron spectroscopy. Using the pulse duration retrieval and restricting the durations to a regime where individual X-ray pulses are shorter than the single-core-hole life time in neon atoms, we reveal an otherwise hidden peak-intensity dependence in the nonlinear dynamics of double-core-hole formation. Our results shed light on highly transient intermediate steps in complex electronic dynamics of systems driven far off their equilibrium state, and thus promise to help build the conceptual bridge between fundamental physics processes and real-world photo reactions.
