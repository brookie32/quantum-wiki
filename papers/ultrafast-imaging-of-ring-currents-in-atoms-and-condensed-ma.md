arXiv:2611.27420v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Ring currents are well known to generate magnetic fields. Despite the rich phenomena and detection of the ring current, an experimental reconstruction of the ring current at the atomic scale is still unsolved. In this work, we propose a method of resolving the ring current in a sample at the atomic scale by ultrafast electron diffraction. Inspired by the Rosenbluth formula, we study the electron diffraction of a sample with a ring current and separate the scattering cross sections into three kinds of response functions, which stand for the electric part, the magnetic part and the cross term, respectively. The ring current in the sample can be reconstructed from measuring the response function in different scattering configurations. Also, following the Rosenbluth formula, we propose that the response functions can be measured by fitting over fixed momentum transfer with different incident energy and relative direction, which is similar to the psi-scan in crystal diffraction. With high-precision ultrafast electron diffraction, the magnetic part will be resolved despite being much weaker than the electric part. We simulate three examples with ring currents -- an ideal hydrogen atom, a circularly polarized helium atom ensemble, and a lithium crystal. Our model offers a promising way to resolve the current structure at the atomic scale.
