arXiv:2611.33283v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: In this work, we study local operator quenches in thermal two-dimensional conformal field theories with complex-time evolution, which can be viewed as an effective description of conditional dynamics in a system interacting with an environment. In contrast to quenches of the vacuum, the thermal initial state allows several conformal families to participate in the evolution. We show that their competition can produce cascades of approximately homogeneous energy plateaux, with successive plateaux approaching the energies of different primary states. Using conformal Ward identities on the cylinder and torus, we determine the local energy density and illustrate this behavior in free-field theories, the Ising model and Wess--Zumino--Witten models. We develop a cold-limit spectral description in which the dominant primary channel determines the height of each plateau, while conformal dimensions and operator matrix elements govern the transitions between them. For a fixed compact unitary CFT, we show that only finitely many primary channels can contribute to the leading plateau sequence. Finally, we discuss the large-central-charge regime, relating light-operator plateaux to few-particle sectors in thermal AdS and examining the limitations of this picture for heavy quenches.
