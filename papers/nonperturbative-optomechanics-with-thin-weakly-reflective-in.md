arXiv:2610.15749v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Levitated dielectric sensors are often treated as perturbative probes of a pre-existing optical field. We show that this picture fails for a novel sensor geometry: a dielectric plate whose transverse extent captures the full optical mode. Even an optically thin sensor with modest refractive index can heavily restructure the intracavity fields, effectively partitioning a cavity into two subcavities with substantial cross-coupling and producing new optical resonances and trapping sites with properties which deviate from the standard intuition. Conventional perturbative expressions for the cavity frequency shift and optomechanical coupling depart from the exact solution at thicknesses as small as simlambda/(10n). Furthermore, cavity locking decreases the coupling and increases the trap stiffness by orders of magnitude, and the coupled subcavity structure permits appreciable optical cooling at zero detuning, a distinct behavior unique to this nonperturbative regime. These effects, sharing a common origin, establish a distinct regime of cavity optomechanics in which the sensor is no longer merely a probe of the cavity field, but an element that helps define it.
