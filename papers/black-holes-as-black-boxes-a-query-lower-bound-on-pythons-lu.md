arXiv:2611.21723v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The python's lunch conjecture relates the complexity of bulk reconstruction in holographic tensor networks to an exponential cost associated with post-selection. We establish a tight query lower bound for this decoding problem in a Haar random oracle model, with a python's lunch circuit that applies Haar random unitary U to a logical input and fixed ancillas, followed by post-selection on m output qubits. Given one copy of the normalized encoded state and coherent oracle access to U and U^agger, including controlled queries, we prove that recovering even a single logical bit with constant average advantage requires Omega(2^{m/2}) queries. Together with the state-independent amplitude amplification upper bound for approximately isometric encodings, this establishes query complexity Theta(2^{m/2}) at constant decoding accuracy. We also comment on the decoding cost for other "admissible'" ensembles of unitaries U which give rise to families of approximately isometric holographic maps.
