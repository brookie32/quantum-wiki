arXiv:2611.27707v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Lieb-Robinson bounds (LRBs) have become a standard tool for perturbative results in quantum many-body physics, including gap stability and many-body adiabatic theory. Here, we use LRBs to locally approximate weakly interacting many-body dynamics with one-body dynamics on large, perturbative time scales. This allows us to use one-body transport results to establish the expected many-body transport behavior on finite time scales in the following two illustrative settings: (i) We prove quantum diffusion, as quantified by a certain operator spreading diagnostic, in weakly interacting and weakly disordered Fermi systems in dimensions Dgeq2 on large finite time scales based on the non-interacting diffusion result of Black, Drogin, and Hernandez [arXiv:2506.06468]. (ii) We show for Fermi-Hubbard models with initially low spin-down density, that all local multi-time spin-up correlation functions are approximated by their free counterparts. In particular, we obtain an OTOC (out-of-time-ordered correlator) counterpart to the patterns of short-time entanglement related to lattice path counts observed numerically by Bippus et al. [Phys. Rev. B 113, 035152 (2026)].
