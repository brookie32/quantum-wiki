arXiv:2610.23560v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We prove that a quantum walker cannot search a two-dimensional lattice in Grover's O(sqrt N) time, resolving the long-standing open question posed by Aaronson and Ambainis in 2003. A walker that hops between neighbouring sites of an Limes L lattice, N=L^2, needs Omega(sqrt{Nlog N}) local steps at least to find a marked vertex, even with unlimited memory and free queries. Hence, Tulsi's algorithm is asymptotically optimal. Our proof rests on a surprising correspondence with classical electrostatics. We treat the marked vertex as a point charge and weight the overlap between the walker's states with and without the mark by the potential drop, which vanishes where queries act. Only local steps change this weighted overlap, at a rate limited by the field energy, so the lower bound is set by the self-potential of the charge. In the plane this self-potential grows logarithmically with system size, like the Coulomb potential, leading to the extra root-logarithmic factor. Several marked vertices turn the charge into a conductor, and the lower bound depends on their arrangement through its capacitance.
