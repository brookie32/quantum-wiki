arXiv:2603.19207v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Interfacial hydrodynamic instabilities in multicomponent superfluids provide a versatile platform to explore nonequilibrium quantum dynamics beyond classical fluid analogues. We study dynamical interfacial instabilities in a quasi-two-dimensional three-component Bose-Einstein condensate confined in a harmonic trap, where differential effective rotation between neighboring components is generated through selective phase imprinting. A component with an imprinted vorticity l_j acquires an azimuthal phase winding phi_j = l_jheta, producing a superfluid velocity v_heta(r_j) = l_jhbar/mr_j at interface radius r_j and an effective angular velocity Omega_j^{eff} = l_jhbar/mr_j^2. The differential effective rotation DeltaOmega_j^{eff} = Omega_j^{eff} - Omega_{j+1}^{eff} between neighboring components constitutes the sole driver of interfacial shear and the Kelvin-Helmholtz instability (KHI). Three instability regimes are examined: KHI in the strongly immiscible limit, counter-superflow instability in the partially miscible regime, and a parameter window where both mechanisms coexist. A hydrodynamic pressure-balance model is derived, showing that the KHI onset condition depends only on DeltaOmega_j^{eff}. Time-dependent Gross-Pitaevskii simulations and a Bogoliubov-de Gennes analysis identify the dominant unstable modes, whose spatial structures are consistent with the observed density modulations. The dominant azimuthal mode is set by a competition between shear-driven destabilization and surface-tension stabilization, confirmed by numerical simulations with different imprinted vorticities. The results show that a three-component condensate with two tunable interfaces provides a controlled platform for studying multicomponent quantum hydrodynamics, with instability dynamics significantly richer than those of binary mixtures.
