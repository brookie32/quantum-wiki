The authors report room‑temperature optically detected magnetic resonance from negatively charged boron vacancies in hot‑pressed, polycrystalline hexagonal boron nitride (hBN). The random orientation of hBN grains creates an ensemble of defect spin axes that produces a robust, alignment‑free scalar magnetometric response—albeit with broadened linewidths and reduced contrast compared to single‑crystal samples. Numerical simulations incorporating hyperfine coupling and anisotropic broadening reproduce the observed spectra, demonstrating that such polycrystalline hBN can serve as a practical ambient‑condition magnetic field sensor without requiring precise device alignment.
