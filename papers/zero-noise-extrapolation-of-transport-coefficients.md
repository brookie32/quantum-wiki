arXiv:2611.05481v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: NISQ devices routinely estimate properties of the noiseless system from measurements at varying noise strengths gamma. These zero-noise extrapolation (ZNE) schemes fail to estimate transport coefficients of conserved quantities when the noise violates the associated conservation laws. We show how to extrapolate measurements in noisy, one-dimensional, non-integrable spin chains to obtain the spin transport coefficients governing linear response in the zero-noise limit. As the wavevector of the Fourier mode of a conserved quantity goes to zero, its decay rate vanishes. The coefficient of the leading momentum dependence defines a transport coefficient in the noiseless system, and an analogous effective coefficient in the noisy one. Naive ZNE implicitly assumes that this effective coefficient is analytic in gamma as gammao 0. Our main result is that universal non-linear corrections to hydrodynamics can produce non-analytic dependence on gamma. In particular, we argue that the effective spin diffusion coefficient of a dissipative Heisenberg chain and the effective subdiffusion coefficient of a dipole-conserving fracton approach their noiseless value as gamma^{1/2}. We benchmark these analytical predictions numerically.
