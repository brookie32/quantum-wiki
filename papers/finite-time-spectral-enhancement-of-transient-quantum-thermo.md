arXiv:2611.17522v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Transient quantum thermometry extracts temperature information before a probe reaches thermal equilibrium. We study how finite interaction times affect thermometric precision for a harmonic-oscillator probe prepared in a number state and weakly coupled to a thermal bosonic reservoir. A second-order time-convolutionless master equation retains the finite-time reservoir response, allowing modes away from the probe frequency to shape the populations and their temperature dependence. For the baseline parameters, this response raises the quantum Fisher information about fivefold above the Markovian prediction, in agreement with an exact solution of the model. When the probability of leaving the initial state is small, we derive an analytical condition for this enhancement. To assess its operational significance, we examine the temperature information acquired per unit time. Under the exact dynamics, this rate is largest within the reservoir memory time. With negligible preparation and readout overhead, the optimum is about five times the best Markovian rate and 30 times an upper bound on the equilibrium rate. The advantage survives smooth switching, although overhead and longer coupling ramps reduce it.
