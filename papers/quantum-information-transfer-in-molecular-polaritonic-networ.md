arXiv:2611.21949v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Achieving high-fidelity transfer of an arbitrary quantum state between spatially separated qubits is a central challenge for reliable quantum hardware. We address this question within a polaritonic framework by introducing Projected-Block Van Vleck Perturbation Theory (PB-VVPT). Based on quasidegenerate perturbation theory, this approach describes a molecular network coupled to an optical cavity and local phonons through a generalized Holstein--Tavis--Cummings Hamiltonian. Quantum information is encoded in a coherent superposition of exciton-cavity states, while phonons constitute a weakly coupled environment. In the nonadiabatic regime of weak exciton-phonon coupling, the method incorporates phonon-induced renormalization of polaritonic states and their dynamics while reducing the Hamiltonian to independent fixed-phonon-number sectors and coupled polaron-polariton subblocks. This structure provides efficient access to the coherence and population propagators governing quantum-state transfer without invoking a Markovian approximation. We benchmark the approach against exact dynamics for a molecular wheel network, considering both molecule-to-molecule and molecule-to-cavity transfer. PB-VVPT accurately reproduces short- and long-time transfer dynamics, including thermal decoherence and fidelity revivals consistent with non-Markovian memory effects, with prediction errors below 1% over most of the simulated time intervals. This accuracy is achieved with an estimated diagonalization cost more than two orders of magnitude lower than that of the exact reference calculations. These results establish PB-VVPT as an accurate and computationally efficient approach to quantum information transfer in molecular polaritonic networks.
