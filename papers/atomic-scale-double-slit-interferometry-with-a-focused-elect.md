Atomic‐scale double‑slit interference is achieved by a STEM‑focused electron probe delocalized over two adjacent Si [110] atomic columns separated by 1.36 Å.  
The resulting interference fringes remain visible from 300 K to 900 K, indicating that correlated thermal vibrations between neighboring atoms preserve coherence while uncorrelated motion would destroy it.  
Analysis of this preserved visibility provides direct experimental access to inter‑column vibrational correlations and the anisotropic stiffness of the atomic bond, yielding insight into low‑energy phonon dynamics that govern thermal transport.
