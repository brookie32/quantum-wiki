arXiv:2512.16682v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Bell nonlocality is an intriguing property of quantum mechanics with far-reaching consequences for information processing, philosophy, and our fundamental understanding of nature. However, standard Bell nonlocality is a statement about correlations at a fixed point in time only. It does not take into account dynamics, i.e., time evolution of those correlations. Consider a dynamic situation where the correlations remain local for all times. Then, at each moment in time, there exists a local hidden-variable (LHV) model reproducing the momentary correlations. Can the time evolution of the correlations then be captured by evolving the hidden variables? We make this question precise by defining dynamical LHV models and motivating additional physical attributes such as locality and continuity of the dynamics. We then establish a series of complementary go and no-go theorems that together map out which combinations of these properties LHV dynamics can and cannot have. In particular, noninteracting quantum dynamics can be represented on the hidden-variable level in a local way, but LHV dynamics can fail to exist for interacting systems: Even though the observed correlations are local at all times, their time evolution may nevertheless be incompatible with local hidden-variable dynamics.
