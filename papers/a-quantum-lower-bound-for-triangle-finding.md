arXiv:2611.06751v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Triangle finding is a fundamental problem in graph theory that has played a central role in the development of quantum algorithmic techniques. For N-vertex graphs given by their adjacency matrices, a long line of work has improved the quantum query upper bound from O(N^{3/2}) to O(N^{5/4}). However, the best known lower bound has remained the elementary Omega(N) bound obtained from unstructured search. We prove the first superlinear quantum query lower bound for triangle finding, showing that Omega(N^{13/12}) queries are necessary. Our result also implies the same lower bound for triangle detection and for detecting every fixed connected non-bipartite graph. The proof builds on the compressed oracle approach of Zhandry and the transfer approaches of Belovs. It compares the computation on graphs with a planted triangle to the computation on random graphs conditioned to be triangle-free, through a map on the input registers that almost commutes with every query. We average over local changes to the input while preserving its distribution, through a procedure known as conditional resampling. Our main technical contribution is a set of estimates that let us control the query error under this averaging, despite the dependence introduced by conditioning a dense graph to be triangle-free.
