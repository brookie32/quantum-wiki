arXiv:2611.27873v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: We prove a quantum analogue of Dobrushin's comparison theorem for lattice fermions. As in the classical case, it is a tool for proving uniqueness of equilibrium states, decay of correlations, and stability of local expectations under distant perturbations. We apply it to fermions with weak many-body interactions added to a quadratic Hamiltonian, allowing pairing, spatial inhomogeneity and long-range interactions. If the quadratic Hamiltonian is gapped, there is a coupling strength below which the thermal equilibrium (KMS) state is unique at every positive temperature and its correlations decay uniformly in the temperature. Its local expectations converge to those of the unique gapped ground state exponentially fast in the inverse temperature. In the same regime, local perturbations perturb locally in a strong sense (strong LPPL): every equilibrium state of a system with a distant defect is locally close to the equilibrium state without the defect, however strong or extended the defect is. The same holds for finite-volume Gibbs states with arbitrary even boundary terms. Without a gap, uniqueness, decay of correlations and strong LPPL hold at each positive temperature for interactions below a coupling strength that depends on the temperature. At high temperature, they hold for general interactions.
