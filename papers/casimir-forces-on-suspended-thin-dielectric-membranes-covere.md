arXiv:2610.21437v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We investigate the Casimir force on thin suspended dielectric layers covered with graphene, both theoretically and experimentally. Thermal effects of the Casimir force on graphene occur at separations significantly smaller compared to ordinary metals and dielectrics. At such small separations, the Casimir force is sufficiently strong to be measured experimentally. However, the presence of substrates to support the graphene significantly reduces the enhancement. We show theoretically that replacing the solid substrate beneath graphene with a thin dielectric membrane partially restores this strong thermal dependence. One of the interacting objects is a solid gold plate. The other object is a suspended dielectric membrane made of either 15 nm of silicon oxide or 13 nm silicon nitride, covered with graphene. At a separation of sim500 nm, theoretical predictions yield changes in the Casimir pressure of up to 54 % and 40 % for silicon oxide and silicon nitride, respectively, when the temperature is increased from 10 K to 294 K. Measurements were performed for graphene on a suspended silicon nitride membrane at room temperature, using a gold-coated sphere attached to a cantilever as the force sensor. The measured force gradient is consistent with the theoretical prediction, although the thermal dependence could not be resolved, in part because of uncertainties in the optical properties of silicon nitride. Replacing a bulk substrate with a thin dielectric membrane covered with graphene provides a substantial reduction of the Casimir force, which may facilitate the stable operation of neutral nanomechanical elements at small separations.
