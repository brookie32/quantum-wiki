arXiv:2611.33299v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Quantum computing offers the prospect of surpassing classical computation on certain tasks by leveraging quantum coherence and entanglement. However, a significant gap remains between current quantum hardware and the requirements to transcend what classical computers can achieve, with decoherence limiting the capacity of storing quantum information and the number of logical steps that can be executed reliably. In the long term, fault tolerance bridges this gap, but simply changing the numerical base from binary to higher base d is a viable way to 'squeeze more juice'---get more quantum information processing---from a quantum computer of given size. Additionally, for quantum simulations, qudits offer a natural encoding scheme for high-dimensional physical degrees of freedom. Here, we review recent experimental progress in high-fidelity preparation, control, and measurement of qudits across various platforms such as trapped ions, superconducting circuits, solid-state nuclear spins, photonic systems, and neutral atoms. We highlight early demonstrations of qudit-based quantum simulation, computation and error suppression enabled by these developments, heralding the transition of qudits from conceptual constructs to viable building blocks for quantum technologies. Finally, we outline key challenges and potential future directions for scalable qudit quantum information processing.
