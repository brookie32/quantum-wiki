arXiv:2611.01293v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: After a particle forms its first receptor--ligand bond near a cell membrane, stable attachment often requires a threshold total bond number m. This paper develops a biophysical kinetic theory of this post-capture stage. The bond-number dynamics are modeled as a finite birth--death chain on n=0,1,ots,m, where 0 is loss of all bonds and m is the stabilization threshold. The mesoscopic rates lambda_n and mu_n encode the local energy landscape, accessible receptor--ligand pairs, steric and orientational constraints, and cooperative interactions within the growing cluster. The probability of reaching the threshold before losing all bonds is derived in closed form for general rates and, in the constant-rate case, reduces to a geometric series controlled by the kinetic bias ratio rho=mu/lambda. The mean time to failure or stabilization follows from a tridiagonal backward equation. Conditioning on successful attachment is treated as a trajectory-selection effect: the conditioned chain is a Doob h-transform whose dissociation rate at n=1 vanishes, because successful trajectories cannot lose all bonds before stabilization. A minimal interface between the spatial first-capture probability and the bond-number chain is formulated, together with the conditions under which the stable capture probability factorizes into their product. The analysis shows that hitting probabilities constrain only the rate ratios, whereas the absolute rate scale requires time-resolved or dwell-time data. The threshold m is a model-defined stabilization criterion, not a directly measured ligand count. The rates and threshold are effective parameters specified independently or inferred from kinetic data. The theory provides a quantitative framework for interpreting attachment probabilities, lifetimes, and the transition from first bond formation to a model-defined stabilization threshold.
