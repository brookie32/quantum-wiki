arXiv:2509.06584v5 Announce Type: replace Abstract: In our work [Sharoglazova et al., Nature 643, 67-72 (2025)], we reported the first measurement of the speed of tunnelling particles by comparing two orthogonal motions in a coupled-waveguide system. Here we respond to objections raised in comments, referee reports, preprints, and articles. In particular, we distinguish two questions that are often conflated: whether Bohmian mechanics reproduces the measured density, and whether the standard guiding equation assigns the correct state of motion to the particles. The first point follows under the usual quantum equilibrium assumptions. By contrast, identifying the guiding velocity with particle motion is an additional physical postulate, since the standard guiding equation does not follow from the Schrodinger equation alone. We argue that, in the evanescent regime, the state of motion assigned by the standard guiding equation is in disagreement with the speed following from our experiments. To make the distinction explicit, we also present a bidirectional Bohmian model that reproduces the same stationary density while assigning finite speeds compatible with the speed inferred in the evanescent regime.
