arXiv:2610.22476v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The non-stabilizerness, or "magic", of a quantum state is a common metric for the hardness of simulation on a classical computer, and is often quantified by the stabilizer Renyi entropy (SRE). We show how random superpositions of classically simulable states saturate the SRE to the symmetry-restricted Haar-random maximum. For any large-N random pure-state ensemble satisfying the conditions of Schur's lemma, the average SRE of the m-component superposition langle M_2(m)rangle=N-log_2[langle W(m)rangle] is governed by an average witness langle W(m)rangle that universally decomposes into a Haar floor and an ensemble-specific excess. As the number of components m increases, the excess is suppressed, resulting in a logarithmic scaling of the SRE before plateauing at the Haar-valued maximum. We derive a general closed form for langle W(m)rangle in the thermodynamic limit, and numerically verify our result for random stabilizer states and random Gaussian states.
