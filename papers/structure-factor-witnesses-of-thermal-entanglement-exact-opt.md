arXiv:2611.33284v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Which entanglement witness is optimal for a thermal many-body state, and what does its structure reveal about the equilibrium physics? We address these questions for the spin-1/2 XY chain in a transverse field, using the covariance-matrix criterion (CMC), which requires only one- and two-spin correlations. We show that for thermal states the optimal CMC witness is fixed by the minima over momentum of the three static spin structure factors, which are accessible to scattering experiments and can be evaluated directly in the thermodynamic limit. The optimal witnesses select the collective spin modes with the smallest thermal fluctuations, with their form dictated by symmetry rather than by criticality. They certify entanglement, and can also bound entanglement measures, up to temperatures of the order of the exchange coupling, including in states without any pairwise entanglement. A systematic investigation of entanglement in small chains reveals that the CMC is nearly as powerful as a moment-matrix hierarchy supplied with all spin correlations, and close to the XX point it detects thermal states that are PPT across every bipartition. In addition, we observe that the same witnesses can also certify entanglement generated by a sudden quench from a thermal state in which none is detected.
