arXiv:2610.29404v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Controlling transition rates is a central objective across quantum optics, condensed-matter physics, and chemical physics, encompassing processes such as spontaneous emission, energy and charge transfer, spin relaxation, and chemical reactions. Although Fermi's golden rule provides a powerful framework for calculating such rates of weakly coupled transitions, conventional calculations require the interaction to be specified in advance; the range of rate responses attainable over a broader interaction space remains unknown. We formulate spectral-overlap golden-rule rates as quadratic forms of the interaction and derive rigorous bounds on rate modulation over a specified interaction space. As an example, application to energy transfer from a donor to a molecular vibration coupled to an optical cavity reveals that enhancement and suppression depend jointly on the interaction and cavity detuning, including continuous reversal of the rate response for a fixed interaction. The same approach may elucidate rate control for other golden-rule processes, including excitation-energy transfer, nonadiabatic electron transfer, nonradiative electronic transitions, vibrational and spin relaxation, and spontaneous emission.
