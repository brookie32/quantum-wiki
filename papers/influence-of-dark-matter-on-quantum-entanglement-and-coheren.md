arXiv:2507.16142v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Dark matter (DM) has not yet been directly detected, and phenomenological models such as perfect fluid dark matter (PFDM) provide analytically tractable settings for investigating its possible gravitational effects. Here PFDM denotes the conventional name of an anisotropic effective source, rather than a perfect fluid in the usual isotropic sense. In this work, we study the entanglement and coherence of bosonic and fermionic test fields near a Schwarzschild black hole surrounded by PFDM. These quantum fields probe the background geometry and are not assumed to be microscopic constituents of PFDM. Under our sign convention, only the alpha0 branch is retained solely as a formal extension of the same metric family. Within the positive-density PFDM sector, entanglement and coherence vary monotonically with the PFDM parameter, while the complete extended parameter range exhibits a nonmonotonic dependence arising from the corresponding variation of the Hawking temperature. Bosonic entanglement is more susceptible to this geometric effect than fermionic entanglement, whereas fermionic coherence shows a stronger response than its bosonic counterpart. These results characterize how particle statistics and the choice of quantum resource govern the sensitivity of test fields to PFDM-induced modifications of the spacetime geometry.
