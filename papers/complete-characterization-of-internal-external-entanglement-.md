arXiv:2610.35183v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: We characterize the exact trade-off between internal and external entanglement in asymmetric two-mode Gaussian states at fixed local symplectic eigenvalues. Internal entanglement is quantified by the negativity N_{A:B} between the two modes, while external entanglement is quantified by the negativity N_{AB:CD} across a Gaussian purification. Numerical sampling suggests the boundary structure of the attainable region in the (N_{AB:CD},N_{A:B}) plane, which we then determine analytically. Parametrizing the global symplectic spectrum yields a closed admissible interval for the remaining spectral parameter at fixed N_{AB:CD}. We prove constructively that every point in this interval corresponds to a physical covariance matrix, implying that all intermediate values of N_{A:B} between the resulting sharp bounds are achievable. The upper bound is attained for opposite-sign correlations of equal magnitude, while the positive branch of the lower bound occurs when one global symplectic eigenvalue equals unity. We also identify two external-entanglement thresholds, derive a saturated monogamy-type inequality, and recover the symmetric-state limit. Because the external negativity depends only on the spectrum of the reduced two-mode state, the bounds hold for any purification, including non-Gaussian purifications.
