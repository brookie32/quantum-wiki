arXiv:2611.27650v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Long-range couplings in quantum many-body systems challenge conventional notions of locality and causality. Here, we show that macroscopic particle transport nevertheless exhibits emergent causality --- an effective light cone that becomes strict in the thermodynamic limit. For power-law hopping with a decay exponent alphain(D,D+1) in D spatial dimensions, the emergent causality cone expands super-ballistically, and we establish that its scaling is optimal. Our results imply lower bounds on the equilibration time of long-range systems. Finally, we construct an example of a long-range free-fermion chain with alphain (1,3/2), whose macroscopic density equilibrates super-ballistically, i.e., in a time sub-linear in the system size. While long-range interactions are usually associated with slow equilibration due to increased energy barriers, our example demonstrates that long-range hopping can lead to accelerated equilibration.
