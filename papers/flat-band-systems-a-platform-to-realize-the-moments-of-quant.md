arXiv:2611.08479v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The quantum metric is a fundamental geometric property of Bloch bands that governs a wide range of physical phenomena, yet experimentally accessing it remains challenging. We show that the momentum-space variation of the quantum metric can be probed through the real-space dynamics of a Gaussian wave packet prepared in an isolated flat band. Following the application of a weak constant force, we demonstrate analytically that the leading order change in the wave-packet variance is linear in time and contains a contribution proportional to the wave-packet averaged gradient of the quantum-metric trace. This geometric contribution remains finite despite the absence of band dispersion. We illustrate the effect using a gapped Lieb lattice with an exactly flat middle band and vanishing Berry curvature, allowing the quantum-metric contribution to the spreading to be isolated. The numerical results agree with the analytical prediction and confirm the quantum-metric contribution to the wave-packet spreading. The required lattice preparation, tunable hopping amplitudes, applied force, and density measurements are compatible with established ultracold-atom techniques, providing a possible platform for observing the predicted response. Our results establish wave-packet spreading as a dynamical probe of quantum geometry in flat-band systems.
