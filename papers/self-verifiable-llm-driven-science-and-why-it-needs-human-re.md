arXiv:2611.26754v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Language models are now used throughout all sciences - for reading literature, extracting numbers from tables and spectra, analyzing data, drafting conclusions - and their answers arrive in the same polished confident tone whether they are right or wrong, so a result has to be re-derived by a human researcher before it can be relied on. The cost of that re-checking, not the ability to generate results, is what currently limits AI-assisted research. In the field of mathematics, this problem is solved by proof assistants like Lean 4 and Coq that let any formal statement and its proof be written down and checked - a perfect match for LLMs, which are now actively used to search for proofs with every step automatically validated, rather than relying on a human to judge the result. In this paper, we propose an extension of this approach to non-mathematical sciences. We describe an AI agent harness that aims to make LLM's scientific reasoning verifiable: the starting assumptions are proposed by LLM and reviewed by a human, and everything built on top of them is checked by a machine with Lean 4. We illustrate how this mechanism works in practice on several examples and our results show that scientific arguments in these tasks can be expressed as formal proofs from explicit assumptions, including experimental results, known theoretical statements, and additional assumptions, produced by the agent. We suggest that this approach could change how scientific research is conducted across all sciences.
