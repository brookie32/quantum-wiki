Efficient ultrafast homodyne detection is achieved by exploiting temporal correlations in detector signals, where the optimal temporal weighting function for extracting quadrature values is determined via a generalized Rayleigh quotient that incorporates shot‑ and electronic‑noise autocorrelations. This optimized signal processing markedly increases the signal‑to‑noise ratio of continuous‑variable quantum states, improving detection efficiency and enhancing both squeezing and anti‑squeezing levels observed experimentally. The approach demonstrates that tailored weighting can overcome conventional limitations in ultrafast quantum optical measurements.
