arXiv:2611.18510v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Boson sampling machines have become a fruitful tool for establishing new concepts, which connect fundamental physics with computer science applications. Here, we exploit the intrinsically high-dimensional correlations generated by the measurement outcomes of a multiphoton boson sampling machine and demonstrate how a reconfigurable architecture can be programmed to autonomously model the time evolution of nonlinear dynamical systems that describe complex fluid flows. By extending on the original scattershot boson sampling (SBS) scheme to include higher photon number components -- a scheme which we label bright scattershot boson sampling (BSBS) --- this scheme overcomes scaling issues inherent to SBS-based schemes by utilizing all randomly generated input photon number distributions. We illustrate the capability of this scheme for two complex fluid-flow models: an eight-dimensional Lorenz-type model of two-dimensional thermally driven convection and a 16-dimensional model of global atmospheric circulation. Using a digital twin of the BSBS architecture based on multiphoton statistics, we show that the photonic approach accurately reproduces the temporal dynamics of both systems while being resilient to photon losses of up to 50 per cent. We also verified that the BSBS case outperforms a comparable run with coherent light. Moreover, its prediction horizon exceeds that of a classical reservoir-computing model with the same query complexity. These results highlight the potential of near-term photonic quantum computing for addressing relevant real-world tasks on noisy intermediate-scale devices.
