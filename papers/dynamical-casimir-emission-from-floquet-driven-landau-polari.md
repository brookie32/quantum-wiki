arXiv:2611.14339v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Ultrastrong light--matter coupling of linear systems, such as Landau polaritons formed by a two-dimensional electron gas in a terahertz cavity, is described by the bosonic Hopfield model, and gauge invariance forbids a superradiant phase transition (SRPT). Modulating the magnetic field alone has been predicted to enhance the cycle-averaged coupling and induce a Floquet SRPT. We develop an exact theory of periodically driven Landau polaritons. Including the electric field induced by the modulated flux (Faraday's law), the problem reduces exactly, by Kohn's theorem, to the electronic center-of-mass mode coupled to the cavity, whose Hamiltonian is a sum of squares at every instant. A theorem of Krein then guarantees stability to all orders above a computable drive frequency, the Krein frequency, for which we give a self-contained proof. Below it, the system is stable except within resonance tongues where the drive creates pairs from the vacuum, the dynamical Casimir effect. At high drive frequency the electrons experience the mean field plus a ponderomotive trap (the Fock--Darwin problem), not an enhanced coupling. A gauge theorem excludes a Floquet photon condensate for any band structure, including Kane nonparabolicity, for which we derive a generalized Thomas--Reiche--Kuhn (TRK) sum rule. The predicted transition arises from averaging the coupling in a fixed Landau-level basis, which yields an effective model that violates the TRK sum rule; under driving, gauge invariance also requires the induced Faraday field. The rule to modulate and average the minimal-coupling Hamiltonian, not the parameters of its projection, applies to any linear, minimally coupled system. Instead of an SRPT, magnetic modulation produces dynamical Casimir emission: Faraday-driven polariton--magnetoplasmon pairs at low threshold, and two-color photon pairs with up to 5--10~dB of two-mode squeezing.
