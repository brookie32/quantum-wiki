arXiv:2610.36663v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: For two Gaussian preparations of a detuned degenerate parametric amplifier below threshold, the trace distance shows a lasting Mpemba reversal, whereas the relative entropy eventually restores the initial order and the Hilbert--Schmidt distance alternates indefinitely. All three diagnostics agree on the initial order, and these orders persist on open sets of preparations and quadratic generators. The disagreement is possible because a real relaxation mode and a complex-conjugate pair share the leading decay rate, a coincidence that holds for any single-mode quadratic oscillator with linear damping in the elliptic regime. Number symmetry hides their competition in the undriven trace-distance tail, and parametric driving exposes it. Near a pure stationary state, the relative entropy strongly weights changes of the symplectic eigenvalue, which approximately follow the nonoscillating energy deviation; thermal occupation weakens this preference. A first-order splitting rule shows that weak number dephasing and two-photon loss favor the real branch, whereas a Kerr interaction with the same sign as the detuning favors the pair. We quantify the thermal-occupation and dephasing tolerances and estimate the measurement precision needed to observe the disagreement. Whether a Mpemba reversal is lasting therefore depends on the diagnostic and on the observation window.
