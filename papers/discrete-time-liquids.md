arXiv:2610.30266v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Discrete time crystals exhibit robust subharmonic oscillations, spontaneously breaking the discrete time-translation symmetry of a periodic drive. Since their emergence, the question of whether other types of temporal many-body order can exist has attracted considerable attention. Here we identify discrete time liquids -- a new form of spatiotemporal order characterized by short-range temporal coherence as a result of time-solitons. We study a two-dimensional spin system coupled to a finite-temperature cavity, whose fluctuations provide a noisy, approximately periodic drive with only finite temporal coherence. Remarkably, the spins nevertheless form a metastable state with robust period-doubled dynamics over an extended parameter regime. Unlike a time crystal, this state has only finite temporal coherence: individual trajectories stochastically switch between the two symmetry-related temporal configurations of the period-doubled response. We identify these switches as time-solitons: topological defects in temporal order that preserve local period doubling while destroying long-range temporal coherence, thereby producing the time liquid state. We propose a minimal model which reproduces the distribution of temporal correlations of the many-body system, suggesting a general description of the time liquid as a time crystal with a finite density of time-solitons. Because these solitons can occur at arbitrary times, ensemble averaging restores continuous time-translation symmetry, raising the intriguing possibility that discrete time liquids can exist in thermal equilibrium.
