arXiv:2611.09322v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Moment-based tomography efficiently reconstructs Gaussian states, but stochastic phase noise generally produces mixed, non-Gaussian outputs. For approximately Gaussian outputs, we formulate reconstruction as a problem in quantum learning theory under model misspecification. Our trace-distance guarantee separates the intrinsic Gaussian approximation error from statistical errors in estimating the first moments and covariance matrix. Using recent Gaussian trace-distance bounds, we obtain linear dependence of the statistical contribution on moment errors when the covariance inverses remain bounded, improving on the square-root dependence on covariance error in earlier guarantees. A Fock-space truncation procedure provides certified, convergent lower and upper bounds on the intrinsic error. Under an explicit sub-Gaussian condition on the heterodyne outcomes, the sufficient sample count scales inversely with the square of the allowed statistical error remaining after accounting for the approximation error. We verify the statistical condition for stochastic phase rotations and heralded unitary-averaged states with bounded input squeezing. We apply the framework to probabilistic unitary averaging for phase noise reduction, quantifying reconstruction error and accounting for heralding overhead in the total number of estimation attempts.
