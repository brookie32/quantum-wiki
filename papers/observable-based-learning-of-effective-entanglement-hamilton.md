arXiv:2611.07353v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Determining the entanglement Hamiltonian (EH) of an interacting quantum many-body system remains a fundamental challenge beyond special analytically tractable cases. Here we develop an observable-matching framework for learning effective EHs, which we demonstrate using response estimates from quantum Monte Carlo. As applications of this framework, we first calibrate the overall prefactor of the lattice Bisognano--Wichmann (LBW) construction using equal-time correlations and then learn spatially resolved couplings beyond its prescribed profile, without assuming a specific functional form for their spatial dependence. Trained solely on local correlations, the learned EHs reproduce benchmarked von Neumann and second Renyi entropies in 1D and second Renyi entropies in 2D. For a strong-bond cut associated with special surface criticality at the (2+1)D O(3) Wilson--Fisher critical point, free-form learning outperforms the calibrated LBW ansatz in predicting the second Renyi entropy and reveals spatially nonuniform coupling renormalizations. Importantly, the learned EH also provides access to the von Neumann entropy in this setting, revealing a negative subleading correction and extending previous Renyi entropy findings to the von Neumann case. Directly evaluating the von Neumann entropy in such (2+1)D interacting quantum many-body systems remains challenging for conventional numerical methods. These results establish observable-based learning as a route to constructing effective entanglement Hamiltonians beyond prescribed spatial profiles in higher-dimensional interacting systems.
