arXiv:2611.07021v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Preparing ground and thermal states of quantum many-body systems is a central task in quantum simulation. Dissipative protocols have emerged as a promising solution for both near-term and fault-tolerant hardware, but most constructions rely on weak system-bath coupling, leading to slow cooling. Here, we construct cooling protocols at strong system-bath coupling, and demonstrate accurate state preparation within even a single cooling cycle. Our protocols combine dynamically ramped ancilla energy levels with time-dependent couplings to optimize the trade-off between cooling error and physical time. For a general class of free-fermion systems, we reduce the dynamics exactly to a two-level scattering problem: ground-state cooling becomes a control problem of maximizing resonant excitation transfer while suppressing non-adiabatic heating. We then construct a family of ``Mobius-Ingham'' protocols, which reach optimal error scaling for gapless spectra and almost-exponential suppression of the leading heating error for gapped spectra, as a function of the cycle period. In our free-fermion benchmarks, Mobius-Ingham cooling lies on the Pareto frontier between steady-state error and cooling time among the controls tested. We further demonstrate its effectiveness in interacting Ising and t-V chains and the two-dimensional Fermi-Hubbard model. Finally, we extend our construction to Gibbs state preparation, showing how free-fermion thermal states can be prepared in a single cycle.
