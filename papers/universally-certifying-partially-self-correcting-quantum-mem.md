arXiv:2611.06469v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Achieving passive protection of quantum information at finite temperature is a central problem at the intersection of quantum information and quantum many-body physics. Yet a general, computable criterion for determining self-correcting behavior directly from the form of local interactions remains elusive. Here we develop a unified algebraic-geometric framework for certifying partial self-correction in three-dimensional translation-invariant local stabilizer codes, encompassing the cubic code as a special case. From the algebraic curves defined by the codes' defining polynomials, we construct valuation matrices on the boundaries of their normalized projective closures and derive a computable rank criterion. Together with a polynomial-time decoder, this criterion guarantees super-Arrhenius memory lifetimes for code families satisfying it under the specified local Davies dynamics. We apply the framework to certify several classes of partially self-correcting quantum memories, including nearest-neighbor models with stabilizer weight as low as six and long memory lifetimes at modest system sizes. These results establish an algebraic-geometric connection between local stabilizer structure and the recoverability of quantum information at finite temperature, providing a systematic approach to certifying and designing passive quantum memories.
