arXiv:2401.09007v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Quantum singular value transformation (QSVT) provides a unifying framework for quantum algorithms by applying the polynomials of quantum signal processing (QSP) to block-encoded matrices. We connect this framework with spectral analysis, design and topological protection in condensed matter physics. Extending QSVT beyond matrices to possibly infinite-dimensional operators (operator QSVT), we establish the following. First, it transforms every block of the operator by the QSP polynomials; no singular value decomposition is needed. Second, a spectral mapping theorem determines the full spectrum of the QSVT operator, including continuous spectra, from the embedded operator through the QSP polynomial, allowing spectral analysis without translation invariance. Third, the spectral mapping turns QSP into spectral design: the QSP phases can stretch, fold and separate the bands and place the eigenvalues. Finally, the kernel sectors that conventional QSVT usually sets aside carry a topological index, invariant under the choice of QSP phases, local perturbations and gap-preserving deformations, which guarantees topologically protected states. These results unify two quantum walks: Szegedy's walk from quantum algorithms and the split-step walk from condensed matter physics. Their kernel sectors encode the graph's Euler characteristic and the difference of bulk winding numbers, respectively. For the split-step walk, QSP breaks chiral symmetry but not the topological protection, and can move the protected state into a continuous band to form a bound state in the continuum (BIC).
