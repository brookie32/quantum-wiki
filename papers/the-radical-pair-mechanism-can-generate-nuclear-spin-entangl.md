arXiv:2611.17497v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The radical pair mechanism is a quantum-mechanical process underlying weak magnetic field effects in chemical and biological systems. While nuclear polarization and electronic entanglement in this mechanism have been studied extensively, the possibility of creating entanglement between nuclear spins has, to the best of our knowledge, remained unexplored. Here, we show that nuclear-spin entanglement can develop from initially maximally mixed nuclear spin states and evolve over repeated radical pair cycles. Its generation and persistence depend on the initial electronic state, the available reaction channels, and the interplay between recombination rates, hyperfine interactions, and the magnetic field. We demonstrate this behaviour in both a minimal two-nucleus model and a five-nucleus model inspired by the photosynthetic radical pair P700--A_1. Radical pair mechanism generated nuclear-spin entanglement offers a potential new resource for quantum technologies and opens avenues for exploring its role in biological systems.
