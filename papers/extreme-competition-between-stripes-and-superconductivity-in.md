arXiv:2610.30321v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: The two-dimensional Hubbard model was originally proposed as the minimal model of uniform d-wave superconductivity in the cuprate high-temperature superconductors, but recent calculations with relevant parameters in a realistic doping regime have often found other ground states. Further, uncertainty remains over such results due to the intrinsic bias of different computational methods. Here we address this uncertainty through state-of-the-art neural tensor network and neural backflow state simulations, enabled by large-scale GPU computation, that together reach an unprecedented variational accuracy on lattices with up to 1024 sites. At 1/8 doping in the pure Hubbard model, as well as with next-nearest-neighbor hopping relevant to the hole-doped cuprates, we reveal a near degeneracy among a low-energy uniform d-wave state, superconducting stripes, and non-superconducting stripe states on the milli-t energy scale. Our work rebalances the narrative that the Hubbard model supports a single dominant order in relevant parameter regimes towards a picture of extreme competition between stripes and superconductivity.
