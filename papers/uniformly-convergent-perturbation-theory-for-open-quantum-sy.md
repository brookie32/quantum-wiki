arXiv:2611.23604v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Near a Liouvillian exceptional point, spectral perturbation theory becomes ill-conditioned, yet the observable dynamics of a finite-dimensional open system remains regular in the perturbation. Using a Dyson expansion, which requires no diagonalization of the perturbed generator, we analyze the scaling, stability, and convergence of the perturbative corrections at, near and away from exceptional points. We derive scaling laws showing that nonperipheral Jordan blocks raise the degree of the polynomial-in-time factors multiplying decaying exponentials, which are present for any generator as resonances, and we quantify this excess. We further establish a uniform-in-time error estimate for the perturbative series, valid for any relaxing finite-dimensional Lindbladian and controlled by a parameter q = |arepsilon|R, where arepsilon is the perturbation strength and R is a constant built from unperturbed data. All constants of the bound remain continuous through an exceptional point when the coalescing eigenvalues are kept in one spectral cluster. The estimate extends to the frequency domain via the Laplace transform, and to time-dependent perturbations. We test it in a driven two-level system with a Liouvillian exceptional point, including perturbations that break its symmetry.
