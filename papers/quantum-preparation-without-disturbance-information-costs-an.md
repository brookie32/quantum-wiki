arXiv:2611.18135v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We determine the minimum information cost of preparing either of two quantum states with one common device while preserving its memory. Measured by the Umegaki relative entropy between the two possible memory states, this minimum is the Belavkin-Staszewski relative entropy of the target states. If the memory may instead be consumed, the minimum is their Umegaki relative entropy. More generally, for any divergence satisfying data processing, memory preservation selects the maximal extension of its classical restriction. We also study the cost of requiring only finitely many outputs, with the costs increasing from Umegaki to Belavkin-Staszewski relative entropy as the number of outputs grows. Finally, we consider hypothesis testing of an unknown device over independent runs. The optimal Stein exponent per run converges to the Belavkin-Staszewski relative entropy whenever the number of outputs per run diverges. This gives an operational characterization of the Belavkin-Staszewski relative entropy in quantum statistics.
