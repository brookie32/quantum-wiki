arXiv:2611.07271v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Chen, Mele, Mele and Preskill proved a crossover for bosonic Gaussian tomography: cold states need Theta(m^3/epsilon^2) copies under single-copy or few-copy measurements, warm ones Theta(m^2/epsilon^2). The author proved Omega(m^3/epsilon^2) for adaptive single-copy tomography of mixed fermionic Gaussian states. This note shows the crossover has no fermionic counterpart and extends the fermionic bound to blocks of k copies. There is no warm regime: on the quadratic Gibbs balls eta|h|_Fleeta_0 of unknown Hamiltonian h, the single-copy rate is Theta(m^3/epsilon^2) for every fixed eta_0>0, at accuracies that resolve the ball, with a lower-bound constant independent of eta_0. At the maximally mixed state a replicated-parameter Fisher budget gives N>m^3/(8{,}553{,}600,k,epsilon^2) for blocks of at most k copies and all epsilonle1/3600; an exact formula for the maximal block Fisher information, via spinor tensor powers of mathfrak{so}(2m), shows that the budget mt^2 behind it is asymptotically attained for every fixed block size; whether 1/k is the true rate is open. Near the vacuum, block protocols need Omega(m^3/(epsilon^2min{sqrt k,m})) copies, with no logarithm, via an exterior-power embedding of qudit tomography; parity superselection changes no rate, and number superselection costs nothing on number-conserving inputs. The embedding also carries upper bounds: a particle-number measurement followed by the k-copy qudit protocol of Nayak and Zhou attains the lower bound for every k on number-conserving fermionic Gaussian states with bounded fugacity trace and on passive bosonic Gaussian states with bounded photon number, and a heterodyne stage that undoes displacement and squeezing extends this to all cold bosonic Gaussian states. This closes their k-entangled gap on the cold class; intermediate temperatures remain open.
