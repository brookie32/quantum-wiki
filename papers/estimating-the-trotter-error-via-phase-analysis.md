arXiv:2611.33438v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Good Trotter error estimates are crucial to correctly assess the resources required to perform an approximate time evolution with a target accuracy. General bounds for finite-size quantum systems are found to be very loose in practice, and it is therefore highly desirable to improve on them. In this work, we analyze the Trotter error in terms of the spectrum of a phase operator that we construct from the Hamiltonian. In the limit of a small Trotter step size, we obtain an estimate of the Trotter error written in terms of a sum of nested commutators between terms of the Hamiltonian. The structure of this estimate allows us to naturally explain the looseness of general bounds. We show that even for a finite step size, our estimate typically remains more accurate than general bounds. Moreover, we argue that it becomes an upper bound for the Trotter error per step in the limit of a large number of steps, reflecting the destructive interference of the errors across steps. Our results provide useful practical heuristics for resource optimization in quantum simulations.
