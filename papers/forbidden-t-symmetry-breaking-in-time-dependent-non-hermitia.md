arXiv:2611.27600v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Parity-time (PT) symmetry has attracted significant interest in photonics and related fields in recent years. With sufficiently weak gain and loss, these systems typically exhibit entirely real energy levels, with all corresponding wave functions remaining PT symmetric. As the strength of gain and loss increases, complex-conjugate energy levels are expected to emerge, signaling the onset of PT symmetry breaking. Similar properties can be found in non-Hermitian systems with other antilinear symmetries as well, where parity is replaced by another operator such as rotation (R) or simply the identity operator, resulting in RT-symmetric and T-symmetric non-Hermitian systems. In this work, we demonstrate that this typical scenario of non-Hermitian symmetry breaking in a finite Hilbert space is forbidden in a class of time-dependent systems with gain and loss and T symmetry: the system remains in its T-symmetric phase as we increase the strength of gain and loss, even when the spatial distribution of gain and loss is random. Furthermore, this behavior is also independent of the duration or period of the temporal modulation. In contrast, this forbidden non-Hermitian symmetry breaking does not occur when the same systems are modified to display PT symmetry instead. Our findings highlight contrasting behaviors in time-dependent and time-independent non-Hermitian systems, creating new possibilities and opportunities for future investigations.
