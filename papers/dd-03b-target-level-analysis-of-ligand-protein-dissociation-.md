arXiv:2604.06761v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Protein-ligand interactions are conventionally described by a structure and a binding affinity (Kd). Kinetic simulation datasets have grown rapidly, but most analyses remain structure-centred and RMSD-based, and the kinetic information carried by the trajectories is rarely extracted. Here we analyse 766,550 complete dissociation trajectories for 15,540 protein-ligand complexes grouped into 3,687 UniProt accessions. Each trajectory is reduced to a uniform record of 119 descriptors-ligand and structure size, trajectory statistics, the pathway distribution of escape events, and pocket and escape-volume descriptors-so that records from different systems can be compared, joined and searched. This tokenisation shows that the pocket and the ligand act on the same quantity at different levels: the pocket defines the repertoire of exits, and the ligand sets the weights. In open sites the repertoire collapses to one broad mode and affinity tracks ligand size and contact; in confined pockets the exit ensemble splits into several channels whose weights follow ligand size rather than binding affinity. UniProt grouping is a convenient classification above the PDB entry but not the right final unit: two systems illustrate domain separation and degeneracy, and a structural classifier would be preferable. We present the framework as a general analysis paradigm for molecular dynamics trajectory libraries.
