arXiv:2611.27163v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Carrier dynamics and recombination in perovskite thin films are often explored through transient photoluminescence (PL) measurements and subsequent analysis based on single/multi-exponentials. In contrast, here we show that several experimental PL transients reported in literature vary as power laws with time and are not multi-exponentials. Puzzlingly, the existing theoretical methods are ill-equipped to explain the origin and implications of such power law trends. Our analysis resolves this puzzle and correlates the power law exponents with the trap distributions. Curiously, we show that PL transients could vary as t^{-2} even in the presence of dominant monomolecular recombination while the existing theory associates this unique trend exclusively with dominant bimolecular recombination. These findings identify the limits of applicability of concepts such as differential lifetime and highlight the need for predictive modeling synergistic with insightful data analysis towards performance optimization of perovskite optoelectronic devices.
