arXiv:2611.19141v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: In classical statistics, local asymptotic normality provides a general framework for characterizing optimal testing power and constructing asymptotically efficient procedures. Extending this framework to quantum statistical models introduces additional challenges arising from the noncommutativity of observables and the need to optimize over quantum measurements. In this paper, we develop a theory of asymptotic efficiency for quantum hypothesis testing using quantum local asymptotic normality and the asymptotic representation theorem. We establish sharp nuisance-maximin power bounds for one-sided tests and two-sided tests within the unbiased class by reducing the corresponding classical--quantum Gaussian shift experiments to classical testing problems. This reduction is justified through a quantum Hunt--Stein argument that accommodates both commuting and noncommuting Gaussian coordinates. Under suitable asymptotic invariance conditions, the resulting bounds transfer to the original quantum experiments and are attained by scalar symmetric logarithmic derivative tests. We further develop a two-stage adaptive construction that achieves the same local asymptotic power without knowledge of the reference parameter. As an application, we study testing of smooth spectral functionals of quantum states. We compare scalar symmetric logarithmic derivative tests with the weak Schur sampling plug-in test and with optimally weighted linear statistics of its fluctuations. This comparison distinguishes loss from the plug-in weighting from a gap between the best such spectral statistic and the scalar score procedure. We characterize equality of the corresponding variances and illustrate the two gaps through explicit examples.
