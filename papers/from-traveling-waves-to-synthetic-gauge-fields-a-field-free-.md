arXiv:2610.29865v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: The superconducting diode effect requires the forward and reverse bias currents to differ, a scenario achieved via inversion and time-reversal symmetries broken together. The latter, and the more crucial one, is inevitably achieved by a magnetic field. Here, we show that a spatio-temporally driven chemical potential of the form, mu_j(t)=mu+Vos(omega t+kd j), achieves both at once. Applied to a helical Shiba chain, the drive transmutes a traveling Peierls phase via generating an odd-in-momentum term, equivalent to an imaginary next-nearest-neighbour Haldane-like hopping in real space. Remarkably, the protocol is spin-blind, which renders the time-reversal breaking via a synthetic gauge flux rather than an effective Zeeman field. The rectification is odd in the drive wavevector, with exact zeros at kd=npi and an analytical optimum at kd=2pi/3. It is however, even in the exchange coupling, J and persists even at J=0. Furthermore, the rectification scales inversely with the pair-breaking gap, yielding approximately 50% rectification in the topological phase and 10% in the trivial phase. Symmetry breaking is thus drive-controlled, allowing it to be switched on, tuned, and reversed without an external magnetic field.
