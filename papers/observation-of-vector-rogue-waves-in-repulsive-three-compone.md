The study reports the first experimental observation of vector Peregrine solitons (rogue waves) in a highly particle‑imbalanced, pairwise‑immiscible three‑component Bose–Einstein condensate composed of different Rubidium hyperfine states. A generalized reduction scheme for imbalanced N‑component systems with arbitrary intra‑ and inter‑component interactions shows that the minority components can acquire an effective attractive character, permitting simultaneous modulation instability and attractive potential wells that drive the formation of reproducible vector rogue waves, including single and double Peregrine peaks. Experimental results agree quantitatively with three‑dimensional mean‑field simulations and demonstrate that tuning interatomic interactions via hyperfine state selection enables systematic exploration of a broad family of vector rogue‑wave patterns.
