arXiv:2611.33723v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: The electromagnetic U(1) gauge invariance and superconductivity are examined by employing the Berry connection from many-electron wave functions and the fluctuations of it that give rise to the probabilistic random transitions among quantum states. The gauge invariance in the classical electrodynamics is regarded as the requirement that extracts unaffected parts by the gauge fluctuation. In quantum mechanics, dissipation-free states from the gauge fluctuation exist, and we identify them as superconducting states. We explain the reversible superconducting-normal phase transition in a magnetic field including the force balance. The present superconductivity theory lifts two fundamental disagreements between theory and experiment existing in the current standard theory with keeping the major results by the BCS theory. Thus, it may be considered as a superconductivity theory encompassing the current standard one.
