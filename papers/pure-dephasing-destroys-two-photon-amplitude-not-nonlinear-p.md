arXiv:2610.30098v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: A two-level emitter chirally coupled to a waveguide imprints a pi conditional phase on a two-photon pulse, a key component assumed in quantum photonic neural network architectures, but whether it survives the pure dephasing of a solid-state emitter is unknown, since no two-photon scattering treatment includes it. We derive the dephased two-photon amplitude in closed form and validate it against an independent Fock-state master equation. The conditional phase is pinned at exactly pi at every dephasing rate examined: dephasing destroys the amplitude carrying the nonlinear phase rather than rotating it. The one- and two-photon channels are not attenuated equally, so dephasing does not simply scale the node down but reweights it between photon-number sectors---an error no phase setting can train away. The standard factorization of two-photon correlators, exact for arbitrary loss, fails at first order in gamma_phi, discarding 16--81% of pairs over gamma_phi/gamma=0.1--1.
