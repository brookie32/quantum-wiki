The authors present a computational problem that can be solved with near‑certainty by constant‑depth, 3D‑local quantum circuits even when gate noise is present. Conversely, any unbounded fan‑in classical AC⁰ circuit of subexponential size fails on a uniform random instance with near‑certain probability. This demonstrates an experimentally accessible, fault‑tolerant separation between current noisy quantum devices and classical computation, providing the strongest known complexity‑theoretic evidence for quantum advantage.
