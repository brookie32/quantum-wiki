arXiv:2610.20897v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: We study the zero-temperature Casimir effect of a three-dimensional Bose fluid with finite-range corrections to its Bogoliubov spectrum. Beyond the universal acoustic contribution scaling as L^{-3}, the long-distance expansion contains a nonuniversal L^{-5} correction that can compete with the phononic term. We derive this crossover analytically within a minimal isotropic finite-range model and test the asymptotic result against the full dispersion. A regulator-independent Poisson-resummed calculation confirms that sign reversals of the Casimir energy are possible, while showing that the apparent low-coupling zero inferred by balancing only the L^{-3} and L^{-5} terms is not quantitatively controlled. Robust sign changes instead occur in the stable regime close to roton softening, where the Casimir energy acquires an oscillatory finite-momentum contribution. Strongly dipolar atomic gases provide a natural motivation for microscopic interaction lengths in the nanometric regime. We further discuss how the resulting nonlinear force law could be probed through the frequency response of a driven nanomechanical resonator.
