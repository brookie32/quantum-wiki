arXiv:2611.13089v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Kinematic quantum geometry describes fast degrees of freedom (DOFs) that adiabatically follow states parametrized by both the coordinates and the velocities of slow DOFs. It extends Born-Oppenheimer geometry, which depends on coordinates alone, introduces mixed Berry curvatures, and modifies the effective theory governing the slow DOFs. We test its predictions in two setups: spinful particles moving in magnetic textures, and Dirac electrons in graphene coupled to an LC circuit. In the first, kinematic quantum geometry produces a velocity-dependent effective mass tensor and generates a Lorentz-like force nonlinear in velocity that can arise even in coplanar textures. In the second, it renormalizes the circuit capacitance and generates a Hall current nonlinear in the electric field. For small velocities, it reproduces recent results for electronic dynamics in external electromagnetic fields and in pseudospin textures. However, the framework can remain valid at large velocities beyond the perturbative regime, provided the kinematic states are sufficiently slowly varying.
