arXiv:2611.21898v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Aggregate molecular systems are routinely understood by decomposing them into local structural units, such as monomers in J/H-aggregates, chemical moieties, motifs, and functional groups. Implicit in such decompositions is the assumption that these local structures remain meaningful within the aggregate. Here, we ask how the symmetries of constituent structures relate to aggregate symmetry, and how this relation determines the constraints that global symmetry imposes on transitions between local symmetry sectors. We construct a covariant local-to-global representation from framed local symmetry data and show that its reconstruction map intertwines with a global quantum representation. By identifying which global symmetry sectors overlap each local symmetry sector, we resolve symmetry-enforced dynamical exclusions into site-resolved pathways. Through Frobenius reciprocity, these exclusions can be determined from standard character tables of the local and global symmetry groups, with the character values in the inner product paired according to the relative positions and orientations of local structures within the aggregate. Although motivated by molecular aggregates, the construction is formulated for arbitrary geometries modeled by a homogeneous space of an ambient transformation group.
