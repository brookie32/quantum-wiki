arXiv:2611.13154v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We introduce a finite-memory repeat-until-success (RUS) framework for implementing rational spectral filters on a quantum computer, extending spectral filtering beyond the polynomial transformations implemented by quantum signal processing (QSP) and quantum singular value transformation (QSVT). Our approach interleaves queries to a block encoding of a Hermitian operator X, whose spectrum lies in [-1,1], and mid-circuit measurements that either accept, reject, or continue the computation without resetting the quantum state. Although the protocol may run for arbitrarily many rounds, its terminal statistics are determined by a finite-dimensional Lyapunov equation. Under uniform termination and phase-inversion symmetry, we characterize exactly the spectral filters R(X) realizable upon acceptance: R can be any real rational function with no poles on [-1,1] and values in [0,1]. We provide a constructive compiler for any admissible R, with bounds on memory and expected query cost, and characterize the accepted quantum operation through a spectral coherence kernel. Applications to regularized resolvents and Lorentzian spectroscopy yield exact low-degree realizations and query-cost advantages over the fixed-depth QSVT polynomial-postselection baseline considered here, with all repetitions and independent restarts after rejection included in the cost.
