Researchers have observed individually trapped circular Rydberg atoms at room temperature that live longer than 10 ms, a hundred‑fold increase over conventional low‑angular‑momentum counterparts, by suppressing blackbody radiation via Purcell effects. These long‑lived states can be coherently controlled up to principal quantum numbers of n = 103 and are trapped with optical tweezers for durations exceeding one tenth of a second. The extended lifetimes and control capabilities enable higher‑fidelity quantum computing, simulation, and sensing applications using neutral atoms.
