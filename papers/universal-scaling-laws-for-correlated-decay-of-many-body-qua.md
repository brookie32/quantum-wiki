The paper derives universal upper and lower bounds for the maximal decay rate of Markovian many‑body quantum systems, showing that computing this rate is equivalent to solving an intractable ground–state energy problem. For a broad class of systems, especially large atomic arrays in free space, these bounds become asymptotically tight, yielding scaling laws that depend only on the array’s dimensionality and are insensitive to short‑range details. The resulting limits provide fundamental constraints on decoherence for all quantum states and have direct implications for the scalability of atom‑based quantum processors and simulators.
