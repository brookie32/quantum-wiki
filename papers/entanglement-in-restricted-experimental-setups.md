arXiv:2611.22481v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Entanglement is a necessary resource for steering and Bell nonlocality, yet whether a state is separable takes no account of what an experiment can prepare or measure. We define two notions of separability relative to an experiment, one on either side of separability. Inner separability confines the local states to the preparable sets. Outer separability lets the local factors be self-adjoint operators positive on the available effects but not necessarily positive semidefinite. We find exactly when each departs from separability and that outer separability keeps the operational content of separability for the available measurements: outer separable states, entangled or not, admit local hidden variable models for every behaviour these measurements generate and, when the steered party is unrestricted, local hidden state models for every assemblage the steering party's measurements generate. Such hidden state models do not imply outer separability in general, but we identify conditions under which they do.
