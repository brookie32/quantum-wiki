arXiv:2611.26695v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Gauge theories constitute the basis of our microscopic description of fundamental physical laws, and they are a cornerstone of contemporary scientific research. Yet, how such systems thermalize far from equilibrium remains a fundamental open question at the crossroads of statistical, high-energy, and condensed-matter physics. In this work, we investigate the thermalization properties of non-Abelian lattice gauge theories, focusing on the discrete gauge group D_3, which is ideally suited for current qudit-based quantum simulators. Specifically, we study a linear plaquette chain in a gauge-fixed formulation that removes unphysical degrees of freedom. A numerical analysis of the Hamiltonian spectrum and expectation values of local operators shows a behavior broadly aligned with the Eigenstate Thermalization Hypothesis (ETH). We also study thermalization through the real-time dynamics of the entanglement spectrum, confirming the emergence of random-matrix level statistics after an initial transient. Our results expand the rich phenomenology of dihedral non-Abelian gauge systems. Once the relevant symmetry structure is properly resolved, the model displays the same qualitative ETH-based thermalization scenario observed in Abelian gauge models and generic non-integrable quantum many-body systems.
