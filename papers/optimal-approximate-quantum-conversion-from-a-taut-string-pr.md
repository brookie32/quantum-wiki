arXiv:2610.28015v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Optimal approximate state conversion is a fundamental task in quantum resource theories, yet existing solutions are largely tailored to specific distinguishability measures and resource theories. We show that every optimal approximate-conversion problem in the three paradigmatic majorization-based quantum resource theories of non-uniformity, bipartite pure-state entanglement, and pure-state coherence reduces exactly to one of only four elementary taut-string constructions. This holds for problems generated by Schur-convex spectral distances and by a broad family of quantum divergences satisfying a natural reduction property with respect to the corresponding equivalence group. The resulting framework encompasses a broad class of physically relevant distinguishability measures, including the trace distance, all unitarily invariant norms, quantum relative entropy, maximal quantum f-divergences, and the alpha-z Renyi relative entropies. The increment vector of the corresponding taut string completely determines both the optimal approximation error and a canonical optimal state. Our results establish a common geometric framework for approximate quantum resource conversion, revealing that a broad class of seemingly different optimization problems is governed by the same taut-string geometric principle.
