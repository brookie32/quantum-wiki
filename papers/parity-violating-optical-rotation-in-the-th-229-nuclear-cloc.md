arXiv:2611.26784v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The exceptionally low-energy nuclear transition in ^{229}Th provides a new opportunity to study hadronic parity violation (PV) by optical methods. The PV nucleon--nucleus interaction admixes opposite-parity nuclear states into the 5/2^+ ground state and the 3/2^+ isomer, thereby inducing an electric-dipole amplitude E1_{rm PV} that interferes with the magnetic-dipole clock transition amplitude M1. In a single-valence-neutron Nilsson model, we find |E1_{rm PV}/M1| sim 2imes10^{-5}. This interference can be detected as a parity-violating nuclear optical rotation (PVNOR) of resonant laser light transmitted through a ^{229}Th-doped crystal. For a highly-doped CaF_2 crystal fiber, the estimated rotation is of order 10^{-6},{rm rad}. A crossed-polarizer measurement can reach this sensitivity with existing ultraviolet laser technology. We also analyze systematic effects in the form of optical birefringence, stray magnetic-field-induced Faraday rotation, and local crystal defects. Such a measurement would provide the first direct probe of the PV neutron-nucleus interaction constant, g_n.
