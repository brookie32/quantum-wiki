arXiv:2611.33312v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Continuous global symmetries can obstruct logarithmic-depth symmetric unitary k-designs and pseudorandom unitaries (PRUs) through long-range correlations. We ask whether gauge symmetries impose analogous limitations. For U(1) lattice gauge theories, we identify a randomization scale c, the minimum patch size needed for matter to screen every allowed boundary-flux mismatch, and a diffusive scale governing boundary-flux correlations. Glued circuits built from gauge-symmetric unitaries with subdiffusive support are efficiently distinguishable from gauge-symmetric Haar-random unitaries, whereas local controlled designs can be glued into global approximate designs when overlaps exceed the diffusive scale multiplied by a logarithmic factor. Our analysis suggests incomplete screening limits design guarantees to constant order, whereas strictly locally random systems with complete local screening (c=1) admit a local tensor-product description, permitting superpolynomial design orders and logarithmic-depth designs and PRUs. We construct a toy model of charged black-hole evaporation in which insufficient screening makes uninterrupted complete discharge exponentially unlikely in c, whereas strict local randomization removes this screening obstruction. This motivates a quantum-information analogue of the weak gravity conjecture, requiring the available charged spectrum to support complete local screening in addition to superextremality. We conjecture that this condition allows gauge-symmetric systems to scramble as rapidly as unconstrained ones and support this by constructing logarithmic-depth approximate strong designs and strong PRUs.
