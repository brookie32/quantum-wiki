arXiv:2611.25038v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Pseudorandom unitaries (PRUs) are efficiently implementable unitary ensembles that are computationally indistinguishable from Haar-random unitaries, providing efficient substitutes for Haar-randomness in quantum information and cryptography. Strong PRUs require this indistinguishability to hold even when the adversary is given access to all of U, U^agger, U^*, and U^T. In this work, we prove birthday-scale non-adaptive security for the DPFC (Clifford-Phase-Permutation-Clifford) strong PRU candidate. For an N-dimensional unitary, allowing arbitrary entangled inputs and coherent superpositions of query types, we show that DPFC can withstand t approx sqrt{N} queries. This matches the best known query complexity even for PRUs that are not strong, compared with t approx N^alpha for alpha < 1/2 in previous strong-PRU analyses. Our proof utilizes two techniques from previous works: the EPR-sector decomposition of Schuster et al. (arXiv 2025), previously used for unitary designs but not for PRUs, and the path-recording framework of Ma and Huang (STOC 2025), previously used for adaptive security analysis. We complement the non-adaptive security result with an upper bound in the adaptive setting, showing that DPFC cannot remain secure for t gg sqrt{N} queries.
