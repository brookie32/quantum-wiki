arXiv:2611.12002v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Accurate prediction of molecular reactivity requires both accurate and computationally efficient description of electron correlation and spin across various molecular geometries and electronic states. We introduce SATE, a spin-adapted transferable neural wavefunction architecture with effective core potentials. SATE combines a transferable neural wavefunction, the spin-adapted antisymmetrization method, and effective core potentials into a single foundation model architecture pretrained on compositions containing selected elements from hydrogen to bromine. After pretraining and adaptation, our model is able to predict 3d transition-metal spin-state gaps, bond dissociation energies, and reaction energies by addressing two key issues. First, including the core potentials into the model allows us to reduce the number of explicitly treated electrons. Second, using spin-adapted antisymmetrization enforces the prescribed total spin by construction. By testing our model with 3d transition metal chemistry, we evaluate our model's description of strong electron correlation and competing electronic states in such compounds. We find that pretraining improves most tested component energies at matched adaptation budgets, while improvements in spin-state gaps, dissociation energies, and reaction energies remain system dependent. Ultimately, our work lays the foundation for more accurate and transferable modeling of molecular systems by combining the computational efficiency of effective core potentials with explicit control over total spin.
