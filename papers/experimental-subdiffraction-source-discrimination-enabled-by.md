arXiv:2605.15929v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: We experimentally demonstrate a universal, parameter-independent test for asymmetric source discrimination below the diffraction limit. The test exploits spatial-mode demultiplexing (SPADE) and single-photon detection to discriminate the presence of a faint source in the vicinity of a brighter one. We develop a complete theory of the test in the presence of arbitrary modal crosstalk and show that the crosstalk-affected test retains a favorable error exponent over diffraction-limited direct imaging, provided that the crosstalk remains below a threshold C_{max}simeq 1/9. This establishes a quantitative condition under which the subdiffraction advantage of SPADE survives realistic imperfections. We experimentally test the scheme for a range of source separations and intensity ratios, without requiring prior knowledge of these parameters. The measured false-negative error rates are in good agreement with the theoretical model and systematically outperform direct imaging. These results demonstrate that SPADE provides a robust and experimentally accessible advantage for asymmetric subdiffraction hypothesis testing in the photon-starved regime, even in the presence of realistic modal crosstalk.
