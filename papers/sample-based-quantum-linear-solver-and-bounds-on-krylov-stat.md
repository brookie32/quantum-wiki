arXiv:2610.23138v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: To solve linear systems Ax=b, we propose a sample-based quantum linear solver combining quantum Krylov-state sampling with classical variational diagonalization. Rather than simulating the cost Hamiltonian H_b(A)=A^agger(I-|branglelangle b|)A, we measure quantum Krylov states e^{-iAT}|brangle generated by the original Hermitian operator A in the computational basis. We then classically diagonalize the projected cost Hamiltonian to reconstruct an approximate solution. Using a representation of A^{-1}|brangle as a linear combination of quantum Krylov states, we derive lower bounds on computational-basis sampling probabilities from solution amplitudes. We establish support-discovery guarantees for finite nonnegative-time grid sampling and signed-time sampling, which permits both positive and negative evolution times. The former bounds the maximum evolution time, whereas the latter improves ideal shot complexity at the cost of occasional long evolutions. For a condition-number bound kappa on A, a lower bound eta_L on the solution probability weight of each of L target basis states, and failure probability elta, signed-time sampling achieves O(kappa^2eta_L^{-1}log(L/elta)) shots, matching a worst-case lower bound up to constants within an evolution-only observation model in a specified parameter regime. Finally, we analyze the effects of finite-length qDRIFT compilation and circuit reuse on sampling and derive an infidelity bound for the reconstructed solution.
