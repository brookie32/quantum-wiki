arXiv:2506.23345v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Operator scrambling describes how initially local operators spread under many-body dynamics and is closely connected to quantum chaos. In this work, we reveal a connection between operator scrambling and the reliability of quantum simulations. We show that the Trotter error in an observable expectation value is bounded by a scrambling correlator between the evolved observable and the multiplicative Trotter error operator. This bound retains the dependence on both the input state and the observable. We then derive entanglement-based bounds showing that sufficient entanglement brings both the observable and error-operator factors in the bound to their normalized Frobenius scales. This indicates that, for observables that are normalized sums of local Pauli terms, the leading system-size dependence of the upper bound may be reduced from O(N) to O(1) relative to the spectral-norm estimate in systems undergoing entanglement growth and quantum thermalization. Even when the evolving state is weakly entangled, the effective states generated by the observable or error operator can have larger subsystem entanglement, thereby improving the error bound. Our results clarify the fundamental connection between Trotterization, scrambling, and entanglement, and provide refined guarantees for quantum simulation of many-body dynamics.
