arXiv:2611.21745v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Two-dimensional electrons in a strong magnetic field exhibit two seemingly incompatible ground states: incompressible quantum Hall liquids with a quantized Hall conductance, and electron crystals in which interactions drive the charge into ordered patterns. Moire superlattices in transition-metal-dichalcogenide bilayers offer a tunable arena for this competition. Using neural quantum states (NQS) together with several methodological advances, we show how this competition unfolds. In particular, we demonstrate that the two end points are separated by an extended regime in which molecular charge order coexists with a quantized Hall response. We map the ground-state phase diagram of electrons at Landau-level filling nu_B=1 in the presence of a moire superlattice potential, with three electrons per moire cell. In the deep triangular potential limit, the electrons form the topologically trivial "Wigner molecular crystal'' of three-electron artificial atoms, a state recently imaged at zero magnetic field in moire bilayers. As the potential depth is lowered, molecular charge order remains while quantized Hall response emerges. At the honeycomb point, where each cell contains three electrons and two degenerate moire minima, wells are either singly or doubly occupied and the doublons provide an emergent Ising degree of freedom. Starting from modest interaction strength, we find short-range doublon correlations within the Chern insulator which grow with interaction strength and eventually break sublattice symmetry, forming a Neel CDW with long-range order between doublons. The topological molecular regime lies at twist angles and magnetic fields directly accessible to current experiments. The methodological advances presented provide a scalable framework for exploring a broad range of questions in correlated quantum matter in the presence of magnetic fields.
