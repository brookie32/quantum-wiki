arXiv:2611.18388v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Optical spectra of dissipative molecular systems depend on both complex resonance energies and the way each resonance couples to an optical probe. In a non-Hermitian description, these con- tributions are determined by complex residues involving left and right eigenvectors. We introduce a quantum protocol that reconstructs the resonance poles and their biorthogonal optical residues within a common spectral-filtering and measurement framework. A Hermitian dilation embeds the left and right eigenvectors into a zero-energy subspace. Gaussian-filtered correlators evaluated with unitary circuits, together with ratios of complex off-diagonal measurements, yield the residues with- out separate variational optimisation of the eigenstates. We demonstrate spectral reconstruction for dissipative plasmon-molecule models on IBM quantum hardware, resolving how probe-dependent modal contributions produce interference-induced doublets or accompany a separation of resonance energies. We also analyse the sensitivity of residue reconstruction near exceptional points. By mak- ing complex optical residues an explicit computational output, the protocol connects non-Hermitian eigenstructure to measurable optical response.
