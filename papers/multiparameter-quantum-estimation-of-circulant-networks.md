arXiv:2611.13948v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Networks provide a natural description of interaction patterns, transport media, and communication architectures. Accurately characterizing their coupling structure is a key challenge in quantum technologies. We investigate the multiparameter quantum estimation of coupling strengths in circulant networks probed by continuous-time quantum walks. We derive the quantum Fisher information matrix, identify the optimal probe states and determine the ultimate precision allowed by quantum measurements, together with its scaling with the number of unknown couplings. We further analyze the precision achievable through position measurements of the walker, comparing the corresponding classical Cramer-Rao bounds for optimal and localized probe states with the quantum limit. Finally, we consider finite-resolution detection modeled by coarse-grained position measurements. Although coarse graining generally reduces estimation precision, we find that lower-resolution measurements can, in some instances, outperform other measurements with finer spatial resolution. Our results provide a systematic characterization of the fundamental and practical limits of coupling estimation in quantum-walk-based network inference.
