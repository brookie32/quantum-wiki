arXiv:2602.23183v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: We construct a probability distribution, induced by the Perron--Frobenius eigenvector of an exponentially large graph, that cannot be efficiently sampled by any classical algorithm, even when given the best-possible warm-start distribution. In the quantum setting, this problem can be viewed as preparing the ground state of a stoquastic Hamiltonian given a guiding state as input, and is known to be efficiently solvable on a quantum computer. We further show that even estimating the ground-state energy of that Hamiltonian remains classically hard. Our results suggest that no efficient classical algorithm can solve a broad class of stoquastic ground-state problems. Our graph is constructed from a class of high-degree, high-girth spectral expanders (such as the LPS Ramanujan graphs) to which self-similar trees are attached. This builds on and extends prior work of Gilyen, Hastings, and Vazirani (Quantum 2021, STOC 2021), which ruled out dequantization for a specific stoquastic adiabatic path algorithm. We strengthen their result by ruling out any classical algorithm for guided ground-state preparation or energy estimation, while also providing a derandomized construction.
