arXiv:2611.24227v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: We develop a model, that we call local semiclassical gravity, based on the distributional semiclassical Einstein field equations (EFEs) sourced by the local quantum state, i.e. the reduced density matrix of the local system updated by collapses (only) in its past light cone. We propose this as the basis of a relativistically causal generally covariant gravitisation of relativistic quantum theory in 1+3 spacetime dimensions. We derive the weak-field limit which exhibits the explicitly causal dynamical structure of the theory, and recover the Schrodinger-Newton (SN) system in the nonrelativistic limit. This proposal can thus be understood as a causal completion of the SN equation, a nonlinear extension to quantum theory. We analyze relativistic quantum measurements in this nonlinear setup, showing that these do not lead to superluminal signalling if quantum states update (only) in the future light-cone of the measurement region. We give explicit examples of quantum measurements on a spatial superposition and on entangled subsystems showing how, in the weak field limit, discontinuities in the expectation value of the stress-energy tensor can be consistent with distributional solutions to the (linearised) semiclassical EFEs. We also highlight that the theory allows non-standard measurements via its nonlinearities. These effective ``readout devices'' can approximately determine a quantum state without affecting it through the state's influence on the classical gravitational field and hence can implement approximate quantum state cloning. Nonetheless, the theory does not allow superluminal signalling, although it does violate the quantum no-signalling principle. We further show that the classical gravitational field is generally not locally causal in the sense of Bell. We present several paths to test the model against low-energy quantum gravity in tabletop experiments.
