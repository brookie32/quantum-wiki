arXiv:2611.23861v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: When a common bias reverses two bistable fields, the system can pass through either of two single-reversal configurations on its way to the favored state. We show that interference between these two orders of reversal can block the final step. In a dimer of coupled quantum Rabi cavities, the four field configurations form a loop, and a complex photon-hopping amplitude threads it with an Aharonov-Bohm flux. Near half a flux quantum, the two paths to complete reversal interfere destructively: in microscopic simulations, a slow uniform sweep reverses one field with probability 0.998, although the final bias favors reversing both. A four-state model calibrated at zero bias predicts how the window of this Aharonov-Bohm blockade narrows with sweep time. The blocked state remains coherent. A differential pulse followed by a return sweep converts the relative phase of the two single-reversal configurations into a fringe in the joint field signs, with contrast 0.95. In a trimer, a phase gradient imprinted between sweeps determines whether the next sweep adds or removes a reversed field: reversing the second of two gradients changes the most probable outcome from three reversed fields to one.
