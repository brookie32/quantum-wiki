Bound states in the continuum are localized states that exist within a scattering energy spectrum but remain trapped because destructive interference cancels their coupling to open channels; they have been predicted to arise when two Feshbach resonances share a common continuum.  The authors realized such a state experimentally by Floquet‑engineering two tunable Feshbach resonances in ultracold ^6Li collisions, reaching a critical point where both elastic and inelastic couplings vanish, producing a molecular bound state above the dissociation threshold that eliminates resonant scattering features and makes the atoms effectively transparent to one another.  Coupled‑channel simulations and a minimal non‑Hermitian model quantitatively agree with the data and identify the observed state as a Friedrich–Wintgen bound‑state‑in‑the‑continuum
