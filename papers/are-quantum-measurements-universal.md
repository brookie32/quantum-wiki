arXiv:2610.37873v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: In 2014, with D. Aerts, we showed, in the extended Bloch representation of quantum mechanics, that the Born rule results from a universal average over all the ways the measurement-interaction can be selected, computed by averaging over discretized (cellular) densities and taking a continuum limit. N. Shackel has since criticized this construction, in the context of Bertrand's paradox, for depending on the discretization adopted, for the constancy of its averages, and for the unclear sense in which the average is over 'all' densities. His criticisms prompt the present re-examination. What makes a random way of selecting a measurement 'universal', we argue, is that it privileges no portion of the structure of the available interactions, a property we call indifference and adopt as definitional. We prove that every universal measurement, however constructed, produces exactly the Born probabilities: the universal average is unique, the 2014 construction is one realization among infinitely many, and for cellular measurements the result is exact at every finite resolution, with no limit to be taken, which explains the constancy of the averages. Non-trivial universal measurements exist, but must involve deterministic measurements: Dirichlet processes are universal measurements whose support contains every way of selecting an interaction, whereas one free of deterministic components selects the uniform density, so that for it the Born rule holds with certainty, not merely on average. In sequences of measurements, deterministic components produce deviations from quantum statistics governed by a single parameter, in principle accessible to experiment. The only geometric input is the Euclidean length of the membrane and the orthogonal landing of the point-particle, where Gleason's theorem enters. We indicate the generalization to any number of outcomes and the bearing on Bertrand's paradox.
