arXiv:2611.22320v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: The inverse Faraday effect converts the angular momentum of circularly polarized light into a quasistatic magnetization, providing a fundamental route to ultrafast optical control of magnetic fields. We develop an analytical quantum theory of this effect in intrinsic graphene, where massless Dirac fermions, electron-hole symmetry, and weak spin-orbit coupling suppress conventional population-based interpretations. We show that in intrinsic graphene, the helicity-dependent orbital magnetization arises from the off-diagonal zero-harmonic component of the second-order density matrix, rather than from intraband carrier circulation or diagonal population imbalance. We obtain closed expressions for the induced temporal surface magnetization, its finite-pulse dynamics, and the nonlocal stray magnetic field generated by the two-dimensional (2D) source. Optical helicity, frequency, pulse envelope, and beam profile independently control the response. Our results identify intrinsic graphene as an ideal platform for coherent orbital optomagnetism in 2D Dirac matter.
