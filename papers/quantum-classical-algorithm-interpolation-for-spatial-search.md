arXiv:2610.19255v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Quantum-walk algorithms can quadratically reduce the dependence of spatial search on the classical hitting time HT. Realising this speedup using standard quantum-walk algorithms, however, requires deep coherent circuits, limiting their applicability on near-term and early fault-tolerant quantum devices. We derive a parameterised family of hybrid quantum-classical spatial search algorithms that continuously interpolates between the classical and fully coherent quantum regimes, trading off circuit depth for total query complexity. For any gammain[0,1], our algorithms use circuits of depth widetilde{O}(HT^{gamma/2}) and find a marked vertex with constant success probability at total cost widetilde{O}(HT^{1-gamma}mathsf{S} + HT^{1-gamma/2}(mathsf{U} + mathsf{C})), where mathsf{S}, mathsf{U}, and mathsf{C} denote the setup, update, and checking costs, respectively. We also derive an optimal continuous depth-query trade-off for fixed-point amplitude amplification, providing a potential building block for other hybrid designs.
