arXiv:2512.24233v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Analogue gravity describes sound as a field propagating on an effective spacetime set by the background flow, which is usually treated as given; in reality, however, quantum fluctuations also feed back on the medium that defines this spacetime. This raises the question of whether a condensate modified by this backreaction still defines a spacetime for the quantum field, which has not been established so far. Using a number-conserving expansion of a Bose-Einstein condensate, which keeps the classical background and the quantum fluctuation distinct while retaining their mutual coupling, we show that in the sound-wave and eikonal approximations the first-order phase fluctuation still propagates on an acoustic metric of the backreaction-corrected condensate, but is no longer massless: it obeys a Klein-Gordon equation with a spacetime-dependent mass that is generated by the backreaction sources in the continuity and Euler equations and cannot be absorbed into the metric. Backreaction in a Bose-Einstein condensate is thus a change of the analogue spacetime, supplemented by a mass term that has no geometric counterpart. Solving the coupled condensate-Bogoliubov dynamics for a finite, quasi-one-dimensional gas quenched from a noninteracting state, we find that, while the depletion remains below 2%, backreaction enhances the canonically normal-ordered equal-point phase correlation by about 10% in the bulk and up to about 20% near the walls, and that the bulk enhancement survives a finite-resolution imaging model.
