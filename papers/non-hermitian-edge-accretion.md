arXiv:2611.20881v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Under the non-Hermitian skin effect (NHSE), it is generally taken for granted that the open boundary condition (OBC) spectrum is encircled by the periodic boundary condition (PBC) spectrum. Intuitively, this is because boundaries tend to block the directed NHSE amplification, thereby suppressing operatorname{Im}(E). In this work, we analytically and numerically demonstrate how this conventional scenario can be dramatically violated: In a phenomenon which we term non-Hermitian edge accretion (NHEA), strongly coupled competing NHSE channels conspire to amplify a state whenever it bounces off an edge, instead of attenuating it. This unexpected behavior arises from underappreciated consequences of non-Bloch eigenspinor orthogonality, which control the reflection coefficients as well as the balance between propagating and evanescent waves. Operating in the strongly coupled regime, NHEA is fundamentally distinct from conventional critical NHSE scaling, which is characterized by amplification feedback generated in the bulk, not the edges. Requiring only strongly coupled antagonistic NHSE chains with suitable mode orthogonality, the NHEA is platform-agnostic, and can be identified through periodically growing edge bounces in generic non-Hermitian metamaterials and quantum simulators.
