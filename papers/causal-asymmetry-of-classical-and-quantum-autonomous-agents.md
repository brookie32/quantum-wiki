The study introduces **process causal asymmetry**, a measure of how much past information an autonomous agent must retain to transform one stochastic process into another compared with the transformation in the opposite direction. The authors demonstrate that while classical agents exhibit a fixed directional bias (e.g., a ticking clock degrades under noise but rarely improves), this asymmetry can be reversed when the agent uses a quantum memory, implying that the natural “simplification” path of a process depends on whether quantum information processing is permitted.
