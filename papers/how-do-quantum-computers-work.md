Quantum computers manipulate quantum bits (qubits) that can occupy multiple states simultaneously and become entangled, enabling quantum gates to process a superposition of many possibilities in parallel. This allows algorithms such as Shor’s (for factoring) and Grover’s (for search) to solve specific problems exponentially faster than classical computers. Recent breakthroughs have accelerated development, raising expectations that practical quantum machines capable of tackling intractable problems could be available within the next decade.
