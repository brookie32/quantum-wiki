arXiv:2611.04015v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: We propose an interaction-driven route to Weyl superfluidity from topologically trivial noninteracting bands in a three-dimensional optical Raman lattice. The scheme builds on a pairing mechanism arising from the interplay between a large Zeeman splitting and an attractive Hubbard interaction. The large Zeeman splitting renders the noninteracting bands topologically trivial and suppresses spin-flip hopping. These otherwise off-resonant spin-flip processes are resonantly restored when the attractive Hubbard interaction compensates the energy mismatch, generating occupation-dependent spin-orbit coupling that coherently couples neighboring spin-down fermion pairs to onsite doublons. Combining this density-dependent in-plane spin-orbit coupling with density-dependent spin-conserving interlayer tunneling extends the pairing mechanism to three dimensions. A self-consistent mean-field treatment of the microscopic model yields Weyl superfluids with two, four, or eight nodes, together with fully gapped weak topological superfluids. Tuning the interaction strength and particle filling drives transitions between these phases and controls the number of Weyl nodes. We further propose an experimental implementation with ultracold {}^{40}K atoms. Our results provide a route to Weyl superfluidity enabled by interaction-assisted spin-orbit coupling, without requiring nontrivial single-particle band topology.
