arXiv:2611.27408v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Matrix product state time evolution relies on compression to control the growth of bond dimensions, but compression can break the conservation laws of the equation being solved. A truncation is not the flow of a Hermitian generator, so even a small truncation error can violate exact algebraic identities of the Schrodinger equation. For any matrix product state integrator that leaves the state in canonical form with an orthogonality center, a scalar correction restores an admissible global invariant after compression whenever the resulting scalar quadratic constraint equation admits a real root, without enlarging any bond dimension. Rank-preserving recovery searches other canonical centers when that correction fails. The geometry of the low-rank manifold provides sufficient conditions for conservation by the fixed-rank projected flow and bounds what corrections within a frozen center frame can achieve. Numerical experiments demonstrate that recovery can be required even along accurate truncated trajectories.
