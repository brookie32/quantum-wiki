arXiv:2606.29312v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: For two identical particles, adding labels reduces the total overlap of their label states and decreases their exchange indistinguishability as measured by Hong--Ou--Mandel interference. For more than two particles, the opposite effect can occur: introducing additional labels can boost multiparticle indistinguishability. We present an explicit seven-particle example in which additional labels increase multiparticle indistinguishability by 7.38%, as quantified by the symmetric projection weight of the seven-particle label state, while decreasing the mean squared pairwise overlap by nearly half. We trace this effect to the cancellation of collective multiparticle phases. Phases associated with one set of label states cancel those associated with the other, thereby turning destructive contributions to the symmetric weight into constructive ones. We also give sufficient conditions that rule out this effect. For any number of identical particles, additional label states independent of the original label states cannot increase indistinguishability when the original symmetric weight is at least 1/2. Independent additional label states also cannot increase the symmetric weight of any number of identical particles prepared independently in the same mixed label state.
