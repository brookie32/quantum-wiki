arXiv:2207.13539v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: The combination of interaction-free measurement and the quantum Zeno effect has been shown to both increase the signal-to-noise ratio of imaging, and reduce the energy absorbed by the imaged object. So far, however, it has not been used for quantitative polarimetry, the reconstruction of a sample's Mueller matrix. In this paper, we extend this to the polarimetry of diattenuating samples. For polarisation components that the sample strongly absorbs, the protocol identifies the sample with far less absorbed energy than a single-pass measurement, a benefit for delicate samples probed with high-frequency radiation. For weakly absorbing components the repeated passes through the sample absorb more, so the benefit is confined to strongly diattenuating features. A Fisher-information analysis shows that, for a sample with a lossless transmitting axis, the protocol locates the absorbing axis with far fewer absorbed photons than single-pass polarimetry, while it measures partial transmittances no more efficiently per absorbed photon, and that the same sorting of lossy components to one output port responds to retardance and depolarisation.
