Vacuum birefringence— a quantum‑electrodynamic effect where the vacuum acquires polarization‑dependent refractive indices in ultra‑strong magnetic fields—has been empirically probed for the first time with the radio magnetar 1E 1547.0–5408. Phase‑ and energy‑resolved X‑ray polarimetry from IXPE, combined with NICER timing and Parkes/Murriyang radio observations, reveal polarization degrees up to ≈80 % at 2–3 keV (phase‑averaged ≈65 %) that drop markedly between 2 and 4 keV, while the associated polarization angles follow the rotating vector model. These results contradict surface emission models assuming non‑refractive light propagation and provide strong evidence that vacuum birefringence governs magnetospheric photon transport in magnetars.
