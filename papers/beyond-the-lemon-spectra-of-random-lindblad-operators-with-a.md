arXiv:2610.19261v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Generators of continuous-time quantum Markovian evolution, known as Lindblad operators, play a key role in the theory of open quantum systems. The question of how to define and characterize random Lindblad operators has attracted considerable attention in recent years. Purely dissipative random Lindbladians have been extensively studied using toolboxes of free probability and non-Hermitian random matrix theories, which helped, among other results, to uncover a characteristic lemon-like spectral support. In this work, we focus on random Lindbladians with a Hamiltonian component. We analyze three ensembles of increasing complexity, designed to illustrate the joint effects of dissipative and Hamiltonian dynamics. While the first two ensembles allow for an exact analytic treatment with standard probabilistic and free-probability methods, the generic case of independently random Hamiltonian and dissipative components requires a novel framework that combines operator-valued free probability with sequential noise averaging. For this ensemble, our approach preserves the underlying tensor-product structure of the Lindblad operator and derives a general equation for the spectral boundary. We characterize the effect of Hamiltonian-induced spectral squeezing and explain how the spectral support changes with the relative Hamiltonian strength and the rank of the dissipative component.
