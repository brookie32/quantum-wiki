arXiv:2604.17427v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Metal-site density strongly influences oxygen reduction activity and selectivity in M-N-C electrocatalysts, but the descriptors that predict these trends remain under debate. Here, we compare spin state and the potential of zero charge (PZC) as predictors of density-dependent oxygen reduction behavior in Fe-N-C and Co-N-C catalysts. Using constrained-magnetization calculations combined with Landau analysis, we find that the ground-state magnetic moments vary only weakly across a broad range of metal-site densities, suggesting that magnetic descriptors alone cannot account for the pronounced performance changes. In contrast, explicit-solvent simulations reveal systematic density-dependent shifts in PZC, which alter the interfacial electric field and thereby modulate field-sensitive adsorption energetics of ORR intermediates. Incorporating these PZC shifts into a pH-field-coupled microkinetic model captures the density-dependent activity trends and reproduces the experimentally observed increase in two-electron selectivity at lower site densities under acidic conditions. Experimental PZC measurements further support the predicted trend. Within the investigated Co-N-C and Fe-N-C series, PZC-dependent electrostatics consistently rationalize the density-dependent ORR trends, whereas the examined magnetic descriptors show comparatively weak variation.
