arXiv:2611.14458v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Thermal amplification enables sensitive control of heat currents, providing a basis for heat management and thermal signal processing in quantum devices. For a harmonic oscillator coupled to emitter, collector, and base thermal reservoirs, we recover the absence of thermal amplification when system-reservoir interactions permit only single-quantum exchange. A controlled weak-nonlinearity expansion, supported by numerical stationary solutions, shows that two-quantum exchange at the emitter enables amplification and predicts its onset. When two-quantum exchange dominates at the emitter and collector, varying the base temperature redistributes populations between even and odd oscillator sectors. This changes the emitter and collector heat currents far more than the base current, enabling large thermal amplification. We propose a realization using a longitudinally coupled auxiliary two-level system and bath spectral filtering, specifying conditions for its elimination to yield the required oscillator dynamics. These results establish nonlinear system-reservoir coupling as a resource for controlling heat flow and enabling thermal amplification.
