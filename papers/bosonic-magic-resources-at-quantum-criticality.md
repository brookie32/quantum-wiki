arXiv:2611.33306v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Non-Gaussianity is a resource for bosonic quantum information processing, providing a necessary ingredient for universal quantum computation in continuous-variable systems. Here we quantify non-Gaussianity in the ground state of the one-dimensional extended Bose-Hubbard model using quantum relative entropy (QRE), a measure determined solely by one-body correlations. The scaling of the QRE with system size reveals quantum phase transitions involving hidden and conventional order. We connect QRE scaling in the weakly interacting superfluid phase to Luttinger-liquid correlations and identify distinct critical signatures in its extensive and subleading contributions. At the Mott-Haldane transition, subtracting the extensive background reveals a pronounced peak in the subleading contribution, providing a clear signature of the onset of hidden string order. At the Haldane-density-wave transition, the extensive contribution to the QRE instead exhibits scaling consistent with Ising criticality. These results establish non-Gaussianity as a probe of many-body criticality, linking bosonic quantum resources to strongly correlated matter.
