arXiv:2611.33729v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Bell-type inequalities are commonly associated with quantum nonlocality, yet their mathematical structure also reflects more general constraints on the joint probability distributions assigned to measurements performed in different contexts. Here we examine this distinction through the Wigner--Bell inequality using a classical optical field with two nonseparable degrees of freedom. Starting from a subsystem response-factorization assumption, we derive a Wigner-type probability bound requiring the pairwise measurement statistics to be compatible with a single nonnegative global joint distribution. For a classically nonseparable optical field, the corresponding joint probabilities are represented directly by normalized joint intensities and obey Malus' law. We show that these Malus-law probabilities violate the Wigner--Bell bound over a finite range of analyzer settings. The violation does not imply spatial nonlocality or a failure of classical electrodynamics. Rather, it demonstrates the incompatibility of context-dependent optical projection statistics with the global probability assignment underlying the Wigner construction. We further derive an extended Wigner-Bell bound for general imperfect correlations and propose an interferometric experiment for reconstructing the required joint probabilities from intensity measurements. These results provide a transparent optical realization of the probabilistic structure behind Bell-type inequalities and help distinguish consequences of Hilbert-space structure, nonseparability, and measurement context from those specifically associated with quantum nonlocality.
