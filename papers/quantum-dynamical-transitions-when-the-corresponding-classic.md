arXiv:2611.25299v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: In classical Hamiltonian systems with a slowly time-dependent parameter, adiabaticity breaks down at a separatrix, leading to probabilistic post-adiabatic transitions between different phase-space regions described by the Kruskal-Neishtadt-Henrard (KNH) theorem. Quantum mechanically, dynamical tunneling allows adiabaticity to persist where it breaks down classically, making the quantum-classical correspondence for separatrix crossing non-trivial. We recently established a quantum version of the KNH theorem (QKNH) by introducing the concept of a sweep-rate-dependent quantum separatrix in the instantaneous energy spectrum of the time-dependent system. The QKNH theorem relates the splitting of a quantum ensemble between the outgoing dynamical sectors at the quantum separatrix to their relative growth rates, and thus provides a quantum analog of the classical KNH rule. Here, we demonstrate that these findings are not restricted to double-well systems with tunneling through a potential barrier. We investigate a slowly driven quantum pendulum, in which the avoided crossings arise from above-barrier reflection. The resulting Landau-Zener probabilities have the same local lattice form as in the double-well problem, allowing the application of the QKNH theorem.
