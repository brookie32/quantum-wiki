arXiv:2604.00118v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: In Hamiltonian systems, level statistics provide a faithful diagnostic of quantum chaos. By analogy, Lindbladian spectral statistics are widely used for open quantum systems, following the Grobe-Haake-Sommers conjecture that systems with chaotic classical counterparts exhibit level repulsion. Here we identify a fundamental limitation of this analogy. Unlike Hamiltonian spectra, the Lindbladian spectral bulk contributes little to long-time dynamics, which are governed by modes with real parts closest to zero. Bulk modes typically decay on microscopic time scales unless concentrated near the imaginary axis. Consequently, perturbations must influence the dynamics before these modes decay to produce observable effects. Yet level statistics resolve much finer spectral scales, allowing perturbations to alter spectral correlations without appreciably changing evolution. Strong Lindbladian non-normality amplifies this separation, enabling drastic changes in level statistics under dynamically negligible perturbations. We demonstrate examples exhibiting Ginibre level repulsion without signatures of dynamical chaos at any time. Moreover, we construct pairs of Lindbladians whose dynamics become indistinguishable for all observables and times in the thermodynamic limit, despite displaying contrasting spectral statistics, with and without level repulsion. Our results challenge Lindbladian bulk statistics as universal chaos diagnostics, suggesting possible reliability only for approximately normal generators with spectra concentrated near the imaginary axis.
