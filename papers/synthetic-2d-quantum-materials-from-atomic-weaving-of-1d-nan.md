The authors introduce an atomic‑scale “Lego” strategy that weaves graphene nanoribbons (GNRs) into stable two‑dimensional networks, enabling precise control over lattice geometry, stacking sequence, and edge configuration through interlaced flat or curved ribbons. This bottom‑up van der Waals assembly allows the ribbon width to define the band gap while the spacing between woven strands tunes the bandwidth, even producing flat bands near the Fermi level. By employing curved GNRs, the approach can generate specific lattice motifs such as kagome and square lattices, demonstrating a versatile platform for designing 2D quantum materials with tailored electronic properties.
