arXiv:2611.18071v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Quantum algorithms based on the classical post-processing of measurement samples have emerged as effective and robust methods to approximate low-energy wavefunctions of many-body quantum systems on present-day and early-fault-tolerant quantum devices. This paper introduces a generic framework for enforcing spin symmetry within these approaches. We tailor this framework to SqDRIFT, a sample-based quantum diagonalization variant that uses the qDRIFT randomized compilation protocol to generate the quantum samples. We discuss how three key components of SqDRIFT (the initial-state preparation, the construction of the time-evolution circuits, and the classical post-processing) can be adapted to incorporate spin symmetry. We show that spin-pure circuits alone are not sufficient, since the sampled determinants generally span a subspace that is not closed under hat{S}^2, and we restore this closure by completing the sampled electronic configurations. In classical simulations of 4-aminobenzonitrile and of an [ext{Fe}(ext{NH}_{3})_{4}]^{2+} model complex, and in experiments on an IBM quantum processor for the oxo-Mn(Salen) complex, this spin-adapted post-processing yields roots that are exact spin eigenfunctions at every subspace size. On hardware, it does so with diagonalized subspaces two to three orders of magnitude smaller than those of the conventional constructions for the same circuit depth.
