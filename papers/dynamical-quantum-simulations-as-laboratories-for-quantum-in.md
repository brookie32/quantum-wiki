arXiv:2610.37589v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Dynamical quantum simulations are commonly designed to access a particular response function or correlation function. Yet the underlying protocol prepares a family of many-body states that contains information well beyond the observable for which the simulation was originally designed. In this work, we explore the idea of using these states as laboratories for quantum information. As a case study, we consider the local-pulse protocols used to compute Green functions in finite XX and XXX spin chains, and we interpret opposite source rotations as encoding a classical bit into the many-body system. This allows us to follow how the resulting record propagates, how it is encoded across local subsystems, and how readily it can be accessed with different measurements. We also find a clear separation between representation and readout. While fermionic covariance data exactly reconstruct the signed response in the XX chain, simple spin measurements recover a larger fraction of the locally available information in the XXX chain. This reversed ordering persists when we move from finite-pulse discrimination to local source-angle estimation. Going beyond the source record, the same reduced states also allow us to reinterpret results in terms of Bell state recovery and quantum communication rates, as well as providing direct access to the evolving bipartite and genuine multipartite entanglement of the system. Our results show that dynamical quantum simulations developed for response calculations can naturally be used to ask a much broader set of quantum-information questions about the many-body states they already prepare.
