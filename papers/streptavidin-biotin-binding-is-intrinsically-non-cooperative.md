arXiv:2610.41363v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The biotin-streptavidin interaction is a key component of nanobiotechnology, yet the nature of its cooperativity remains a debated question. Here, we introduce a label-free spectroscopy approach based on confocal UV autofluorescence microscopy to probe the binding cooperativity of this high-affinity system. By leveraging the intrinsic autofluorescence of tryptophan residues, we avoid artefacts associated with extrinsic fluorescent labels, enabling precise measurements of fully biotin-saturated streptavidin across a wide range of molar ratios. Our experimental results, supported by Monte Carlo simulations, provide conclusive evidence for the absence of cooperativity. However, we show that false signs of apparent positive cooperativity can emerge from local concentration inhomogeneities occurring when rapid reaction kinetics outpace mixing. By establishing the non-cooperative nature of biotin-streptavidin binding and identifying the key conditions to avoid cooperativity artefacts, these insights resolve a longstanding debate and offer critical implications for streptavidin-based biotechnological applications.
