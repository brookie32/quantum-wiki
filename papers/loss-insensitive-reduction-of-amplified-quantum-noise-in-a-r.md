arXiv:2602.22567v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: A quantum amplifier usually adds extra noise inevitably through coupling to internal degrees of freedom while amplifying the signal. The introduction of quantum correlations can effectively suppress this extra noise. In this work, we utilize the established quantum correlation between the Stokes field and atomic spin waves in the Raman amplification process to feed back a portion of the Stokes field into the amplifier. This leads to a reduction in amplified quantum noise that is independent of the feedback loss at high gain. A maximum of 6 dB noise reduction is observed. The single-path feedback amplifier is found to be sensitive to the feedback phase due to quantum interference, and we experimentally demonstrate an improvement in the signal-to-noise ratio of 4.4pm 0.7dB for a phase signal encoded in the feedback loop compared to the case without feedback. This general concept can be extended to integrated optics and fiber-optic systems with potential applications in quantum precision measurement.
