arXiv:2611.26755v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Gravity-induced entanglement provides an operational test of whether gravitational dynamics are compatible with local operations and classical communication (LOCC), but the weakness of the gravitational interaction and the presence of force noise make direct entanglement generation extremely demanding. We introduce a controllable additional coherent interaction that biases two test masses close to, but within, a separability-preservation bound, so that the weak Newtonian coupling supplies only the remaining coherent contribution needed for entanglement. If the additional interaction alone is shown to be incapable of generating entanglement, while entanglement is observed when gravity is included, the gravitational interaction provides a non-LOCC contribution. Since gravity cannot be turned off, we use a magic-angle null of the projected tidal coupling to calibrate the additional interaction and force noise. If the additional dynamics is certified to preserve separability at the magic angle and entanglement is subsequently observed in a non-null configuration, the gravitational contribution is incompatible with an LOCC description within the Markovian model. The protocol trades the stringent absolute force-noise requirement of direct schemes for precision in controlling the additional coupling, its backaction, and angle-dependent systematics.
