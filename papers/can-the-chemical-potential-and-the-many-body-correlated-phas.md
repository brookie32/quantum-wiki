arXiv:2611.33289v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: A central challenge in quantum many-body physics is to connect microscopic interactions to experimentally accessible macroscopic states of matter, where correlations extend across multiple length scales. Using quantum Monte Carlo (QMC) simulations of two-dimensional repulsive dipolar bosons, recently realized across various experimental platforms, we show that correlations within the dipolar fluid lead to an unexpected simplification. Specifically, we find that these correlations induce a pronounced depletion region around each particle that suppresses short-range physics and yields an emergent universality, where the fluid is governed by dipolar interactions rather than material-specific details. Exploiting this universality, we establish a unified, quantitatively validated analytical framework for the dipolar fluid, taking indirect (interlayer) excitons as a model system. We show that analytical interaction energies derived in an earlier study agree with exact QMC results across three correlation regimes: a classical gas, a quantum gas, and a high-density liquid. In contrast, the widely used uncorrelated plate capacitor formula severely overestimates the numerical values. Building on these expressions, we show that the chemical potential is accurately captured by a three-component decomposition: an ideal Bose-gas kinetic term, a regime-dependent dipolar interaction term, and a high-density quantum confinement term. Finally, we address a key experimental challenge: extracting exciton densities from measured energy shifts. Our QMC-benchmarked calibration curves for typical gallium arsenide (GaAs) and transition metal dichalcogenide (TMD) heterostructures offer a reliable tool for experimentalists to determine both the fluid's density and collective state. This universal framework extends to other two-dimensional systems, including ultracold dipolar atoms and polar molecules.
