arXiv:2611.23712v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Resonantly driven two-level quantum batteries (QBs) suffer from strong pulse-area dependence and energy backflow inherent to Rabi oscillations. To address this limitation, we propose charging of a two-level QB with chirped pulses. We investigate four charging protocols formed by pairing hyperbolic secant (sech) or Gaussian pulse envelopes with linear or hyperbolic-tangent (tanh) frequency sweeps. We show that sufficiently strong chirp modulation converts the charging mechanism from pulse-area-governed Rabi rotation to adiabatic rapid passage. We analyze the underlying adiabatic dynamics and derive the criteria for robust chirped charging, which incorporate the sweep bandwidth, nonadiabatic transitions, and dissipative effects. Under strong chirp modulation, the final stored energy and ergotropy converge to stable plateaus that are nearly insensitive to the pulse area across an extended parameter window. Of the four schemes, the sech pulse with tanh chirp delivers the widest ergotropy plateau and the weakest pulse area sensitivity. Compared with counterdiabatic driving, the sech tanh protocol attains stable charging performance without demanding an extra drive quadrature. Furthermore, this chirp-enabled charging is resilient to static detuning and weak-to-moderate dissipation. This work establishes a practical route toward robust charging of quantum batteries using chirped pulse control.
