Network science informs the design of novel materials that distribute applied deformation over their structure, exhibit tunable stiffness, and manage vibrational wave propagation. By incorporating the heterogeneity and variable component sizes characteristic of complex networks into material architectures, researchers can engineer mechanical properties that mirror those found in biological, chemical, and social systems. The approach demonstrates how principles uncovered by Barabási and Albert—such as non‑uniform connectivity and robustness—can be translated into tangible advances in materials engineering.
