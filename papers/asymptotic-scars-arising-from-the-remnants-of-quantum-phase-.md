arXiv:2610.32037v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Many-body localisation, Hilbert space fragmentation and quantum many-body scars (QMBS) are well-established mechanisms of ergodicity breaking in quantum systems. Recently, asymptotic quantum many-body scars (AQMBS), non-thermal states whose stability grows with system size, have been proposed theoretically and probed experimentally. Here, we show that AQMBS arise naturally as remnants of certain quantum phase transitions. For a broad class of frustration-free Hamiltonians, we prove that their gapless excitations survive deformation into a scarred Hamiltonian as states whose energy variance is bounded by the excitation gap. Consequently, the gapless excitations at a quantum critical point become AQMBS of the scarred Hamiltonian. Their fidelity relaxation time is then set by the critical scaling of the gap, determined by the dynamic critical exponent. Modelling the critical Fredkin and Motzkin chains and the two-dimensional PVBS model, we obtain AQMBS whose variances directly reflect the dynamic exponents of the underlying transitions. Where the gap of the Motzkin chain closes super-exponentially, we find exponentially stable AQMBS, for which we also observe numerical evidence of volume-law entanglement.
