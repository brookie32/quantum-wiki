arXiv:2611.21999v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We propose a superconducting circuit in which two resonators couple through DC-biased SQUIDs to a common lossy auxiliary mode. Adiabatic elimination yields collective damping and correlated noise with an overlap xi whose magnitude is tuned by the flux biases and whose sign follows the relative sign of the couplings. The full three-mode dynamics confirm this reduced description. For detuned resonators, we derive the exceptional-point condition, above which the mean amplitudes lock to a common frequency. For resonant modes, unequal collective decay produces transient synchronization, antiphase or in phase depending on the relative sign of the couplings, with an analytical synchronization time set by the collective decay splitting and the initial mean amplitudes. For initially separable squeezed states, the same relaxation converts local squeezing into transient entanglement, quantified by logarithmic negativity. We obtain closed-form expressions for the squeezing threshold above which entanglement appears and for the time at which it disappears. Thermal noise suppresses entanglement without changing the mean-amplitude synchronization time at fixed parameters. For realistic circuit parameters, these effects occur within a few hundred nanoseconds and are accessible through output-field measurements. A single tunable shared channel thus produces an exceptional point, transient synchronization, and transient entanglement, and provides a controllable source of correlated noise for superconducting circuits.
