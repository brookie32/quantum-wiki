arXiv:2611.14200v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Disorder can localize quantum particles, even in the presence of interactions, but its influence on quantum fluctuations beyond mean--field dynamics remains poorly understood. Here, we investigate the propagation of quantum fluctuations in weakly interacting disordered bosonic systems by comparing the Bose--Hubbard model with the Gross--Pitaevskii equation. We find that, in contrast to ballistic spreading in the disorder--free system, quantum fluctuations exhibit logarithmic spreading at intermediate times under both random and quasiperiodic disorder, before eventually saturating. The spreading rate decreases algebraically with increasing disorder strength, and this behavior persists across different particle numbers and extends to two dimensions. Using a time--dependent Bogoliubov--de Gennes framework, we identify the localized mean--field wavefunction as a time--dependent pairing background that generates quantum fluctuations. The exponential suppression of fluctuation--generation rates with distance provides a physical mechanism for the logarithmically advancing fluctuation front. Our results reveal a distinct regime of disorder--controlled quantum--fluctuation dynamics and establish disorder as a means of controlling their spreading.
