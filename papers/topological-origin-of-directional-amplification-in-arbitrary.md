arXiv:2611.23989v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: In non-Hermitian lattices, combinations of gain, loss and nonreciprocal couplings can generate exponentially growing directional amplification with applications in signal processing and precision measurement. An intriguing question is whether such amplification has a universal topological origin in arbitrary dimensions. Here we establish a dimension-independent framework to uncover the topological origin of directional amplification based on the concept of amoeba, which is the logarithmic image of the singularities of the response generating function. The complement of the amoeba in the logarithmic parameter space consists of connected components, each carrying a distinct and discrete topological label. We show that the asymptotic response is governed by the component extending toward negative infinity. When the origin of the logarithmic parameter space is outside this component and not on its boundary, there must exist some directions in which the response is exponentially amplified. Otherwise, there is no directional amplification in any direction. Our results provide a general topological criterion for classifying the responses in arbitrary-dimensional non-Hermitian lattices.
