arXiv:2611.17890v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Quantum limit cycles exhibit harmonic Liouvillian spectra, yet their underlying orbital U(1) structure is not generally explicit in the absence of physical rotational symmetry. Here, we construct a unitary representation of orbital phase translations through a similarity transformation that maps the harmonic nondecaying Liouvillian sector onto a Hermitian angular-momentum Hamiltonian. The resulting phase states possess homogeneous Fubini--Study geometry independently of the physical orbit's shape. For ideal quantum limit cycles, the corresponding density matrix also exhibit homogeneous Bures geometry. At finite size, we show how phase diffusion damps slow Liouvillian harmonics while preserving the effective orbital U(1) symmetry. The resulting loss of phase-state distinguishability and contraction of the effective geometry are quantitatively predicted by the microscopic slow Liouvillian spectrum. Our results establish a connection between orbital phase symmetry, quantum geometry, and finite-size phase diffusion in open quantum systems.
