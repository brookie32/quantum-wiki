In 2026, a high‑brightness muonium beam was produced by extracting the μ⁺e⁻ bound state from a thin layer of superfluid helium, achieving a mean longitudinal speed with an exceptionally narrow velocity spread and intensities comparable to the best diffuse sources. The resulting superthermal beam enables interferometric measurements that could determine muonium’s gravitational acceleration at the percent level, while its unprecedented brightness permits sub‑kilohertz 1S–2S spectroscopy for precise determination of the muon mass and stringent tests of bound‑state quantum electrodynamics.
