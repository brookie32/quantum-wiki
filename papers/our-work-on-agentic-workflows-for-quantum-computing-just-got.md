Our work on agentic workflows for quantum computing just got picked up by one of science's most-read journals, @Nature, under the headline: "AI tool lets researchers 'vibe code' in the quantum realm." The idea: turn a plain-English description of a quantum simulation into a running result. The agent reasons about hardware constraints, encodes the problem into the geometry of atoms trapped by laser light, validates the code on a classical emulator, then runs it on a live neutral-atom QPU, with the researcher staying in charge of every scientific decision along the way. As our CTO Loïc Henriet explains in the news article, this translation from a physics concept to running quantum hardware has traditionally required expertise in both the physics being simulated and quantum computing. This is exactly what our agentic workflows are starting to change: making quantum computing more accessible without taking the scientist out of the loop. Read the Nature news article: https://doi.org/10.1038/d41586-026-02495-4 Preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25834
