arXiv:2604.01157v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: We investigate entanglement in and out of equilibrium in a one-dimensional Bose gas in its low-energy Bogoliubov regime. In this Gaussian setting, the state is fully characterized by its covariance matrix, which allows us to detect and quantify entanglement directly from the density and phase fluctuations of the gas. For thermal states, we determine the optimal entanglement witness arising from the covariance matrix criterion and show that it has a remarkably simple form: it depends only on the density fluctuations of the lowest normal mode and on the phase fluctuations of the highest one. For Dirichlet boundary conditions, this gives the exact temperature below which the gas is entangled, which, for many pixels, is given by k_BT_{rm sep}simeq0.83,hbar c_0/Delta, where c_0 is the speed of sound and Delta the size of the pixels used to resolve the gas. We then study out-of-equilibrium dynamics induced by unitary compression and show that entanglement can be generated even from initially separable thermal states. This requires a non-adiabatic compression, since an adiabatic compression acts separately, and in the same way, on every pixel and therefore conserves entanglement. Moreover, the amount of entanglement created is limited by the compression ratio. We finally discuss how a subsequent contact with a thermal bath, modeled by global or local master equations, affects entanglement. Our methods and results provide a unified and physically intuitive picture of how entanglement emerges and evolves in 1D quantum Bose gases, and identify a simple optimal witness that is relevant more broadly to the analysis of entanglement in quadratic bosonic models and of its role in thermodynamic transformations.
