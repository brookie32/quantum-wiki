arXiv:2611.22341v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: For weak-phase objects, scanning transmission electron microscopy (STEM) with a far-field detector transfers at most half of the phase information per electron that Zernike phase-contrast (ZPC) conventional transmission electron microscopy (CTEM) achieves. We propose APEX (Apex Phase-plate EXit-wave imaging), a scanned-probe mode that combines a defocused probe, a pi/2 phase element in a magnified image of the probe crossover, and in-focus image-plane detection at each scan position. It applies the generalized Zernike framework of Dwyer and Paganin to a scanned probe. In a linear model of a thin weak-phase object with an ideal phase dot, APEX transfers nearly the full Zernike Fisher information over a wide band, substantially more than diffraction-plane four-dimensional STEM (4D-STEM). In idealized simulations of myoglobin in vitreous ice at a cryo-EM dose (elastic scattering only, no chromatic aberration, full coherence), ideal-dot APEX at a probe defocus of 1 mum reconstructs the phase more accurately than defocused 4D-STEM ptychography with one untuned probe setting. At a defocus of about 25 mum, a crossed laser phase plate (XLPP) acts as the pi/2 element, and APEX of a periodic specimen cell comes close to CTEM with the same plate. In two dimensions, this mode is equivalent to laser phase-plate CTEM of a small, scanned area. A single simulated APEX scan with the XLPP also separates two myoglobin phase screens 150 nm apart in vacuum, at known depths, which is not possible with one ZPC CTEM exposure.
