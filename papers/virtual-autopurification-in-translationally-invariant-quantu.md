arXiv:2611.24448v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We introduce virtual autopurification (VAP), a protocol for virtual purification that operates entirely within a single translationally invariant system. Virtual purification suppresses thermal fluctuations and decoherence noise by amplifying the dominant pure component of a mixed state, but its standard implementation requires multiple copies of the state and global entangled measurements. VAP avoids the multiple-copy overhead by constructing virtual copies from spatially separated subregions and performing entangled measurements only between them. Assuming clustering of correlations, we show that VAP reproduces the expectation values of the standard virtual purification protocol, with discrepancies that decay exponentially in both the buffer length and the inter-subregion distance. Numerical simulations support the theoretical predictions and demonstrate accurate implementation of virtual cooling with reduced resource requirements. Our results establish VAP as a resource-efficient and scalable approach to quantum-state purification in near-term devices.
