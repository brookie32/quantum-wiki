arXiv:2611.27328v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Optical cavities are widely used in atomic physics experiments to control the spatial geometry and spectral properties of atom-light interactions. Recently, an optical lattice generated by the pristine mode of an optical cavity was utilized to hold and extend the quantum coherence of atomic spatial superpositions. Here, we describe an in-vacuum optical cavity designed to enhance the spatial and temporal coherence of optical lattice atom interferometers to one millimeter wavepacket separations and one minute measurement durations. The cavity uses a rigid Zerodur spacer to suppress the negative effects of thermal drift and mechanical-acoustic vibrations on the atomic motional state. The 60 cm-long cavity spacer provides ample optical access for the laser beams and imaging optics necessary for the preparation, quantum control, and read-out of the atomic state, as well as room for various source mass configurations for localized gravitational measurements. An integrated differential pumping structure enables compatibility with ultrahigh-vacuum operation and optical interferometry allows for in-situ vibration monitoring. The performance of the optical cavity is methodically characterized: we measure mirror vibration noise with amplitude below 5 nm/sqrt{rm Hz} in a wide frequency range of 2 Hz - 100 kHz and a slow 15 MHz thermal drift of the cavity resonance over 47 hours, which can be readily stabilized with an integrated piezo. The optical cavity supports both 852-nm light for Raman transitions and 1064-nm light for the optical lattice, with a free spectral range matched to an integer divisor of the cesium ground-state hyperfine splitting.
