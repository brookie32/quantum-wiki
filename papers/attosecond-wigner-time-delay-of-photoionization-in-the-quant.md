arXiv:2611.27569v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The critical phenomena and phase transitions are profound concepts of many-body physics, yet their signatures in the photoelectron spectra remain largely unexplored. Here we report how the attosecond Wigner time delay in photoionization behaves in quantum critical systems, extending from stretched molecular hydrogen to periodic Hubbard model, as well as models of Bardeen-Cooper-Schrieffer (BCS) and high-temperature superconductors. Based on theoretical analysis of these examples, we first prove that the Wigner time delay, i.e. the time the photoelectron takes to become a free electron, is a thermodynamic quantity. And the time delay exhibits distinctive characters in the crossover and phase transition of many-body systems, such as sign-flip and singularity at the critical points, implying drastic modulation of photoelectron velocity in the quantum systems undergoing phase transition. We showcase that the sign of Wigner time delay is relevant with the evolution of electron correlation in the phase transition and is topologically protected, as the flip of the photoionization delay sign is governed by a change in the winding number of the complex photoionization amplitude around the origin of the complex plane. Our findings establish the attosecond photoionization time delay as a probe of quantum critical phenomena and the degree of electron correlation in the phase transitions, with applications to the study of strongly correlated systems such as the Mott insulator, metal-insulator transition (MIT) and superconductivity and ultrafast light-driven nonequilibrium phase dynamics.
