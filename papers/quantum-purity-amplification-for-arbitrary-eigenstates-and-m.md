arXiv:2605.21570v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Quantum purity amplification (QPA) is the task of coherently transforming n copies of a mixed state into high-fidelity copies of a chosen eigenstate. We develop a general theory of QPA for n input copies, m output copies, and arbitrary choices of target eigenstate, local dimension d, and input spectrum. We introduce the overhang-removal protocol, derive its all-site and one-site fidelities across output regimes, and show that it is optimal in several regimes, both asymptotically and nonasymptotically. For the asymptotic analysis, we use a path-graph parametrization to show that, when the target eigenvalue has a constant spectral gap D_{k,min}, achieving worst-case all-site error arepsilon scales as O(m/(arepsilon D_{k,min}^2)). When m/n approaches a constant, the performance exhibits phase-like regimes, which we characterize explicitly. For the nonasymptotic analysis, we develop a theory of generalized Young diagrams that yields tight sample complexity bounds and provides the first tight dimension-uniform guarantee for optimal QPA. We also provide asymptotically efficient implementations of the overhang-removal protocol. Together, these results establish QPA as a rigorous example of coherent quantum information processing with dimension-uniform sample complexity, supplying the technical foundation for the coherent-incoherent separation developed in the companion work.
