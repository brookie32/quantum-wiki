arXiv:2611.01321v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: The first barrier to receptor-mediated endocytosis is not binding but arrival within a few nanometers of the membrane. In a crowded extracellular environment, this pre-contact approach is driven by thermal fluctuations and entropic depletion, yet continuum models usually start from established contact or treat the approach deterministically. We formulate a thermodynamically consistent stochastic theory for this stage. The sphere--plane depletion geometry uses the exact Asakura--Oosawa spherical-cap overlap, not a fitted cap, with either ideal van~'t~Hoff or pressure-only Carnahan--Starling (CS) pressure. Near-wall mobility enters with its essential and often omitted noise-induced drift. The first specific reaction is a Robin radiation boundary; the outer boundary is reflecting or absorbing. Three first-passage quantities are obtained analytically by quadrature: the mean reaction time, the reaction-before-escape probability, and the conditional success time, the last encoded by a Doob transform that exposes the selection bias of conditioning on capture. The local Damkohler number diagnoses the boundary reaction-to-diffusion ratio, while the mean-time crossover follows from the exact reaction and diffusion terms. At baseline, ideal and CS contact depths are (2.33) and (5.95,k_{rm B}T), and the reflecting-outer mean reaction time is approximately (0.20,{rm ms}); with an absorbing outer boundary the conditional success time is shorter, but the two observables use different boundary protocols. Numerical checks reproduce the analytic results and the radiation-boundary mass balance. This provides a quantitative theoretical framework for crowding-assisted capture before membrane wrapping.
