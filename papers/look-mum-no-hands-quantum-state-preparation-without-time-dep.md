arXiv:2611.23382v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: How much can be prepared with a quantum system whose Hamiltonian cannot be modulated in time? Common intuition suggests that the answer is not much. Without a knob to turn during the evolution, a system seems confined to a fixed trajectory through its state space, while time-dependent control can steer it almost anywhere. We put this intuition to the test by studying autonomous control, i.e., state preparation with a time-invariant Hamiltonian, where the only freedom lies in the static coupling strengths and the evolution time. We characterize basic mathematical properties of the autonomously reachable set and introduce two criteria of unreachability: a certificate that is cheap to evaluate and rigorously excludes that a target state can be reached for any choice of control parameters, and a stronger criterion based on exhaustive numerical optimization. With these tools we estimate the probability that a typical state in Hilbert space is unreachable under randomly drawn control terms. The dimensionality of the reachable set turns out to be independent of the system, yet the fraction of Hilbert space lying close to it depends strongly on the choice of control Hamiltonians. Strikingly, for sufficiently random controls and long enough evolution times, almost every state can become approximately reachable, even with the fewest generators that permit full controllability with time-dependent couplings. Autonomous control can approach the expressivity of time-dependent control, at the cost of longer evolution times.
