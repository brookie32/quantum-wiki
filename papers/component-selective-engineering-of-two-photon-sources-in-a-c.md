arXiv:2607.06932v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: In multiphoton blockade, drive-induced excitations are not necessarily fully converted into cavity photons, leaving unwanted lower-photon-number contributions that degrade source purity. This motivates a source-oriented component-selection strategy, in which the internal composition of the resonant two-excitation state is tailored to favor the component associated with the desired quantum-light output. In a cavity-coupled two-atom system, by engineering the resonant-state composition and controlling the interference between excitation pathways, we suppress both single- and three-photon backgrounds at matched two-photon populations, although a residual single-photon contribution remains. This residual background originates from a structural tradeoff between the photonic character of the target state and the spectral anharmonicity required for photon-number selectivity. Furthermore, selecting the double-atomic-excitation component at another operating point enables the generation of strongly correlated fluorescence photon pairs, demonstrating two distinct two-photon sources within the same physical platform.
