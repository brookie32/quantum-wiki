arXiv:2611.15757v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Sustained fluorescence detection of generic molecules has been challenging due to the lack of cycling transitions and limited photon budgets. We demonstrate a nondestructive method for continuous fluorescence of strongly rovibronically open molecular ions, ^{232}ThF^+. Effective quasi-cycling transitions are established by room-temperature buffer-gas collisions, which replenish the population back to the laser-addressed states. In situ images and rotationally resolved spectra are obtained without optical repumping or auxiliary atomic readout ions. Combined with low-background spectroscopic detection, this approach achieves a signal-to-noise ratio of approximately 6 in a 1 s exposure on the P(1) line of ^{232}ThF^+, despite a thermal population of only approximately 0.17% in the addressed state at room temperature. The ability to repeatedly interrogate trapped ions is particularly promising for scarce molecular samples, broadband spectroscopic searches, and in situ monitoring of molecular reactions.
