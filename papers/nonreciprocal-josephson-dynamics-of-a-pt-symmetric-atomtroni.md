arXiv:2611.14291v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We investigate non-Hermitian Josephson dynamics in an atomtronic quantum interference device (AQUID), formed by a ring Bose Einstein condensate with balanced atomic gain and loss regions and two moving optical barriers. The balanced gain and loss profile establishes a parity time symmetric potential while breaking spatial inversion symmetry, providing the essential ingredients for nonreciprocal Josephson transport. The dynamics are monitored nondestructively through the phase quadrature spectrum of an optomechanical cavity driven by Laguerre Gaussian beams coupled to the condensate. We demonstrate that increasing the local gain and loss strength softens the parity time symmetric collective mode towards an exceptional point, as verified by Bogoliubov de Gennes analysis. The associated coherent population exchange produces an additional characteristic splitting in the cavity output spectrum. For linearly translating barriers, non-Hermiticity provides an additional control parameter for tuning the critical current. When the barriers undergo simultaneous translation and rotation, the rotation induced phase modifies the interference between counter propagating currents, offering further control over the nonreciprocal response. Under periodic barrier modulation, we find more pronounced Shapiro steps in the current voltage characteristics due to the intrinsic parity time symmetric collective mode. Finally, we propose an alternative Hermitian realization using a tilted optical lattice, providing an experimentally accessible route to engineering biased transport and population transfer. Our results establish parity time symmetric atomtronic SQUIDs as a versatile platform for controlling nonreciprocal Josephson transport and collective excitations in driven quantum fluids.
