arXiv:2610.40880v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: We investigate collective plasma-wave dynamics in a two-dimensional electron fluid subected to a travelling periodic modulation of the effective plasma velocity. Using a hydrodynamic description combined with Floquet-Bloch theory, we show that the travelling modulation acts as a moving plasmonic crystal whose band structure is governed by an effective drift velocity in the comoving frame. We demonstrate that the closure of the Bragg gap at the edge of the Brillouin zone gives rise to an exceptional point and triggers a Krein-type instability through the coupling of positive- and negative-energy modes. Analytical expressions are derived for the instability threshold and growth rate, revealing that self-amplification can occur even when the electron drift remains subsonic in the laboratory frame. Numerical simulations of the nonlinear hydrodynamic equations confirm the theoretical predictions and show the evolution towards a saturated regime dominated by a coherent Floquet-Bloch mode. These results identify travelling plasmonic crystals as a novel platform for wave amplification and non-Hermitian band dynamics in electronic fluids.
