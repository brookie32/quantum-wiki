arXiv:2611.33338v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Many multi-phase thin films grown by self-assembly involve co-deposition of both metallic and oxide phases that grow into the same film. Under such conditions, the oxide species may be present in stoichiometric molecular form on the surface of the metallic phase. Quantifying the stability, binding, and diffusion of such molecular oxide states on metallic surfaces is critical for understanding self-assembly. In this work, we investigate the binding and stability of CeO2 molecules on Au surfaces using spin-polarized density functional theory calculations. Specifically, we examine how the configuration of the CeO2 molecule, its binding energy and binding free energy, structural stability, effective charge, and projected electronic density of states vary among various binding sites on Au(001), Au(110), and Au(111) surfaces. Molecular CeO2 was found to be more favorable on all Au surfaces than its atomic constituents. It was also found that the bent CeO2 molecule configuration is more stable than the linear counterpart, with the Ce atom on the hollow site and the O atoms on the top sites. In addition, the configurations of the CeO2 molecule on Au surfaces with a binding energy around or weaker than -1 eV were found to have a low probability of existence above room temperature. Furthermore, the charge transfer was found to occur in such a way that the CeO2 molecule becomes polarized on all surfaces, but the polarization is stronger only for the strongly bonded states on Au(001) and Au(110) surfaces. Finally, three different cases of how the CeO2 molecule binds to Au(001), Au(110), and Au(111) were identified: case 1: chemisorption with symmetric bonds, case 2: chemisorption with asymmetric bonds, and case 3: physisorption with symmetric bonds. These three cases of CeO2 binding are present on Au(001) and Au(110), but only cases 2 and 3 occur on Au(111).
