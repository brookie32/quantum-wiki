arXiv:2610.40141v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Distributed inner-product estimation (DIPE) is a fundamental task in quantum information, aiming to estimate the inner product between two unknown quantum states prepared on separate quantum platforms. In multimode continuous-variable (CV) systems, the infinite-dimensional Hilbert space poses additional challenges. In this work, we propose a protocol for CV-DIPE that requires the two platforms to share a common phase reference and perform local homodyne measurements at the same randomly sampled phase settings, followed by classical post-processing of the measurement data. We derive explicit upper bounds on the sample complexity of this protocol. These bounds depend on the available physical prior information. Specifically, we provide bounds for two natural cases: finite-energy states and Gaussian states. Through numerical simulations, we demonstrate the theoretical performance of this estimation scheme. Our findings offer a CV-DIPE protocol that relies solely on homodyne measurements and remains applicable even when neither of the two quantum states is fully characterized.
