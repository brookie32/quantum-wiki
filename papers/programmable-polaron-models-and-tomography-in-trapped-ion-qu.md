arXiv:2610.18970v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Strong coupling between mobile charge carriers or excitons and lattice vibrations gives rise to polarons: quasiparticles formed by charge carriers dressed by a cloud of phonon excitations. Polaron formation determines transport properties of systems ranging from transition-metal oxides and organic semiconductors to light-harvesting complexes. In excitonic systems that admit a hard-core boson description, mapping the excitations to pseudospins provides a natural framework for studying polaron physics, including the nonperturbative regime in which hopping amplitudes, phonon energies, and coupling strengths are comparable. Here, we propose a trapped-ion blueprint for realizing spin-phonon Hamiltonians with programmable spatial coupling between excitation density and phonon displacements, encompassing local Holstein and long-range Frohlich-type interactions. To access strongly dressed quasiparticles whose bare spectral weight is suppressed, we introduce displaced Green's functions implemented through spin-dependent phonon displacements. Their spectral response reveals the polaron band spectrum, including binding energies and phonon-excitation sidebands, while spatially resolved measurements probe the structure of the phonon dressing cloud, providing a route to polaron tomography. We further complement this spectroscopy with direct and ancilla-assisted measurements of spin-phonon correlations that track the coupled dynamics of the excitation and its environment. These approaches connect the emergent quasiparticle spectrum to the microscopic buildup of its dressing cloud, enabling controlled studies of how the spatial range of coupling shapes polaron formation and mobility.
