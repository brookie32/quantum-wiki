arXiv:2610.12098v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Protecting quantum information from noise is essential for reliable quantum computation and quantum memories. Quantum state purification addresses this challenge by processing multiple noisy copies of an unknown quantum state to produce an output state of higher fidelity. Here we establish nearly tight upper and lower bounds on the non-Clifford resources required to implement the symmetric-projection purification protocol. For n copies of a d-dimensional system, we construct a Clifford+T circuit that block-encodes the symmetric projector with operator-norm error at most epsilon using widetilde{O}(n) T gates, where the tilde-O notation suppresses polylogarithmic factors in n, d, and 1/epsilon. Conversely, using stabilizer nullity, we prove that any Clifford+T implementation of such a block encoding with error epsilon<frac{1}{2}sqrt{1-1/n} requires at least 2n-2 T gates, even allowing arbitrary positive normalization and clean stabilizer ancillas. Taken together, these results establish the nearly optimal scaling of non-Clifford cost with the number of copies and uncover the fundamental resource requirements for coherent symmetry projection in quantum state purification.
