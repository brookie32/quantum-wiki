arXiv:2611.08913v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: In cyclic three-level systems, phase-sensitive interference can convert a coupling-sign reversal into a population contrast, providing a controllable analogue of handedness. We emulate this mechanism in a superconducting transmon qutrit using two resonant single-photon drives and a phase-controlled effective two-photon drive. Effective loop phases (Phi_L=+pi/2) and (Phi_R=-pi/2), separated by (pi), reverse the auxiliary contribution to the dark--bright coupling, canceling the intrinsic nonadiabatic term for (L) and reinforcing it for (R). Time-resolved trajectories reveal dark-pathway transfer for (L) and bright-manifold participation for (R). Measured late-time averages give a target-state population contrast of 0.929. Loop-phase measurements establish phase selectivity, while pulse-area and common-mode amplitude scans characterize the operating regime and control sensitivity. These results provide direct time- and phase-domain access to sign-selective counterdiabatic interference in a programmable cyclic qutrit.
