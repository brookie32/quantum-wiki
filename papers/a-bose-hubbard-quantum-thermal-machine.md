arXiv:2611.24483v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: This work proposes a quantum thermal machine comprised of interacting ultracold bosons confined in a one-dimensional optical lattice and modeled by the Bose-Hubbard Hamiltonian. We consider an Otto-inspired cycle with strokes that change the interaction strength between bosons, in addition to standard thermalization and compression/expansion strokes. We find that these interaction changes can be manipulated to enhance the performance of the thermal machine, outperforming non-interacting cycles. In particular, when operating as a heat engine, the cycle can achieve its largest work output and near-Carnot efficiency by compressing the medium at strong repulsion into a Mott insulator state. Additionally, we find that decreasing interactions after the compression stroke favors and enhances the engine operation, whereas increasing interactions favors refrigeration cycles. The findings of this work further show that controlling the interactions across quantum phases of matter can be harnessed as a useful thermodynamic resource.
