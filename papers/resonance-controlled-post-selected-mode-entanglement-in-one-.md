arXiv:2611.26960v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We study resonance-controlled post-selected mode entanglement in a one-dimensional quantum-scattering model. Two incoming electrons scatter independently from a symmetric double-barrier structure and the sector with one reflected and one transmitted electron is selected. The idealised delta-function barriers used in earlier work are replaced by exponentially localised cusp barriers, U(x)=U_0exp(-|xmp d/2|/a), for which the single-barrier scattering problem is exactly solvable in terms of modified Bessel functions of imaginary order. We derive the corresponding reflection and transmission amplitudes, verify their current normalisation and validate them independently by direct numerical integration. Two separated cusp barriers are then combined through a Fabry-Perot composition, whose accuracy is quantified by solving the full two-centre Schrodinger equation. In the well-separated regime the approximation reproduces the full resonance positions to high accuracy. The concurrence zeros are controlled by the resonance phase condition elta(kappa)+kappa d=npi, with a continuously unwrapped single-barrier reflection phase elta. We also calculate the post-selection probability P_{RT}, showing explicitly that large conditional concurrence can occur in a low-probability sector. Systematic scans in barrier width, integrated strength and reference momentum show that finite width can shift off-resonant concurrence maxima and, depending on the reference momentum, reduce their attainable height. The results identify resonance-controlled conditional entanglement as a robust feature of the well-separated double-barrier geometry rather than an artefact of the zero-range idealisation.
