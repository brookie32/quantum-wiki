A helicene-based molecular machine was fabricated by suspending a chiral helix between two metallic electrodes. An applied voltage induces torque‑driven rotation of the molecule with a 100 % directional preference that can be reversed by changing the bias polarity; above a voltage threshold the steady‑state current is markedly enhanced, and this onset can be tuned by mechanically stretching or compressing the helix. These rotation‑induced conductance enhancements differ from earlier helical junctions due to distinct structural and operational parameters.
