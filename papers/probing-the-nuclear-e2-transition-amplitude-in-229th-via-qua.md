arXiv:2611.24136v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The nuclear E2 transition amplitude of ^{229}Th probes nuclear deformation and configuration mixing, yet remains poorly constrained by measurements dominated by M1 radiation. We propose to determine its magnitude and sign relative to the nuclear M1 transition amplitude through quantum interference in ^{229}Th^{3+}. Two coherent laser fields couple a superposition of initial hyperfine states to a common nuclear isomeric state through the electronic shell. Varying the mixing angle controls interference between the excitation pathways, allowing the signed E2/M1 transition amplitude ratio to be extracted using the calibrated laser-field amplitude ratio, without absolute intensity calibration. An independent M1 reference then determines the E2 magnitude. Dynamical calculations support the feasibility of the scheme, predicting appreciable nuclear excitation with milliwatt laser power on millisecond timescales. We evaluate the extraction sensitivity to experimental and atomic-structure uncertainties. The approach extends to other ^{229}Th ions, whose rich level structures offer a broader selection of transitions and promising routes to suppress systematic uncertainties.
