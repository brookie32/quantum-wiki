arXiv:2610.35467v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: We study the entanglement between an electron and the photon it scatters, resolved in real space and time. Starting from nonrelativistic QED in Fermi's oscillator representation, we write Fock's coupled equations for components with fixed photon number in the photon configuration space, in the length gauge, where the photon is created around the atom through a nonlocal kernel. Truncated at one photon, the equations are solved numerically without the rotating-wave approximation for a one-dimensional model atom in a waveguide with a single transverse mode and enhanced coupling. The atom is driven by a short, off-resonant laser pulse, with the electron and photon represented on real-space grids. The global electron-photon entanglement is largely transient. It peaks during the pulse and decreases as the polarized electron cloud returns to the core, leaving a state close to a product of the electron state and a superposition of the field vacuum with a single scattered photon. Local entanglement, obtained from walkers sampled across both photon-number components, exhibits opposite spatial patterns for the two particles. The electron entropy is smallest at the nucleus and maps the local strength of the dipole coupling, whereas the photon entropy is largest near the atom, where photons are created and absorbed, and smaller in the outgoing wave packets.
