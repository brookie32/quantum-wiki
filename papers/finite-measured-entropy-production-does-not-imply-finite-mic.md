arXiv:2611.13110v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Coarse-grained measurements provide a lower bound on entropy production, but how much microscopic irreversibility can they conceal? We construct a driven quantum system in which an imperfect counter registers a finite, strictly positive arrow of time while the microscopic entropy production is infinite. The system is a degenerate parametric oscillator below threshold, coupled to a thermal reservoir through repeated interactions with bosonic ancillas. The oscillator sustains a finite, positive heat current into the vacuum bath, where absorption is forbidden and forward and reverse trajectory laws have different supports. A photon counter with finite collection efficiency, gated time bins, and independent dark counts defines a common coarse-graining of the aligned trajectory laws. For fixed observation duration mathcal T and detector parameters, we prove that the observed relative entropy remains uniformly bounded as the bath temperature T_b vanishes and is strictly positive at vacuum. The hidden contribution obeys Sigma_{hid}(mathcal T,T_b)=mathcal Tot Q_0/(k_BT_b)+O(1), where ot Q_0>0 is the stationary vacuum heat current. False positives conceal the support mismatch while preserving measurable time asymmetry. Thus, finite and informative measurements of entropy production can coexist with infinite microscopic irreversibility.
