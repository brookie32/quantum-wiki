The authors show that a spatially varying imaginary scalar potential can generate a non‑Hermitian skin effect whose spectrum and eigenstate distribution are unchanged by the choice of boundary conditions—an anomaly not predicted by existing theories. They formulate a unified framework that classifies skin phenomena into two types: electric (driven by imaginary scalars) and magnetic (driven by imaginary vectors), and predicts a phase transition between them in which all eigenstates become delocalized. This theory provides a concrete method for engineering and controlling skin modes in systems such as photonic crystals and cold‑atom setups.
