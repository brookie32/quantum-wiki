arXiv:2504.02808v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Complex numbers are central to the standard formulation of quantum theory, but whether they are fundamentally necessary remains unsettled. Familiar real formulations reproduce quantum predictions but fail to be representation-local: local physical operations need not correspond to local entities in the mathematical representation. Here we show that representation-local formulations based solely on real numbers nevertheless exist. Our construction is based on the observation that not every structural feature of finite-dimensional density-matrix quantum theory over the complex numbers need be retained in a real formulation. Specifically, we reconsider two features inherited from the complex formalism: the use of the Kronecker product as the system-combination rule and the representation of states and effects by positive semidefinite matrices. Relaxing only one of these assumptions at a time, we first obtain a representation-local real quantum theory with a modified combination rule that closely parallels the conventional complex formulation. We then show that even with the usual Kronecker-product rule a representation-local real formulation exists, provided states and effects are not required to be positive.
