arXiv:2605.03600v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Nonstabilizerness plays an essential role in the efficient simulation of quantum systems on quantum computers. In this work, we investigate its role in the context of quantum batteries (QBs). To this end, we consider a system of N spin-1/2 particles, with the left half serving as the charger and the right half as the battery. By studying different classes of interactions between the charger and the battery, we quantify the amount of nonstabilizerness required to store work in the QB. Our results reveal a strong correlation between the ergotropy stored in the battery and the total nonstabilizerness of the composite system whenever the Hamiltonian preserves a U(1) symmetry. In contrast, this correspondence is generally lost for more generic interactions. A similar behavior is observed for unitary circuit dynamics. Finally, we examine the complementary scenario in which the battery is initialized in a nonstabilizer state and subsequently charged through Clifford evolution. In this case, we find that the maximum average charging power is nonmonotonic in the initial nonstabilizerness. Remarkably, the highest average power can be achieved even when the initial state carries no magic (nonstabilizerness), demonstrating that initial magic is not a necessary ingredient for generating optimal charging power.
