arXiv:2605.21457v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Standard quantum inference converts quantum data into classical outputs. We study an alternative inference setting in which the desired output is quantum. Such settings include quantum purity amplification (QPA), random purification and approximate cloning, and density matrix exponentiation. We show that such protocols can achieve exponentially lower sample complexity than incoherent, measurement-mediated protocols, establishing, to the best of our knowledge, the first nontrivial separations between dimension-independent coherent and dimension-dependent incoherent sample complexity. For QPA with principal-eigenstate targets and d-dimensional inputs, at fixed output number and spectral gap, coherent processing achieves error arepsilon using O(1/arepsilon) copies, whereas any incoherent protocol requires Omega(d/arepsilon) copies. Together, these separations motivate a unified picture of coherent quantum inference, in which an entanglement-breaking limit identifies the optimal incoherent counterpart.
