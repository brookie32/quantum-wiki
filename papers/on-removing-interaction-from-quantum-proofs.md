arXiv:2609.38387v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: An important open question in quantum cryptography is the construction of publicly-verifiable NIZKs for QMA. Classically, one can construct NIZKs for NP in the random oracle model (and sometimes in the standard model) by compiling an honest-verifier ZK (HVZK) Sigma-protocol for NP using the Fiat-Shamir transformation. Broadbent and Grilo introduced a quantum analog of a Sigma-protocol (which they call a Xi-protocol) in which the prover's first message is quantum, and show that HVZK Xi-protocols exist for QMA. However, it is not clear how to compile such protocols into NIZKs in the (Q)ROM, because the Fiat-Shamir transformation seems to be incompatible with quantum messages. In this work we give formal evidence that this is indeed the case: we show that if generic "Fiat-Shamir-like" compilers for quantum protocols exist in the QROM (with small completeness and soundness error) then QMA = BQP.
