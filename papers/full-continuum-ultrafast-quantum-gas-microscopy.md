arXiv:2610.18994v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Quantum gas microscopy provides single-shot access to many-body configurations, but their microscopic reconstruction has so far largely relied on a predefined discrete spatial basis during fluorescence readout. Here we realize ultrafast atom-resolved fluorescence microscopy of an ultracold ^{162}Dy gas directly in continuous space, with all external confinement removed during imaging. Exploiting the large mass and broad optical transition of dysprosium together with a high numerical aperture in-vacuum objective, we detect single atoms within 4,mus while maintaining an effective point-spread function with a full width at half maximum of 254.8(3),nm, and an isolated single-atom classification fidelity of 99.72(3)%. We use the measured single-atom response to construct a statistical configuration-inference model that recovers atomic positions and multiplicities, coming from overlapping atoms, directly from single fluorescence snapshots. Benchmarking our model on Monte-Carlo generated images shows a total reconstruction fidelity of 98.7%. Our approach extends quantum gas microscopy beyond predefined detection lattices, enabling atom-resolved measurements of counting statistics and higher-order spatial correlations directly on the intrinsic length scales of continuum many-body systems.
