arXiv:2608.24493v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The guided Hamiltonian problem is the following: given access to the unitary U=e^{i H} for some Hamiltonian H, and given access to a unitary that prepares a guiding state promised to have overlap at least gamma >0 with the ground space of H, estimate the ground-state energy of H within additive error elta > 0 and success probability at least 1-arepsilon , arepsilon>0. How many applications of U and its inverse U^{-1} are necessary and sufficient? An upper bound O(log(1/arepsilon)log(1/gamma)/gammaelta) was known, and was improved to O(log(1/arepsilon)/gammaelta) very recently [JW26]. A matching lower bound was known whenever one of the three parameters elta,gamma,arepsilon was held constant [MdW26]. In this paper we prove the joint lower bound Omega(log(1/arepsilon)/gammaelta) with the tight arepsilon-dependence provided the dimension of H is at least log(1/arepsilon)/gamma^2. Furthermore, we show that this same lower bound (with slightly larger dimension) holds for both the special case in which the ground state is guaranteed to be unique and H has a gap of elta between its first and second eigenvalue; and for ground-state preparation, where elta denotes the spectral gap and arepsilon now is the approximation error. The lower bounds also apply when the Hamiltonian can be accessed via its block-encoding, and when fractional powers of U are allowed, as in continuous-time Hamiltonian simulation. Lastly, improved upper bounds are known when H is nonnegative and presented as a sum of squares; and our results imply the lower bound Omega(log(1/arepsilon)/gammasqrt{elta}) for this case.
