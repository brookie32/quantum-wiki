arXiv:2611.13553v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We investigate the quantum localization of two interacting particles moving in a one-dimensional quasicrystal with unequal hopping amplitudes. Using a two-body Green's function approach, we determine the localization phase diagram of attractively and repulsively bound pairs, showing that increasing the hopping amplitude of one constituent while keeping the other fixed raises the critical quasiperiodic strength, promoting pair delocalization. The localization threshold nevertheless remains bounded above by the Aubry-Andre critical point of the slower constituent. Effective analytical descriptions capture pair localization in the limits of strong hopping imbalance and strong interactions. The dynamics of an initially on-site pair provides signatures of delocalization induced by unequal hopping amplitudes. In particular, the effective spreading exponent of the root-mean-square width of a pair distribution reveals a progression from localized to subdiffusive and eventually superdiffusive behavior as the hopping ratio increases. Our results establish unequal hopping amplitudes as a means of tuning the transport properties of interacting pairs in quasiperiodic lattices, with direct relevance to ultracold atoms in optical lattices.
