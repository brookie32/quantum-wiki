arXiv:2611.33979v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Mueller dipole branching at small x is a stochastic process in which splitting rates depend on transverse geometry. We construct a coherent quantum circuit for a regulated finite-state version of this process. Poisson-clock uniformization controls the number of updates, while orthogonal event-history registers retain the splitting paths, including those leading to the same final configuration. The circuit prepares transition amplitudes from local splitting rates rather than loading a precomputed evolved distribution. Its statevector multiplicity probabilities reproduce the truncated-clock Markov result to numerical precision and agree with the full continuous-time Markov-chain benchmark within approximately 7imes10^{-4}. To examine fluctuations beyond the small circuit, we independently simulate the Mueller cascade with continuous transverse coordinates. At the largest rapidity studied, its normalized fourth factorial moment is 1.81, compared with 2.84 for an equal-mean Yule process. We also determine multiplicity entropies and correlations between dipole-size sectors. Applying a fixed negative-binomial dipole-to-charged-particle response reproduces the rising trend of the H1 charged-particle multiplicity entropy toward smaller x, but does not fully describe the measured multiplicity distributions. These results demonstrate an explicit coherent encoding of finite-state dipole branching and clarify its relation to continuous-cascade fluctuations and DIS phenomenology.
