arXiv:2611.09450v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Symmetry breaking in mixed quantum states exhibits a rich strong-to-weak hierarchy, yet the use of symmetry as an operational probe of dissipative criticality remains largely unexplored. In this work, we develop a symmetry-based framework that connects semiclassical phase space, trajectory-level dynamics, and ensemble responses, and demonstrate it in the two-dimensional dissipative XYZ model. We show that symmetry breaking manifests semiclassically as a bifurcation of the Husimi-Q distribution and dynamically as a sharp change in a trajectory-level symmetry residual. At the ensemble level, the weak-symmetry sensitivity to a perturbed symmetry operator exhibits pronounced finite-size scaling, yielding an estimate of the dissipative critical point. Finally, we propose a trajectory-based quantum-circuit algorithm in which mid-circuit measurements condition quantum jumps and allow the proposed indicators to be extracted from circuit observables. Our work provides a symmetry-centered route to diagnosing dissipative phase transitions and critical scaling in open quantum many-body systems.
