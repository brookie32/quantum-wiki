arXiv:2611.18552v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Probing real-time dynamics is essential for understanding non-perturbative scattering and interactions in quantum field theory. A common challenge in real-time simulation is preparing accurate asymptotic incoming states. Through efficiently solvable eigenvalue problems, we jointly optimize a superposition of single particle momentum eigenstates and a single site interpolating operator to maximize preparation fidelity from the interacting vacuum. This leads to a localized excitation in the interacting theory analogous to a position eigenstate. Weighted sums of the resulting interpolating operator over lattice sites prepare wave packets of arbitrary spatial extent. To ensure that this construction produces the correct continuum limit, relative phases among single particle momentum eigenstates in this superposition are calibrated using a relation derived from Lorentz symmetry. Analyzing single particle wave packet propagation through the position space profile reveals oscillatory Airy distortions, which originate from interference between saddle point contributions at different momenta that share the same lattice group velocity. These oscillations systematically weaken when approaching the continuum limit, and reliable regimes are identified as a function of simulation time and wave packet momentum profile. Two-particle scattering simulations in interacting scalar field theory are also implemented, revealing artificial inelasticity arising from coarse local field digitization or deviation from the Nyquist-Shannon sampling point. With these artifacts under control, present simulations provide emerging evidence for physical inelastic processes in phi^4 theory. The constructions introduced in this work may provide new understanding of state preparation for the quantum simulation of scattering.
