arXiv:2606.25650v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: In a separable harmonic waveguide, transverse confinement leaves the longitudinal bulk Hamiltonian unchanged. Earlier simulations nevertheless found that stronger confinement suppresses prompt detection of a spin-1/2 particle at a spin-coupled absorbing boundary. Here we identify the local boundary response underlying this effect. We treat the boundary as an effective detector, identifying the squared-norm loss rate with the detection-time probability density. The incident transverse ground state has a distribution of momenta parallel to the detecting plane, with characteristic scale proportional to sqrt{omega}, where omega is the confinement frequency. For each parallel momentum, we resolve the boundary relation into two spin channels. At fixed normal wave number and detector parameter, local matching gives these channels equal absorption probabilities but generally different reflection phases. Their common absorption probability decreases with the magnitude of the parallel momentum. Simulations over several observation windows distinguish reduced detection probability from changes in recorded-click timing. The mean detection time capped at horizon T is summarized by the empirical fit A_T+B_Tsqrt{omega} over the sampled range, with setup- and horizon-dependent coefficients. The local response makes detection sensitive to transverse confinement; propagation, interference, and repeated encounters of the undetected wave shape the detection-time distribution.
