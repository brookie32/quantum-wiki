Measuring intracellular temperature is essential for studying cell biophysics but remains challenging because quantum nanosensors are hampered by shot‑noise limits and poor biocompatibility. Existing options—nanodiamonds with nitrogen–vacancy centers, quantum dots, and fluorescent proteins—suffer from defect variability, nonuniformity, toxicity or insufficient thermometric specificity, limiting their accuracy for precise temperature monitoring inside living cells. Consequently, reliable intracellular thermometry requires materials that are both biologically compatible and possess uniform, highly specific quantum properties.
