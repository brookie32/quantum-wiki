arXiv:2411.16942v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Quantum and classical optical signals sharing a fiber can interact through Kerr cross-phase modulation without interchannel photon transfer, potentially degrading phase- and mode-sensitive quantum communication even when direct spectral leakage is negligible. We develop a two-channel quantum-stochastic propagation framework based on the positive-P representation for a weak coherent pulse train co-propagating with a strong, quadrature-amplitude-modulated classical signal. Both fields are treated quantum mechanically, incorporating self- and cross-phase modulation, chromatic dispersion, group-velocity mismatch, attenuation, and Kerr-induced quantum diffusion. We characterize the classical-channel influence using three complementary observables: the frequency-resolved quadrature-fluctuation spectrum, complex temporal-mode overlap, and adjacent-pulse interference visibility. Simulations of a 100-km standard-fiber link reveal markedly different responses across these observables. As classical launch power increases, the extremal quadrature-fluctuation spectra remain close to the shot-noise level, whereas the temporal-mode and visibility penalties reach approximately 4.5% and 6.0%, respectively, at 100 mW. Applying a 1% mean-penalty criterion gives launch-power thresholds of approximately 46 mW for temporal-mode overlap and 40 mW for visibility, yielding a joint Kerr-limited threshold near 40 mW for the investigated configuration. These results demonstrate that temporal-mode deformation and reduced pulse-to-pulse coherence can occur while the evaluated quadrature-fluctuation spectra remain near shot noise, establishing a complementary criterion for selecting classical launch power in quantum-classical fiber co-propagation.
