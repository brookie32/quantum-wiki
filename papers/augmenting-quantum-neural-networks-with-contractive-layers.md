arXiv:2611.27019v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Classical neural networks combine diverse layers with complementary roles to achieve rich expressive capabilities across complex learning tasks. Rapid advances in quantum science and technology have motivated growing interest in their quantum counterparts, quantum neural networks, raising the question of how their expressivity can be enhanced through the design of new quantum layers. Of particular interest are layers that enrich the accessible function space while introducing only a small number of additional trainable parameters. In this Letter, we introduce the contractive layer as a promising building block for quantum neural networks. This layer comprises all-to-all couplings governed by a single trainable parameter, enabling efficient control of quantum information flow while maintaining experimental relevance to Rydberg atom arrays. Using observable learning as a benchmark, we show that contractive layers enhance expressivity more efficiently than simply increasing the depth of conventional brick-wall circuits. This advantage stems from a systematic increase in the accessible target-space dimension per trainable parameter. We further demonstrate similar improvements in image recognition tasks. These results highlight contractive layers as a practical route to expressive quantum neural networks on near-term quantum devices.
