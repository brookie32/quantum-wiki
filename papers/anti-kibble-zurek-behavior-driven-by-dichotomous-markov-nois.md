arXiv:2611.16237v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: The anti-Kibble-Zurek behavior may arise under noise, adding a noise-induced contribution to the defect density beyond the conventional Kibble-Zurek term. Most investigations of such behavior have so far been restricted to Gaussian noise. For Gaussian colored noise (GCN), the power spectrum dictates a monotonic variation of this term with the correlation time. Here, we focus on dichotomous Markov noise (DMN), a prototypical non-Gaussian colored noise, in the quantum Ising chain. We show that the noise-induced defect density, taken as the response function, exhibits generalized stochastic resonance: it is nonmonotonic in the correlation time, growing in the fast-noise regime and decreasing in the slow-noise regime. This resonance stems from the anomalous AKZ behavior in the slow-noise regime, where the DMN-induced trend is nonmonotonic, in contrast to the monotonic GCN case. The underlying mechanism lies in the sparse realizations of DMN in the slow-noise regime, a feature not captured by the power spectrum. Our work thereby reveals a fundamental distinction between Gaussian and non-Gaussian noises in the nonequilibrium critical quench dynamics of the quantum Ising chain.
