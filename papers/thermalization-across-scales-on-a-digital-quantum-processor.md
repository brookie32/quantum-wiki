arXiv:2603.12236v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Thermalization of isolated quantum many-body systems normally unfolds via dynamical spreading of entanglement across progressively increasing spatial scales. The speed of this dynamical process, however, can be affected by the presence of local disorder. Here we study the crossover between slow and fast thermalization in a disordered two-dimensional Heisenberg Floquet model using digital quantum simulations with up to 100 superconducting qubits. Our experiment measures subsystem purities and Z-basis marginal collision entropies for patches of up to 3imes 3 qubits. We resolve the onset of thermalization across scales by tracking each quantity's approach to its asymptotic value. In regimes where the system is moderately entangled, we validate our calibrated quantum simulations against state-of-the-art classical simulations. Near the crossover to fast thermalization, the classical methods no longer reliably converge due to volume-law growth of entanglement. By exploring this computationally hard regime, our work establishes a frontier of applications for digital quantum computers in probing complex many-body thermalization.
