arXiv:2608.10052v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The crossover between topology and nonequilibrium dynamics has emerged as a rich frontier, in which quantum systems can exhibit unique dynamical phenomena that lie beyond the reach of equilibrium. Of particular interest are quench dynamics across topological phase, as it may reveal the information about the underlying Majorana zero-energy states. Here, we investigate the fate of Majorana boundary modes in a quenched fermionic superfluid using self-consistent time-dependent Bogoliubov-de Gennes theory. For quantum quenches within the topological regime, Majorana boundary modes survive but undergo coherent boundary oscillations arising from the nonadiabatic deformation of their wave functions. Furthermore, a pronounced ripple pattern appears in the post-quench density distribution following a sudden topology-changing quench. Here we identify that these ripple structures originates from the coherent hybridization and interference of the initially separated Majorana boundary states. Our findings establish nonequilibrium boundary dynamics as a new approach for probing Majorana physics, which is complementary to conventional equilibrium measurements.
