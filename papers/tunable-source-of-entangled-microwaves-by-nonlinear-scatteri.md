arXiv:2611.25207v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We propose a route to prepare entangled states of microwaves propagating in spatially separated transmission lines using the intrinsic nonlinear dynamics of ferromagnetic spin waves (magnons). We first show that a driven magnon mode can produce entangled magnon pairs at low temperatures by spontaneous parametric down-conversion enabled by three-magnon processes. The anisotropic magnon properties in thin films result in strict phase-matching conditions which allow only a well-defined pair of magnon modes to be amplified. This mode selectivity allows to out-couple each amplified mode to an independent microwave transmission line, producing non-degenerate propagating microwave entanglement with current experimental parameters. In addition, we prove that the frequency difference between the two entangled microwaves can be dynamically tuned across a few-GHz range by changing the orientation of a bias magnetic field. Our figures of merit - .g., the amount of microwave entanglement - can be largely improved by optimizing material and geometry. Our work establishes that quantum states of propagating magnons can be created by their nonlinear dynamics alone, and opens a pathway toward integrating magnons in hybrid quantum platforms.
