arXiv:2610.13250v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The Zak phase provides a fundamental geometric characterization of one-dimensional band structures and, when quantized by symmetry, serves as a bulk indicator of polarization and boundary phenomena. Conventionally, its exact quantization is tied to a global symmetry of the Bloch Hamiltonian, such as inversion or reflection symmetry. Here, we demonstrate a distinct mechanism by which the Zak phase remains exactly quantized even when the corresponding protecting symmetry is explicitly broken at the level of the full Hamiltonian. The mechanism is based on local support symmetry and destructive interference: symmetry-breaking degrees of freedom are coupled to a topological parent system in such a way that the relevant Bloch states cannot leak into them. The preserved states therefore retain their parent wave functions and inherit their quantized Zak phase exactly, despite the absence of the protecting global symmetry. We establish this mechanism for general one-dimensional insulators and demonstrate it in an inversion-broken minimal model and a reflection-broken armchair nanoribbon, where a quantized pi Zak phase and associated boundary-localized states persist. We further show that perturbations violating the exact destructive-interference condition continuously dequantize the Zak phase, while a finite regime with a near-pi Zak phase and boundary response can survive. Our results establish wave-function support, rather than global symmetry alone, as a mechanism for protecting quantized quantum geometry in one-dimensional systems.
