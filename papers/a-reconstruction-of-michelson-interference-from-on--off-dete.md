arXiv:2610.19569v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: This paper reconstructs the Michelson interference phenomenon based solely on the binary (on-off) statistics of a single-photon counter and macroscopic path-difference data, without the a priori assumptions of complex wave superposition or prior spectral information. In contrast to the conventional approach that assumes light as a continuous complex wave function and considers interference between them, this study focuses on the algebraic properties inherent in discrete detection statistics. We verify that the discrete response data collected across various path differences form a positive semi-definite matrix, and apply Herglotz's theorem to this result. Consequently, we show that the harmonic form of the interference fringe is not a consequence of adopting a wave model as an axiom, but rather it is uniquely decomposed into a cosine average of wavenumbers dictated by testable algebraic constraints satisfied by the statistical data. Through this approach, the logical basis of the interference phenomenon can be described by the characteristics of the binary data itself, perfectly aligning with the modern optical experimental environment that relies entirely on discrete event statistics.
