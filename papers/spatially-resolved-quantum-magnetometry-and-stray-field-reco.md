arXiv:2609.21630v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Transferable hexagonal boron nitride (hBN) hosting negatively charged boron-vacancy (VB^{-}) spin defects offers a versatile platform for integrated quantum magnetometry, yet quantitative imaging of magnetic microstructures remains challenging. Here, we integrate a transferred hBN flake with a 4 mum-diameter permalloy (Py = Ni{81}Fe{19}) microdisk and perform spatially resolved optically detected magnetic resonance measurements at room temperature. An applied in-plane magnetic field distorts the vortex-state magnetization, generating edge-localized magnetic surface charges and pronounced stray-field signatures at opposite disk edges. By referencing each pixel to its local zero-field splitting and correcting for a residual out-of-plane bias field, we quantitatively reconstruct the out-of-plane stray-field distribution, revealing peak fields of approximately 11.2 mT. An edge-charge model reproduces the spatial distribution and amplitude of the reconstructed field, linking the ODMR response to the field-driven evolution of the vortex state. These results establish transferred hBN VB^{-} sensors for quantitative magnetometry of magnetic microstructures.
