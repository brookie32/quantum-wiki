arXiv:2611.27422v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Sample-driven cross-reactivity remains a fundamental bottleneck for sensitive and selective biosensing in complex biological environments, particularly in microfluidic systems where high surface-to-volumes ratios amplify non-specific interactions. Here, we address this challenge with a one-step, biomimetic surface functionalization strategy based on engineered lipid-based nanocarriers that self-assemble within minutes into immunoassay-ready, antifouling interfaces that couple pegylated-lipid passivation with biorthogonal, site-specific probe conjugation. This approach enables rapid, tuneable, and reproducible surface functionalization, which suppresses sample-specific cross-reactivity even in undiluted serum, and supports direct, label-free digital biosensing assays directly in native biological environments. When integrated into an optofluidic platform combining digital in-line holography and microfluidics, this approach allows time-resolved quantification of tetraspanin-positive extracellular vesicle secretion dynamics from two-dimensional and three-dimensional lung cancer cell models using 50 {mu}L of sample within 10 min, without isolation or purification. Because the presented surface functionalization proceeds via self-assembly rather than covalent surface modification, this one-step modular-approach is intrinsically decoupled from device fabrication and applies to both glass and PDMS, coating the entire microfluidic channel, thereby establishing a general route for integrating biomimetic nanostructures into robust, high-performance immunoassays, advancing biosensing beyond end-point measurements, and towards rapid and continuous real-time monitoring.
