arXiv:2611.13871v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Cosmological particle production, the excitation of field quanta by a dynamical spacetime, underlies the primordial perturbations observed in the cosmic microwave background and has been realized in analog quantum simulators with ultra-cold atoms. We ask the inverse question: given the excitation spectrum, can the cosmological history that produced it be reconstructed, and is that reconstruction unique? We approach this problem through a mapping of particle production to one-dimensional quantum scattering, in which the expansion history plays the role of a scattering landscape and the produced spectrum the role of reflected and transmitted waves. This mapping reveals a rich interplay between scattering theory and cosmology. The scale factor enters as a zero-energy resonance, an exceptional case of scattering theory, while cosmic singularities correspond to bound states, so that regular cosmologies are governed entirely by the scattering continuum. We find an interesting isospectrality: histories related by an inversion of the scale factor (expansion contraction) yield identical particle numbers, differing only by a pi-shift in phase. Given both the particle number (or alternatively the amplitude) and phase of the cosmological power spectrum, the reconstruction is unique for regular, asymptotically static cosmologies. We demonstrate the reconstruction both analytically, for exactly solvable scattering landscapes, and numerically, recovering common logistic expansion histories to better than 0.02%. We further quantify its robustness to the momentum-space resolution and measurement uncertainties characteristic of analog quantum simulation. This work establishes a direct link between the observable power spectrum and the cosmological history that produced it, offering a new perspective on the classification of early-universe scenarios and the construction of alternatives to inflation.
