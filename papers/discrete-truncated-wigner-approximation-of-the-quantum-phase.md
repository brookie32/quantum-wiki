arXiv:2611.34004v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The discrete truncated Wigner approximation (DTWA) has been a workhorse for large-scale spin dynamics, but whether it captures dynamics across a quantum phase transition has not been tested systematically. In this work, we use the transverse-field Ising model (TFIM) to study the quantum phase transition with DTWA from one to three dimensions and with all-to-all connectivity, using slow linear ramps and fast ramps. We find that DTWA cannot capture the dynamics on the one-dimensional chain, and that its behavior is consistent with classical thermalization, in which its classical spins relax toward a thermal state at a finite effective temperature that does not vanish for slow ramps, while a one-dimensional chain cannot order at any finite temperature. For square and cubic lattices, in contrast to the chain, DTWA builds order, and the order sets in close to the quantum critical point found by quantum Monte Carlo (QMC). However, its correlations do not grow with the Kibble-Zurek exponents, so DTWA captures the onset of order but not the universal dynamics of the transition. For all-to-all coupling, DTWA follows the exact growth of the order parameter along the entire ramp. Our results suggest that DTWA becomes more reliable as each spin interacts with more partners, and that beyond one dimension it can indicate where a quantum phase transition lies but cannot capture its critical scaling.
