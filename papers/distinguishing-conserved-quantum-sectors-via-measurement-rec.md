arXiv:2611.24029v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Conservation laws partition the Hilbert space of a quantum system into invariant sectors that are often difficult to identify directly. Repeated measurements on an ancillary probe, however, generate a measurement record that provides information about the occupied sector. Here we develop a framework for identifying conserved sectors using a (K+1)-dimensional feature vector of record statistics, comprising the average outcome frequency and pair-outcome correlations up to K measurement cycles apart. For fixed K, the feature vector can be evaluated at a cost linear in record length. In quantum non-demolition measurement models, the average outcome frequency suffices for accurate sector identification. For an interacting spin bath with nontrivial dynamics within each sector, incorporating temporal correlations between successive and more widely separated measurements into the record feature vector substantially improves classification fidelity. Our framework provides an efficient approach to conserved-sector identification and demonstrates the importance of temporal correlations in distinguishing the underlying sector dynamics.
