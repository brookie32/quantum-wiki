arXiv:2611.13465v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We study the time-dependent physical spectrum of light for a two-level atom coupled to a large, but discrete, number of modes of an electromagnetic cavity. Radiation emitted by the atom, placed at the center of the cavity, returns after the round-trip time t_r, re-excites it, and is re-emitted, producing a sequence of atomic population revivals. For a coupling approximately flat over the atomic linewidth, the emission dynamics reduces to a delay-differential equation of Bixon--Jortner type. Using its analytical solution in terms of Laguerre polynomials, we investigate how successive emission and reabsorption cycles shape the time-dependent spectrum. The physical spectrum exhibits revivals during which a transient frequency comb of spacing 2pi/t_r develops. The comb results from the quantum interference between successive emission stages; its teeth sit not at the cavity modes but at the resonances of the effective two half-cavities created by the resonantly reflecting atom. The visibility scales as e^{-Gamma t_r} with the filter bandwidth Gamma, and at long times the comb converges to the dressed-mode spectrum, which we also obtain analytically in quantitative agreement with exact diagonalization. We show that the physical spectrum resolves the feedback phase, to which the atomic population is insensitive in the regime of well-separated emission stages, and provides additional frequency-resolved signatures of the atomic position. We discuss two feasible experimental implementations of our results with transmons (artificial atoms) coupled to microwave and surface-acoustic-wave resonators.
