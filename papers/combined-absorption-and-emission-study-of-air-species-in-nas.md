arXiv:2610.19346v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: A suite of tunable diode laser absorption spectroscopy (TDLAS) diagnostics targeting rovibrational states of nitric oxide (NO) in the mid-infrared (MIR) and electronic states of atomic oxygen (O) and nitrogen (N) in the near-IR (NIR) was combined with optical emission spectroscopy (OES) for overlapping wavelengths at the NASA Electric Arc Shock Tube (EAST) facility for three selected high-temperature air experiments. Absorption features were probed at rates up to 1 MHz to provide quantitative, time-resolved measurements of temperature and species number density profiles behind incident shocks, enabling comparisons to spatially-resolved emission measurements, with the two typically agreeing within their respective uncertainties. Inferred time-histories are further compared to incident shock simulations from two one-dimensional models - one leveraging the Park two-temperature rates and the other more recent rate values - as well as the Data Parallel Line Relaxation (DPLR) code. For the two low-velocity cases (3.81 and 3.98 km/s) in which NO is probed, inferred temperatures are typically well-captured by all three models, although the NO number densities show stronger variation, agreeing better for the model with updated NO formation and dissociation rates. Finally, one high-velocity case (7.03 km/s) demonstrates the simultaneous resolution of three O-atom and six N-atom electronic states, with all states showing a steady increase in number density over time - possibly due to shock trajectory effects. Together, this combined absorption and emission study demonstrates resolution of many states of nonequilibrium air species, while directly comparing ground- and excited-state populations via the combined diagnostic strategy.
