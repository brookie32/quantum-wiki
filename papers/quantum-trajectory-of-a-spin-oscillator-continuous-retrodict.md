arXiv:2611.08104v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The Heisenberg uncertainty relation in quantum mechanics limits the precision of predicting the outcome of measurements of any non-commuting observables. However, this restriction does not apply to retrodictive guessing of the outcomes of past measurements. Here, we experimentally demonstrate this using a spin oscillator consisting of 5.17 imes 10^{10} rubidium atoms. Through weak continuous measurements, we monitor canonical spin observables hat{x}_{A} and hat{p}_{A} with [hat{x}_{A},hat{p}_{A}] = ihbar. By combining information obtained before and after time t, we retrodict the outcomes of measurements of hat{x}_{A} and hat{p}_{A} at time t with variances that remain, concurrently over 1.4 hours, sim 1.01 dB below the bound set by the Heisenberg uncertainty relation. We also infer and demonstrate the ability to retrodict, with the same precision, the measurement outcomes for arbitrary quadratures of the spin oscillator. This brings interesting prospects for further research on quantum measurements, and for quantum-enhanced sensing that combines trajectory and entanglement strategies.
