Spatially defined Rabi spectroscopy is introduced to enable continuous, dead‑time‑free interrogation of optical lattice clocks. Ultracold ⁸₈Sr atoms are transported at 16 mm s⁻¹ through a 12‑mm interaction region in a moving optical lattice; a transverse magnetic mixing field localises the clock excitation, preserving Lamb–Dicke confinement and suppressing Doppler broadening. The resulting 1.2‑Hz wide spectrum—close to the transit‑time Fourier limit—demonstrates that continuous atom delivery can decouple interrogation from preparation and detection, thereby mitigating the Dick effect and improving clock stability.
