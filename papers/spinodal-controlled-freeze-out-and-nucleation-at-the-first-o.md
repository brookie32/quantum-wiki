arXiv:2610.12511v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Dipolar quantum gases in elongated traps become supersolid through a transition that is continuous at intermediate density but first order at low and high density; at the continuous point, ramps of the scattering length follow Kibble--Zurek scaling. We study ramps across the first-order superfluid--supersolid transition of ^{164}Dy in an infinite tube using the three-dimensional extended Gross--Pitaevskii equation with truncated-Wigner noise. With quantum noise only, the uniform superfluid survives the metastable window and transforms after its spinodal, where the roton softens. For onsets occurring during the linear ramp, the delay measured from this spinodal has a fitted exponent zeta = 0.36 pm 0.02, close to the continuous-transition result and the mean-field value 1/3. The quoted uncertainty combines statistical and systematic sensitivities. Reverse ramps show a comparable delay beyond the fold of the crystal branch, producing dynamical hysteresis. Initial thermal noise with nominal temperatures of 2.5--10 nK produces crystalline regions inside the metastable window during holds, with faster nucleation closer to the spinodal and at higher initial temperature. Without a heat bath these effective rates depend on preparation: ramps crossing the window at the same rate nucleate early in 2 of 32 or 11 of 16 realizations depending on their starting point. A crossover time inferred from hold rates is therefore an estimate, not a measured boundary for the ramp protocol. At low density, slower ramps select larger spacings in a crystal of separated droplets, and droplets survive far above the transition on reverse ramps.
