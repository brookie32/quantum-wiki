arXiv:2611.22546v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The spectrum of artificial atoms coupled to resonators in the ultrastrong coupling regime have been analyzed using the quantum Rabi model over a wide range of coupling strengths. However, the accuracy of models obtained by truncating multilevel artificial atoms to two levels, as well as the correspondence between effective parameters extracted from spectral fitting and the bare parameters of the circuit Hamiltonian, depends on the multilevel structure of the system. In this study, I analyze the spectrum of an ultrastrongly coupled circuit consisting of a multilevel superconducting artificial atom and a resonator using a circuit Hamiltonian, and investigate the effects of truncating higher atomic levels on the low-energy spectrum. Based on Lowdin partitioning, I evaluate the contributions of virtual transitions through higher atomic levels and show that diagonal energy corrections reduce the errors arising from simple two-level truncation over a wide parameter range. I also show that there are parameter regimes in which readjustment of the effective Rabi parameters successfully reproduces the multilevel spectrum, while systematic deviations remain in other regimes. These results demonstrate the necessity of separately evaluating two-level truncation, perturbative corrections, and effective-parameter fitting in ultrastrongly coupled systems, and provide guidance for quantitative spectral analysis of multilevel quantum circuits.
