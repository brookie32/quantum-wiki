arXiv:2607.24474v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: A system property is considered to be elementary, when any further refined specification is impossible. Any two elementary properties of a classical system mutually exclude each other. However, this does not hold anymore for a quantum system, where mutual exclusivity becomes a special relation. This orthogonality relation becomes our first axiom. It is sufficient to define states in an abstract setting and we then introduce two simple, but far-reaching further basic axioms for the states, which mimic the quantum mechanical correspondence between the pure states and the rank-one projections. All examples that we can identify origin from operator algebras or certain Jordan algebras and require the Gleason theorem. The identification of further cases is an unresolved challenge. If some exist, they might result in promising new mathematical models for an extended quantum theory. The non-existence would mean that our extremely basic postulates bring us unexpectedly close to common quantum theory. In any case, the distinction between inequality and mutual exclusivity of elementary system properties appears to be a major origin of quantumness. Our basic approach does not encompass the non-atomic von Neumann algebras of type II and III, which play a crucial role in quantum field theory, and we present a modification of the axioms to include them.
