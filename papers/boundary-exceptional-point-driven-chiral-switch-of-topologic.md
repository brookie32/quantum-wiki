arXiv:2610.29316v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We theoretically investigate the interplay of topology and localized non-Hermiticity in a finite Su-Schrieffer-Heeger chain characterized by non-Hermitian potentials at its boundaries. By tuning the boundary gain and loss parameters, we exploit the finite-size hybridization of two mid-gap topological edge states to analytically derive the exact coordinates of boundary-induced exceptional points (EPs), Gamma_{rm EP}=pm i v^{N}/w^{N-1}, at which these states undergo coalescence. We demonstrate that dynamically encircling these boundary EPs facilitates a robust, chiral edge state-transfer protocol. Depending on the orientation of the encircling loop, this protocol allows a topological edge state localized at one boundary to be either transferred to the opposite edge with perfect fidelity or returned to its starting position through a double switching process. Importantly, since the non-Hermiticity remains strictly confined to the boundaries, this chiral transport occurs without affecting the energy spectrum of the bulk bands. These results establish a minimal framework for a large-scale topological chiral switch that is experimentally verifiable and capable of being controlled through boundary non-Hermitian driving.
