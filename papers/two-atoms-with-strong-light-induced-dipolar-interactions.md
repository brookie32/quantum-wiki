arXiv:2611.14082v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: We study how two atoms scatter light when they are closer than an optical wavelength and observe multiple new phenomena due to the strong interactions between laser-induced electric dipole moments. We identify a narrow range of densities where a fraction of the scattered light is coherent and shows a collective enhancement factor of two compared to independent atoms. The dynamics of the two-atom wavefunction can be observed through momentum distributions which reveal resonant ejection and depletion. Due to the tight confinement, the total fluorescence intensity strongly depends on the scattering length, since it originates from a narrow resonant (Franck-Condon) region. Furthermore, we measure how the probe laser intensity modifies the scattering length, which establishes a new way to shift Feshbach resonances. Our work provides microscopic insight into what occurs during photoassociation and parity projection in optical tweezers.
