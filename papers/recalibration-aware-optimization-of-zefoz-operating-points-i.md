arXiv:2611.08396v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Erbium-doped solids like ^{167}Er^{3+}:Y_2SiO_5 have properties that make them suitable for quantum-network memories, however, their unquenched effective electronic spin causes rapid magnetic decoherence. Operating at Zero First-Order Zeeman (ZEFOZ) points allows us to mitigate this, where we evaluate the ZEFOZ landscape for Site-2 ^{167}Er^{3+} by incorporating a full-Hessian spin-Hamiltonian treatment with the anisotropic magnetic-field covariance derived from the native ^{89}Y crystal lattice directly. Operating-point screening criteria were adopted to devise a Pareto-optimal frontier of four transition pairs to balance modeled Y-only magnetic pure-dephasing lifetimes (T_{phi,Y}^{(2)}) against microwave control authority. The epsilon_{14}leftrightarrowepsilon_{15} transition exhibits the longest modeled Y-bath-induced quadratic pure-dephasing time, T_{phi,Y}^{(2)}>2.2imes10^5~s, while the epsilon_{12}leftrightarrowepsilon_{13} transition stood out, achieving a Rabi frequency coefficient of 1.248 imes 10^5~Hz/mT. An assumption-based Hamiltonian-uncertainty analysis demonstrates that while uncompensated deviations shift the ZEFOZ condition away from its nominal magnetic-field coordinate, the shifted operating points can be recovered via numerical vector-field recalibration. The Pareto-optimal coordinates were mathematically recovered for all sampled Hamiltonian perturbations, and the 84.1^{st}-percentile field relocations ranged from 10.95 to 27.97~mT. The intended operation near 10~mK must be taken into account for the suppression of Er-induced spectral diffusion through electronic-spin polarization, while the extended Y-bath pure-dephasing timescales also remain conditional on the adopted temporal-correlation model.
