arXiv:2611.33274v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Integrable systems can exhibit diffusive or Kardar--Parisi--Zhang (KPZ) scaling in two-point correlations even when their current full counting statistics (FCS) differ from those usually associated with these universality classes. We explain this separation using a multispecies particle gas that preserves the scattering structure of integrable spin chains. For anisotropy Delta>1, large particles are advected by a mobile bath. The resulting FCS has a Gaussian-mixture form and approaches the M-Wright distribution at late times, with finite-time rounding due to mobile species. At the isotropic point Delta=1, all particles are mobile, and the charge transfer by large particles is discrete. A self-consistent Poisson distribution describes the FCS, with a nearly Gaussian bulk and model-dependent, non-Gaussian tails. By contrast, two-point correlations follow from the statistics of a large tracer particle. For Delta>1, it undergoes Brownian motion, yielding Gaussian spreading. At Delta=1, we provide evidence from fluctuating hydrodynamics for the emergence of the KPZ Prahofer--Spohn scaling function in dynamical correlations and trace it to the finite coupling of the largest particles to a continuum of slower ones, with long-ranged velocity fluctuations. Our framework shows how universal dynamical two-point correlations coexist with distinct model-dependent current statistics.
