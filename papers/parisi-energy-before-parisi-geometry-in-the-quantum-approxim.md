arXiv:2611.23645v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The fractured low-energy landscape of spin glasses presents a notorious barrier to optimization. While high-depth calculations indicate that the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) can approach the Parisi ground-state energy of the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick spin glass, how it does so remains elusive. Here, we treat the QAOA distribution as a statistical-physics object and probe its geometry directly in the thermodynamic limit. We draw two independent bitstrings from the same problem instance and couple them through their macroscopic similarity. We find that typical QAOA samples remain macroscopically uncorrelated, even as their sensitivity to this coupling grows with circuit depth p. By contrast, the low-energy tail shows a much stronger tendency to overlap and reveals a Parisi-like phase with the same critical behavior as the thermal spin-glass transition. The onset of this organization moves toward the typical output as the circuit deepens, consistent with the separation vanishing only as poinfty. Together, these findings suggest a route distinct from structure-exploiting classical algorithms and thermal sampling, one in which the QAOA approaches the Parisi energy before Parisi geometry becomes typical.
