arXiv:2611.13099v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Randomized measurements offer a way to probe nonlinear properties of quantum states without reconstructing their full density matrices. For states shared between distant laboratories, we study products of three outcome probabilities measured at the same local settings and then averaged over independent local random unitaries. We determine, for an arbitrary number of parties, which third-order local-unitary invariant combinations these averages retain and which they cannot separate. The accessible information is encoded in algebraic invariants of the quantum state and is reconstructed by analysing the permutation symmetries induced by the distributed measurement protocol. Exploiting these symmetries, the reconstruction problem collapses, regardless of the number of measurement outcomes, to the symbolic inversion of a 3 x 3 matrix. As a result, nonlinear information can be extracted without inverting the exponentially large matrices that typically arise in distributed multipartite quantum systems.
