arXiv:2611.18489v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We study entanglement generation between two spin qubits mediated by a single, thermally mixed, high-spin (S=5/2) magnetic impurity, using the Lindblad master equation to compare coherent and dissipative control of the mediator. We show that no coherent drive, however strong or resonant, can purify the mediator beyond its thermal value --- a direct consequence of unitary evolution exactly preserving state purity --- while a dissipative channel purifies it up to the limit set by the optical pumping cycle itself, Tr[rho_M^2]approx0.8. Modeling this channel with rates fixed by the measured optical pumping of a single Mn spin, we find that the entanglement it enables is not monotonic in the pump strength: it peaks at a finite rate and then decreases, since the same channel leaks decoherence to the qubits through the coupling that mediates their entanglement. At the experimental pumping rate, a single 80ns pulse purifies the mediator, the resulting polarization persists for a timescale three orders of magnitude longer than the entangling dynamics, and the first-recurrence negativity rises from 0.18 to 0.64 at 12K, remaining above 0.6 up to 30K. These results establish dissipation, not as noise to be suppressed, but as the very resource that makes robust entanglement generation possible.
