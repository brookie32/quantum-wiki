arXiv:2611.17431v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: How far can quantum theory depart from a classical worldview? Bell inequality violations show that no theory can simultaneously uphold absoluteness of observed events, freedom of choice, relativistic causality, and classical causal explanations. While generalized notions of causality, capable of incorporating quantum resources, can account for Bell correlations, the Local Friendliness theorem showed that, in extended Wigner's friend scenarios, the conflict between quantum theory and causal explanations persists even after classical causality is abandoned. This revealed a new form of nonclassicality stronger than what was revealed by Bell's theorem. However, Local Friendliness has been confined to Bell causal structures so far, and it remains unknown whether a related form of nonclassicality persists in more general causal networks. Here we answer this question by considering a Wigner's friend version of the instrumental network, a paradigmatic causal structure in which communication between parties is allowed, and, unlike the Bell scenario, relativistic causality does not impose any restriction. We show, both theoretically and in a proof-of-principle photonic experiment, that quantum correlations are incompatible with Causal Friendliness, which is the conjunction of the absoluteness of observed events and the generalized nonclassical causal constraints imposed by the network. Such constraints extend relativistic causality from Bell scenarios to networks of arbitrary structure. These results establish a form of quantum nonclassicality stronger than previously observed in instrumental-network violations of classical causality, generalizing Local Friendliness beyond Bell-Wigner scenarios and uncovering a new class of correlations with potential applications in future quantum technologies.
