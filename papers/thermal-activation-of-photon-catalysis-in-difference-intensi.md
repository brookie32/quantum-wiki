arXiv:2610.22004v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Realistic non-Gaussian operations, which can be implemented by mixing a nonclassical Gaussian state with a Fock state at a beam splitter and then heralding on a photon-number-resolving detector, are known to improve phase estimation in a Mach-Zehnder interferometer. The existing studies have considered the single-mode squeezed vacuum for difference-intensity and parity detection, and the two-mode squeezed thermal state for parity detection alone as the initial Gaussian resources. However, a real squeezer in the lab actually produces single-mode squeezed thermal light, which has not been studied in a coherent-plus-resource interferometer. We take up this case and using the phase space Wigner picture, we obtain the heralded state produced by photon subtraction, addition and catalysis on a single-mode squeezed thermal resource, and evaluate the corresponding phase sensitivities. Single-photon catalysis, which is useless on squeezed vacuum, becomes a useful operation for any thermal occupation above nthsimeq0.2, and since it heralds with a probability near one-half, it turns out to be the best success-weighted operation over the whole thermal range. In the vacuum limit our results reproduce the known hierarchy, where two-photon catalysis is the best operation. The quantum Fisher information computations confirms that thermal noise enhances the phase information encoded in the catalyzed state, demonstrating that difference-intensity detection closely approaches the ultimate precision bound across most operational regimes, and, together with a loss analysis, verifies the robustness of the effect.
