arXiv:2603.21793v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: We show that, given an evolving quantum system and the quasiprobability distribution generated by the spatiotemporal generalization of the Born rule in pseudo-density matrices (PDMs), this distribution deviates from the sequential measurement probability distribution, given by the Luders-von Neumann distribution, if and only if the non-signaling in time is violated; therefore, macroscopic realism can be tested using the spatiotemporal Born rule. Furthermore, we study a definition of temporal entanglement according to the structure of the PDMs that is analogous to the definition of spatial entanglement in density matrices, and we employ our results to explore the relationship between the negativity of the PDM, temporal entanglement, violation of temporal Bell inequalities, and macroscopic realism. From the examples, a clear distinction between violation of macroscopic realism and of temporal Bell inequalities is made, as well as simple examples where a clear causal witness is present while non-signaling in time and macroscopic realism are respected.
