arXiv:2505.16889v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: We present a formulation for the emergence of classical dynamics in a quantum world using a path integral approach that incorporates continuous measurements. It complements decoherence and coarse-grained measurement frameworks. The formulation provides the joint statistics of a sequence of measurements, with each Feynman path picking up a random phase proportional to the measurement strength, and we give conditions under which trajectories near classical paths dominate the probability amplitude. Information proliferates across the environment, a key feature of quantum Darwinism, via plane-wave probe scattering. In the continuous limit, momentum kicks from the probes add a further phase to each trajectory--the origin of the back-action force. We provide conditions under which measurements yield enough ``which-path'' information while keeping the wave packet localized. This describes the quantum-to-classical transition at the level of individual measurement records, complementing the ensemble description by density matrices. The same scattering that decoheres a trajectory also heats it, tying decoherence and measurement back-action together. This bounds how redundantly a classical trajectory can be recorded before back-action randomizes it into Brownian motion. The bound is not restrictive for macroscopic systems. For a trapped particle, it is set by the resolution in units of the zero-point motion and collapses to a single record where the semiclassical description of the orbit fails; for a free coordinate it marks the loss of deterministic objectivity even while the classical action remains large. In both cases, it delimits the regime in which objective classical trajectories exist. The crossover is driven by photon recoil, already resolved in levitated optomechanics; the bound recasts it as the point where a redundantly recorded trajectory, still objective, stops being deterministic.
