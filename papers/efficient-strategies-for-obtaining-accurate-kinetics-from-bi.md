arXiv:2611.22137v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: It has been demonstrated that one can recover the unbiased rate constants for slow conformational transitions by observing transitions driven by a bias potential added along one or several collective variables (CVs). However, the choice of CV, biasing scheme, biasing parameters and the rate estimator can all affect the results and it is not obvious which to choose for a given problem. In this study, we compare accuracy versus computational cost for a previously established benchmark system, a coarse-grained protein with computationally accessible unfolding kinetics. We apply bias to four CVs of decreasing quality in both a time-dependent and quasi-static manner, and compare different estimators for the unfolding rate constant in terms of absolute accuracy to the estimated reference unbiased rate. We show that in the case of gentle biasing, the exponential average time-dependent rate (EATR) framework, which simultaneously fits the unbiased rate constant and a coordinate quality metric gamma, recovers accurate kinetics in nearly all cases.For poor CVs, estimators equivalent to assuming a good coordinate (gamma=1) systematically fail regardless of biasing method or parameters. We introduce heuristics to show when we expect the rates to be inaccurate, and identify which combinations of biasing scheme, estimator and number of runs reach a given accuracy at the lowest cost.
