arXiv:2611.33340v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We study the sample complexity of testing monotonicity of Boolean functions when the tester is given only copies of the quantum function state |psi_frangle=frac{1}{sqrt{2^n}}sum_{xin{0,1}^n}|x,f(x)rangle. We give a tester with sample complexity Oleft(minleft{frac{n}{arepsilon^3}, frac{n^{3/2}}{arepsilon^2}right}right), improving on the previous widetilde O(n^2/arepsilon^2) upper bound of [CNS26]. We also prove the first nontrivial lower bounds for monotonicity testing from quantum function states, matching our upper bounds over a broad range of arepsilon. Our upper bounds use two approaches. The first approach refines the Fourier-based tester of [CNS26] by handling high-influence functions separately, adapting an idea from [KMS18]. The second approach uses the classical path tester of [KMS18] by constructing a quantum observable whose expectation equals the path tester's violation probability. To prove the lower bounds, we place independent functions in mutually incomparable regions of the Boolean hypercube, building on the classical constructions of [BB16, CWX17]. We introduce a new argument for the quantum setting that replaces a fixed number of copies with a Poisson-distributed number of copies, allowing the contributions from different regions to be analyzed independently. This same framework also allows us to prove an Omega(n^2) lower bound for testing union-closedness and 2-monotonicity from quantum function states.
