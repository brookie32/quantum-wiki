arXiv:2610.31792v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Understanding how collective phenomena such as pairing and deformation emerge in interacting fermionic systems, and how the correlations underlying them shape the resources required for their simulation, is a central challenge in many-body physics. We investigate various aspects of quantum complexity in a fermionic system exhibiting two main forms of collectivity, specifically deformation and pairing, through entanglement, non-stabilizerness, and fermionic non-Gaussianity. In the deformed phase, high entanglement coexists with low non-stabilizerness, while the superfluid pairing phase, by contrast, exhibits substantial multipartite entanglement and non-stabilizerness, together with maximal fermionic anti-flatness. In a second step we examine how to describe the phase diagram of the system in a classically scalable and symmetry-preserving way, by combining stabilizer-state expansion and variational basis optimization. A physics-guided decomposition of the Hamiltonian identifies the relevant stabilizer states, which can be constructed from graph states. A single stabilizer state captures the deformed phase to a large extent, and, although the number of stabilizer states required to describe the pairing phase grows exponentially with system size, a symmetry-preserving basis rotation yields a high-fidelity approximation using far fewer states. This rotated state can then serve as a practical starting point for quantum refinement.
