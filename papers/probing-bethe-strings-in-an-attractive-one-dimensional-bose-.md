Bethe strings—bound-state “soliton‑like” clusters that appear as fundamental excitations in integrable one‑dimensional quantum systems—have been experimentally realized as intermixtures of many, including large, strings in nearly integrable ultracold Bose gases. By dynamically tuning the interatomic interaction from repulsive to attractive, the study measured binding energies indicating bound clusters of more than six atoms and confirmed their presence through features in the momentum distribution and Tan’s contact. The experimental data quantitatively agree with theoretical predictions for Bethe‑string binding, demonstrating a viable platform to investigate collective effects of these excitations in both equilibrium and non‑equilibrium settings.
