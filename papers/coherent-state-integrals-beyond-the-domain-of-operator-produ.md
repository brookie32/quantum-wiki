arXiv:2610.19702v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Inserting a resolution of the identity between two operators turns a matrix element of their product into a phase-space integral. For unbounded operators the product need not exist on the vector at hand, while the integral may still converge. We follow this gap through the chain e^{mu a^2}e^{lambda a^{agger2}} of two-photon exponentials, where it can be traced completely. Five regions of parameter space appear: where the Bochner conditions of the strong formulation hold, where the product exists as a vector, where it exists as a sesquilinear form, where the coherent-state integral converges, and where the Fock-space series converges. The first three are strictly nested inside the intersection of the last two, and these two overlap without either containing the other. A single Hermite-type function agrees with the matrix element where it exists and with the integral and the series wherever they converge, so the integral carries the analytic continuation of the matrix element beyond the domain of the product and beyond the convergence of its Fock series. On the real slice of parameter space, an iterated form of the integral also reaches every point where the series converges. The analysis rests on a weak composition lemma whose hypotheses concern only the bra, the ket and the resolving family. For bounded chains the same identity is unconditional, and worked examples of that kind are given in a companion paper.
