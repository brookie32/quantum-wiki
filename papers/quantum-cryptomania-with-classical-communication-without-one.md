arXiv:2610.32145v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: In classical cryptography, one-way functions are necessary for most of cryptography. In the quantum world, however, Kretschmer at {TQC'21} and subsequent works have demonstrated that quantum versions of various primitives in minicrypt, such as signatures and private-key encryption, do not require quantum-computable one-way functions (extsf{qOWF}s). However, the question of the necessity of extsf{qOWF}s for primitives in Cryptomania like public-key encryption with classical public keys and ciphertexts (extsf{qPKE}) has remained open. We provide a comprehensive picture of the relationship between extsf{qPKE} and extsf{qOWF}. First, our main result is a black-box separation between extsf{qOWF}s and extsf{qPKE}. In particular, we show that relative to a unitary, real, and self-inverse oracle, there exists a extsf{qPKE} scheme (with classical keys and ciphertexts), but extsf{qOWF}s do not exist. Further, our oracles admit a point-function obfuscation construction, thus ruling out black-box construction of extsf{qOWF} from point function obfuscations. On the positive side, we construct extsf{qPKE} from quantum indistinguishability obfuscation for quantum circuits with classical obfuscated descriptions and efficiently verifiable one-way puzzles, which is known to be a potentially weaker assumption than extsf{qOWF}s, due to an existing black-box separation. Our construction is the quantum analog of the classical construction of extsf{PKE} from one-way functions and indistinguishability obfuscation in the quantum computation with classical communication (QCCC) setting.
