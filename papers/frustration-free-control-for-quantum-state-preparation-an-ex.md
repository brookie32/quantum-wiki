arXiv:2611.13094v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Quantum circuits based on local measurements and feedback can autonomously prepare and stabilize entangled many-body states. In frustration-free control, the target manifold is specified by local constraints, whose violations are detected by measurement and removed by feedback. We develop an exact theory of this mechanism for the preparation of Dicke states of N_q qubits. Dicke states span the permutation-symmetric subspace, one per magnetization sector. Local singlet projectors define the constraints and detect departures from permutation symmetry. The resulting dynamics closes exactly on the span of the N_q(N_q-1)/2 singlet-pair projectors and is equivalent to a classical first-passage process: measurements move the pair endpoints, while feedback absorbs pairs on measured bonds. The closure reduces the exponentially large many-body Liouvillian to a generator of polynomial size. In one dimension we solve this process in closed form. Its slowest rate, 2[1-os(pi/N_q)] in units of the measurement rate, is an exact Liouvillian eigenvalue. For any scheme of this class, the relaxation time is bounded below by the inverse fraction of pair configurations on which feedback acts; local feedback reaches O(N_q) of O(N_q^2) configurations, requiring a time at least linear in N_q. A second exact closure determines the final state reached from any initial condition. Extensions to modified transport, restricted feedback, and physical errors yield an exact transport-to-absorber crossover, geometric preparation laws, and a bound on the stationary leakage. An exact second-moment closure determines the ensemble fluctuations: the measurement noise is conserving, while feedback generates removal shot noise and pair correlations. Preparation, acceleration, robustness, and fluctuations are thus governed by one exactly solvable process: the transport and absorption of local constraint violations.
