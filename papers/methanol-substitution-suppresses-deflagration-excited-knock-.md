arXiv:2611.17449v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Knock restricts the performance of hydrogen spark-ignition engines and is usually attributed to end-gas autoignition. To the authors' knowledge, this is the first study in which methanol is used to replace part of the hydrogen in a hydrogen engine at constant fuel energy addition, and it shows that near the knock boundary the limiting oscillation is excited by the deflagration itself, which methanol removes without derating the performance. Port-injected methanol replaced 20--50% of the hydrogen energy in a variable-compression Cooperative Fuel Research engine at 600 rpm and compression ratios of 8--12. With pure hydrogen, oscillation in the 4--15 kHz range began before 10% of the charge had burned in 97--100% of cycles, and its maximum amplitude (MAPO) followed the maximum pressure-rise rate across 2499 cycles (Spearman rho=0.94). Methanol slowed the deflagration: it replaced high-flame-speed hydrogen, cooled the intake by 24--25 K and, at unchanged intake pressure, enleaned the mixture by 10--13%. At 50% methanol, the 10--90% combustion duration lengthened 2.7--3.0-fold, oscillation onset moved 4.5--8.3^ircCA after CA10 and mean MAPO fell by 89--93% although the spark timing was advanced by 13--19^ircCA, and IMEP and indicated thermal efficiency rose by 2.4--7.8%. Methanol was thus worth more than this advance, and at matched CA50 MAPO was still about 90% lower. The measured combustion duration of all 25 data sets scaled with the computed laminar flame speed as S_L^{-0.58} (R^2=0.99); fuel replacement accounted for about three quarters of the reduction in S_L, and the end-gas autoignition integral stayed below 0.07, against 1 at autoignition. The chamber kept its cylindrical acoustic modes, whose frequencies fell by 8--9%. The trade-off is formaldehyde emissions, which rose five- to sixfold while NO_x fell by 49--85%.
