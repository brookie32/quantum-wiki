arXiv:2608.16364v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Designing electrolyte molecules for lithium batteries requires balancing electronic stability with appropriate Li+ solvation, yet the structural basis remains unclear across chemically diverse molecules. High-throughput screening expands the searchable space, but ranked candidates alone do not reveal recurring motifs or their applicability limits. We searched nearly one billion GDB13 structures using electronic--solvation descriptors without explicit functional-group preferences or scaffold constraints. Across descriptor weights, high-ranking populations separated into a nitrile-dominant regime and a coexistence regime containing substantial fractions of both nitrile- and ether-containing molecules. These regimes together define a nitrile-centered, ether-assisted motif hierarchy: nitrile remains favored across broad weight ranges, whereas ether becomes prominent under stronger electrostatic and polarity constraints. Encoding this hierarchy in a generative model expands the candidate space beyond GDB13 and yields high-scoring fluorinated structures without an explicit fluorination reward. Explicit-solvent molecular dynamics simulations show weak, exchangeable coordination of representative candidates without displacing ethylene carbonate from the dominant first solvation shell around Li+; effects on ion association and transport depend on molecular structure and concentration. These results establish a quantitative, interpretable and physically bounded motif hierarchy that systematizes established nitrile and ether chemistry for lithium-battery electrolyte design.
