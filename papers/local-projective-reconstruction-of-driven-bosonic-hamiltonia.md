arXiv:2605.22796v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Inferring a Hamiltonian from a single quantum trajectory is generally non-unique. This ambiguity limits inverse use of projective quantum geometry. Here we show that a nearest-neighbour structural prior makes the problem locally identifiable. For a smooth real time-dependent Jacobi Hamiltonian initialized at a ladder endpoint, the physical-time projective jet through order M+1 reconstructs the first M couplings, their logarithmic rates, and diagonal energy differences, up to an overall scalar-energy gauge. A canonical osculating-frame representation encodes the same data in a gauge-fixed connection. Pair-driven bosonic dynamics provides a direct realization because the vacuum evolves on the even-Fock Jacobi chain. In a nonstationary test, shallow reconstructions fit early projective data yet strongly overpredict later occupation growth; adding the projective order that resolves each hidden nonlinear layer restores the self-limited dynamics. The result supplies an inverse local counterpart to forward Krylov/Lanczos constructions and specifies when phase-resolved short-time state information identifies a sparse driven Hamiltonian.
