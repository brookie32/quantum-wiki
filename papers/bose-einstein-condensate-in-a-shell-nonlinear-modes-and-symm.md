arXiv:2610.32249v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: The state of a quantum particle confined to a spherical surface is commonly described using spherical harmonics, which play a crucial role in solving many scientific problems featuring spherical symmetry. A mean-field description of a system of many interacting quantum particles confined to a spherical shell naturally leads to nonlinear generalizations of spherical harmonics. In this study, we analyze the emergence of such nonlinear modes in a shell-shaped Bose-Einstein condensate with both repulsive and attractive two-particle interactions. We find several classes of nonlinear modes. The first kind is an analytical continuation of a single spherical harmonic or their superposition for nonvanishing atom-atom interaction. Some of these solutions have planar counterparts in the form of Townes solitons and Townes vortices, which either appear stable even in the case of attractive interactions or can be stabilized by three-particle interactions. The second kind originate from symmetry-breaking bifurcations in the case of a repulsive interaction, and therefore have no linear counterparts. Moreover, we demonstrate the existence of a breathing dynamics that oscillates primarily between an unstable soliton and a stable vortex necklace state. Our findings show that the physics of nonlinear matter waves on curved geometries differs substantially from that in planar systems, suggesting the feasibility of observing these unconventional wave patterns experimentally.
