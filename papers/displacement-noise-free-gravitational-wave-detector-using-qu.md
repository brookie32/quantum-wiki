arXiv:2610.21112v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Gravitational waves (GWs) induce quantum transitions between photon orbital-angular-momentum (OAM) states with amplitudes alpha_{pm}. Exploiting this effect, we present a displacement-noise-free detection framework that extracts GW information from the OAM Hilbert space. Parametrized quantum state tomography of the GW-induced OAM sidebands enables a single V-shaped detector to resolve all seven wave parameters: the frequency, propagation direction, two polarization amplitudes, and initial phase of each polarization. Direct detection of the sidebands yields a weak signal of approximately 10^{-15},W. We therefore introduce a stronger reference state r |0>_{P}, with r=0.01, to enhance the detected signal. For an arm length of L=4,m, N=160 round trips, a paraxial parameter of gamma=0.1, an incident light power of P_{BS}=10,kW, a wavelength of lambda_{opt}=1064,nm, and GW amplitudes of A=10^{-18}, we estimate sideband transition amplitudes of |alpha_{pm}|sim10^{-10} and signal powers of order 1,nW, scaling as P_{BS}alpha_{pm}propto P_{BS}ANLgamma/lambda_{opt}. The shot-noise-limited sensitivity floor is S^{1/2}_n=1.6imes10^{-20},Hz^{-1/2}. Because the scheme measures OAM transition amplitudes rather than differential arm lengths, it is intrinsically free from displacement noise and provides a broadband probe of GWs, specifically bridging the 0.1--10,Hz mid-frequency gap.
