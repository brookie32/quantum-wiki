arXiv:2611.14460v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: We say that a system undergoes a quantum phase transition when the mean-field ground state of a given Hamiltonian spontaneously breaks its symmetries depending on the Hamiltonian parameters. It is a widely accepted view that this phenomenon enables the mean-field state to absorb the maximum amount of correlations without losing its mean-field structure. However, it is still uncertain how those correlations are incorporated within the mean-field state and how the correlation structure changes when we get closer to the exact ground state by restoring the symmetries. In this work, we answer these questions by studying the single-j model, which is a realistic fermionic nuclear model with abelian and non-abelian broken symmetries. We find that both types of symmetries have very different effects for the correlations within the mean-field state. This makes unclear the concept of the symmetry breaking process as a mechanism to absorb the maximum amount of correlations. Moreover, the symmetry restoration redistributes existing `fake' correlations between paired orbitals to more complex subsystems.
