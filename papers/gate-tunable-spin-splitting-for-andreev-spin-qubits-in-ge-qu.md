arXiv:2611.18888v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Ge-based super-semi heterostructures are a promising platform for implementing Andreev spin qubits (ASQs). For example, they can be isotopically purified to eliminate nuclear spins that cause decoherence. However, spin-orbit coupling (SOC), which is a key ingredient for ASQs, is cubic in momentum for hole-based materials like Ge, in contrast with more-conventional ASQ materials (e.g., InAs wires), for which the SOC is linear in momentum. In this work, we study the interplay between geometrical properties like the channel width, and gate-tunable properties like the chemical potential, on ASQ operations. Intriguingly, we find that the spin splitting in a planar Ge Josephson junction does not depend on the junction width, in contrast with InAs nanowires. We explore the possibility of exploiting this property to suppress the harmful effects of charge traps. However, this prospect is spoiled by the fact that charge traps also affect the chemical potential, in addition to the channel width. Our results provide insights into the fundamental mechanisms governing spin splitting in ASQs and offer guidance for optimizing ASQs in other materials with cubic SOC.
