arXiv:2611.25074v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Self-testing certifies quantum states and measurements from observed correlations alone, without assumptions on the inner workings of the devices. Its standard formulation, however, models the devices as manipulating qudits, of arbitrary or even infinite dimension. Yet febits, information encoded in the presence or absence of a fermion in a mode, can produce correlations in quantum networks that no qudit model reproduces under the same locality constraints. We first show that the modeling choice in terms of qudits or febits matters for self-testing: some correlations that self-test a unique qudit strategy admit two fermionic realizations inequivalent under local parity-preserving isometries. We then develop a fermionic self-testing framework and show that the correlations of a three-party rewiring network single out febit states and measurements up to such isometries, providing the first genuine fermionic self-test.
