arXiv:2608.27850v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Catalytic hydrogenation of CO2 to formate on copper is a key elementary step for CO2 utilization. Previous experimental and theoretical studies suggested an Eley-Rideal mechanism for this reaction, promoted by bending vibrational excitation, yet direct state resolved evidence remains lacking. Here, we present accurate dynamical predictions for CO2 hydrogenation on Cu(111) based on a full-dimensional first-principles neural network potential energy surface. Our calculations near-quantitatively reproduce the measured reaction probabilities, including their nozzle-temperature and incidence-energy dependence. Our state-resolved results indicate that while vibrational excitation of the bending mode enhances reactivity, it alone cannot account for the observed reactivity increase with nozzle temperature. Instead, rotational excitation plays a dominant role, mainly attributable to the significant change in anisotropy of the molecular polar orientation as CO2 accesses the transition state. This mode-specific mechanistic insight revises the conventional interpretation and lays the foundation for the precise control of CO2 hydrogenation on catalysts.
