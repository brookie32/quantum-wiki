The authors introduce a theoretical framework in which electromagnetic zero‑point fluctuations (the Casimir effect) can stabilize otherwise fluctuating electronic nematic phases by lowering the system’s total energy.  Focusing on quantum Hall stripe order, they show that embedding the material within an anisotropic dielectric crystal generates an orientation‑dependent Casimir energy that is up to three orders of magnitude stronger than competing stabilization mechanisms, suggesting experimental feasibility with existing technology.  The study connects these results to recent quantum Hall stripe experiments and outlines broader prospects for “Casimir control” of electronic order in novel materials.
