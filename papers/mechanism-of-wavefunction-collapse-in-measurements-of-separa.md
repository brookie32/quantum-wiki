arXiv:2605.20111v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: The specific advance of this work is to propose a mechanism by which superpositions collapse during measurement of the separated subsystems of entangled quantum states. It is shown how the phase that locks together entangled states could play a special role in the measurement of isolated subsystems. This `contextual' phase is installed into separated subsystems of an entangled state, and decides the measurement outcomes for the subsystems by directing the collapse of each superposition to a particular classical outcome when a subsystem is measured. The measuring apparatus thus obtains a classical read-out of the quantum correlations embedded in an entangled state. More broadly, these results suggest insights into the theory of measurement of quantum superpositions.
