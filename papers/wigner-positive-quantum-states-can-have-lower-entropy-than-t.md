arXiv:2609.24670v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The Wigner entropy conjecture posits that pure Gaussian states minimize the Shannon entropy of non-negative Wigner functions, known as the Wigner entropy. We show that mixing a specific pure Wigner-negative state with another suitably chosen quantum state can restore Wigner positivity while retaining a Wigner entropy that is slightly - but strictly - below the vacuum entropy. As a consequence, we disprove the Wigner entropy conjecture, as well as the stronger Wigner majorization conjecture, by deriving simple analytical counterexamples with violations of the entropy bound no larger than 10^{-3}. Such states with sub-vacuum Wigner entropy can be found arbitrarily close to the vacuum and can also exhibit sub-vacuum Wigner-Renyi alpha-entropy for 0<alpha<2. We identify the physical mechanism behind the existence of such states, which arises from a subtle interplay between the uncertainty principle and non-Gaussianity. The key idea of this paper was developed with the help of AI tools, building on the characterization of extreme non-negative Wigner functions.
