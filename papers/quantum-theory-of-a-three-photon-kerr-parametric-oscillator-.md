arXiv:2605.21036v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: We investigate the quantum properties of a nonlinear Kerr oscillator driven by a three-photon pump. We identify an exact spectral degeneracy at which the interacting ground state admits a nonperturbative analytical solution. In the large-amplitude regime, the resulting manifold becomes threefold quasi-degenerate and forms delocalized three-headed quantum superpositions. Away from the exact degeneracy, we derive an approximate description of the threefold quasi-degenerate manifold in terms of Z_3-symmetric superpositions of squeezed coherent states. These states differ qualitatively from conventional three-component cat states due to the presence of squeezing with a distinctive parametric dependence. By varying the pump-oscillator detuning, the squeezing can be enhanced, suppressed, or even reversed, leading to a squeezing-to-anti-squeezing transition. In the large-amplitude regime, the threefold manifold defines a noise-biased bosonic qutrit, which we term a Kerr-cat qutrit. We show that photon loss predominantly induces cyclic shift errors, while clock errors are suppressed, and quantitatively characterize the resulting noise bias. We further show that the detuning controls the noise bias through the squeezing. Going beyond stabilization, we construct arbitrary operations on a single Kerr-cat qutrit together with an entangling two-qutrit gate, providing a complete set of logical operations for universal qutrit quantum computation, and validate their action through full Lindblad simulations. Our results establish the three-photon Kerr oscillator both as a rich nonlinear quantum system with analytically tractable macroscopic states and as a platform for noise-biased bosonic qutrit quantum computation.
