arXiv:2611.24291v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Rapid, high contrast modulation of optical parameters such as the refractive index can radically reshape a wave's frequency spectrum as it propagates. Non-commuting frequency and time operators can be used to describe this reshaping, analogous to the position and momentum operators of quantum mechanics. Here we develop the connection between this theory and semi-classical quantum mechanics, showing that: (i) different microscopic models of time-varying materials are equivalent to choices of operator ordering; (ii) wave propagation is governed by an unusual non-Hermitian Schrodinger equation; (iii) we can take a semi-classical limit, finding a generalization of Hamilton's equations governing the central frequency and arrival time of a wave spectrum as it propagates through a modulated material. The theory can be applied to design dispersive time modulations that `focus' a wave in frequency and time, analogous to caustics in semiclassical quantum mechanics. Our results not only deepen the connection between classical wave propagation and quantum mechanics, but can also simplify overly complex calculations that have been previously applied to media where time reflection effects are negligible.
