arXiv:2611.33270v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We introduce techniques for the study of randomness extraction against quantum side information (privacy amplification) by relating this problem to variants of hypothesis testing tasks with quadratic constraints. Building also on the recently introduced measured smooth entropy approach of [Regula and Tomamichel, arXiv:2603.04493], we precisely determine the optimal scaling of the error of quantum privacy amplification in several regimes. Our results apply to the standard operational security criterion based on trace distance and focus in particular on universal classes of hash functions. On the technical side, our investigation reveals connections with operator space theory methods used in non-commutative Khintchine inequalities. As one application, we give a one-shot converse bound that shows the leftover hash lemma of [arXiv:2603.04493] to be tight up to a constant factor for all strongly 4-universal hash functions. This allows us to evaluate the asymptotic i.i.d. error exponent of universal randomness extraction for all rates, matching the bound of [Dupuis, arXiv:2105.05342] and improving also earlier classical asymptotic results of [Hayashi, arXiv:1010.1358]. We furthermore give new one-shot achievability and converse bounds on the error in the strong converse regime, which we show to match up to a constant factor. This establishes the exact strong converse exponent of quantum privacy amplification under 2-universal hash functions, complementing the converse bound of [Shen, Gao, and Cheng, arXiv:2202.10263].
