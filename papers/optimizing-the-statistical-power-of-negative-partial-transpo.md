arXiv:2502.19624v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: A key requirement of any separable quantum state is that its density matrix has a non-negative partial transpose. For continuous bipartite quantum states, violation of this condition may be tested via the hierarchy of negative-partial-transpose (NPT) based entanglement criteria introduced by Shchukin and Vogel [Phys. Rev. Lett. 95, 230502 (2005)]. However, even for a known state, a procedure to determine the optimal NPT-based criterion within the hierarchy has, thus far, remained outstanding. Here, we introduce a theoretical framework and numerical toolbox NPyT to compare criteria performance via the statistical power of hypothesis testing for a known input state to enable the optimal entanglement criterion to be determined. Our framework takes into account environmental noise and finite number of measurements, and optimally allocates measurement resources. To demonstrate the utility of our approach, we apply our statistical framework to prominent examples of Gaussian and non-Gaussian states, including the two-mode squeezed vacuum state, the quanta-subtracted two-mode squeezed vacuum state, and the two-mode Schrodinger-cat state. We discuss the optimal criteria identified for these states and compare them to other commonly employed criteria. For example, we identify an entanglement test for the two-mode squeezed vacuum state that has high statistical power and outperforms Simon's criterion. This work illustrates that the non-Gaussianity of the entanglement test distribution increases with test matrix size, and highlights that smaller test matrices generally provide better performance. Beyond bipartite inseparability, our framework can be extended to a wide variety of other matrix-based tests of nonclassicality and multi-mode entanglement.
