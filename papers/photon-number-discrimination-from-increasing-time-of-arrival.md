arXiv:2610.32154v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Measurements of photon-number-dependent time-of-arrival (TOA) distributions in wide NbN superconducting microstrip single-photon detectors (SMSPDs) are presented. Two SI{10}{m-long devices with widths of SI{3.2}{m and SI{6.4}{m are characterized under pulsed SI{515}{nm} excitation. The TOA histograms are described by sums of exponentially modified Gaussian (EMG) components, with the wider device exhibiting components assigned to photon numbers up to (n=8). Adjacent TOA peaks become more distinguishable relative to their widths at higher photon number, in contrast to the compression of neighboring photon-number timing shifts observed in conventional narrow-wire SNSPDs. For the components assigned to (n=7) and (n=8), the FWHM-normalized peak separation is (2.15), and the fitted EMG distributions yield an equal-prior pairwise TOA-assignment fidelity of (99.6%). The EMG exponential-tail parameter (au_n) decreases strongly with photon number, while the Gaussian-location parameter (mu_n) continues to shift between neighboring components. A phenomenological model motivated by absorption-site-dependent vortex processes and collective current redistribution captures these timing trends. These results support wide SMSPDs as a platform for TOA-based photon-number discrimination at high photon number, relevant to multiphoton-state characterization and photonic quantum information processing. The same geometry also provides a setting to examine how the spatial distribution of photon absorption affects vortex-assisted detection.
