arXiv:2510.15370v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Nonunitary quantum critical phenomena remain largely unexplored, hindered by the scarcity of exactly solvable models and the numerical challenges in non-Hermitian systems. Here we demonstrate that a general boundary condition drives fundamentally new nonunitary boundary quantum criticality in an exactly solvable lattice model. The exact solution yields a closed-form expression for the boundary-induced equal-time correlation function. The algebraic long-distance behavior of the boundary-induced correlation is governed by the single-particle reflection amplitude at the Fermi level, whose anomalous amplification is forbidden in unitary scattering and therefore provides a direct scattering signature of the nonunitary boundary-critical regime. We use the biorthogonal entanglement entropy as a many-body probe of this boundary-critical behavior and find that it retains logarithmic scaling, while the associated effective central charge, continuously tunable by the boundary parameters, changes from real to complex. The real branch admits a unitary defect conformal field theory (CFT) description, whereas the complex branch lies beyond analytic continuation from the unitary theory. These results establish minimal boundary engineering as an exactly solvable platform for nonunitary boundary quantum criticality and provide a benchmark for broader studies of nonunitary critical phenomena.
