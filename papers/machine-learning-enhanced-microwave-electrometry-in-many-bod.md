Machine-learning (ML) combined with principal component analysis (PCA) is used to translate distorted, noisy EIT spectra from interacting Rydberg atoms into accurate microwave electric-field values, bypassing the limited linear-response formula of Autler–Towns splitting.  
A simple PCA‑augmented regression lowers the minimum detectable field by a few-fold relative to traditional methods, while a four‑class ensemble ML scheme further reduces detection limits by two orders of magnitude toward the noise‐dependent physical limit.  
This approach turns many‑body interaction effects into useful spectral signatures, enabling near‑optimal sensitivity for microwave quantum sensing in strongly interacting regimes.
