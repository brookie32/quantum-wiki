arXiv:2611.17417v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The spread complexity characterizes the spreading of a quantum state over the Krylov basis and has been proposed to capture the correlations between energy levels. How such correlations manifest in the dynamics has been studied mainly numerically for large matrices. Here we consider three-level systems, the smallest setting with spacing correlations, and derive exact results for the spread complexity, the Krylov variance, and the Krylov entropy. At infinite temperature, the Krylov basis depends on the spectrum only through the ratio of consecutive level spacings, and the spectral variance enters only as an overall time scale. For Gaussian ensembles, these variables are statistically independent, and the averaged spread complexity and variance reduce to one-dimensional integrals over the spacing-ratio distribution encoding the level statistics. We establish universal late-time values of the complexity and variance, and an algebraic relaxation with exponents set by the Dyson index. We also outline the extension to d levels, with the ensemble-averaged spread complexity expressed as a (d-2)-dimensional integral over the joint distribution of consecutive spacing ratios.
