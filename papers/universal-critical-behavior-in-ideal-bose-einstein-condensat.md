arXiv:2606.11338v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: The experimental realization of photonic Bose-Einstein condensates has renewed the interest in the critical behavior of the ideal Bose gas, a paradigmatic system generally considered to be well understood. Here we establish a universal thermodynamic classification of ideal Bose-Einstein condensation in terms of experimentally measurable critical observables. We identify three universality classes with distinct critical exponents and singular thermodynamic behavior, arising from the low-energy scaling of the single-particle density of states. Depending on the low-energy scaling exponent of the density of states, which is controlled by dimensionality and confinement, the transition displays either the usual algebraic divergences of thermodynamic susceptibilities, divergent behavior with marginal logarithmic corrections, or a more subtle form of criticality, where only the correlation length diverges. Our work provides a unified framework for equilibrium criticality in noninteracting bosonic systems. This classification applies broadly to atomic, photonic, polaritonic, and magnonic condensates, where dimensionality, confinement, and spectral engineering can strongly reshape the density of states. We connect our thermodynamic classification with previously identified universality classes based on condensate fluctuations and order parameters.
