Koziol and Schmidt (2026) used superconducting‑qubit quantum annealers to determine ground states of Ising lattices with algebraically decaying, competing long‑range interactions by applying a unit‑cell optimization scheme that extrapolates finite‑size results to the thermodynamic limit. The method was validated on three benchmark problems: the magnetization Devil’s staircase in a triangular‑lattice Ising model under longitudinal field, the zero‑field Kagomé lattice ground state relevant to artificial spin ice, and frustrated Ising models with additional nearest‑neighbor terms. Their results demonstrate that existing quantum annealing technology can realistically tackle complex long‑range interacting systems across condensed‑matter, statistical mechanics, and materials science research.
