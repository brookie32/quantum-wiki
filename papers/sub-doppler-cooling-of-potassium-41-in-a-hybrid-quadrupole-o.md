arXiv:2611.23466v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Sub-Doppler cooling processes rely on the near-degeneracy of the Zeeman sub-levels of the atom. Effective sub-Doppler cooling is only achieved when the ac-Stark shift of the molasses light is significantly larger than the Zeeman shift. This condition is typically achieved by switching off all magnetic gradients, and using Helmholtz coils to cancel Earth's magnetic field. Another way to achieve a nearly zero magnetic field gradient is to superimpose a second pair of oppositely oriented quadrupole coils with a different spatial geometry. At the ideal current ratio, this creates an octupole-symmetry magnetic field for the atoms. The resulting octupole magnetic field has a gradient increasing quadratically from zero at the center. In this work, the effectiveness of sub-Doppler cooling and trapping using D2 and D1 light is evaluated in such a hybrid trap for ^{41}K. The temperature, lifetime, and phase-space density of the atoms are measured. For both D2 and D1 light, the octupole MOT is found to have a longer lifetime, while maintaining a similar temperature to the zero-field case. D1 gray-molasses temperatures down to 3.8(1)~muK and phase-space density up to 5.3(4)imes10^{-5} are measured for 14.3(1)imes10^6 ^{41} atoms. The effectiveness of the hybrid quadrupole-octupole trap suggests the possibility of increasing the loading time for atoms into an optical dipole trap with active sub-Doppler cooling.
