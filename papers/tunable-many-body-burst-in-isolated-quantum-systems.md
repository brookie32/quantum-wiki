arXiv:2602.09665v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Thermalization in isolated quantum many-body systems can be nonmonotonic depending on an initial state. We demonstrate that a burst---a transient deviation from thermal equilibrium---can be created at a designated time from a low-entangled initial state for a nonintegrable mixed-field Ising chain, by developing a systematic numerical method to construct such a low-entangled state. Contrary to the typical spreading of information, the created burst is accompanied by a slow or even negative entanglement growth. We analytically show that a burst becomes probabilistically rare after a long time. Our results suggest that a nonequilibrium state is maintained for an appropriately chosen initial state until quantum scrambling becomes dominant. Our predictions can be tested by quench experiments with programmable quantum simulators.
