arXiv:2611.09456v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Within classical nucleation theory, crystal nucleation requires sufficient supersaturation, but is suppressed at low temperatures due to reduced molecular mobility, leading to a maximum nucleation rate typically above the glass transition temperature T_g. Here, we experimentally show that this conventional picture breaks down in a biocompatible polymer poly(2-methoxyethyl acrylate)(PMEA)/water mixture. We find that the number of nuclei increases dramatically below T_g, with the effective nucleation rate exhibiting an unusual maximum at approximately T_g-10 K. Calorimetric experiments reveal a strong temperature-history dependence of cold crystallization of water in hydrated PMEA, which is explained by the observed nucleation behavior. We propose that the unusual enhancement of nucleation below T_g provides a key mechanism linking high glass-forming ability and cold crystallization upon heating, thereby explaining the striking asymmetry in crystallization behavior between cooling and heating. Our findings suggest that a hidden nucleation-rate maximum below T_g may also underlie cold crystallization in other glass-forming liquids.
