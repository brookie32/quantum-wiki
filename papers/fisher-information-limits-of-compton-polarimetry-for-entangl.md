arXiv:2611.21022v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: The polarisation correlation of the two annihilation photons, measured by Compton polarimetry, has been proposed as a PET biomarker of tissue microstructure. We quantify how many events this measurement needs, what limits that number and whether a small-animal CZT system reaches tissue-level contrasts. We parameterise the measurement by the correlation coefficient kappa (kappa=1 maximally entangled, 1/2 separable), derive the classical Fisher information of double-Compton events and compare it with the quantum Fisher information, relate kappa to the annihilation channels of positronium in tissue, and simulate pixelated CdZnTe (CZT) rings. The model is validated against three published measurements and an independent GATE/Geant4 simulation. A double-Compton event carries at most 0.12-0.14 units of Fisher information about kappa and 0.015 on average, 0.1-0.6% of the quantum limit. Event-wise maximum likelihood reaches the Cramer-Rao bound, whereas the conventional ratio R needs 1.2-2.3 times as many events. The observed coefficient is kappa_{obs}=1-alpha(1-kappa_{pick}) with a pick-off share alpha of 0.13-0.29; it is sensitive to lipid fraction but not to oxygen, and published porous-polymer data give kappa_{pick} = 0.34-0.58. In a 15-mm CZT module, ordering ambiguity, position errors and electron transport halve the information per event, and sequence reconstruction recovers it. With ^{18}F and an eight-module ring, kappa is measured to pm0.01 in a 10-mm tumour with half of its decays, whereas separating tissues by kappa alone requires 6-74 times more decays than available.
