arXiv:2611.22358v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: A photon pulse occupies a range of frequencies, so suppressing reflection at its carrier need not minimize the error of the transmitted pulse. We study two separated emitters coupled to a common waveguide at several contact points. Interference between the contact paths can make the coupling that drives each emitter quadratic in frequency detuning. With matched propagation and emitter resonances centered on the carrier, the reflected fields cancel once more. Their combined reflection probability is then sixth order at zero loss. Separating each local double zero into two simple zeros can nevertheless improve transmission of a finite pulse. We minimize the leading narrowband error within this common static adjustment, counting reflection, absorption, and distortion of the transmitted mode. For a Gaussian pulse without loss, the minimum is two-fifths of the error for coincident zeros. Independent emitter loss favors a smaller separation because photons entering separate reservoirs do not share the cancellation of the reflected fields. The optimum follows from the pulse spectrum and the relative weight of these two processes. Uniform and Su--Schrieffer--Heeger waveguides realize the mechanism. In the latter, coordinated contact and resonance adjustments also preserve the full ratio of complex transmission amplitudes for opposite incidence directions. Finite-band calculations determine how the benefit changes with bandwidth and contact errors.
