arXiv:2601.14754v3 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: How localization emerges in open wave systems can differ fundamentally from the familiar picture in closed materials. Here we show that a quasiperiodic chain with spatially disordered non-reciprocal couplings supports a form of localization in which the relevant boundary does not separate spreading and trapped waves, but two different kinds of confinement. One regime shows erratic skin accumulation at disorder-selected positions inside the lattice, whereas the other shows conventional Anderson localization with centers distributed across the system. Adding longer-range hopping turns this transition into an anomalous mobility edge between these two localized sectors. We identify the transition using real-space profiles, Lyapunov exponents, spectral winding, and wave-packet evolution. For individual disorder realizations, wave packets drift in a direction fixed by spectral topology, while averaging over disorder cancels opposite drifts and restores conventional transport. These results provide experimentally accessible signatures of anomalous localization in engineered open systems.
