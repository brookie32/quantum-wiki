arXiv:2611.18457v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We investigate quantum state transfer in the poly-bi-[8]-annulenylene lattice using isotropic Heisenberg and Hubbard models. For a 16-site lattice, reflection symmetry separates the single-excitation Hamiltonian into independent triplet and singlet sectors, allowing analytical determination of the energy spectra. Although perfect state transfer is forbidden for positive couplings, appropriately chosen coupling ratios enable pretty-good transfer with arbitrarily high fidelity. Remarkably, the triplet transfer probability is independent of the interchain rung coupling, while singlet transfer can be selectively enhanced or suppressed, enabling symmetry-selective quantum transport without time-dependent control. Extending the analysis to the Hubbard model, we show that the Heisenberg transfer dynamics emerge in the strong-interaction limit, while finite interactions delay all high-fidelity transfer peaks of a symmetry sector by the same fraction of their transfer time, set by the spectrum, and reduce the peak fidelity, even at the corrected time, by an amount that grows with the transfer time. These findings are relevant to future quantum-network architectures, where symmetry-controlled state transfer could provide a building block for connecting quantum memories and emitter-based interfaces within quantum nodes. Overall, our results demonstrate how molecular network geometry and symmetry can be exploited to engineer and control quantum-state transport in interacting spin systems, providing a theoretical framework for quantum information transfer in molecular-inspired quantum architectures.
