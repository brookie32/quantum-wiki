Ultra‑broadband anti‑jamming communication is demonstrated using a waveguide‑coupled Rydberg atomic receiver, enabling real‑time frequency‐hopping spread spectrum (FHSS) from 100 kHz to 20 GHz with a maximum hopping rate of 100 khop/s.  
Within the 2.4 GHz ISM band the system achieves about 8 channels per MHz, and by exploiting its continuous wide bandwidth it can support over 150,000 simultaneous channels.  
Experimental results show a 51 dB improvement in narrowband interference tolerance compared with conventional single‑frequency receivers, confirming strong anti‑jamming capability.
