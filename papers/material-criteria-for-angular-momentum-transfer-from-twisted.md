arXiv:2610.32121v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: We study the material requirements for transferring the total-angular-momentum (TAM) projection of a twisted photon to the orbital angular momentum (OAM) of a photoelectron emitted from an n-type semiconductor surface. Within a single-valley effective-mass model of surface photoemission, we calculate OAM-resolved spectra for GaAs, InSb, InAs_{0.34}Sb_{0.66}, and Hg_{0.76}Cd_{0.24}Te irradiated by twisted photons with Laguerre--Gaussian--like momentum profile. The OAM transfer is characterized by the mean electron OAM, its root-mean-squared width, and the fraction of electrons with OAM-projection equal to the photon TAM-projection. Efficient transfer requires the transverse momentum of the conduction electron to be much smaller than the transverse momentum width of the photon, which favors low temperatures, small conduction-band effective masses, and shallow donor levels. GaAs is less favorable within the present model because of its larger effective mass and deeper donor levels. We also discuss the demanding photon-wave packet parameters, possible extensions to Dirac and Weyl semimetals, and post-emission OAM selection. Although the generation of vortex electrons with a vanishing OAM uncertainty via photoemission seems challenging, we argue that it is still possible to generate single-electron vortex states with a finite OAM dispersion.
