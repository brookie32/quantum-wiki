arXiv:2610.27070v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: In quantum optics and related fields, cavity quantum electrodynamics provides a powerful framework for modeling the low-energy properties of bound charges coupled to electromagnetic fields, using a minimal set of atomic levels and isolated photonic modes. Here, we develop a similar framework for non-Abelian gauge theories, focusing on the pure-gauge dynamics of SU(2) fields as the conceptually simplest example. Starting from the corresponding Yang-Mills Lagrangian, we derive an SU(2) cavity Hamiltonian in the Coulomb gauge, represented in terms of only three localized bosonic modes. Unlike its non-interacting electromagnetic counterpart, this model exhibits a nontrivial excitation spectrum featuring bound composite gauge particles ('glueballs') as the lowest-energy configurations. We extend this framework to one-dimensional arrays of coupled SU(2) cavities and discuss the driven excitation of a glueball condensate and the clustering of multi-glueball states as two elementary interaction-induced processes in these models. These examples offer a glimpse into the multitude of novel many-body phenomena that arise within this cavity SU(2) framework and we show how this physics at the interface between particle physics and quantum optics can be experimentally explored using state-of-the-art superconducting quantum circuits.
