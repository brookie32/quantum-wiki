arXiv:2604.24760v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Recent years have seen a growing interest in the use of belief propagation (BP), an algorithm originally introduced for performing classical statistical inference on graphical models, for approximate, but highly efficient tensor network contraction. BP is a corner case of generalized belief propagation (GBP), a family of classical inference methods in which messages are passed within a hierarchy of overlapping regions. Here, we detail how to apply GBP to tensor network contraction. We implement GBP with different region choices on finite and infinite tensor networks in two and three dimensions, solving the corresponding fixed point equations numerically and, in tractable cases, analytically. We consider a range of quantum and classical examples, including frustrated Ising models, three-dimensional ice models, independent-set counting on irregular graphs, and the norms of the deformed AKLT state and random tensor network states. Across our examples, including the quantum ones, converged GBP often reduces errors by several orders of magnitude compared with BP.
