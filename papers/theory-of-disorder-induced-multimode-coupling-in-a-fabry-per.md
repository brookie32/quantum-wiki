arXiv:2610.12687v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Strong light--matter interactions have attracted significant interest in physical chemistry due to the potential for modifying molecular processes by hybridizing with cavity photons and forming polaritonic states. However, for a disordered molecular ensemble within an optical cavity capable of supporting multiple photon modes, theoretical understanding of multimode polaritons remains incomplete, particularly regarding whether and how distinct photon modes are mutually coupled through molecules. In this work, we generalize the collective mode expansion formalism to incorporate multiple photonic modes and show that spatial and orientational disorder in the light--matter coupling strength (originating from dipole orientation fluctuations and photonic field inhomogeneity) can mediate cross-mode coupling. This disorder-induced coupling allows for energy transfer to otherwise unexcited photon modes through a mechanism termed disorder-induced multimode activation (DIMA). Using an effective one-dimensional Fabry--Perot cavity as a test system, we demonstrate that the cross-mode energy transfer through DIMA can be controlled by changing the molecular layer thickness and the optical properties of the mirrors. Furthermore, we show that DIMA is robust against weak energetic disorder. This mechanistic understanding lays the foundation for a systematic control of multimode photon coupling using disorder-induced effects.
