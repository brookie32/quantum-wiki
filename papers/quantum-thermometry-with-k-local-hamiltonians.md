arXiv:2611.18327v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: In equilibrium quantum thermometry, the precision of temperature estimation increases with the heat capacity of the probe thermometer. For an n-body thermometer, the maximum heat capacity is attained by a Hamiltonian that requires n-local interactions, making its physical implementation challenging. In the thermodynamic limit, the optimal heat capacity scales quadratically with n. Although more experimentally accessible 2-local Hamiltonians can achieve this optimal scaling, their performance remains significantly below the theoretical maximum for realistic system sizes. Here, we restrict to this scenario and show a substantial enhancement in the thermometric performance with only a minimal increase in locality in some Hamiltonian models. We further introduce k-local generalizations of these models and show that their thermometric performance systematically approaches the theoretical optimum as the locality is increased. By analyzing their spectra, we relate this enhancement to the progressive emergence of the spectral structure underlying optimal equilibrium thermometry. Lastly, we discuss the heat capacity as a witness of Hamiltonian locality.
