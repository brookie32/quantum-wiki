arXiv:2311.01533v5 Announce Type: replace Abstract: The work here enables a cost scaling of ilde{O}left( frac{T ^{1+o(1)},{rm log}^2 (1/epsilon)}{arepsilon}right) and maximum depth ilde{O}left( frac{T^{1+o(1)},{rm log} (1/epsilon)}{arepsilon}right) for estimating observables after some Hamiltonian evolution e^{i H T} using no extra block-encoding qubits, where epsilon is the algorithmic error of the target Hamiltonian evolution and arepsilon is the statistical error from the observable estimation. Throughout, ilde{O} suppresses factors that are doubly logarithmic in 1/epsilon (i.e. {rm polyloglog}(1/epsilon)), which are subdominant to the displayed {rm polylog}(1/epsilon) factors. This is achieved through product formulas, stable interpolation (Chebyshev), and to calculate the needed fractional queries, cardinal sine interpolation is used. This is an improvement over similar work using interpolation methods which have a superlinear scaling of the form 5^p T^{1+1/p}, where p is the order of the product formula used and an improvement on required qubits over Quantum Signal Processing methods while maintaining scaling with {rm polylog}{1/epsilon}. This is accomplished through an optimized and variable (with T) order p. The slightly better log^2(1/epsilon) compared to log^3(1/epsilon) comes from the use of cardinal sine interpolation compared to other methods using Chebyshev interpolation and product formulas. The work presented here also provides an alternative to Quantum Signal Processing techniques to calculate observables or amplitudes after a fractional query evolution with a cost O(Tlog{1/epsilon}) through this cardinal sine interpolation.
