arXiv:2607.23179v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Cooling a mechanical mode in finite time is a routing problem: excitation must reach a lossy mode before thermal noise rebuilds the population. Exceptional points are non-Hermitian degeneracies at which two hybrid modes and their eigenvectors merge, so a loop around them can exchange connected spectral branches. The main obstacle in testing whether this topology improves cooling is causal. Independently optimized enclosing and non-enclosing protocols normally have different waveforms, so topology and waveform geometry change together. We remove this ambiguity in a three-mode optomechanical system with a strongly damped main cavity and an exactly lossless auxiliary cavity. We keep one power modulation and one detuning shape fixed and change only their relative phase. The shift preserves the matched local control resources but changes the loop from non-enclosing to a braid around both exceptional points. Certified dynamics show that the two-exceptional-point protocol lowers the final mechanical occupation by more than 55 percent. The advantage survives the complete admissible phase family, deliberate waveform changes, a finite neighborhood of nearby smooth controls, and the restoration of counter-rotating heating processes. The auxiliary cavity acts as a coherent buffer, while the main cavity remains the only optical loss channel.
