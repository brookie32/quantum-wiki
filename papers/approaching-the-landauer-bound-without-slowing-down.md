arXiv:2407.03096v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Finite-time memory erasure is conventionally understood to incur an additional energy cost beyond the Landauer bound. Whether this bound can be approached without taking the infinite-time limit remains open. Based on a quantum speed limit, we identify coherence between degenerate states as a resource that allows the Landauer bound to be approached at a finite operation time. For both the 2N-state and collective spin models, we show that the minimum dissipated heat and the heat generated by continuous protocols approach the Landauer bound at finite operation times, with the corresponding finite-time penalty scaling inversely with system size. We further validate these theoretical predictions numerically for systems coupled to the Ohmic bosonic heat bath with various driving protocols. Our results reveal that operation time is a substitutable resource for approaching the fundamental thermodynamic bound of information erasure.
