arXiv:2611.33328v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Quantum dynamical systems driven by both unitary evolution and measurements open a new landscape of nonequilibrium phases of matter. The role of topology in enriching this landscape remains less understood than that of entanglement and symmetry. A particularly interesting question concerns protected dynamical modes localized on spacetime domain walls between topologically distinct dynamical phases. These dynamical topological domain-wall modes (DTDMs) are well understood only for 0+1D walls in 1+1D fermionic dynamics. In this work, we study 1+1D topological domain walls in 2+1D charge-conserving free-fermion dynamics, modeled by topological adaptive circuits that steer the two sides of each wall toward a Chern insulator and a trivial insulator through measurements and feedforward corrections. Although the circuits target no particular wall state, each wall hosts 1+1D chiral DTDMs. At long times in each quantum trajectory, they exhibit logarithmic scaling of entanglement and charge variance and algebraically decaying density correlations, surprisingly consistent with the ground state of a 1+1D chiral free-fermion conformal field theory. This contrasts with purely 1+1D charge-conserving free-fermion dynamics, which exhibits only area-law entanglement at finite measurement rates. We also show that the chiral DTDMs exhibit slow purification dynamics. When the system starts from a maximally mixed state, the DTDMs' entropy decays algebraically, accompanied by a vanishing Lyapunov gap, whereas each domain purifies on an O(1) timescale. Finally, we propose a physical picture for the long-time behavior of the DTDMs by treating the adaptive circuit model as a quantum cooling algorithm. Our results show that topological phases of adaptive dynamics can protect chiral modes on spacetime domain walls against repeated wavefunction collapse and Born-rule randomness.
