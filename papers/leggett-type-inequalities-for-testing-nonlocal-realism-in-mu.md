arXiv:2108.03665v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Nonlocal realism represents the last classical cornerstone in conflict with quantum theory, and has been shown to be largely untenable in bipartite systems [Nature 446, 871 (2007); Nature Physics 4, 681 (2008)]. We extend the Leggett-type nonlocal realistic model to arbitrary N-partite systems with polarizer settings, and derive rigorous inequalities that distinguish quantum predictions from nonlocal realistic theories. The derivation yields a fundamental double inequality that is universally valid, from which the Leggett-type bounds follow under specific measurement settings. As an illustration, we demonstrate quantum violations of these inequalities for Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ) states, with maximal violation 2(sqrt{5}+1). Our results show that nonlocal realism in multipartite systems is experimentally testable.
