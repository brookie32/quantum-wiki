arXiv:2610.40808v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Driven-dissipative quantum devices are routinely operated with time-dependent drives and exposed to temporally correlated classical noise. When these perturbations vary on timescales comparable to the system's intrinsic dynamics, rotating-wave and Markovian approximations can fail, making effective static descriptions unreliable. As a result, accurately modeling systems in this regime often relies on simulating the full time-dependent master equation. This is particularly demanding for processes requiring long integration times, such as rare switching between metastable states, or for stochastic perturbations requiring averaging over many noise realizations. To address these challenges, we introduce Liouvillian spectral snapshots (LSS), a reduced-basis method that enables efficient simulation of such systems by projecting the master equation onto a low-dimensional subspace capturing their coarse-grained dynamics. LSS constructs this subspace by computing the slowly decaying, low-frequency eigenoperators of the Liouvillian at a few adaptively sampled perturbation values and compressing them into a common basis. This basis is built once and can be reused across noise realizations and perturbation parameters, reducing the cost of ensemble averages and parameter sweeps.
