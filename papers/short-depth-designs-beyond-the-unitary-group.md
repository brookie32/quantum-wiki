Short‑depth quantum circuits cannot approximate full designs for several important symmetry groups unless they have lightcones or depth scaling with system size. For the Clifford, orthogonal, symplectic, and matchgate groups we prove that any design must involve a circuit lightcone at least as large as the system, and sub‑linear depth is insufficient on any architecture. Nevertheless, weaker forms of randomness—such as additive‑error state designs and anti‑concentration—do emerge at logarithmic depths in many cases.
