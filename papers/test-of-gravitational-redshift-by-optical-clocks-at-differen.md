arXiv:2611.27588v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: General relativity predicts that frequency comparisons between clocks are affected by both gravitational and kinematic time dilation. While the kinematic contribution has been tested with high accuracy, experimental tests of the gravitational redshift remain comparatively less stringent. Here, we measure the frequency shift between two Earth-fixed optical atomic clocks at different potentials and compare it to the redshift predicted by general relativity using independent geodetic measurements. The observed shift agrees with the prediction, testing the gravitational redshift at the 4.6 imes 10^{-5} uncertainty level, improving ground-based tests by a factor two. Previous ground-based tests separated the clocks mainly vertically. For clocks at different latitudes, their height difference is governed not only by the gravitational potentials at their locations but also by the centrifugal potentials caused by Earth's rotation. As both contributions can partially cancel, the gravitational contribution can be considerably larger than their height difference suggests. In this work, a separation of the clocks by 4^irc in latitude enhances their sensitivity to a gravitational redshift violation threefold, to the equivalent of an approximately 1200 m difference in height for small latitudinal separation. Our results establish Earth's rotation as a so far unexploited resource for advancing ground-based tests of the gravitational redshift towards the sensitivity of previous space-based experiments.
