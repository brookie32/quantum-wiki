arXiv:2611.27981v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Electron transport is typically described in terms of relaxation times, which measure how quickly Fermi-surface deformations decay. We show here that the thermopower of a two-dimensional Fermi liquid instead probes the structure of these deformations in the form of their local temperature change, which we call the thermal dressing. This structure leads to an overshoot of the thermopower over its hydrodynamic value - s/(ne) with increasing wave vector, before it falls to half that value in the ballistic limit. At low temperatures, the excess thermopower does not have the linear-in-temperature scaling expected of a Fermi liquid, but decreases only logarithmically as 1/ln(T_F/T) at optimal wave vector. The overshoot is caused by collinear scattering, which redistributes energy over the Fermi surface and induces the thermal dressing, the strength of which grows with the angular harmonic index m of the deformation. In contrast to tomographic effects in the charge conductivity, this overshoot does not rely on an odd-even effect in quasiparticle lifetimes and defines a distinct thermographic transport regime. The thermopower overshoot survives realistic disorder and should be detectable in a thermocouple with high-mobility samples.
