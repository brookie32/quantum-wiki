arXiv:2611.14271v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: The zero-point energy of a circuit measures the polarization fluctuation of an insulator coupled to it. In a crystal this is the bulk quantum weight K_bulk, which the Chern number C bounds below by |C|. We show that a finite Chern flake adds an edge term that survives as the flake grows. The low-frequency transitions of its chiral edge carry a dipole weight, the sum of their squared dipole matrix elements, K_edgegeq|C| per unit area, a floor that follows from the positive absorption of both circular polarizations by every transition and the known cancellation of circular dichroism between edge and bulk. A weakly coupled circuit slower than the edge modes therefore reads K_bulk+K_edge, averaged over two field directions, while a faster one below the bulk gap approaches K_bulk, so which quantum weight is measured depends on whether the flake is made large or the circuit slow first. Exact free-fermion calculations on lattice flakes bear out the floor and this dependence on the circuit frequency. The result holds at weak coupling, which a growing flake at fixed coupling eventually leaves. Above the floor, the edge weight of one channel is a number fixed by the shape of the boundary and the edge velocities, which square and rectangular lattice flakes follow to within 0.003. Each further edge channel adds its own such number, while the circular dichroism of the edge counts only the net chirality.
