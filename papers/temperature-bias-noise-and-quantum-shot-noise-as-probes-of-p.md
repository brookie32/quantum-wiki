arXiv:2603.20069v3 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Quantum noise has long served as a powerful probe of quantum transport in mesoscopic junctions. Recently, temperature-driven noise, or Delta_T noise, has attracted growing interest due to its presence even in the absence of average charge current. In this work, we investigate a normal metal-insulator-iron-pnictide junction and demonstrate how zero temperature quantum shot noise, finite temperature quantum noise and Delta_T noise can discriminate between S_{++} and S_{+-} pairing symmetries, which are relevant to iron-based superconductors. We introduce Delta_T noise as a novel probe for distinguishing between the two pairing symmetries. In contrast to conductance, which exhibits a single peak for both S_{++} and S_{+-} states with only a difference in magnitude, the Delta_T noise reveals qualitatively distinct features: a twin-peak structure for the S_{++} pairing symmetry and a single-peak profile for the S_{+-} state. A similar symmetry-dependent contrast is observed in both zero temperature quantum shot noise and finite temperature quantum noise, where the S_{++} state consistently exhibits a twin-peak structure, while the S_{+-} state shows a single-peak response. Our results demonstrate that noise-based measurements form a mutually reinforcing set of probes that enables reliable identification of superconducting gap symmetry in Iron Pnictide superconductors.
