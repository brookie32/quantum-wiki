arXiv:2512.13357v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Quantum networks promise scalable distributed quantum information processing. Realizing this potential depends critically on the efficient use of nonclassical resources. Sequential sharing of quantum nonlocality provides a resource-efficient route for recycling entanglement across multiple observers. In network settings, however, such recycling is typically constrained by a trade-off between the number of active branches and the achievable sharing depth. Here we investigate this problem in star networks using probabilistic projective measurements. We establish an explicit entanglement threshold above which nonlocality can be shared across all branches up to any target depth, and below which we determine the optimal trade-off between the number of active branches and the achievable number of sequential rounds. We further analyze the protocol's robustness to noise and its experimental observability, clarifying its practical feasibility. These results establish quantitative design principles and entanglement requirements for scalable nonlocality recycling in quantum networks.
