Phonon focusing—directional, wave-like heat transport—has been demonstrated at room temperature for the first time in boron arsenide, a high‑thermal‑conductivity crystal. Ray‑shaped temperature patterns and heat‑propagation dynamics measured experimentally agree with first‑principles Boltzmann‑transport simulations, revealing that they arise from spatial redistribution of long‑lived, non‑equilibrium phonon waves. The study identifies crystallographic orientation‑dependent transport symmetries, experimentally confirmed across multiple samples, opening avenues for directional control of phonon waves and phonon–carrier interactions in practical thermal and quantum devices.
