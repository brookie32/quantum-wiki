Monolayer NbSe₂, a superconductor whose properties quickly deteriorate in air, has been difficult to produce as large‑area, uniform films using conventional growth techniques.  
Zheng and collaborators developed an encapsulation‑epitaxy process that employs a two‑dimensional layer (graphene or h‑BN) as both a growth template and protective cap; by placing the substrate face‑to‑face with a niobium oxide film in a furnace containing selenium and NaCl, they achieved continuous monolayer NbSe₂ on SiO₂/Si.  
This method yields high‑quality, scalable superconducting films that can be integrated into electronic circuits.
