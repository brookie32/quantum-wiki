arXiv:2611.23809v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Classical time series analysis converts temporal dependence into structure that can be learned from a single observed trajectory, through stationarity, ergodicity, spectral representation, and finite-memory models. Quantum experiments are naturally time series: every run is a sequence of interventions and outcomes, and the memory of an open system is written in its temporal statistics. However, the classical foundation cannot be translated directly: measuring a process and forgetting the outcome differs from not measuring at all, so no single family of joint probability distributions describes a quantum process. We develop a process-tensor framework for stationary quantum time series and establish its first structural theorems. For stationary combs of finite quantum Markov order l relative to a fixed informationally complete causal break, we construct a finite-dimensional causal transfer map of dimension at most d^{4l}, where d bounds the system Hilbert-space dimension at each step. ... These results supply the stationary foundation on which quantum spectral analysis, model identification, and single-trajectory inference can be built.
