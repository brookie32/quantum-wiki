The editorial reflects on how physical and mathematical intuition evolve as students progress in physics, using the classic cannon‑ball versus feather experiment to show that everyday expectations can be wrong even in a vacuum. It argues that higher‑level physics develops an intuition based on deeper theoretical understanding rather than sensory experience, citing historical debates—such as those in the 1920s about determinism versus probabilistic quantum mechanics—that reshaped common‑sense concepts like causality and heliocentrism. The piece references work by Flavio Del Santo and Nicolas Gisin that illustrates how these new insights alter intuitive thinking in contemporary research.
