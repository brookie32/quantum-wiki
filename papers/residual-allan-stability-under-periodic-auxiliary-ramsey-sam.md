arXiv:2611.24928v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: The timing of auxiliary Ramsey measurements can change the frequency stability associated with a drifting pulse shift at a fixed nominal Fisher-information rate for a constant shift. For periodic instantaneous observations of an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck shift with causal prediction between updates, we derive the exact residual covariance averaged over the update cycle and the corresponding Allan variance at arbitrary averaging times. Relative to a continuous Kalman reference at the same information rate, periodic estimation increases the cycle-averaged estimation-error variance, reduces the short-time residual Allan variance, and increases its long-time value. Its covariance decays more slowly at the origin and has a larger integral. We extend the result to instantaneous sensing of the sum of finitely many independent Ornstein-Uhlenbeck modes. Integrated observations preserve the short-time ordering for the scalar shift, and numerical calculations resolve the dependence of the crossover on window duration. A zero-order-hold comparison shows that the short-time ordering can change when the same estimator drives a different correction command. A three-level Ramsey model of coherent population trapping (CPT) maps the discriminator response to an effective information budget and illustrates how the dark times affect the choice of schedule.
