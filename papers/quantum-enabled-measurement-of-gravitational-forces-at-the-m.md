arXiv:2611.08619v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Gravity is by far the weakest among all known interactions. While it dominates the large-scale structure of the universe, gravitational interactions between small masses in the microscopic range remain underexplored because they are too weak to be experimentally accessible. The interface between gravity, described by general relativity, and quantum mechanics, which describes the microscopic world, is of particular interest. From a theory standpoint, these two major descriptions of nature have not been combined in a satisfactory manner. Technology is gradually becoming mature enough such that probing gravity at the microscopic scale, where quantum mechanics and gravity potentially meet, is becoming accessible. Explorations towards genuine quantum gravity have to be carried out under conditions where the gravitating masses possess non-classical properties, which necessitates ultimate protection against disturbances, such as ultralow temperatures and a small range of motion. Here, we present an experiment at millikelvin temperatures, where the gravity due to a micron-range coherent displacement of an 87 mg source mass imprints on the motion of an oscillating membrane loaded with a 1.3 mg test mass. Vibrations of the membrane are measured in the microwave optomechanical scheme, which is a key setup for investigating non-classical motional states of mechanical oscillators. When considering the combination of total source and test masses, our setup presents two orders of magnitude progress in sensing gravity at the small scale, as well as a 35 dB improvement of gravitational sensitivity at kHz frequencies or above. Our setup offers a plausible approach for studying gravitational interactions between masses prepared in non-classical motional states.
