arXiv:2603.05137v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Classical shadows allow many properties of a quantum system to be estimated from relatively few measurements. Their standard guarantees, however, assume independent and identically distributed experimental rounds and a fixed analysis procedure. These assumptions can fail when the source drifts or the analysis adapts to previous measurement outcomes. We show that average expectation values along the run can be estimated with the usual classical-shadow sample-complexity scaling, even when the prepared states depend arbitrarily on the past and the analysis learns from earlier outcomes. For bounded shadow estimators, we further derive error bars that adapt to the observed data and remain valid throughout the experiment, allowing measurements to stop once the desired precision is reached. We illustrate these results numerically by certifying entanglement hidden by time averaging and reducing the measurements needed to estimate energy densities in many-body systems with small fluctuations.
