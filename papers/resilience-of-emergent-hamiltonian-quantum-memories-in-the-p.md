arXiv:2611.25286v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Emergent Hamiltonians can freeze an entangled state prepared through coherent evolution, since that state is an eigenstate of the Hamiltonian under which it is then stored. We show that the freedom left in this construction allows the storage spectrum to be adapted to a noisy environment without changing the stored state. For a product of Bell pairs prepared from a density wave in an engineered chain, a selection rule prevents local density noise from connecting the target to any of its degenerate partners, so that rescaling the storage Hamiltonian by a factor alpha moves every transition this noise drives. Under 1/f noise, the storage time then grows in proportion to alpha, a result we confirm by evolving the memory under explicit classical 1/f fields, whereas for an Ohmic bath the most favorable scale depends on its cutoff. Fermionic loss, treated as a solvable model, includes a transition that rescaling leaves at zero frequency, and an energy shift mu per particle moves it away from zero, with an exact orbital solution giving the resulting lifetimes. Finally, among number-conserving quadratic reference Hamiltonians, alpha and mu are the only controls that keep the storage Hamiltonian nearest neighbor at every time along the preparation, so that the protocol combines local freezing with control over the storage spectrum.
