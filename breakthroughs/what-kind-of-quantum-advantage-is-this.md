arXiv:2611.21239v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Quantum advantage encompasses fundamentally different claims, from resource reductions to exponential separations in sample complexity. Evaluating these claims requires distinguishing how an algorithm achieves an advantage from what is necessary for the separation and how strongly it is supported against classical alternatives. We propose a multi-label taxonomy that makes these distinctions explicit, classifying structural, interference, and coherent mechanisms while separately recording their necessity and the evidence for the classical comparison. Coherent mechanisms are further divided into memory and output, reflecting distinct restrictions on quantum information processing. A two-part entanglement-breaking test distinguishes loss of information from loss of algorithmic efficiency. The hidden subgroup problem illustrates the distinction: among the cases examined, cross-copy coherence is information-theoretically necessary only for the symmetric group. We present this as a diagnostic methodology rather than a theory of quantum advantage: for a new claim, it identifies the relevant adversary, separates the mechanisms a protocol uses from those shown to be necessary, and records the strength of the evidence and the quality of the advantage separately.
