In 1996, Coppersmith established the classical small-root bound for univariate modular equations, which was subsequently proved to be asymptotically optimal within the standard Coppersmith framework. In contrast, a general theoretical characterization of optimal small-root bounds for bivariate modular equations, together with a method for computing them, has remained elusive. The main difficulty is that the additional degrees of freedom introduced by a second variable turn shift selection into a complex combinatorial optimization problem. We prove that, for standard triangular shift constructions in Coppersmith’s method, optimal small-root bounds for bivariate modular equations admit a unified characterization and can be computed effectively. To this end, we introduce a class of geometric objects called quasi-Newton polygons, which provide an exact geometric characterization of these optimal bounds and yield a method for computing them. This result extends the theory of optimal small-root bounds for Coppersmith’s method to the general bivariate setting, marking its first breakthrough beyond the univariate case in three decades.
