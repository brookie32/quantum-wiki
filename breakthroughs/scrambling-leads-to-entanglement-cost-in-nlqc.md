arXiv:2611.23378v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: In a non-local quantum computation (NLQC), shared entanglement along with a single simultaneous round of quantum communication is used to implement a joint channel. NLQC has many applications, most of which benefit from an understanding of lower bounds on the entanglement needed to implement a given channel as an NLQC. However, the properties of a channel that lead to it having an entanglement cost in NLQC have remained unclear, with most lower bounds only applying to specially constructed examples. Here, we give a new lower bound technique for unitaries based on scrambling: we find that a unitary has zero entanglement cost if and only if it does not scramble. Further, we find that the tripartite mutual information, an entropic object that quantifies the degree of scrambling, provides a quantitative lower bound on entanglement cost in NLQC. This is the first known lower bound on entanglement cost for unitaries in NLQC which is faithful. Our results also relate scrambling and NLQC for the first time. Leveraging the faithful scrambling based bound, we then show that the controllable correlation, an earlier studied quantity, is also a faithful lower bound, resolving an earlier conjecture of Cleve and May.
