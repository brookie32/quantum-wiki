Flatiron Institute researchers introduced a new tensor‑network scheme capable of accurately simulating the time evolution of Ising spin glasses in two and three dimensions. On these problems their purely classical simulations outperform D‑Wave’s Advantage 2 quantum annealer, challenging the notion that quantum hardware naturally yields higher accuracy for many‑particle systems. The results raise the benchmark for classical methods and suggest that advanced tensor‑network techniques are a serious contender in the ongoing comparison between classical and quantum simulation of complex quantum mechanics.
