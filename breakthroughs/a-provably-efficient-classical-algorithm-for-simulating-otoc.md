arXiv:2611.24640v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Out-of-time-order correlators (OTOCs) are quantities of broad interest in many-body physics due to their connection with information scrambling and quantum chaos. Recently, estimating higher-order OTOCs in random quantum circuits was proposed as a promising avenue for quantum advantage, yet the classical hardness of this task remains poorly understood. Even for first-order OTOCs, where growing evidence suggests classical tractability, no efficient classical algorithm with rigorous guarantees has been established to date. In this work, we introduce "window propagation," the first provably polynomial-time classical algorithm for approximating first-order OTOCs in 1D Haar-random brickwork circuits, at arbitrary depths and with inverse-polynomial mean-squared error. We also conduct numerical experiments comparing our simulation results to exact computation on up to 512 qubits for Clifford circuits with 768 T-gates and up to 22 qubits for Haar-random circuits. We emphasize that our results do not directly apply to second-order OTOCs in 2D, which is the subject of recent quantum advantage proposals. Nevertheless, our work provides a rigorous step towards understanding the classical complexity of OTOC estimation and represents a family of non-trivial quantum circuits for which a provable, polynomial time algorithm is now known, establishing foundations for understanding these widely used diagnostics of quantum chaos.
