arXiv:2611.22906v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Accurate magnetic-field evolution of spectra requires a common wavefunction representation that consistently describes multiple states across different field regimes. Discrete Variable Representation (DVR) methods combine accurate basis-based kinetic-energy evaluation with flexible real-space potential representation, enabling arbitrary non-analytic potentials to be treated. Here, we develop a magnetic-field-dependent Hermite function-based (Hermite-B) DVR that adapts naturally to magnetic confinement, efficiently representing compressed wavefunctions across a wide range of field strengths while remaining centered in regions relevant to the scalar confinement potential. This ensures accurate treatment of the field-dependent kinetic energy while addressing two key challenges: maintaining accuracy from the Zeeman to Landau regimes and accommodating diverse confinement potentials. We also introduce a spatial restriction scheme that confines Hermite DVR grid points to a fixed-length domain, enabling compact representations for smooth potentials. The method is benchmarked for two-dimensional quantum dots in perpendicular magnetic fields against analytical models and compared with finite-grid, sine DVR, and field-free Hermite DVR calculations. The performance of field-independent DVR methods for magnetically confined systems is also investigated for the first time. Excellent agreement with exact analytical spectra and wavefunctions is obtained, with Hermite-B requiring the fewest basis functions and smaller Hamiltonian matrices, improving computational efficiency. Applications to arbitrary confinement potentials and tilted magnetic fields demonstrate its versatility beyond analytically solvable systems. The proposed method offers a robust, accurate, and efficient framework for large-scale calculations of realistic semiconductor nanostructures and molecular systems in external magnetic fields.
