In August 2026, scientists led by Shi‑Ling Zhu at South China Normal University performed the first direct experimental test of Richard Feynman’s 1948 “path integral” postulate, which predicts a quantum particle’s most probable trajectory by summing over all possible paths. Using advanced measurements of a photon propagator—an object describing how a photon's quantum state evolves—the team confirmed that the path‑integral calculation accurately matches observed behavior. This result validates a foundational principle that underpins most modern quantum physics and computational techniques.
