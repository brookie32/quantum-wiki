arXiv:2610.27065v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Understanding how photon loss changes the classical complexity of BosonSampling is central to assessing the robustness of near-term photonic quantum advantage. In this work, we present a polynomial-time classical simulation algorithm for lossy BosonSampling that applies whenever a constant fraction of the input photons survives. We consider the standard fixed-loss model, where N input photons propagate through an M-mode interferometer and exactly N-n photons are lost uniformly at random, leaving n surviving photons at the output. For any constant surviving fraction n/Nin(0,1), the algorithm samples from a distribution within total variation distance arepsilon of the fixed-loss BosonSampling distribution with probability at least 1-elta over Haar-random interferometers, in time operatorname{poly}(M,N,arepsilon^{-1},elta^{-1}). We further extend the fixed-loss analysis to the uniform-transmission-loss model and obtain analogous polynomial-time simulability for any fixed transmission rate etain(0,1). These results substantially extend the known provably simulable regime of n=O(sqrt{arepsilon N}) surviving photons or eta=O(sqrt{arepsilon/N}) transmission rate, and demonstrate that even the experimentally relevant linear-survival regime can admit efficient classical simulation under the conditions considered here.
