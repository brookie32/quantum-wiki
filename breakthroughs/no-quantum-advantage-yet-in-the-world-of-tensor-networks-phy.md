Researchers at the Flatiron Institute developed a classical tensor network scheme that simulates Ising spin glass evolution more accurately than recent quantum annealers running the same problem.   The results scale in two and three dimensions, raising the bar in competition between classical and quantum computers for simulating quantum many-particle systems.   This challenges a March 2025 "beyond-classical" computation claim by researchers using D-Wave's quantum annealing processor.
