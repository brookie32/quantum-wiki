arXiv:2611.34029v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Quantum mechanisms satisfying quantum local differential privacy (QLDP) have been shown to achieve a quantum advantage over classical local differential privacy (LDP) mechanisms in certain hypothesis-testing problems. While previous demonstrations have considered the transmission of quantum states from users to a server, we investigate whether this advantage can persist under entanglement-assisted classical communication (EACC), where users and the server share entanglement in advance and communicate only classical messages. We formulate an EACC-based hypothesis-testing framework and introduce measurement local differential privacy (MLDP) to protect private inputs against arbitrary shared quantum states and server-side measurements. We establish that QLDP classical-to-quantum channels admit MLDP-compliant EACC simulations and that, in the high-privacy regime, certain optimal QLDP mechanisms can be realized with one-bit classical communication, albeit using high-dimensional entanglement. We then demonstrate that a strict quantum advantage persists even when the pre-shared entanglement is restricted to a single qubit pair and each user communicates only one classical bit. Using EACC protocols based on the geometry of qubit measurements, we establish this advantage for both asymmetric and symmetric hypothesis testing and every input alphabet of size v geq 3 in the high-privacy limit. The ratio of the achieved error exponent to the optimal one under the LDP constraint is at least 5/4 for all such alphabets and approaches 3/2 as v grows. These results demonstrate that quantum advantage in locally private hypothesis testing can persist under simultaneous constraints on entanglement dimension and classical communication.
