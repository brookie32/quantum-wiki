arXiv:2611.09563v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Local response measurements track how perturbations spread in quantum simulators and nonequilibrium many-body systems. Locality bounds constrain the propagation speed, but do not determine whether the response can distinguish computational outcomes at fixed measurement precision as the distance increases. Here we construct time-independent dynamics with bounded nearest-neighbor interactions in which a fixed single-site detector responds to a remote bond phase after a time proportional to their separation. The susceptibility has a positive lower bound independent of distance. With product-state preparations, estimating this susceptibility to constant additive accuracy is complete for bounded-error quantum polynomial time (BQP), with a decision gap independent of distance. Two source-phase settings give the susceptibility with a distance-independent shot count at fixed accuracy and confidence. Inverse-polynomially weak local couplings between the computational register and the chain carrying the excitation preserve the gap. The leading correction vanishes when exchanging the two internal states of the excitation reverses the interaction. Varying the source and observation time makes all bond phases locally identifiable near the zero-phase reference setting. We also prove a conditional quantum advantage for distribution-free response prediction.
