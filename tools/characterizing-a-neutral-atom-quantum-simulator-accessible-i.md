arXiv:2611.25131v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Quantum simulators have become a reality in recent years, with several platforms now remotely accessible through the cloud. These systems give researchers the opportunity to run quantum programs without requiring either a dedicated laboratory or extensive experimental expertise, and, in several cases, they have already enabled the execution of relevant protocols with remarkable results. However, despite the impressive progress in the performance of these machines, they still suffer from various noise sources, some of which are not always completely clear to the end user, which limits their applicability to tasks that are sufficiently robust against noise of various kinds. In this work, we investigate the behavior of Fresnel, a remotely accessible neutral-atom analog quantum simulator provided by Pasqal. We design and perform a series of experiments probing the machine's response to various pulse sequences and experimental settings, and identify the main noise sources affecting its dynamics. Based on these data, we construct an effective model of the system's noisy evolution and use numerical optimization to infer the noise strengths. The resulting model accurately reproduces the simulator's dynamics, showing how effective noise models can be built for cloud-based quantum resources.
