arXiv:2611.12784v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: We review the basic theory of quantum channels and develop a strategy for simulating them on digital quantum computers, drawing upon the Choi-Kraus operator-sum representation and Stinespring dilation to provide a unified approach for various applications. We discuss three types of applications, offering explicit examples for each: the preparation of mixed states, measurements described by positive operator-valued measures (POVMs), and the simulation of the evolution of Markovian quantum systems. We recover fundamental concepts from quantum theory, such as the purification of a mixed state and the Naimark dilation associated with a POVM. Regarding the simulation of the evolution of a Markovian quantum system, we introduce an operator, termed the residual operator, to construct, from the system's Liouvillian, a quantum channel that serves as a first-order approximation of the quantum dynamical semigroup. This allows the simulation of quantum dynamics to be performed in a manner analogous to the Euler method for ordinary differential equations, avoiding Trotterization. We prove all the theoretical results mentioned, with the exception of the fundamental Choi-Kraus theorem concerning the operator-sum representation of quantum channels, for which we provide the appropriate references. The explicit simulations discussed were implemented using the AerSimulator from IBM's Qiskit SDK, with the corresponding code available on GitHub.
