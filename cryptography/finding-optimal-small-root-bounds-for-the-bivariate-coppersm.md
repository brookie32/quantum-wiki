Since Coppersmith introduced his lattice-based method for finding small roots of bivariate polynomial equations over the integers in 1996, a general theoretical characterization of the optimal small-root bounds achievable by this method has remained elusive. In this paper, we resolve this long-standing open problem by providing rigorous proofs of optimality and an explicit method for computing these bounds. Specifically, using our newly introduced notion of quasi-Newton polygons, we rigorously determine the optimal small-root bounds achievable by Coron's lattice construction (EUROCRYPT 2004). We further prove that computing these bounds reduces to solving a univariate equation uniquely determined by the vertices of the Newton polygon. Applying our results to cryptanalysis, we systematically evaluate the small-root bounds reported in the literature for more than twenty cryptographic settings and prove that the vast majority of these bounds are optimal. We believe that our approach will help resolve further optimality questions in cryptanalysis and provide a theoretical basis for assessing the security of the relevant cryptographic schemes.
