The LLL potential Psi^2(B)=prod_ketGram(b_1,ots,b_k), minimized over the bases of a lattice L and normalized by its determinant, defines an isodual lattice invariant eta(L). Its supremum eta_n over n-dimensional lattices, introduced in ePrint 2007/105, is the staircase case of the generalized Hermite constants of Watanabe and Meyer, for which explicit values were known only for nle4. We prove that eta_5=6, attained only by D_5 and its dual: the flag analogue of Korkine and Zolotarev's value of gamma_5. The natural reduction to four-dimensional lattices, through a function Phi_5, does not suffice: the global maximum of Phi_5 is 2^{16}/(3^4dot5^3)approx6.4727, attained not at D_4 but at an even 135-modular lattice M_*, which we have not found in the literature. A second stage, over the lifts of the remaining region, completes the proof; both stages are computer-assisted, with about 9imes10^9 leaves, each re-proved in interval arithmetic by a separately written checker; code and data are public. We also prove eta_8=2304, attained by E_8, and show that E_6, E_7, E_8 and their duals are strict local maxima of eta (for n=6,7 these are the conjectured extremal lattices). Asymptotically, logeta_n=(frac14+o(1)),n^2log n. For lattice reduction we introduce the Psi-Hermite constant mu_m, the largest first Gram–Schmidt norm in a potential-minimal basis of an m-dimensional lattice, and bound it through eta_m. This gives mu_m=gamma_m, Hermite's constant, for mle5, and mu_mleexp(O(log^2 m)) in general.
