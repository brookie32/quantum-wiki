arXiv:2611.24478v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The protocol introduced by C. H. Bennett, G. Brassard and N. D. Mermin in 1992 (BBM92) is one of the most important and widely adopted quantum key distribution (QKD) protocols. As with any other QKD protocol, its practical security depends on satisfying the assumptions underlying its theoretical security proofs. Here, we examine two common implementations of BBM92: a conventional passive receiver and a time-multiplexed configuration that halves the number of detectors. We characterise the passive polarisation analysers common to both architectures and present experimental results obtained with the time-multiplexed receiver, employing a continuous-wave (CW) pumped spontaneous parametric down-conversion (SPDC) source of polarisation-entangled photon pairs and threshold detectors. We investigate the challenges encountered during post-processing when attempting to satisfy the requirements of existing security proofs and discuss the gap between theoretical security assumptions and the extraction of a provably secure cryptographic key.
