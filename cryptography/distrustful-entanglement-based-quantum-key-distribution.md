arXiv:2611.21759v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Quantum key distribution (QKD) is the one of the most explored application of quantum information theory. While practical QKD systems are being deployed around the world, a major issue of these setups are that one has to trust the measurement devices of both parties. On the other hand, the most secure among such schemes are called device-independent (DI) QKD protocols, where no component of the setup is assumed to be trusted. However, due to their high technological demand, their loophole-free version currently remain out of practical reach. Inspired by the real world, we consider the traditional QKD in a distrustful measurement setting, that is, the measurement devices can only be trusted upto some level of confidence, which might still be large but not completely trusted. We then derive a general lower bound on the asymptotic key rate in this scenario. Then considering perfect detectors and source, and the entanglement-based BB84 (BBM) protocol, we show that to obtain secure keys one needs to trust their devices more than 82 %. However, we observe that such a protocol can not be realised with any level of distrust using the realistic detectors and sources at the current technology. Finally, we also consider the BBM protocol with arbitrary dimensional systems and observe that they are more robust to distrust than qubit systems. Consequently, with the same level of distrust, along with same detector and source efficiences, one can get better key rates with high-dimensional systems.
