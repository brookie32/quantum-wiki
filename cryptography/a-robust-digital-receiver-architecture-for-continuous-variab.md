arXiv:2610.13090v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Continuous-variable quantum key distribution utilizes advanced digital signal processing techniques to mitigate channel impairments, driving the progress of high-performance quantum secure communications. Specifically, digital heterodyne receivers are commonly implemented using down-conversion algorithms to detect both components simultaneously where the signal is modulated onto an intermediate frequency to avoid low-frequency noise and bypass stringent optical phase-locking requirements. However, a rigorous analysis of the quantum security implications of the digital down-conversion algorithm remains absent. In this paper, we investigate the algorithm process from the perspective of quantum operator evolution, revealing that the frequency folding effect inherent in down-conversion inevitably mixes the image-frequency information into the baseband signal. To address this issue, we propose a novel operator reconstruction detection scheme based on dual-path digital heterodyne receiver. By performing a non-equal weighted combination of the output operators along two optical paths in the digital domain, this scheme perfectly cancels out the image-frequency operator physically. The robust receiver architecture preserves the baseband noise-suppression advantages, inherently achieves perfect mode balancing for asymmetric optical detection losses, and fundamentally closes the image-frequency crosstalk. Practical experiments have validated the feasibility of the proposed architecture and its effective suppression of image-frequency noise, achieving a performance of 11.4 Mbps @ 42 km. This work lays a solid theoretical and algorithmic foundation for future high-performance, highly secure digital continuous-variable quantum key distribution systems.
