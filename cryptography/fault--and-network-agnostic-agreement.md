Classical fault-tolerant consensus protocols are typically designed for a specific timing assumption (e.g. synchrony, partial synchrony or asynchrony) and fault model (e.g., crash or Byzantine). We generalize the notion of a network-agnostic protocol to protocols that are agnostic with respect to network and fault type, establishing tight conditions on feasibility for a natural agreement problem across inom{2 imes 3}{2} = 15 pairs of classical models and comparing the costs and benefits of agnostic consensus with those of classical and mixed fault-tolerant protocols.
