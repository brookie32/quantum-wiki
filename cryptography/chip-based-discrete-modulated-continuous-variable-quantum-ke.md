arXiv:2611.18288v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Continuous-variable quantum key distribution offers a promising platform for scalable quantum networks through its compatibility with standard telecommunications technology and photonic integration. High-order discrete modulation facilitates practical implementation while approaching the key-rate performance of ideal Gaussian modulation, but its finite-size composable security analysis entails substantial computational overhead. Here, we develop an uncertainty-principle-based security framework that enables finite-size composable key-rate evaluation against collective attacks for constellations up to 256-QAM. By introducing a heterodyne-measured ancillary mode into the entanglement-based source description, we incorporate additional source correlations into the security analysis that allow the Heisenberg uncertainty principle alone to provide a tight bound for the unknown correlations in a covariance matrix characterizing the whole system. Matrix symmetrization further reduces the key-rate optimization to a one-dimensional search, while subsequent mode reduction yields an analytical lower bound requiring no numerical search. Experimental demonstrations using 256-QAM on discrete-component and silicon-photonic-receiver platforms achieve finite-size composable secret key rates of 17.436 and 0.519 Mbps, respectively, over a 50 km fiber link. This approach facilitates the practical deployment of high-speed, real-time and compact quantum key distribution systems. Beyond quantum key distribution, the principle of tightening uncertainty-constrained bounds via source-mode expansion offers a paradigm for exploring the information-theoretic properties of complex non-Gaussian systems.
