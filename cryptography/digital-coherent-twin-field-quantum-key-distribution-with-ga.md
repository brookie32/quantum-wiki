arXiv:2611.17970v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Quantum key distribution (QKD) enables information-theoretically secure key sharing between two distant parties. Recently, one promising QKD protocol which can distribute a secret key over long distances called twin-field QKD (TF-QKD) has been developed. However, it requires active compensation for experimental imperfections such as phase, polarization, and frequency fluctuations with expensive devices and this makes its experimental implementation challenging. Here, we propose a long-distance continuous-variable QKD (CV-QKD) protocol which is inspired by TF-QKD. Our protocol can remove these requirements by using Gaussian-modulated coherent states and digital signal processing which is widely used in recent digital coherent optical communication and experimental implementations of CV-QKD. For security analysis, we use a numerical optimization approach with a photon-number cutoff assumption. We propose a new set of constraints for numerical optimization which can efficiently constrain a highly non-Gaussian quantum state shared by two legitimate parties in our protocol. We show that the key rate of our protocol exceeds the key rate of a direct-transmission CV-QKD protocol at long distances and it can overcome the repeaterless bound.
