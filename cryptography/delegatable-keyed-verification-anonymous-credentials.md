Keyed-verification anonymous credentials (KVACs) are a variant of anonymous credentials (ACs) in which the issuer and the verifier are either the same entity or mutually trusted entities. Due to this design, KVACs can avoid some of the practical burdens of publicly verifiable ACs, such as requiring bilinear pairings, making them more practical in certain settings. Delegation of credentials is a powerful feature that enables applications where users, holding a credential, can delegate a restricted version of their credential to another user. This paper proposes the first delegatable keyed-verification anonymous credentials (DKVAC). We provide formal definitions of DKVAC, we design two pairing-free DKVAC constructions with delegation capability, we prove them secure, and we demonstrate their practicality with implementation benchmarks. Our two constructions present trade-offs between expressiveness and performance.
