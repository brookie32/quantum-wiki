Let operatorname{RM}(r,n) be the rth order Reed--Muller code of length 2^n, and let rho(r,n) denote its covering radius. The values rho(2,6)=18 and rho(2,7)=40 are known, whereas the exact value of rho(2,8) remains open. Starting from the current state of the art bound 88leqrho(2,8)leq96, we give a computer-assisted reduction of the upper bound rho(2,8)leq91. We first show that every even-weight Boolean function on eight variables is within distance 90 of a quadratic function. Changing one value then gives the bound 91 for odd-weight functions. Our main lemma addresses rank-four seven-variable quintics: when their second-order distance is 36 and their maximum quadratic-shift first-order distance is 56, they have at least 60 minimizing quadratic cosets and at most 12 cosets of cost 56. The complete proof covers all 68443 quartic classes. For every rank-four quintic, the distance-56 cosets are enumerated outright, and the 5199 words are examined individually. Profile counts, hyperplane contractions, and support moments then give the even-weight bound. We describe the calculations and enumerations, together with the classification upon which the proof depends. Instructions to verify the computer search portion may be found at exttt{https://github.com/EvelynSabo/RM28}.
