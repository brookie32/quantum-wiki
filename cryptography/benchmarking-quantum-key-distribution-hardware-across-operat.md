arXiv:2611.26925v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Quantum key distribution (QKD) is entering national fibre networks, but published secret key rates (SKRs) are measured over different distances, wavelengths, classical traffic loads and clock rates, so systems are hard to compare. We write the SKR as the product of the transmitter clock rate, the channel transmittance and the secret key per received pulse. This third factor, the noise-normalised key rate (NNKR), needs four commonly reported parameters and depends mainly on the equipment wherever the SKR is proportional to transmittance. A companion metric, the implementation efficiency (IE), compares the SKR with the ideal key rate of its channel, including classical-traffic noise. For fixed loss-limited equipment, NNKR is independent of channel loss, whereas the fraction of the repeaterless bound varies 1.6-fold over 2-25 dB. An analytic model relates the flat loss range of NNKR to the detector noise count rate and dead time. We apply both metrics to ten published coexistence experiments, and NNKR to 42,115 measurements from four systems of two vendors and four high-rate laboratory systems. For one production system, NNKR has a 6.9% coefficient of variation over 17 dB of attenuation and agrees within 0.7 dB with three of four published deployments of that platform. Under coexistence, that platform's NNKR and IE stay within 0.5 dB of their standalone values. In decibels, NNKR is a sum of component penalties, as in an optical link budget, and for one laboratory system the budget from its published parameters and measured secure fraction closes within 0.3 dB.
