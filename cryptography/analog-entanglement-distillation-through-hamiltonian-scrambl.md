arXiv:2603.10843v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Efficient entanglement distillation is a central primitive for quantum information processing and future quantum networks. Conventional protocols rely on digital quantum circuits, requiring compiled multi-qubit gate sequences and fine-grained control that place heavy demands on near-term hardware. Crucially, leading network platforms, such as trapped ions and neutral atoms, naturally support coherent many-body interactions suited for continuous-time analog evolution. Motivated by this, we introduce the Hamiltonian entanglement distillation protocol, which uses information scrambling under native dynamics for analog quantum error detection. We establish a quantitative link between out-of-time-order correlators and distillation performance, showing that efficient scrambling directly yields high output fidelity. This capability is generic across interacting many-body Hamiltonians, with typical random Hamiltonians achieving near-optimal error detection in a constant evolution time independent of system size. Extending this framework to entanglement-based quantum key distribution (QKD), we show that analog error detection combined with classical post-processing saturates the fundamental 33.3% depolarizing noise-tolerance bound. Numerical simulations of device-native Rydberg-atom and trapped-ion Hamiltonians confirm that high-fidelity distillation is achievable within current experimental coherence times. Together, these results establish native analog dynamics as a practical, low-overhead route to entanglement distillation on near-term quantum platforms.
