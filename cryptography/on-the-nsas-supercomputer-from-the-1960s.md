Harvest was a dedicated cryptanalysis machine constructed in the 1960s by IBM for the U.S. National Security Agency (NSA). Designed to break encryption codes, it represented an early example of supercomputing applied to intelligence work. The project is notable both for its technical innovations and for illustrating the NSA’s early efforts to harness advanced computing resources for cryptographic analysis.
