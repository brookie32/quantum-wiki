arXiv:2610.21469v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Two-way classical communication improves the noise tolerance of quantum key distribution, but the limits of adaptive parity processing remain unclear. For the six-state protocol with equal three-basis error rate pin[p_*,1/2], where p_*=(5-sqrt5)/10simeq27.64%, we construct a single compatible collective attack that defeats a broad class of linear postprocessing. Each public message may be an affine function of the sender's raw key, chosen adaptively from the public history. For every finite input length and every accepted transcript of positive probability, Eve's optimal probability of recovering any affine function of Alice's raw key is at least Bob's. Consequently, reliable agreement on a linear final key is incompatible with secrecy. The same conclusion holds for arbitrary publicly seeded privacy amplification after reliable recovery of a linear candidate. The proof conditions on the complete transcript, which restricts the two raw keys to affine subspaces. A local degradation of Eve's state, a known Renyi-entropy comparison, and a coset-probability inequality then give the recovery comparison. Both inequalities are proved in self-contained appendices. The result allows block splitting, regrouping, nonzero-syndrome selection, and adaptive linear reconciliation.
