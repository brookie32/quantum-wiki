arXiv:2611.09182v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Recent experimental demonstrations of Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) have attracted significant attention for their ability to establish information-theoretically secure encryption keys for next-generation communication systems. As practical deployment accelerates, the realization of compact, scalable, and high-throughput discrete-variable (DV) QKD systems has become increasingly important. While advances in photonic integration are enabling low-size, low-weight, and low-power QKD hardware, the security and performance of these systems rely heavily on efficient post-processing techniques to generate identical, secure keys. This tutorial presents a comprehensive algorithm-level review of practical post-processing techniques for DV-QKD, with particular emphasis on information reconciliation methods based on Cascade, Winnow, low-density parity-check (LDPC), and polar codes. We also analyze the theoretical asymptotic and finite-size secret key rates (SKRs) of DV-QKD and benchmark these bounds against the practical SKRs achieved using actual post-processing algorithms. Furthermore, we present a server-client model to implement the entire QKD post-processing pipeline. The performance is evaluated in terms of QBER and SKR across various system parameters, considering the complete post-processing pipeline, including sifting, information reconciliation, privacy amplification, and authentication. This tutorial also presents a graphical user interface (GUI) for the QKD post-processing methods, enabling researchers, industry practitioners, and academic users to visualize and analyze the impact of different post-processing techniques and system parameters, such as QBER, on the SKR performance of DV-QKD systems.
