We study the worst-case (2,1)-Quasi-Cyclic Syndrome Decoding problem (mathsf{QCSD}_{n,w}) over R_n = F_2[X]/(X^n - 1). Given explicit polynomials h, s in R_n and an integer w ge 0, the problem asks whether there exist x, y in R_n satisfying x + hy = s with equal block weights |x| = |y| = w. We prove that worst-case mathsf{QCSD}_{n,w} is NP-complete in the low-weight regime w < sqrt{n} by giving a polynomial-time reduction from 3-Dimensional Matching (3DM). We also show that the same reduction establishes the NP-completeness of the relaxed variant requiring only |x|, |y| le w. Additionally, our result extends to prime block lengths and to general fixed (a, b)-QCSD instances.
