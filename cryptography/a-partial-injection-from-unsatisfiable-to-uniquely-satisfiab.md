Let MQ_0(n) and MQ_1(n) be the sets of square systems of n quadratic equations in n variables over F_2 having, respectively, no solution and exactly one solution, and write alpha_0=|MQ_0(n)|, alpha_1=|MQ_1(n)|. It is known that alpha_00.745, uniformly in nge3, on which au is injective into MQ_1(n). We then show that inverting au is equivalent to locating a zero of the image system --- the solvable case of the multivariate quadratic problem, and its one-solution case on the uniquely solvable part of the image --- and that au is randomly self-reducible on each orbit of its symmetry group. Under the standard assumption that solvable MQ is hard on average --- the planted-MQ assumption of MQ-based identification and signature schemes --- au is therefore a one-way function, injective on D, explicit and randomly self-reducible on each orbit, between two sets which are, as languages, complexity-theoretically far apart. We also show that the associated flat-collision problem is NP-complete.
