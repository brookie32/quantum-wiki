arXiv:2609.38986v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Pseudorandom error-correcting codes (PRCs), introduced by Christ and Gunn [CRYPTO 2024], are classical error-correcting codes whose codewords are computationally indistinguishable from uniformly random strings. We initiate the study of quantum analogues of PRCs. We define quantum pseudorandom error-correcting codes (QPRCs). Assuming that Learning Parity with Noise (LPN) is hard for 2^{O(sqrt{n})}-time quantum algorithms, we construct QPRCs whose encodings are indistinguishable from Haar-random isometries. We call these pseudorandom isometric error-correcting codes (PRICs), and our construction is robust to all o(n frac{log log n}{log n})-local quantum noise, where n is the number of physical qubits. Under the same assumption, we also construct QPRCs whose encodings are indistinguishable from the completely depolarizing channel and that are robust to all alpha n-local quantum noise for some constant alpha>0. The latter QPRCs give a direct quantum analogue of classical PRCs. To construct PRICs, we develop two technical ingredients of independent interest. First, we introduce a new classical cryptographic primitive which we call pseudorandom functional error-correcting codes (PRFCs) and construct them under the same LPN assumption. Second, we develop a new efficient decoding procedure within the codeword-stabilized (CWS) framework introduced in [Cross, Smith, Smolin, and Zeng, IEEE ISIT 2008], which is a generic framework for constructing quantum error-correcting codes by combining (possibly nonlinear) classical error-correcting codes and graphs. This resolves the open problem of finding a general method for efficiently decoding such CWS codes based on nonlinear classical codes, raised in [Li, Dumer, Grassl, and Pryadko, Physical Review A 2010].
