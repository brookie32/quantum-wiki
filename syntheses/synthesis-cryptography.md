# Cryptography: Knowledge Wiki Synthesis

## Overview
Cryptography is undergoing a fundamental transition driven by the dual pressures of quantum computing threats and increasingly sophisticated classical attacks. The field is rapidly standardizing post-quantum cryptographic algorithms while simultaneously advancing homomorphic encryption, threshold signing, and hardware security. Enterprise adoption is accelerating as organizations recognize the urgency of migrating legacy systems before quantum decryption becomes viable.

---

## Key Developments
- **Post-quantum cryptography (PQC)** standardization is maturing, with new hardness assumptions (e.g., quantum stabilizer decoding) diversifying the foundation beyond a dangerously small set of problems
- **Hybrid key establishment** protocols combine classical and post-quantum KEMs to provide downgrade resilience during the transition period
- **Homomorphic encryption** (notably CKKS) is advancing in precision and efficiency, enabling computation on encrypted data
- **Threshold cryptography** (e.g., robust ECDSA at scale) is gaining traction for distributed trust in blockchain and financial infrastructure
- **Side-channel attacks** on SHA-256/HMAC remain active research concerns, threatening implementations even when algorithms are sound
- **DNS and public Wi-Fi attacks** highlight that credential theft often bypasses cryptography entirely through infrastructure manipulation
- **Quantum RAM and oblivious computation** are emerging as building blocks for secure delegated quantum queries

---

## Key Players & Organizations
- **NSA / NIST** – historical influence and ongoing PQC standardization
- **IBM** – long-standing cryptographic hardware heritage; quantum computing leadership
- **Keyfactor** – enterprise certificate and PQC migration management ($200M+ ARR)
- **Academic research groups** – driving LWE algorithms, HSP theory, and lightweight cipher analysis

---

## Outlook
The next 3–5 years will be defined by enterprise PQC migration urgency, broader deployment of homomorphic encryption in cloud services, and intensifying scrutiny of implementation-level vulnerabilities. Lightweight cryptography for IoT and ultra-constrained devices will become increasingly critical as connected infrastructure expands.
