# Cryptography: Knowledge Wiki Overview

## Current State
Cryptography is undergoing a fundamental transition driven by the dual threats of quantum computing and AI-assisted attacks. The field is rapidly standardizing post-quantum cryptographic (PQC) algorithms while simultaneously advancing homomorphic encryption, threshold signing, and hardware security. Enterprise adoption of PQC migration is accelerating, with the market reflecting urgent demand for quantum-resistant infrastructure.

---

## Key Developments
- **Post-quantum cryptography** is diversifying beyond a small number of hardness assumptions, with new approaches including quantum stabilizer decoding and hybrid classical/PQC key establishment
- **Homomorphic encryption** (notably CKKS) is advancing with high-precision techniques that reduce modulus consumption during encrypted computation
- **Threshold cryptography** is maturing, with robust ECDSA threshold signing protocols now viable at scale for blockchain and financial applications
- **Side-channel attacks** on SHA-256 and HMAC via Soft Analytical methods reveal persistent vulnerabilities in deployed hash functions
- **DNS and Wi-Fi infrastructure attacks** demonstrate that cryptographic weaknesses in everyday network environments remain actively exploited
- **Quantum memory security** (OQRAM) is emerging to protect delegated quantum computations
- **LWE algorithm advances** are refining the theoretical boundaries of lattice-based cryptographic hardness assumptions

---

## Key Players & Organizations
- **NSA** – longstanding cryptanalysis and standards influence
- **Keyfactor** – enterprise PKI and PQC migration ($200M+ ARR)
- **IBM** – historical (Harvest supercomputer) and ongoing cryptographic hardware
- **Academic researchers** – driving PQC, homomorphic encryption, and quantum cryptography theory

---

## Outlook
The near-term priority is completing post-quantum standardization and enterprise migration before cryptographically relevant quantum computers emerge. Lightweight cryptography for IoT, homomorphic encryption for private computation, and AI-assisted cryptanalysis will define the next generation of both attacks and defenses.
