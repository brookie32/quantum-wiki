## Current State

Machine learning is increasingly intersecting with quantum computing, giving rise to **Quantum Machine Learning (QML)** as a distinct and active research frontier. As of 2025–2026, the field faces both genuine breakthroughs and well-documented limitations, particularly around gradient vanishing and scalability beyond linear objectives.

---

## Key Developments

- **Gradient/scalability problems** persist: algorithms frequently fail beyond linear objectives, with gradients vanishing rapidly in deeper quantum-classical models
- **Hybrid quantum-classical frameworks** (e.g., Harmoniq) are combining quantum circuits with classical data pipelines as a practical workaround
- **Tensor-train surrogate methods** are emerging to reduce the computational cost of repeated quantum circuit evaluations — a key QML bottleneck
- **Compact/edge AI models** (e.g., LittleLamb by Multiverse Computing) are being developed for on-device and agentic deployment

## Outlook

QML remains **promising but pre-mature** for general deployment. Near-term progress will likely focus on hybrid architectures, error mitigation, and surrogate modeling rather than fully quantum solutions. Edge AI and agentic applications represent a parallel, more immediately deployable growth vector.
